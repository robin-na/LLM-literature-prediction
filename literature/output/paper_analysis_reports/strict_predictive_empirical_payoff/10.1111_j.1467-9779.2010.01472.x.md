# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Cooperation and Status in Organizations
Authors: Eckel, C. C., Fatas, E., & Wilson, R.
Journal: Journal of Public Economic Theory
Year: 2010

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- efficiency (group earnings as a percentage of maximum possible earnings)
- individual and group earnings
- contribution gains (difference in contributions with and without punishment)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution levels
- punishment sent and received
- mimicry of central player
- retaliatory punishment
Outcome notes:
The paper reports both payoff-based outcomes (efficiency, earnings) and non-payoff behavioral outcomes (contributions, punishment behavior, mimicry). Efficiency is explicitly analyzed as group earnings relative to maximum possible earnings.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated public goods game with a star network (one central, three peripheral players), the introduction of costly punishment increases contributions and efficiency only when the central player is low-status (assigned by lowest trivia quiz score). In high-status groups (central player is highest scorer), punishment does not increase contributions or efficiency. Peripheral players are more likely to mimic high-status central players, but high-status central players do not set higher contribution levels. Punishment is used more in low-status groups, and low-status central players are punished more and earn less. Efficiency, measured as group earnings relative to the maximum possible, increases with punishment only in low-status groups. The number of rounds does not affect efficiency trends, which remain flat over time.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games is moderated by the status of a central player in a star network. If the central player is low-status, enabling punishment increases efficiency; if high-status, punishment has little or no effect on efficiency. The game design dimensions (player count, network structure, MPCR, punishment cost/tech, information structure) are all clearly specified, making the findings directly relevant for predicting efficiency outcomes in similar lab settings. The results suggest that, in games with a central leader and status manipulation, the status of the leader is a key moderator of punishment's effect on efficiency. Control efficiency (without punishment) is similar across treatments, but the efficiency gain from punishment is present only in low-status central player groups.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 4 players (1 central, 3 peripheral) in a star network.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session has 40 rounds: 20 without punishment, 20 with punishment.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players; interaction is via computerized interface.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players choose how much of 50 ECUs to contribute each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.5 (each ECU contributed yields 0.5 ECU to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost is nonlinear: 1 point costs 2 ECUs, 2 points cost 4 ECUs, up to 10 points costing 60 ECUs (see Table 1).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the target's profit by 10%, up to 100% (10 points).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of rounds; the punishment phase is a surprise restart at round 21.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players observe their own earnings and the decisions of those they are connected to (central player sees all, peripheral sees only central).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is only possible between connected players; the identity of the punisher is known due to the network structure (central can punish all, peripheral can only punish central).

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a specific star network structure with a single central player; results may not generalize to other network structures or fully connected groups.
- Status is induced via trivia quiz performance, which may be a relatively weak or artificial status manipulation compared to real-world status.
- The sample consists of undergraduate students from a single university, which may limit external validity.
- The punishment cost structure is nonlinear and may not match other standard punishment game designs.
- No reward mechanism is present, so findings are limited to punishment-only environments.
- Efficiency is measured as group earnings relative to maximum possible, but the paper does not report raw efficiency numbers for all conditions, only trends and differences.
