## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
This study finds that in a one-shot voluntary contribution mechanism (VCM) with four players, the introduction of costly opportunities to sanction or reward other group members does not significantly increase group account contributions or allocative efficiency compared to a baseline VCM without such opportunities. The mean allocative efficiency is highest in the baseline (77.37%), and is similar or slightly lower in the sanction and reward treatments, with no statistically significant differences. Subjects do use sanctions and rewards, and these are directed as expected (punishing low contributors, rewarding high contributors), but the costs of these actions offset any potential gains in cooperation, resulting in no net efficiency improvement. Making the imposition of sanctions or rewards uncertain (vs. certain) also has no significant effect on contributions or efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
For prediction tasks, this paper provides strong evidence that in one-shot, four-player, continuous-contribution VCMs with a marginal per-capita return of 0.5, the introduction of costly punishment or reward opportunities does not increase efficiency relative to the control. The costs of punishment and reward offset any behavioral increases in cooperation, resulting in similar or slightly lower efficiency. The results suggest that, in one-shot settings, enabling punishment or reward is unlikely to improve group efficiency, and may even reduce it slightly due to the direct costs incurred. The findings are directly relevant for settings with no repeated interaction, no communication, and anonymous groupings.

- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between subjects.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: subjects allocate any number of 10 tokens between private and group account.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is 0.5 (each group token gives $0.50 to each of 4 members, $2.00 total per token).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Each 'green' token used to sanction costs the punisher $0.20 (since unused tokens are worth $0.20 in private account).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Each sanction token reduces the target's earnings by $0.20 (certain) or expected $0.20 (uncertain: 50% chance of $0.40 reduction).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Reward treatments are included; each token can increase another's earnings by $0.20 (certain) or expected $0.20 (uncertain: 50% chance of $0.40).
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Each reward token costs the rewarder $0.20 (since unused tokens are worth $0.20 in private account).
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Each reward token increases the recipient's earnings by $0.20 (certain) or expected $0.20 (uncertain: 50% chance of $0.40).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know each decision situation is one-shot; no repeated interaction.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After the first stage, subjects are informed of the aggregate and individual allocations to the group account and their own earnings.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment and reward are anonymous; individual decisions are not linked to subject identifiers.

## Important Limitations
- The study is limited to one-shot games; results may not generalize to repeated or dynamic settings.
- All groups are of size 4; effects of group size are not tested.
- Punishment and reward are anonymous; no identification of who punished or rewarded whom.
- The magnitude of punishment and reward is fixed and relatively small; larger or more effective sanctions/rewards may yield different results.
- Subjects are undergraduate students from a single university, which may limit generalizability.
- No communication or norm formation is possible; results may differ with communication or pre-play agreements.
