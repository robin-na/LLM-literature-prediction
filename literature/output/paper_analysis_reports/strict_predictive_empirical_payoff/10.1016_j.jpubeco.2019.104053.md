## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper experimentally compares three punishment institutions (individual, democratic, dictator) in repeated 5-person public goods games, under both perfect and imperfect monitoring. Democratic punishment, where a majority vote is required to punish, results in significantly higher average net profits (efficiency) and higher contributions than individual or dictator punishment. This is true in both monitoring conditions, though the efficiency advantage is somewhat reduced under noise. The improvement is due to a sharp reduction in anti-social punishment (punishing cooperators), while pro-social punishment (punishing defectors) is maintained. Dictator punishment does not outperform individual punishment in terms of efficiency. The study provides direct, quantitative evidence on the effect of punishment institution and monitoring noise on group efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong, directly relevant evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in repeated public goods games. It shows that the institutional design of punishment (democratic vs. individual vs. dictator) and the observability of contributions (perfect vs. noisy) are key moderators. Democratic punishment substantially increases efficiency compared to individual punishment, especially by reducing anti-social punishment. However, the efficiency gain is somewhat sensitive to monitoring noise. The paper provides exact values for average net profits under each condition, allowing for quantitative calibration of prediction models. The design dimensions (player count, rounds, MPCR, punishment cost/tech, observability, etc.) are all clearly specified, making the results highly transferable to similar prediction tasks.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use groups of 5 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use 20 repeated rounds.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No chat or communication between players is allowed; decisions are made via computer interface.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is all-or-nothing: each player decides whether to contribute their entire endowment (50 points) or nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.3 (each 50-point contribution yields 15 points to each of 5 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment costs 5 points to the punisher per punishment action (individual, dictator, or democratic).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment action reduces the target's payoff by 15 points (individual) or 60 points (democratic/dictator, i.e., 15 points from each of 4 group members).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the total number of rounds (20) in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, players are shown the (public record of) each group member's contribution and the punishment/votes received.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Group members are assigned fixed IDs and are identifiable throughout the experiment; punishment/vote sources are known.

## Important Limitations
- All experiments use groups of 5; results may not generalize to larger or smaller groups.
- Contribution is all-or-nothing, not continuous; effects may differ in continuous settings.
- No reward mechanism is studied; only punishment institutions are compared.
- The study uses a fixed MPCR (0.3); results may differ with other MPCR values.
- Monitoring noise is limited to a 10% error rate; higher or lower noise may yield different effects.
- Laboratory setting with student subjects may limit external validity.
- The democratic punishment institution uses majority rule; other voting thresholds are not tested.
