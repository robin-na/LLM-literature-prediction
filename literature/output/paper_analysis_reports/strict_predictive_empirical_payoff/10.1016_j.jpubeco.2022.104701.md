# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Social norms, sanctions, and conditional entry in markets with externalities: Evidence from an artefactual field experiment
Authors: Riehm, T., Fugger, N., Gillen, P., Gretschko, V., & Werner, P.
Journal: Journal of Public Economics
Year: 2022

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average earnings
- group output
- relative earnings (with vs. without competition)
- number of individuals/groups benefiting from competition
Non-payoff outcomes:
- average contribution
- cooperation rate
- group ranking
- individual ranking
Outcome notes:
The paper reports both behavioral outcomes (contributions, cooperation) and payoff-based outcomes (earnings, group output, welfare). Efficiency is discussed both as group output and as effective earnings (treating penalties as waste).

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that all-can-win intergroup competition (where multiple groups can be ranked first and avoid penalties) leads to large increases in cooperation and group output across all treatments. The effect is strongest when penalties for not being ranked first are sharp (T1, T2), supporting near-perfect cooperation and benefiting a majority of individuals, though not all. In these cases, average earnings are about the same with and without competition, as severe penalties for a few groups offset gains for the majority. In the weaker-penalty treatment (T3), cooperation increases less, and the effect on earnings is neutral. The results suggest that the design of the penalty function (i.e., the sharpness of incentives) is crucial for both cooperation and efficiency outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that introducing a rank-based, all-can-win intergroup competition mechanism in a standard linear public goods game can substantially increase cooperation and group output, especially when penalties for low-ranked groups are sharp. However, the effect on efficiency (average earnings) is not always positive: while most individuals benefit, severe penalties for a minority can offset group-level gains. For prediction, the sharpness of the penalty function (punishment tech) and the degree of coordination among groups are key moderators. If the control game (no competition) has low efficiency, introducing sharp all-can-win competition is likely to increase group output and may increase average earnings, but the risk of severe losses for some groups must be considered. The findings are directly relevant for predicting efficiency outcomes in PGG-like environments with intergroup competition as a sanctioning mechanism.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Player count per group is 4 in T1, 3 in T2 and T3. Each session has 5 groups.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each part (with and without competition) lasts 10 periods, for a total of 20 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: participants choose any amount from their endowment to contribute.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 in T1 (n=4), 0.5 in T2 and T3 (n=3).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a reduction in group earnings via a conversion factor based on group rank; the 'cost' is the lost earnings for low-ranked groups.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment tech (severity of penalty for low rank) is varied across treatments: T1 has sharpest penalties, T3 the weakest. Stronger punishment tech leads to higher cooperation and more positive efficiency effects for the majority.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are informed of the number of periods in each part.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are informed of their group's rank and conversion factor, which is based on the group account totals of all groups.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a specific form of intergroup competition (all-can-win, rank-based penalties) rather than individual-level punishment or peer-sanctioning.
- The effect on efficiency (average earnings) is not always positive; severe penalties for a minority can offset gains for the majority.
- The study is conducted in a laboratory setting with student subjects, which may limit generalizability.
- No communication is allowed, which may differ from real-world group settings.
- No reward mechanism is tested; only penalties for low-ranked groups are considered.
- Coordination problems and heterogeneity in group cooperativeness can affect outcomes, especially in treatments with weaker penalties.
