# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Punishment Despite Reasonable Doubt-A Public Goods Experiment with Sanctions Under Uncertainty
Authors: Grechenig, K., Nicklisch, A., & Thöni, C.
Journal: Journal of Empirical Legal Studies
Year: 2010

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- efficiency (group earnings relative to maximum possible)
- average earnings
- welfare
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rates
- punishment frequency and intensity
- response to punishment (change in contribution)
- antisocial punishment
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is efficiency (group earnings as a percentage of the maximum possible). The paper also reports average earnings, contribution rates, and detailed punishment behaviors.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a standard repeated public goods game with and without punishment, and with varying levels of noise in information about others' contributions, the paper finds that: (1) Punishment increases efficiency and cooperation only when information is accurate; (2) With moderate or high noise, punishment does not improve cooperation and reduces efficiency below the no-punishment baseline; (3) Subjects continue to punish under noise, but punishment is less targeted and more frequent, leading to more antisocial punishment and welfare loss; (4) The efficiency loss from punishment under noise is substantial and persistent, even though subjects could choose not to punish. The results suggest that the effectiveness of punishment as a cooperation-enforcing mechanism is highly contingent on the accuracy of information about others' behavior.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the efficiency benefit of enabling punishment in public goods games depends critically on the accuracy of information about others' contributions. When information is accurate, punishment increases efficiency; when information is noisy, punishment reduces efficiency below the no-punishment baseline. For prediction, this means that the presence of noise or uncertainty in monitoring is a key moderator: if the game design includes noisy or imperfect information about contributions, enabling punishment is likely to decrease efficiency. If information is accurate, punishment is likely to increase efficiency. Other design dimensions (player count, rounds, MPCR, punishment cost/tech) are held constant in this study, so the main actionable moderator is information accuracy.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games use 4 players per group.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games have 10 periods (rounds).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players choose any integer from 0 to 20 ECU.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (MPCR) is 0.4 (each ECU contributed yields 0.4 ECU to each player).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 1 ECU per punishment point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the punished player's income by 3 ECU.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game has 10 periods (finite, known horizon).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players receive signals about each other player's contribution (with varying accuracy depending on treatment).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed about the number of punishment points received, but not who assigned them (punishment is anonymous).

## Important Limitations
- The only game dimension varied is the accuracy of information about others' contributions; all other design parameters are held constant.
- The study uses a fixed group size (4), fixed MPCR (0.4), fixed punishment cost/tech, and no reward option, so results may not generalize to other parameterizations.
- The experiment is conducted in a lab setting with student subjects, which may limit external validity.
- The study does not explore the effects of communication, group identity, or other social context variables.
- Punishment is always anonymous; the effects of identifiable punishment are not tested.
