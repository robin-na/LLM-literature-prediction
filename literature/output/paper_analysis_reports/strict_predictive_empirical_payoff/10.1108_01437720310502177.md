# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Ostracism in work teams: a public good experiment
Authors: Masclet, D.
Journal: International Journal of Manpower
Year: 2003

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average group payoff
- relative payoff gain
- aggregate earnings
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution level
- frequency of exclusion
- change in contribution after exclusion
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group payoff (average earnings) and contribution levels. The paper explicitly reports the effect of exclusion (ostracism) on group payoffs and contributions. Exclusion is treated as a form of non-monetary punishment.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that allowing group members to exclude (ostracize) others after observing their contributions in a public good game leads to much higher contributions and significantly higher group payoffs compared to a control without exclusion. Both costless and costly exclusion treatments yield similar increases in efficiency, with group payoffs rising by about 28-30% relative to the control. Actual exclusions are rare, but the threat is sufficient to sustain cooperation. The effect is robust to whether exclusion is directly costly or not. The study provides direct evidence that non-monetary peer punishment (ostracism) can substantially improve efficiency in public goods environments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling exclusion (ostracism) in a standard public good game with four players and continuous contributions leads to a large and robust increase in efficiency (group payoff), regardless of whether exclusion is directly costly or not. For prediction tasks, the presence of an exclusion mechanism (even if rarely used) should be expected to substantially increase efficiency relative to a control without punishment, under similar game parameters (n=4, 10 rounds, MPCR=0.4, no chat, continuous contributions, no rewards). The effect is observed even when the control efficiency is low and is robust to the cost structure of exclusion. The findings are directly relevant for predicting the effect of enabling punishment-like mechanisms (ostracism) in similar lab PGG designs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each treatment consists of 10 periods (rounds).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous (subjects choose any amount from 0 to 20 ECU).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each ECU contributed yields 0.4 ECU to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Two treatments: costless exclusion (cost = 0) and costly exclusion (cost = 5 ECU per exclusion). Both increase efficiency similarly.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is exclusion from the second public good; one exclusion suffices for ostracism. Exclusion is highly effective (removes player from next round).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the length of each treatment (10 periods).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects are informed about individual contributions to the first public good before exclusion decisions.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects are never informed about who punished (excluded) them; punishment is anonymous.

## Important Limitations
- The exclusion mechanism is a radical form of punishment (ostracism), not a standard monetary punishment; generalization to other forms of punishment may be limited.
- The experiment uses a fixed group size (n=4) and a specific MPCR (0.4); effects may differ with other parameters.
- No communication is allowed; results may not generalize to settings with chat or richer social context.
- The exclusion only applies to the second of two public good games per period, which is a specific design feature.
- No reward mechanism is present, so interaction effects with rewards are not tested.
- The study is conducted in a lab with student subjects, which may limit external validity.
