Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff

Non-payoff outcomes:

Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group earnings (payoff/welfare) and contribution rates. The paper also analyzes punishment assignment and behavioral responses, but the primary focus is on efficiency and welfare.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated public goods game with groups of 4 over 15 rounds, three treatments were compared: monetary punishment (MP), nonmonetary punishment (NP), and both punishments (BP). All sanctioning systems increased contributions and welfare relative to no-sanction baselines from prior literature. NP (informal) sanctions alone initially produced high welfare but lost effectiveness over time. MP (monetary) sanctions sustained higher contributions but incurred enforcement costs, reducing efficiency. The BP treatment, combining both, resulted in the highest group earnings and contributions in the later rounds, with lower deadweight loss from punishment than MP. The combined system allows for nuanced sanctioning, using costless informal sanctions when effective and costly formal sanctions when needed, maximizing efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a standard VCM with 4 players, 15 rounds, and no communication, enabling both monetary and nonmonetary punishment increases group efficiency (payoff/welfare) above either system alone. The efficiency gain is especially pronounced in later rounds, as informal-only sanctions lose effectiveness. The combined system also reduces the deadweight loss from punishment compared to monetary-only systems. For prediction, if the control (no-punishment) efficiency is known, enabling both types of punishment should be expected to yield a substantial and sustained increase in efficiency, with the combined system outperforming either punishment type alone. The results are directly relevant for standard PGG/VCM designs with similar parameters.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consisted of 4 players; all treatments used groups of 4.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consisted of 15 rounds (periods), known in advance.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat was allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice was continuous: players could contribute any amount from 0 to 20 ECU.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each ECU contributed yields 0.4 ECU to each of 4 group members).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost schedule: 1-30 ECU to assign 1-10 points (nonlinear, see Table 1). Nonmonetary punishment is costless.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each monetary punishment point reduces the target's earnings by 10% of stage 1 earnings, up to 100%. Nonmonetary punishment has no direct payoff effect.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism was available; only punishment (monetary and/or nonmonetary) was possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players knew in advance that there would be exactly 15 periods.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, players saw a summary including their own earnings, total points received, cost of points allocated, own contribution, and total group contribution. During punishment, they saw all group members' contributions (anonymized).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment was anonymous: players saw only the total number of points received, not who assigned them.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a fixed group size (4 players) and a specific MPCR (0.4), so generalizability to other group sizes or MPCRs may be limited.
- No communication or chat was allowed; results may differ in settings with communication.
- The nonmonetary punishment is costless and only signals disapproval; real-world informal sanctions may have different properties.
- The time horizon is 15 rounds; longer or indefinite games may yield different dynamics.
- The experiment was conducted with university students in New Zealand, which may limit external validity.
