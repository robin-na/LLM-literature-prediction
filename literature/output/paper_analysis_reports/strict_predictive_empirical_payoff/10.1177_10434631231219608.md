## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a dynamic common-pool resource (CPR) game with four players and repeated rounds, the introduction of sanctioning (punishment) mechanisms—either strict or graduated—leads to higher cooperation and resource sustainability compared to no sanctioning. Strict sanctioning (fixed, high-cost punishment) initially results in higher resource size and profit, but graduated sanctioning (punishment severity increases with repeated defection) leads to greater cooperation and lower appropriation over time, eventually closing the gap in resource size and profit. When groups can endogenously choose their sanctioning mechanism (via majority vote every five rounds), cooperation and resource outcomes improve further, especially in the long run. However, the differences in group and individual profit between sanctioning types are small and diminish over time. The study provides partial support for the effectiveness of graduated and endogenously chosen sanctioning, especially for long-term cooperation, but strict sanctioning may be more effective in short games or early rounds.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct, high-quality evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in repeated, dynamic CPR games with endogenous resource dynamics and mutual monitoring. The results suggest that both strict and graduated punishment mechanisms increase efficiency (resource sustainability and group profit) relative to no punishment. Strict punishment is more effective in the short term, but graduated punishment becomes equally or more effective over longer time horizons. Endogenously chosen punishment mechanisms (via group voting) further enhance cooperation and resource outcomes, though the effect on profit is small. For prediction tasks, the paper supports the expectation that enabling punishment will increase efficiency, with the magnitude and time course depending on the punishment regime and whether the institution is endogenously chosen. The findings are most applicable to settings with 4 players, 40 rounds, continuous contribution/appropriation, no chat, and mutual monitoring.

- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. 10 rounds without sanctioning, then 40 rounds with sanctioning. The time horizon is important: strict sanctioning is better early, graduated sanctioning catches up over time.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Appropriation/contribution is continuous: players choose any amount from 0 to 50.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Returns per unit appropriated depend on resource size; at maximum resource, return is 3 per unit invested (see formula). Not a classic fixed MPCR, but functionally similar.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Strict: cost to punisher is 5 per punishment, punished loses 15. Graduated: cost is 1, 3, 5, or 7 depending on defector's history. Punishment cost increases with repeated defection.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: each unit spent by punisher reduces target's payoff by 3. Graduated sanctioning increases severity with repeated offenses.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the total number of rounds (10 without sanctioning, then 40 with sanctioning).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each period, players observe what the other fishermen have done (appropriation).

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a student sample in a laboratory setting, which may limit generalizability to real-world CPR contexts.
- The game is limited to 4 players and 40 rounds; effects may differ with larger groups or longer/shorter time horizons.
- The CPR game is not a classic linear public goods game; resource returns are dynamic and depend on prior group behavior.
- No pure punishment-off control is included in the 40-round main experiment; only a pre-treatment phase without punishment.
- No reward or positive sanctioning mechanism is tested.
- Players know the number of rounds, which may affect end-game behavior.
- No communication or chat is allowed, which may differ from real-world commons management.
