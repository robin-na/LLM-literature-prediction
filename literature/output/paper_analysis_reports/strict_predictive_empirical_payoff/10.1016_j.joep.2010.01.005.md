The paper reports both contribution rates and group/total earnings, with explicit discussion of efficiency and net payoff effects. Efficiency is primarily proxied by group earnings, with discussion of the impact of punishment costs.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study implements a public goods game with a novel random punishment mechanism: after each round, each group member is excluded from the group benefit with probability 1-R, where R is the normalized group contribution. This 'blind' punishment does not target individual defectors. The experiment compares three conditions: standard VCM (no punishment), random punishment without redistribution (RP), and random punishment with redistribution (RPR). Results show that both RP and RPR significantly increase contributions compared to VCM. However, only RPR increases group earnings (efficiency), as RP's punishment cost outweighs the contribution gains. The effect of random punishment is similar whether introduced after a baseline or from the start. Behavioral analysis shows that high contributors reduce their contributions if punished, but overall, conditional cooperation persists and the net effect is positive for contributions. The mechanism does not fully prevent the decline in contributions over time.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that random, non-individually-targeted punishment can increase contributions in a standard public goods game, but its effect on efficiency depends on whether the penalty is redistributed. For prediction tasks, the presence of a punishment mechanism that is not individually targeted but is probabilistically linked to group performance will likely increase contributions, but may not increase efficiency unless the punishment cost is recycled within the group. The effect is robust to order and does not depend on communication or knowledge of individual contributions. The marginal per-capita return (mpcr) is 0.5, and the group size is 4. The findings suggest that, for similar designs, enabling random punishment will increase contributions and may increase efficiency if the punishment cost is not lost to the group.

- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication between participants was strictly forbidden.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous from 0 to 100 ECU; not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (mpcr) is 0.5 (b=2, n=4).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. In RP, the cost of punishment is the loss of group benefit for excluded members; in RPR, the cost is neutral for the group as the penalty is redistributed.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment is probabilistic exclusion from group benefit, with probability 1-R (R = normalized group contribution). The impact is the loss of group benefit for the round.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told the number of rounds in each block (20 rounds per block).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants see the ranked vector of group contributions (not identities), and information about group performance and number of excluded subjects.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is random and not linked to individual behavior; participants are told whether they were punished (excluded) but not who punished whom (since punishment is not assigned by individuals).

## Important Limitations
- The punishment mechanism is not individually targeted; it is random and based on group performance, which may not generalize to settings with targeted or peer punishment.
- Efficiency gains are only observed when punishment costs are redistributed within the group; otherwise, punishment increases contributions but reduces group earnings.
- The design does not include a reward mechanism, so findings may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
- The experiment uses a fixed group size (n=4) and mpcr=0.5; results may differ with other parameters.
- The mechanism may be perceived as procedurally unfair, which could affect long-term cooperation or external validity.
- No communication is allowed; results may differ in settings with communication.
