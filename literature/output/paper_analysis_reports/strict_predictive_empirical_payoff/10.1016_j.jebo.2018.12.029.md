# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Endogenous sanctioning institutions and migration patterns: Experimental evidence
Authors: Cobo-Reyes, R., Katz, G., & Meraglia, S.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average group payoff
- average individual payoff
- payoff inequality
- welfare
- efficiency (relative to full cooperation)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate
- migration rate
- voting for sanctions
- group composition
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are average payoffs (group and individual), payoff inequality, and efficiency. Contribution rates, migration, and voting are also analyzed, but efficiency and payoff are central.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated public goods game with two groups and endogenous migration, the presence of formal sanctioning institutions (a fixed fee plus a fine on private account allocations) leads to higher contributions and significantly higher average payoffs compared to sanction-free groups. When only one group has sanctions (No-Voting treatment), that group becomes overpopulated, and payoff inequality emerges. When groups can vote on whether to implement sanctions (Voting treatment), most groups eventually choose sanctions, group sizes become more balanced, migration decreases, and overall efficiency and average payoffs increase further. The ability to endogenously choose institutions (via voting) leads to more efficient and equitable outcomes than exogenously imposed sanctions. The effect of sanctions on efficiency is robust and positive, and the endogenous choice of sanctions amplifies this effect.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling formal punishment in a repeated PGG with migration increases efficiency and average payoffs, relative to a control without punishment. The effect is even stronger when the sanctioning institution is chosen endogenously by group vote. The design dimensions—such as player count (10, split into two groups of 5), number of rounds (30), absence of chat, continuous contributions, and a formal, automatic punishment mechanism—are all specified. The marginal per-capita return (MPCR) varies with group size but is always less than 1, and the punishment is highly effective (80% fine on private account, plus a fixed fee). There is no reward mechanism. Players are shown summary information about both groups' contributions and rule-sets. The findings suggest that, for similar game designs, enabling punishment will reliably increase efficiency, and allowing endogenous institutional choice (voting) will further boost efficiency and reduce inequality. The control (no-punishment) efficiency is substantially lower than with punishment enabled. This paper is highly informative for predicting treatment efficiency from design dimensions and control efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session has 10 players, split into two groups of 5. This is fixed and directly specified.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. There are 30 periods (rounds) in the main repeated PGG.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players allocate any amount from 0 to 50 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR varies with group size and whether in initial group; for group size 5, MPCR is 0.45 (initial group) or 0.38 (not initial group). See Table 1.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is formal and automatic: each member pays a fixed fee of 15 tokens per period, plus a fine equal to 80% of tokens kept in private account.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is highly effective: 1 token kept privately results in 0.8 token fine (80% effectiveness), plus fixed cost. No discretion; automatic.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment (sanctions) are possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are told there are 30 periods in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, players see: (i) average contribution in their group, (ii) average contribution in the other group, (iii) rule-set in both groups.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is formal and automatic, not assigned by individuals; no identification of punishers is possible or relevant.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a formal, automatic punishment mechanism (not peer-to-peer), so results may not generalize to peer punishment settings.
- Group size is fixed at 10 (two groups of 5), so effects of larger or smaller groups are not tested.
- No communication (chat) is allowed, so results may differ in settings with communication.
- The MPCR and punishment parameters are specific to this design; effects may differ with other parameterizations.
- The initial group assignment coincides with the efficient partition; results may differ if initial and efficient partitions do not coincide.
- No reward mechanism is present, so the interaction of punishment and reward is not tested.
