# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Managing and defending the commons: Experimental evidence from TURFs in Chile
Authors: Chávez, C. A., Murphy, J. J., & Stranlund, J. K.
Journal: Journal of Environmental Economics and Management
Year: 2018

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- individual cumulative earnings
- group cumulative earnings
- resource sustainability (terminal period)
- cumulative harvests
Non-payoff outcomes:
- poaching frequency
- monitoring investment
- resource biomass over time
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are individual and group earnings, harvests, and resource sustainability. Poaching and monitoring are also measured, but the primary focus is on payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a dynamic common-pool resource game with insiders (who can communicate) and outsiders (who cannot), the introduction of poaching without monitoring or sanctions dramatically reduces insiders' earnings and resource sustainability, nearly eliminating the efficiency gains from cooperation. Adding weak external enforcement (low-probability monitoring and fines) or allowing insiders to invest in monitoring (with the same fine) both significantly reduce poaching and increase insiders' earnings and harvests, but do not fully restore efficiency to baseline (no-poaching) levels. Combining external and local monitoring does not further reduce poaching or increase efficiency. The main constraint is insiders' inability to coordinate sufficient monitoring investment. The results are robust across field (TURF members) and student samples.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that, in a dynamic CPR game with communication among insiders, the introduction of punishment (via monitoring and fines for poaching) increases group efficiency relative to a poaching-only control, but the effect is partial and depends on the ability to coordinate monitoring. The effect is similar whether monitoring is provided externally or by insiders, and combining both does not yield additional efficiency. For prediction, the presence of punishment (even if weak) should be expected to increase efficiency above the poaching-only baseline, but not to the level of the no-poaching or full-cooperation baseline. The magnitude of the effect is limited by the monitoring probability and the coordination of monitoring investment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 6 players (3 insiders, 3 outsiders) per session.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Maximum of 10 periods per session; resource can be depleted earlier, ending the game for that group.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Insiders (TURF members) can communicate for 3 minutes at the start of each round; outsiders cannot communicate.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Harvest/contribution choices are discrete but not all-or-nothing; each player can harvest up to 5 units per period.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not a standard linear PGG; resource growth is stock-dependent (dynamic CPR), not a fixed MPCR. Marginal return is 500 pesos per unit harvested, but resource growth is nonlinear.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (fine) is 2000 pesos per unit poached. In local enforcement, insiders pay for monitoring (250 pesos per 5.56% probability increment); in external enforcement, monitoring is exogenous.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the product of monitoring probability and fine (e.g., 11.1% x 2000 pesos per unit). Minimum monitoring to deter is 27.8%.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the maximum number of rounds (10), but the game can end earlier if the resource is depleted.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each round, total harvest in each zone is announced, but individual choices are not revealed. In poaching treatments, only total blue zone harvest is announced.

## Important Limitations
- The game is a dynamic common-pool resource game with poaching and monitoring, not a standard linear PGG; results may not generalize to all PGG-like environments.
- Punishment is only for poaching by outsiders, not for within-group free-riding; insiders cannot punish each other.
- Monitoring probabilities and fine levels are fixed and relatively low; the effect of higher punishment or monitoring is not tested.
- Insiders' inability to coordinate monitoring investment is a key constraint; alternative coordination mechanisms (e.g., voting) are not tested.
- Resource growth is nonlinear and stock-dependent, which may affect generalizability to linear public goods games.
- No reward or positive incentive mechanisms are tested.
- The experiment is framed and conducted in a specific Chilean TURF context, which may limit external validity.
