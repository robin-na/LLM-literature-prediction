## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that costly institutions (minimum contribution/tax and strict enforcement with punishment) are broadly supported by participants, especially those with prior experience of water scarcity. Both types of costly institutions increase average contributions to the public good compared to costless regimes. However, only the minimum contribution (tax) regime leads to higher payoffs and welfare, while strict enforcement with costly punishment increases contributions but does not improve payoffs due to the cost of enforcement. The efficiency of the group (measured as payoff relative to the social optimum) is thus higher under the tax regime but lower under strict enforcement compared to their respective costless counterparts.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that the effect of introducing costly punishment or enforcement institutions in public goods games depends on the specific design. Minimum contribution (tax) regimes can increase both contributions and efficiency (payoff), while strict enforcement with costly punishment increases contributions but can reduce efficiency due to the cost of enforcement. For prediction, the presence of a costly punishment mechanism does not guarantee higher efficiency; the cost and structure of the institution are critical. Control efficiency (payoff) and the specific institutional design (tax/minimum vs. costly strict punishment) must be considered. The findings are highly relevant for predicting treatment efficiency from game design dimensions, especially regarding the cost and type of punishment or enforcement.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each public goods game group consists of 3 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are one-shot; each experiment is a single round per institution.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are continuous (0 to 6 bills), not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (mpcr) is 0.5 (alpha = 0.5).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Strict enforcement regime has an institutional cost of 1000 KHR per group member; cost is subtracted from payoffs.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Under strict enforcement, all rule breakers are detected and lose half their payoff; under weak enforcement, detection is probabilistic (p=0.5).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game is one-shot (single round).

## Important Limitations
- The experiments are one-shot and do not capture repeated interaction or long-term dynamics.
- The strict enforcement (punishment) regime is costly, and the cost structure may differ from real-world settings.
- No peer punishment or decentralized punishment; only institutional (centralized) enforcement is studied.
- No reward mechanisms are included, so findings do not generalize to reward-based institutions.
- The sample is from rural Cambodian farmers, which may limit generalizability to other populations.
- Order effects may be present as the order of experiments was not randomized.
- No feedback or learning between rounds, which may affect external validity for repeated public goods settings.
