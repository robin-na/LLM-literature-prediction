# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Group size and matching protocol in contests
Authors: Baik, K. H., Chowdhury, S. M., & Ramalingam, A.
Journal: Canadian Journal of Economics-revue Canadienne D Economique
Year: 2021

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual bid levels
- overbidding (bid above Nash equilibrium)
- overbidding rate (relative to Nash equilibrium)
- dispersion of bids
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are individual bids, overbidding, and bid dispersion. The study does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or surplus. Overbidding is defined as the difference between actual and Nash equilibrium bids, but this is not a direct measure of efficiency or group payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in repeated Tullock contests, partner matching (fixed pairs) leads to lower bids and less overbidding compared to random stranger matching, but only in two-player groups. In three-player groups, matching protocol has no significant effect on bids or overbidding. Bid dispersion is also lower in smaller groups. The results suggest that identifiability and repeated interaction facilitate tacit collusion in small groups, reducing wasteful overbidding. However, the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes, so the findings are limited to behavioral measures (bids and overbidding) rather than direct efficiency effects.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence relevant to the prediction of efficiency in contest-like environments, especially regarding the effects of group size and repeated interaction (partner matching). While it does not report efficiency or group payoff, the finding that partner matching reduces overbidding in two-player groups suggests that efficiency losses from overbidding may be mitigated in such settings. For prediction tasks, the results imply that small group size and repeated interaction can reduce wasteful resource expenditure (overbidding), potentially increasing efficiency, but this is inferred from bid behavior rather than direct efficiency measurement. The absence of punishment or public goods framing limits direct transfer to PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Group size (player count) is directly manipulated (2 vs 3). Partner matching reduces overbidding only in 2-player groups, not in 3-player groups.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use 25 rounds (periods) of repeated play.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players was allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Bids are continuous between 0 and 180 tokens; not all-or-nothing.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players knew the game would last 25 periods; this was public knowledge.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each period, subjects received feedback on the individual bids in their group and whether or not they won the prize.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes; all results are based on individual bids and overbidding relative to Nash equilibrium.
- The contest environment is not a public goods game or direct variant; results may not transfer directly to PGG-like settings.
- No punishment or reward mechanisms are present, so findings about collusion and overbidding are not about sanctioning effects.
- Only two group sizes (2 and 3) are tested; effects for larger groups are not examined.
- No communication or chat is allowed, so results may not generalize to settings with communication.
