Efficiency as a normalized ratio to full cooperation is not directly reported, but group payoff and individual payoffs are analyzed in relation to parameter changes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that a reputation-based, probabilistic punishment mechanism can robustly promote cooperation and increase group payoffs in spatial public goods games, provided that the co-beneficiary capacity (number of individuals who share in the redistributed benefits) is moderate and that participants' retaliatory sentiment and reputation memory are within optimal ranges. Stronger retaliatory sentiment (lower gamma) and higher synergy factors (r) expand the parameter space where cooperation and high payoffs are sustained. The mechanism is robust to initial conditions and can allow punishers to achieve high payoffs, mitigating the typical cost of punishment. However, if the co-beneficiary capacity is too small (punishment is too weak) or too large (second-order free-riding emerges), or if reputation memory is too lenient or too short, cooperation and group payoffs collapse.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and simulation-based evidence that, in spatial public goods games, enabling a reputation-based probabilistic punishment mechanism can substantially increase group payoffs and cooperation rates, especially when the marginal per-capita return is high and the punishment/reward mechanism is tuned to avoid both excessive exclusivity and excessive inclusivity. The findings suggest that, for prediction tasks, the presence of such a punishment mechanism should be expected to increase efficiency relative to control, provided the co-beneficiary capacity and reputation memory are in the optimal range. The results are most directly applicable to structured (networked) populations with local interactions and reputation tracking.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are presented.
- The model assumes a spatial (lattice) network structure and may not generalize to fully mixed or other network types without modification.
- Efficiency is not reported as a normalized ratio to full cooperation, though group and individual payoffs are analyzed.
- The punishment mechanism is implemented via benefit redistribution, not explicit costly punishment, which may differ from standard experimental PGG punishment treatments.
- Parameter tuning (co-beneficiary capacity, reputation memory, retaliatory sentiment) is critical; outside optimal ranges, the mechanism can fail to promote cooperation or payoffs.
- No analysis of the effect of player count or number of rounds on efficiency.
- No communication or chat is modeled, and no information is provided about knowledge of game length.
