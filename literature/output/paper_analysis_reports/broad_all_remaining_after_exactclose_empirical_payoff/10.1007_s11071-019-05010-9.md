## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper shows that introducing prosocial pool exclusion into a PGG leads to higher average payoffs and can stabilize cooperation through periodic oscillations or fixation to full cooperation, depending on whether second-order exclusion is present. Antisocial exclusion can destabilize cooperation but does not fully prevent the effectiveness of prosocial exclusion. Compared to punishment, prosocial pool exclusion is a more robust and effective mechanism for sustaining high efficiency in the population, even in the presence of antisocial strategies.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that, in infinite well-mixed populations, enabling a prosocial pool exclusion mechanism (where excluders pay a fixed cost to exclude defectors from the public good) increases group efficiency relative to the standard PGG without exclusion. The effect is robust to the presence of antisocial exclusion, and even more pronounced when second-order exclusion is allowed. The findings suggest that, for prediction tasks, the presence of a pool exclusion mechanism should be expected to yield higher efficiency than punishment, especially when exclusion is institutionalized and second-order exclusion is possible. However, the results are derived from deterministic replicator dynamics and may not directly translate to finite or experimental settings.
