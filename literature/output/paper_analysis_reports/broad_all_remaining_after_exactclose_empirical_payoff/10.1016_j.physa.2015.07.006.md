## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that stable cooperation via indirect reciprocity is more robust in environments with a stronger dilemma (higher temptation to defect), as this encourages the evolution of norms that punish second-order free-riders. In contrast, weaker dilemmas allow permissive norms to dominate, leading to fragile cooperation. Allowing agents to interact with others holding different norms undermines indirect reciprocity unless the image score is evaluated by the focal agent. Introducing a small action error (randomness) can help robust, punishing norms emerge by preventing the early dominance of overly permissive norms. The main dependent variable is the cooperation fraction; average payoff is shown but not analyzed in depth.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about the role of norm-based punishment (targeting second-order free-riders) in sustaining cooperation in social dilemma environments. However, it does not use a public goods game, does not implement explicit costly punishment, and does not report efficiency or group payoff as a main outcome. The findings suggest that mechanisms which allow for the punishment of second-order free-riders (whether via norms or explicit punishment) may be important for robust cooperation, especially in more challenging (stronger dilemma) environments. For prediction tasks, this paper supports the idea that the structure of sanctioning (including norm evolution and the ability to punish non-punishers) can be critical, but it does not provide direct quantitative evidence for efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like games.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=65536 agents, with 128 per group and 512 groups/islands. The effect of player count is not analyzed.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 generations; each generation involves repeated one-shot games. No analysis of round number effects.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is a 2x2 prisoner's dilemma: each agent chooses to cooperate or defect (binary choice).

## Important Limitations
- The model is based on a 2x2 prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game.
- Punishment is implemented via norm-based image scoring, not explicit costly punishment.
- Efficiency or group payoff is not a main outcome; the main focus is on cooperation fraction and norm/strategy evolution.
- Findings may not transfer directly to PGG-like environments with explicit punishment and group-level payoffs.
- No direct manipulation or analysis of most game design dimensions relevant to PGGs with punishment.
