# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Robustness of norm-driven cooperation in the commons
Authors: Schlüter, M., Tavoni, A., & Levin, S.
Journal: Proceedings of the Royal Society B-biological Sciences
Year: 2016

## Study Type
theory; empirical subtype=N/A; experimental subtype=N/A

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- resource level
- total production
- individual and group payoffs (utility)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- level of cooperation (proportion of norm followers)
- collapse or maintenance of cooperation
- coexistence of cooperators and defectors
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the level of cooperation (proportion of norm followers). Payoff and resource outcomes are discussed as mechanisms and in figures, but the focus is on cooperation dynamics, not on explicit efficiency or group payoff ratios.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model shows that cooperation enforced by social sanctioning is robust under certain conditions but can collapse with increased resource abundance or small increases in resource variability. High resource variability or scarcity can promote cooperation. The effectiveness of sanctioning depends on the proportion of norm followers (social capital). When two groups are ecologically connected, resource leakage from a more cooperative group can destabilize cooperation in the less cooperative group. The model highlights the complex, nonlinear interplay between social and ecological factors in sustaining cooperation. However, the main outcome is the level of cooperation, not explicit efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for predicting the effects of punishment-like mechanisms (here, non-costly social disapproval) on cooperation in CPR/public goods settings, especially under varying resource conditions. However, it does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or group payoff outcomes, nor does it manipulate or report most of the game design dimensions used in the prediction task. The model's focus on non-costly, endogenous social sanctioning differs from standard experimental punishment treatments. Thus, its relevance for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in public goods games is limited and mainly contextual/mechanistic.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. The model assumes a fixed community size n, but does not systematically vary or analyze player count effects.
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Resource returns are modeled via a Cobb-Douglas production function, but no explicit MPCR parameter as in standard PGGs.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sanctioning is non-costly social disapproval; there is no explicit cost to the punisher.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is modeled as a reduction in utility for norm violators, increasing with the number of norm followers and severity of violation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and agent-based, not empirical or experimental.
- The main outcome is the level of cooperation, not explicit efficiency or group payoff.
- Sanctioning is non-costly social disapproval, not costly punishment as in standard PGG experiments.
- Most game design dimensions relevant for the prediction task are not manipulated or systematically analyzed.
- Resource and sanctioning parameters are stylized and may not map directly to standard PGG parameters.
- No direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff is provided.
