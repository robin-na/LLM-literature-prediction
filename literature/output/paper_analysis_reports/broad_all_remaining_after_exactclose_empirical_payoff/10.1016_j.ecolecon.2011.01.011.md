## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper reviews the literature on CPR and public goods experiments, highlighting that communication (e.g., face-to-face chat) increases cooperation and average earnings, while costly punishment is used by participants but can reduce net average returns unless chosen voluntarily by the group. The paper emphasizes the importance of micro-situational variables (such as group size, communication, and context) and the need for richer data collection to understand decision-making. It also discusses the crowding-out effect of external regulation and the mixed effects of ecological dynamics on cooperation and punishment. However, the paper does not provide new empirical estimates or direct analysis of efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides conceptual and methodological guidance for interpreting experimental results on punishment and cooperation in CPR and public goods settings. It suggests that communication and voluntary punishment can improve cooperation, but costly punishment may reduce net payoffs unless used sparingly or chosen by the group. The paper highlights the importance of context and micro-situational variables, which should be considered when predicting the effects of punishment on efficiency. However, because it does not report new empirical data or directly analyze efficiency outcomes, its value for quantitative prediction is limited to qualitative insights and literature synthesis.
