The main dependent variables are punishment behavior (frequency, strength, targeting) and expected payoffs for PD players. Efficiency per se is not directly calculated, but expected payoffs and payoff matrices are analyzed in detail.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that when third party punishment is visible to another punisher, punishment of defectors becomes much stronger and punishment of cooperators is nearly eliminated. This shifts the incentive structure from a prisoner's dilemma to a coordination game, where mutual cooperation is a Nash equilibrium and cooperating yields a higher expected payoff than defecting. In the standard (low visibility) treatment, punishment is weaker and less well-targeted, and the incentive structure remains a prisoner's dilemma, with defection being more profitable. The findings suggest that increasing the visibility of punishment actions can enhance the effectiveness of third party punishment in promoting cooperation and improving group payoffs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that increasing the visibility of punishment actions (i.e., making punishment observable to other punishers) can substantially increase the effectiveness of third party punishment in promoting cooperation and improving expected payoffs. While the game is a prisoner's dilemma rather than a standard public goods game, the mechanism is closely related. The results suggest that, all else equal, enabling punishment visibility should be predicted to increase efficiency (group payoff) in similar environments. The paper also provides quantitative data on expected payoffs under different punishment regimes, which can inform predictions about the magnitude of efficiency changes when punishment is enabled and made visible.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a prisoner's dilemma with third party punishment, not a standard public goods game, so direct generalization to PGGs may be limited.
- The game is one-shot; repeated interaction effects are inferred but not directly tested.
- Punishment is only by third parties, not by direct peers.
- Visibility is only to another punisher, not to the punished or the whole group.
- Efficiency is not directly calculated; only expected payoffs and payoff matrices are analyzed.
