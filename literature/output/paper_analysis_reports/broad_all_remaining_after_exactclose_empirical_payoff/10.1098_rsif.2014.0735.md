Outcome notes:
The review focuses on the dynamics of cyclic dominance, coexistence, extinction, and pattern formation in evolutionary games, including public goods games with punishment and reward. Payoff-based outcomes such as efficiency, welfare, or group payoff are not directly analyzed; instead, the emphasis is on the maintenance of diversity, oscillatory coexistence, and the stability of strategies.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The review highlights that cyclic dominance can emerge in evolutionary games with three or more strategies, including PGG variants with volunteering, punishment, and reward. In structured populations, spatial structure and mobility play crucial roles in maintaining coexistence and generating complex patterns (e.g., spirals, waves). Punishment and reward can stabilize cooperation through cyclic dominance, but the outcomes are typically described in terms of coexistence and oscillatory dynamics rather than efficiency or group payoff. The review also notes that alliances and subsystem solutions can act as effective strategies, further complicating the dynamics. Theoretical and simulation results are emphasized, with limited direct reference to empirical payoff-based outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical and qualitative evidence that the introduction of punishment (and reward) in PGG-like environments can promote the coexistence of cooperative strategies through cyclic dominance, especially in structured populations. However, it does not provide quantitative predictions or direct evidence about the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff. For the downstream prediction task, this review supports the idea that punishment can stabilize cooperation and prevent collapse to all-defection, but it does not inform the magnitude or direction of efficiency changes. The evidence is most useful for understanding mechanisms and moderators (e.g., spatial structure, mobility, network topology) rather than for direct efficiency prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper

- punishment cost: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Punishment cost is discussed as a factor affecting the stability of punishment strategies and cyclic dominance, but not in relation to efficiency or payoff.
- reward availability: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Reward is mentioned as a mechanism analogous to punishment in promoting cyclic dominance and cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The review is theoretical and synthesizes results from models and simulations, with only limited reference to empirical or experimental data.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are not directly analyzed; the focus is on coexistence, oscillatory dynamics, and pattern formation.
- Game design dimensions are not systematically varied or analyzed in relation to efficiency or payoff.
- Most evidence is qualitative or mechanistic, not quantitative or predictive for efficiency outcomes.
- Some referenced experimental results (e.g., human PGG with volunteering) report frequencies of strategies, not efficiency or group payoff.
