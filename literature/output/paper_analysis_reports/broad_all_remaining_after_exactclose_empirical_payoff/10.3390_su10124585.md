## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of positive cooperation
- evolutionary stability of cooperation strategies
- effect of government intervention (incentives and punishments) on cooperation
Outcome notes:
The paper models and simulates the evolution of cooperation strategies (positive/negative cooperation) among enterprises and URIs under government intervention, focusing on the conditions under which cooperation is stable. Outcomes are frequencies of cooperation strategies, not group payoffs or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that government intervention through incentives and punishments can significantly influence the frequency of positive cooperation between enterprises and URIs in collaborative innovation networks. When the sum of incentives and punishments exceeds the total additional cost (TAC) of cooperation, positive cooperation becomes stable. If incentives and punishments are too low, negative cooperation prevails. The model does not report group payoffs, efficiency, or welfare, but focuses on the conditions for stable cooperation strategies.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the prediction task by showing that punishment (and incentives) can increase the frequency of cooperative behavior in a multi-agent innovation network, but it does not quantify effects on efficiency or group payoff. The model is adjacent to public goods games, as it involves free-riding and cooperation, but the outcomes are behavioral (strategy frequencies) rather than payoff-based. The findings suggest that, in similar environments, increasing punishment and incentives above a threshold can shift the system toward more cooperation, which may imply higher efficiency, but this is not directly modeled or measured.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Three populations: government, enterprises, and URIs. Each population is modeled as a group, not as a fixed number of individual players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Players (populations) choose between positive cooperation and negative cooperation (binary choice).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishments (P_A, P_B) are parameters in the model; their magnitude is varied in simulations. Higher punishment (with sufficient incentives) increases the frequency of positive cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is modeled as the direct impact of P_A and P_B on payoffs; higher values increase deterrence of negative cooperation.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Incentives (f_A, f_B) are modeled as rewards for positive cooperation; their presence and magnitude are varied in simulations.
- reward cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The cost to the government of providing incentives is included as C_0 in the model, but not analyzed as a per-unit cost.
- reward technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward effectiveness is modeled as the direct impact of f_A and f_B on payoffs; higher values increase positive cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are used.
- Outcomes are frequencies of cooperation strategies, not group payoffs, efficiency, or welfare.
- The model is adjacent to public goods games but differs in structure (three populations, government as external punisher/rewarder).
- No explicit mapping to marginal per-capita return (mpcr) or standard PGG payoff structure.
- No analysis of communication, round structure, or feedback mechanisms.
