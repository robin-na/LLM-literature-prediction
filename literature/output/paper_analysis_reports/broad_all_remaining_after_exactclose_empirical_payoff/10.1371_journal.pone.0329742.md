# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Gradual reputation dynamics evolve and sustain cooperation in indirect reciprocity
Authors: Yamamoto, H., Okada, I., & Suzuki, T.
Journal: Plos One
Year: 2025

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- reputation evaluation of donor behavior
- categorization of social norms for indirect reciprocity
- cooperation rate (in theoretical model)
- evolutionary stability of action rules
Outcome notes:
The experimental component focuses on how participants evaluate donor behavior in indirect reciprocity scenarios, classifying reputation updates as good, neutral, or bad. Theoretical modeling explores cooperation rates and evolutionary stability, but does not report group payoff, efficiency, or welfare in a public goods game or equivalent.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experimental results show that people use a three-level (good, neutral, bad) reputation system and update reputations gradually: cooperation increases reputation by one level, defection decreases it by one, except that defection against a bad recipient (justified defection) leaves reputation unchanged. Theoretical evolutionary game modeling demonstrates that, under certain conditions, tolerant norms that cooperate with good and neutral and defect only against bad can dominate and sustain high cooperation rates. However, the study does not use a public goods game or measure group efficiency or payoff; it focuses on reputation dynamics and the evolution of cooperation in indirect reciprocity.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. Its findings are adjacent: it informs how nuanced reputation and justified defection (a form of social sanction) are perceived and how these perceptions can support cooperation in indirect reciprocity. However, it does not manipulate or measure punishment costs, punishment effectiveness, or group payoffs, nor does it use a PGG or similar economic game. Thus, it should be used only as indirect, contextual evidence about the psychological and evolutionary underpinnings of social sanctions and cooperation.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Action choice is binary: cooperate or defect.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Participants are told the reputations of donor and recipient in the scenario, but do not see summary statistics or payoffs.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not use a public goods game or any interactive economic game; it is based on scenario evaluations and theoretical modeling.
- No payoff, efficiency, or group welfare outcomes are measured or reported.
- Punishment is only modeled as justified defection, not as a costly or explicit punishment mechanism.
- The sample is drawn from a Japanese crowdsourcing platform, which may limit generalizability.
- No manipulation or measurement of key game design dimensions such as punishment cost, effectiveness, or group size.
