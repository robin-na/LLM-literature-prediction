# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Incentivizing provision of collective goods: Allocation rules
Authors: Stoddard, B. V., Cox, C. A., & Walker, J. M.
Journal: Southern Economic Journal
Year: 2021

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- group-level provision of the collective good
- individual earnings (ECUs)
- allocator earnings (externally provided, proportional to group provision)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual contribution decisions
- allocation decisions by allocator
- frequency of allocation rules used
- correlation between contributions and allocated shares
Outcome notes:
The primary dependent variable is group-level provision (total tokens contributed to the collective good), which is a behavioral outcome. Efficiency (ratio of actual to maximum possible group payoff) is not directly reported, but group provision is closely related. Individual and group earnings are reported in ECUs, but not as normalized efficiency ratios.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a lab experiment with repeated collective good provision games, the introduction of a third-party allocator who can flexibly assign shares of the group fund (based on observed contributions) leads to higher group provision than a baseline with equal sharing. The two most flexible allocator mechanisms (PA-Flex and PA-Rules) both result in significantly higher provision than the baseline (PE-PE), and also outperform a less flexible allocator mechanism (PA-SWW). The highest levels of provision are observed in the most flexible mechanism (PA-Flex), though a small number of groups with ineffective allocators show low provision. The study does not directly report efficiency as a normalized payoff ratio, but group provision is closely related and higher provision implies higher group payoffs. The findings indicate that flexible allocation rules that allow for contribution-contingent rewards and punishments can strongly increase collective good provision.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in public-goods-like environments where a third-party allocator can flexibly assign shares of the collective good based on contributions, group provision (and thus efficiency) increases substantially compared to equal sharing. The effect is robust across two flexible allocator mechanisms and is strongest when the allocator has maximal flexibility. The results suggest that, when predicting the effect of introducing a flexible, contribution-contingent allocation mechanism (a form of endogenous reward/punishment), one should expect a large positive effect on group efficiency, provided the allocator uses the mechanism effectively. However, the effect may be heterogeneous if some allocators do not use the mechanism to incentivize contributions. The study does not use classic peer punishment, but the allocation mechanism serves a similar incentive function. The findings are most directly relevant for settings where a third party can allocate payoffs after observing contributions, rather than direct peer-to-peer punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups consist of 4 contributors plus 1 allocator in Sequence 2 of allocator treatments; baseline is 4-person groups.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each sequence consists of 10 rounds; two sequences per session.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players; group members are anonymous.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each subject allocates 0-10 tokens per round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each token contributed to the collective good yields 2.4 ECUs for the group; with 4 contributors, MPCR = 0.6.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Allocator can reward high contributors with larger shares of the collective good.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Rewarding is costless to the allocator; their own earnings are externally provided and not affected by allocation decisions.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Allocator can assign up to 100% of the group fund to any contributor(s), subject to sum constraint; high flexibility in PA-Flex.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is finitely repeated with a known number of rounds (10 per sequence); subjects are informed.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contributors see their group's aggregate provision and their own earnings after each round; allocators see all contributors' provision decisions and allocated shares.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Allocators and contributors are identified by anonymous ID letters; contributors know which ID received which share, but not real identities.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not use classic peer-to-peer punishment; instead, it uses a third-party allocator mechanism, which may not generalize to settings without such a role.
- Efficiency is not directly reported as a normalized payoff ratio; group provision is used as a proxy.
- The allocator's own earnings are externally provided and not affected by allocation decisions, which may differ from real-world settings where allocators are insiders.
- Some allocators do not use the mechanism effectively, leading to heterogeneity in outcomes.
- The experiment uses student subjects in a lab setting, which may limit external validity.
- No communication or negotiation is allowed between group members, which may affect generalizability.
