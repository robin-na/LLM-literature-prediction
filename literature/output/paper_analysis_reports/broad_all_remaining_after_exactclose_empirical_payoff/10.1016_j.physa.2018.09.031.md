## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that probabilistic positive incentives (rewards) for cooperators promote the evolution and maintenance of cooperation, while probabilistic negative incentives (anti-social punishment) undermine it. These effects are robust across different network structures (square lattice, Erdös-Rényi, Barabási-Albert) and for both the prisoner's dilemma and snowdrift games. The main dependent variable is the stationary fraction of cooperators. The paper does not report on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, so its findings are limited to behavioral outcomes (cooperation rates) rather than payoff-based outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about the likely direction of punishment and reward effects on cooperation rates in two-player evolutionary games, but does not provide direct evidence about efficiency or group payoff. Its results suggest that positive incentives (rewards) increase cooperation and negative incentives (anti-social punishment) decrease it, but the transferability to public goods games and to efficiency outcomes is limited. The absence of payoff or efficiency analysis means this paper should be used only as weak, indirect support for predicting efficiency effects of punishment or reward in public goods game-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run on networks of 10,000 nodes (players), but the game is pairwise (2-person PDG/SDG) with each player interacting with neighbors. The group size per game is 2, but the population is large.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 60,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with outcomes averaged over the last 10,000 steps to ensure steady state.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete actions: cooperate or defect (binary, all-or-nothing).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Positive incentives (rewards) for cooperators are a main treatment variable; their presence increases cooperation rates.

## Important Limitations
- The paper studies pairwise evolutionary games (PDG/SDG), not public goods games; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate (fraction of cooperators), not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- Punishment and reward are modeled as exogenous probabilistic incentives, not as strategic choices by players.
- No analysis of payoff-based outcomes such as efficiency, surplus, or group earnings.
- No human subjects or empirical data; all results are from agent-based simulations.
