## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that a punishment and feedback mechanism—whereby neighbors of frequent defectors break links with them and form new links with each other—can help maintain a stable fraction of cooperators in a dynamic small-world network playing an n-round Prisoner's Dilemma. The mechanism is robust to the number of rounds played, but its effectiveness diminishes as the average degree (connectivity) and network size increase. The main reported outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not efficiency or group payoff. The model is not a public goods game but is related in its focus on cooperation and punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or their close variants. Its main relevance is in showing that punishment mechanisms based on breaking social ties can help sustain cooperation in networked Prisoner's Dilemma settings, but the outcome is measured as the fraction of cooperators, not group efficiency or payoff. The model is adjacent to PGGs, and the findings may be indirectly informative for understanding how network structure and punishment interact to support cooperation, but they do not provide quantitative or directly transferable evidence for efficiency outcomes in PGG-like environments.

## Important Limitations
- The model is based on the Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game or direct variant.
- The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
