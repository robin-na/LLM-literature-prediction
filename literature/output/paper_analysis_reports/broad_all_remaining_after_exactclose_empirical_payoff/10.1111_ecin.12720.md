# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: EXCLUSION AND REINTEGRATION IN A SOCIAL DILEMMA
Authors: Solda, A., & Villeval, M. C.
Journal: Economic Inquiry
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- withdrawal amount (individual extraction from the group resource)
- exclusion rate (ostracism)
- re-integration behavior after exclusion
- reasons for exclusion (survey responses)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: individual withdrawal (extraction) from the group resource, rates of exclusion (ostracism), and behavior upon reintegration. There is no direct analysis of group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare. The outcomes are primarily about cooperation, norm enforcement, and exclusion dynamics.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment investigates endogenous and exogenous exclusion (ostracism) in a club-good setting where group members can vote to exclude others who over-extract from the group resource. Exclusion is primarily targeted at those who withdraw more than the group average, supporting the idea that exclusion enforces group norms. Upon reintegration, previously excluded members tend to reduce their extraction, indicating some deterrent or corrective effect of exclusion. However, the paper does not report on group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare, so the effect of exclusion on efficiency cannot be directly assessed. The findings are most relevant for understanding behavioral responses to exclusion and the use of ostracism as a sanction, rather than for predicting efficiency outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about the behavioral effects of exclusion (ostracism) as a sanctioning mechanism in public-good-like environments. It shows that exclusion can deter norm violations (over-extraction) and that reintegrated members may adjust their behavior. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment (exclusion) on efficiency. The design dimensions (e.g., group size, exclusion rules) are described, but their effects are only analyzed in terms of behavioral outcomes, not efficiency. Thus, this paper is useful for understanding the mechanisms and behavioral pathways by which exclusion might affect efficiency, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency changes.

All group sizes are included in the analysis, and group size is tested for significance in regressions but found not significant for exclusion or reintegration behavior.
- number of rounds: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. The experiment is repeated over multiple periods, but the exact number of rounds is not specified in the provided text.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Withdrawal (extraction) is a continuous choice, not all-or-nothing.
- punishment cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Exclusion is implemented via group vote; the cost to punishers (voters) is not specified, but exclusion is not costless (requires group action).
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Punishment is exclusion (ostracism) from the group for a fixed period; the effectiveness is discussed in terms of behavioral change, not efficiency.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Participants receive feedback on decisions and earnings of group members during exclusion and reintegration periods.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare outcomes, so the effect of exclusion on efficiency cannot be directly assessed.
- The outcomes are primarily behavioral (withdrawal, exclusion, reintegration), not payoff-based.
- Key design parameters such as marginal per-capita return (mpcr) and the cost of exclusion to punishers are not specified.
- No analysis of how exclusion affects group efficiency relative to a control without exclusion.
