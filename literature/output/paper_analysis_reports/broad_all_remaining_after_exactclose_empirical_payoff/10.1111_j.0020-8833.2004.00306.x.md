Non-payoff outcomes such as punishment frequency and settlement rates are discussed as mechanisms, but the focus is on payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in repeated prisoner's dilemma environments with possible unintentional noncompliance (noise), the design of punishment schemes has a large effect on long-run efficiency (treaty value). Probabilistic trigger schemes, which are analogous to mutual punishment phases after a deviation, are self-punishing and reduce efficiency because both the perpetrator and the victim suffer during punishment. Contrite tit-for-tat (CTFT) schemes, which allow for redress (compensation to the victim) and delay retaliation (generosity), achieve much higher efficiency by minimizing the time spent in mutual defection and ensuring that only the guilty party is punished. The paper provides explicit formulas and comparative statics for how efficiency declines with noise under each scheme, and shows that CTFT dominates trigger schemes in most of the relevant parameter space.

## Prediction Guidance
For the downstream prediction task, this paper provides strong theoretical evidence that the structure of the punishment scheme (especially whether punishment is self-punishing or allows for redress) is a critical moderator of the efficiency impact of punishment in repeated social dilemma games. If the punishment mechanism is costly to the punisher and involves mutual punishment phases (as in standard trigger schemes), efficiency is substantially reduced, especially as noise increases. If the punishment mechanism allows for redress (compensation to the victim) and avoids mutual punishment (as in CTFT), efficiency is much higher. The paper also shows that the speed of retaliation (trigger probability) and the expected duration of punishment phases are important: swifter, shorter punishments are more efficient, but only if they still deter defection. These insights should inform predictions about the efficiency effects of punishment based on the punishment technology, cost structure, and whether redress is possible.

## Important Limitations
- The analysis is entirely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The model is limited to two-player repeated prisoner's dilemma games; results may not generalize to larger groups or public goods games with more than two players.
- The paper assumes binary (all-or-nothing) choices and does not consider continuous contribution levels.
- Noise is modeled as a fixed probability of unintentional defection, which may not capture all real-world sources of error or ambiguity.
- The paper does not address the effects of communication, reputation, or more complex institutional features beyond the punishment/redress structure.
