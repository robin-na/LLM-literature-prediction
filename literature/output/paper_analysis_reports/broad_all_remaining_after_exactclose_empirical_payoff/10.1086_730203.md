## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in repeated games with a patient player and a sequence of short-run players who observe only summary statistics of the last K actions (not the full sequence), the patient player can always secure at least his commitment payoff by building a reputation, regardless of K. However, the welfare of the short-run players (i.e., group efficiency) is maximized in all equilibria only if K is below a threshold (cutoff). For K above the cutoff, there exist equilibria where the short-run players' welfare is strictly below first-best, because longer memory makes it harder to motivate short-run players to punish deviations. Thus, increasing the memory window K can have a non-monotonic or even negative effect on group efficiency. The results are robust to various extensions, including imperfect monitoring and coarser information structures.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that, in repeated games with reputation and limited memory, the ability of short-run players to punish deviations (and thus sustain high efficiency) depends critically on the memory length K. If K is too large, the threat of punishment becomes less effective, and group efficiency can fall below first-best in some equilibria. For the downstream prediction task, this suggests that in environments where punishment is implemented via reputation and memory is limited to summary statistics, increasing the memory window does not necessarily improve efficiency and may even reduce it. The results are most directly relevant to settings where efficiency is determined by the ability to sustain cooperation through reputation and punishment, rather than direct PGG lab experiments. The findings are less directly applicable to standard PGGs with explicit punishment stages, but the mechanism insights are informative for predicting efficiency in repeated or reputation-based games.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental evidence.
- The model is not a direct public goods game or laboratory punishment game, but rather a repeated reputation game with a single long-lived player and a sequence of short-run players.
- Punishment is implemented via equilibrium strategies and payoff structure, not via explicit costly punishment choices as in standard PGG experiments.
- The results depend on the assumption of monotone supermodular payoffs and may not generalize to all repeated games or to games with different payoff structures.
- The model assumes that short-run players cannot observe calendar time or the exact sequence of actions, which may differ from some experimental or real-world settings.
