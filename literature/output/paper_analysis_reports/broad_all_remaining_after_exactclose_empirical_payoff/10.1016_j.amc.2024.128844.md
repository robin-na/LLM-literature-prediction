## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that allowing players to both punish defectors and reward cooperators (PEPG strategy) in a spatial public goods game, especially when combined with heterogeneous payoff allocation based on reputation, leads to higher cooperation rates and higher average payoffs for cooperators. The effect is sensitive to the enhancement factor (r), the importance of reputation in payoff allocation (a), and the strength of the bonus-penalty (gamma). Defectors' average payoffs are always lower than those of cooperators in the steady state. The model demonstrates that the union of PEPG and reputation-based heterogeneity is more effective than either alone.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides simulation evidence that combining punishment and reward (PEPG) with reputation-based heterogeneous payoffs increases cooperation and average payoffs in public goods games. For prediction tasks, the results suggest that enabling punishment (especially when combined with reward and reputation mechanisms) will increase efficiency, particularly when the marginal per-capita return (mpcr, via r), the effectiveness of punishment/reward (gamma), and the importance of reputation (a) are high. However, the model is not a direct empirical test and does not report efficiency as a ratio to the fully cooperative optimum, so quantitative predictions should be made with caution.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each simulation uses a 200x200 lattice (40,000 players), with each player participating in 5 overlapping groups of 5 (self + 4 neighbors).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each simulation runs for 10,000 rounds (iterations), with the last 1,000 used for outcome measurement.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperate (invest c=1) or defect (invest 0). PEPG also invests c=1.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Enhancement factor r (1 < r < 5) is varied; mpcr = r/5. Higher r increases cooperation and payoffs.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. PEPG players pay cost beta=0.1 per punishment/reward action. Lower cost (beta) makes PEPG more effective.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment/reward effectiveness is gamma (0 < gamma < 1). Higher gamma increases effect of PEPG.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward is always present as part of PEPG (simultaneous reward and punishment).
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward and punishment share the same cost parameter beta=0.1.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward and punishment share the same effectiveness parameter gamma.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- Efficiency is not reported as a ratio to the fully cooperative optimum; only average payoffs and cooperation rates are shown.
- The model assumes spatial structure (lattice) and evolutionary updating, which may not generalize to all public goods game settings.
- The PEPG mechanism combines reward and punishment and is not directly comparable to standard punishment-only treatments.
- Payoff heterogeneity is based on reputation, which is not a standard feature in most empirical PGG studies.
