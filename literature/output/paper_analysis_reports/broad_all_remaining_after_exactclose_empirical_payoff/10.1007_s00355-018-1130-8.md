# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The impact of redistribution mechanisms in the vote with the wallet game: experimental results
Authors: Becchetti, L., Pelligra, V., & Salustri, F.
Journal: Social Choice and Welfare
Year: 2018

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- share of cooperators (proportion choosing responsible product)
- individual choice to cooperate or defect
- effect of redistribution mechanism on cooperation
- effect of framing on cooperation
- dynamic evolution of cooperation over rounds
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the share of players choosing the responsible product (cooperation rate). No direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is reported. Payoff tables are provided, but outcomes are reported in terms of cooperation rates, not group earnings or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of an ex post redistribution mechanism (taxing defectors and subsidizing cooperators) in a repeated, all-or-nothing public good game significantly increases the share of cooperative choices compared to the baseline. In the baseline, cooperation decays over time, but with redistribution, cooperation is stabilized at a higher level. Framing the responsible product as socially beneficial also increases cooperation, but the effect of redistribution is robust across frames. The study does not report group efficiency or total payoff, so the effect on efficiency cannot be directly determined, but the increase in cooperation rates suggests likely higher group payoffs under redistribution.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that a centralized, ex post redistribution mechanism (akin to a tax/subsidy rather than peer punishment) can substantially increase cooperation rates in a repeated, all-or-nothing public good game with 10 players and no communication. While efficiency or group payoff is not directly reported, the structure of the payoffs and the increase in cooperation rates imply that group efficiency would also increase under the redistribution mechanism. The findings are most directly relevant for predicting the effect of centralized, automatic sanction/reward mechanisms (not peer punishment) in all-or-nothing, no-communication, 10-player repeated games with known rounds and MPCR of 0.3. The results are less directly applicable to standard peer punishment settings, but suggest that institutionalized redistribution can be highly effective at sustaining cooperation.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session had 10 players; all games were played in groups of 10.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each treatment phase lasted 10 rounds; each subject played 20 rounds (10 base, 10 redistribution, order varied).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat was allowed; choices were made independently and anonymously.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice was dichotomous: buy responsible (A) or conventional (B) product; no continuous contribution.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each responsible purchase (A) generated 3 tokens for each player; with 10 players, MPCR = 3/10 = 0.3.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In the redistribution treatment, cooperators receive a share of the tax collected from defectors (centralized reward).
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is funded by a 2.5 token tax on defectors; no cost to cooperators for rewarding.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each defector pays 2.5 tokens, which is pooled and divided equally among cooperators; effectiveness depends on number of cooperators and defectors.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players knew the number of rounds in advance (10 rounds per phase, 20 total).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each round, the number of cooperators was revealed to the group, but not their identities.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not group efficiency or total payoff; efficiency effects are inferred but not directly measured.
- The redistribution mechanism is not peer punishment but a centralized, automatic tax/subsidy; results may not generalize to peer punishment settings.
- All-or-nothing (binary) contribution structure may limit generalizability to standard continuous PGGs.
- No direct analysis of how the redistribution mechanism affects group surplus or efficiency relative to the social optimum.
- No communication or social interaction; results may differ in settings with chat or reputation.
