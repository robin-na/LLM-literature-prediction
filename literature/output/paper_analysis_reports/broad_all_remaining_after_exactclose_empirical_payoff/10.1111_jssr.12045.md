## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that group formation rules significantly affect both group size and contribution behavior in a public goods game. Restricted entry (majority vote required to join a group) leads to the formation of smaller, more cooperative groups with higher per-member contributions, but these groups earn less overall than the large, less cooperative groups that form under free entry/exit or restricted exit. The restricted exit mechanism (majority vote required to leave a group) leads to declining contributions over time, as high contributors are unable to leave low-contributing groups and eventually reduce their own contributions. The study finds that the specific form of punishment (here, exclusion from groups) can increase contributions but does not necessarily improve overall efficiency or group earnings, especially when group size is endogenous and larger groups are more efficient. The results highlight the importance of the institutional details of group formation and punishment mechanisms for both cooperation and efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that the effect of punishment-like mechanisms (here, exclusion from group entry) on efficiency depends critically on the group formation rules and the relationship between group size and efficiency. In this design, exclusion increases contributions but reduces group size, leading to lower overall efficiency compared to free entry/exit, where large groups form but contributions are lower. Thus, when predicting the effect of enabling punishment (especially exclusionary or entry-based punishment) on efficiency, it is important to consider whether the mechanism will also affect group size, and whether efficiency is maximized at large group sizes. The paper suggests that punishment mechanisms that reduce group size can decrease efficiency even if they increase cooperation. The findings are most relevant for settings where group size is endogenous and efficiency increases with group size.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session had 12 players; group size was endogenous and could range from 1 to 12.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Main phase had 20 periods (rounds) after a 3-round preliminary phase.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No chat or direct communication between players; only information about group sizes and average contributions was shown.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice was continuous: each player could allocate 0-15 tokens between private and group accounts.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (MPCR) is 1.5 per token per group member, but the cost of contributing is nonlinear (increasing marginal cost).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects knew the number of rounds in advance (finite repeated game).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects saw average contribution levels and group sizes for all groups over the previous 5 periods.

## Important Limitations
- The punishment mechanism is not explicit: exclusion from group entry is not labeled as punishment, and there is no direct cost to the punisher.
- The environment is designed so that group size always increases efficiency, which may not generalize to settings with congestion or diminishing returns.
- No explicit monetary punishment or reward mechanism is present; only exclusion and contribution reduction are possible.
- The study does not directly compare to standard punishment treatments (e.g., costly punishment with explicit cost/impact).
- Subjects may not have fully understood the group optimal contribution, as shown by the bonus question results.
- Findings may not generalize to environments where group size is fixed or where efficiency does not increase monotonically with group size.
