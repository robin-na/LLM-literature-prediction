## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a second-order reputation evaluation mechanism in a spatial public goods game leads to a significant increase in the frequency of cooperators, especially as the reputation threshold and the severity of reputation changes (step length) increase. The model demonstrates that high-reputation individuals become influential, spreading cooperation, while defectors lose reputation and influence. The main outcome is the stationary frequency of cooperators, not efficiency or group payoff. There is no explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism; instead, reputation changes act as an indirect incentive structure. The findings are based on numerical simulations, not empirical or experimental data.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it does not include a punishment mechanism or report efficiency or payoff outcomes. Its findings on reputation mechanisms may be contextually informative for understanding how indirect incentives can promote cooperation, but they are not directly transferable to the prediction of efficiency changes due to punishment. The paper is best used as contextual or mechanistic background rather than as direct evidence for the prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 5 players (focal individual plus 4 neighbors) on a lattice of size L=100, but the effective group size is 5.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 50,000 Monte Carlo steps; stationary state measured over last 5,000 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperate (contribute 1) or defect (contribute 0).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. mpcr = r / (k+1), with r > 1 and k+1 = 5; r is varied in simulations.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The main outcome is cooperation frequency, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- There is no explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism; reputation changes act as indirect incentives.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with explicit punishment, reward, or real human behavior.
- No analysis of payoff-based outcomes relevant to the downstream prediction task.
