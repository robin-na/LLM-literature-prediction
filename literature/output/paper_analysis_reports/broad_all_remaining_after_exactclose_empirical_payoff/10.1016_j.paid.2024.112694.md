# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The effect of third-party intervention on normative behavior of bystanders: An explanation of the social norm cueing effect
Authors: Guo, Y. C., Zhao, X. R., Liu, Y. B., & Ma, J. H.
Journal: Personality and Individual Differences
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- sharing behavior (dictator game allocation)
- perceptions of descriptive norms
- perceptions of injunctive norms
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the number of raffle tickets (Study 1) or tokens (Study 2) shared in a dictator game, which is a measure of fairness or norm compliance, not group efficiency or total payoff. No group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that both third-party punishment and third-party compensation, when observed by bystanders, increase the bystanders' fairness in subsequent dictator game allocations. This effect is mediated by changes in descriptive norm perceptions. The effect is present when the intervention is presented as group summary information (Study 1) or as an individual action perceived as representative of group behavior (Study 2). The study does not measure or report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes, and the game is a dictator game, not a public goods game or its direct variant.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that observing third-party punishment or compensation can increase norm-compliant behavior (fairness in allocation) among bystanders, mediated by changes in perceived descriptive norms. However, because the study uses a dictator game (not a public goods game) and does not report efficiency or group payoff, its direct value for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in public-goods-game-like environments is limited. The findings may be useful for understanding norm transmission and the psychological mechanisms by which punishment or compensation can affect behavior, but do not provide quantitative or direct evidence for efficiency outcomes in PGGs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game involves three players: Dictator (A), Recipient (B), and Third Party (C).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Single-shot (one-shot) games; no repeated rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the third-party game, Dictator A chooses between two discrete allocations (e.g., 8:2 or 5:5 in Study 1; 80:20 or 50:50 in Study 2). In the subsequent dictator game, participants can choose any number of tickets/tokens to share (continuous choice).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. In Study 1, Third Party C pays 1 CNY to punish (reduce Dictator A's payoff by 3 CNY) or to compensate (increase Recipient B's payoff by 3 CNY). In Study 2, Third Party C pays 1 token to reduce A's payoff by 3 tokens or increase B's payoff by 3 tokens.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3 units deducted from Dictator A per 1 unit cost to Third Party C.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Third Party C can choose to compensate Recipient B (reward) as an alternative to punishment.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward (compensation) costs 1 unit to Third Party C to increase B's payoff by 3 units.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is 3 units added to Recipient B per 1 unit cost to Third Party C.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game is a one-shot scenario; the number of rounds is clear.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In Study 1, third-party intervention is presented as group summary information (i.e., most third parties intervened or not). This summary information increases bystander fairness behavior.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a dictator game, not a public goods game or its direct variant, so findings may not generalize to PGG-like environments.
- No efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes are reported; only individual sharing behavior and norm perceptions are measured.
- The effect is on fairness/norm compliance, not on group efficiency or total earnings.
- Manipulations are based on written vignettes and hypothetical observation, which may limit ecological validity.
- The representativeness of third-party intervention is measured indirectly and may not fully capture real-world perceptions.
- Sample in Study 1 is university students (convenience sample), which may limit generalizability.
