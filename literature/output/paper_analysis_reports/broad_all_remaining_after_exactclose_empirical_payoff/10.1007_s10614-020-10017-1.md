## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The endogenous shared punishment model, where agents vote on the level and cost of punishment, achieves higher mean payoffs (efficiency) than both the baseline (no punishment) and the cooperation (full punishment) models. In the standard scenario (group size 4, MPCR 0.5, threshold 60, endowment 30), the endogenous shared punishment model yields a weighted mean reward of 40.09, compared to 33.42 for the baseline and 35.04 for the cooperation model. The provision rate is 73.8% for the endogenous shared punishment model, 24.7% for the baseline, and 98.2% for the cooperation model. The endogenous shared punishment model achieves this efficiency at a very low cost of punishment, as pro-punishment votes and punishment costs decline over time as cooperation stabilizes. Adding 'activists' (altruistic contributors) further increases provision rates and mean payoffs. The model's results are robust to variations in group size, threshold, MPCR, and learning parameters.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that introducing a low-cost, endogenous, shared punishment mechanism in threshold public goods games can substantially increase efficiency (mean group payoff) compared to both no-punishment and full-punishment regimes. The efficiency gains are achieved even when the provision rate is not maximized, due to the low cost of punishment and the stabilization of cooperation. The findings are directly relevant for predicting the effect of enabling punishment in TPGG-like environments, especially when punishment is endogenous, low-cost, and shared. The results are most applicable to games with group size 4, MPCR 0.5, and threshold 60, but the model explores a range of parameters. The paper's agent-based approach and focus on payoff outcomes make it highly informative for the prediction task.

## Important Limitations
- The study is based on agent-based simulations, not human experiments; results may not fully generalize to real human behavior.
- The model focuses on threshold public goods games (TPGGs), which are not identical to standard linear PGGs; results may not transfer to all PGG variants.
- Punishment is implemented as a shared, endogenous, system-level mechanism, not as peer-to-peer punishment; findings may not apply to other punishment structures.
- No reward mechanisms are considered; the interaction of punishment and reward is not addressed.
- The model assumes perfect monitoring of contributions for punishment purposes.
- The main results are for group size 4, MPCR 0.5, and threshold 60; effects may differ for other parameterizations.
