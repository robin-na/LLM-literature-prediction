Behavioral outcomes such as actual contribution rates or punishment frequency are not empirically measured but are discussed as part of the theoretical mechanism.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in repeated partnership games with imperfect monitoring, the ability to sustain efficient cooperation through punishment is fundamentally limited by the informativeness of the monitoring technology and the timing of information. When monitoring is imperfect, reducing the interest rate (making players more patient) increases the efficiency of equilibrium outcomes, potentially allowing near-first-best payoffs. However, shortening the period of fixed action or reducing the lag with which information is reported can actually decrease efficiency, sometimes eliminating the possibility of cooperation altogether. Delaying information release can, counterintuitively, increase efficiency by reducing the cost of deterring deviations. The paper provides generalizations to n-player symmetric games and shows that these results are robust to more complex action and signal spaces.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical guidance for predicting the efficiency of repeated public-goods-like games with punishment under imperfect monitoring. It shows that the effect of punishment on efficiency is highly sensitive to the informativeness of monitoring, the timing of information, and the patience of players. In particular, enabling punishment does not guarantee high efficiency unless monitoring is sufficiently informative and the timing of information and action periods is chosen carefully. For prediction, the paper suggests that increasing patience (lower interest rate) is reliably associated with higher efficiency, but increasing the frequency of action or information updates can reduce efficiency when monitoring is imperfect. The explicit formulas and comparative statics in the paper can be used to estimate efficiency given the relevant game design dimensions.

## Important Limitations
- The analysis is entirely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- Results are derived for symmetric equilibria; asymmetric equilibria may allow for higher efficiency in some cases (as noted in the discussion).
- The models assume rational, forward-looking players and do not account for bounded rationality or behavioral factors.
- The monitoring structure is exogenous and may not capture all real-world forms of imperfect information.
- No explicit modeling of communication, reward mechanisms, or other institutional features beyond punishment and monitoring.
