Outcome notes:
The main reported outcomes are the frequency of cooperation (i.e., proportion of cooperators), strategy transition probabilities, and Q-values. There is no direct reporting of group efficiency, total payoff, welfare, or surplus. Payoff is used as an internal variable in the model, but not as a primary outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a multilayer network with inter-layer feedback and reinforcement learning, cooperation is promoted by lower punishment costs and higher punishment severity. The frequency of cooperation is sensitive to these parameters, with cooperation collapsing when punishment is too costly or too weak. The self-adapting Q-learning mechanism enables agents to learn strategies that support higher cooperation rates, especially when learning rates and discount factors are tuned appropriately. However, the main outcome is the frequency of cooperation, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about how punishment cost and severity affect cooperation rates in a spatially structured, multilayer environment with reinforcement learning. While it does not report efficiency or group payoff, the findings suggest that punishment can increase cooperation under certain cost/severity regimes, but excessive punishment cost can undermine cooperation. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper's results are only indirectly informative, as it does not quantify group payoff or efficiency. The model is also not a standard public goods game, but rather a spatial prisoner's dilemma with punishment administered by a management layer.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run on a 200x200 square lattice (N=40,000), with each agent occupying a node in both layers. The player count is very large and fixed in all simulations.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for T=100,000 Monte Carlo steps, with results averaged over the last 5,000 steps. The number of rounds is fixed and known.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are binary: cooperate (C) or defect (D) in the classic layer; punish (P) or inactive (I) in the management layer.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost (rho) is a key parameter. Lower punishment cost increases cooperation; higher cost undermines cooperation. The effect is non-monotonic: too high a cost eliminates the positive effect of punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment severity (beta) is manipulated. Higher punishment severity increases cooperation, especially at higher temptation to defect.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of simulation steps (rounds) is fixed and known to the experimenter; agents are artificial and do not have explicit knowledge of the horizon.

## Important Limitations
- The model is not a standard public goods game; it is a spatial prisoner's dilemma with a management layer administering punishment.
- The main outcome is the frequency of cooperation, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes; results are not directly transferable to efficiency prediction tasks.
- All results are from simulation of artificial agents using reinforcement learning, not from empirical or experimental data.
- No reward mechanism is present, so the effects of positive incentives are not addressed.
- The very large group size (N=40,000) and spatial structure may not generalize to small-group or well-mixed settings.
