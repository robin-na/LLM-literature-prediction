Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the fraction of knowledge contributors (θ), which is a behavioral outcome (contribution rate), not a direct measure of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare. Payoff functions are specified in the model, but simulation results focus on contribution rates rather than total or relative payoffs.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The simulation results show that both punishment and reward mechanisms increase the fraction of knowledge contributors compared to no incentive, but punishment is more effective than reward. Mixed mechanisms (punishment plus reward) are less effective than either alone. The relationship between fine/bonus size and knowledge sharing is nonlinear: moderate fines/bonuses maximize knowledge sharing, while very high or low values are less effective. Peer pressure, time pressure, and coworkers' attitudes significantly affect knowledge sharing rates. The main outcome is the behavioral rate of knowledge sharing, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a PGG-like environment, introducing punishment increases the rate of knowledge sharing (contribution) more than reward or mixed incentives, but it does not report on efficiency or group payoff. The results are based on agent-based simulations, not empirical data. The findings are most useful for predicting changes in contribution rates, not efficiency. The model details (e.g., player count, network structure, incentive parameters) are informative for mapping to design dimensions, but the lack of direct efficiency or payoff outcomes limits the paper's value for predicting treatment efficiency from control efficiency and design dimensions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=1000 players on a network; each player has a variable number of neighbors (average 36).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each simulation run lasts 10,000 time steps (rounds), with results averaged over 20 runs.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: each player either contributes 1 unit or 0 (hoards).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r is varied (examples: r=1.2, 1.8, 3, 5); MPCR = r/(group size). Group size is k+1, where k is number of neighbors.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a fine (δ2) imposed on hoarders after a certain number of hoarding actions (τ2). The cost to the punisher is not explicitly modeled; the fine is subtracted from the hoarder's payoff.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the size of the fine (δ2) and the frequency (τ2). Both are varied in simulations.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward mechanism is implemented as a bonus (δ1) for contributors after a certain number of contributions (τ1).
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is modeled as a bonus added to the contributor's payoff; the cost to the rewarder is not explicitly modeled.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is determined by the size of the bonus (δ1) and the frequency (τ1). Both are varied in simulations.

## Important Limitations
- The study is based on agent-based simulations, not empirical or experimental data.
- The main outcome is the fraction of knowledge contributors (contribution rate), not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- Payoff-based outcomes (efficiency, group earnings) are not reported in the results.
- The cost of punishment and reward to the punisher/rewarder is not explicitly modeled; only the effect on the recipient's payoff is considered.
- The model assumes a large network (N=1000) and may not generalize to smaller groups or different network structures.
- No direct mapping to real-world workplace settings or empirical validation.
