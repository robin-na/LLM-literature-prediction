Outcome notes:
The primary dependent variable is the relative cooperator payoff (RCP), a direct measure of efficiency for cooperators versus defectors. Other outcomes include group structure and assortment, but the main focus is on payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model demonstrates that reputational exclusion, when information about behavior is reliable and flows quickly, allows cooperators to achieve much higher payoffs relative to defectors than capacity constraints. However, if reputation is slow to converge or cooperators are rare, capacity constraints are more effective. The ability of cooperators to join many games under reputation is key to their advantage. The model also finds that increasing the productivity of the public goods game (higher multiplier) increases the relative payoff to cooperators under reputation, but not necessarily under capacity constraints. Limiting the number of games an agent can play reduces the effectiveness of reputation-based exclusion. The model does not include explicit punishment or sanctioning (costly punishment), so direct evidence on punishment is not provided.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it models only exclusion-based institutions (capacity constraints and reputation-based entry) rather than costly punishment. However, it is highly informative about the effects of group entry rules and reputation mechanisms on efficiency and cooperation. For prediction tasks involving punishment, this paper is only adjacent, but it provides strong evidence that exclusion and reputation can be powerful alternatives to punishment for promoting efficiency, especially when information is reliable and cooperators are common. The findings suggest that the design of group entry and information flow can be as important as punishment in determining efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size N is a model parameter (e.g., N=100 in main runs), and group size is manipulated via capacity constraints.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is iterated until equilibrium is reached; time steps are used, but there is no fixed number of rounds as in standard repeated games.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents play pure strategies: cooperate (contribute all) or defect (contribute nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game production parameter r (e.g., r=2.5) is analogous to the multiplier; MPCR is r divided by group size.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents have private information about co-players' behavior in games they share; no global summary, but local observation is key to reputation.

## Important Limitations
- The model does not include explicit punishment or costly sanctioning mechanisms; only exclusion and group entry rules are modeled.
- Findings may not generalize to settings where punishment is available or where agents can switch strategies.
- The model assumes pure strategies (agents are fixed as cooperators or defectors) and does not include learning or evolution of strategies.
- No transaction costs or costs of implementing institutions are included in payoff calculations.
- Agents can participate in multiple games simultaneously, which may differ from standard public goods game designs.
