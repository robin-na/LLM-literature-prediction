## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model shows that group efficiency (average payoff) and cooperation are maximized at intermediate levels of cultural tightness when facing severe environmental shocks. Loose cultures (low tightness) are more resilient and efficient under mild, slow-onset shocks, while very tight cultures often fail to adapt to new shocks and experience lower efficiency. However, very tight cultures can maintain high efficiency in the face of regular, severe shocks if cooperative norms are already established. Increasing group size undermines cooperation and efficiency unless benefit parameters scale accordingly. Synergistic effects between different types of collective action (public goods) enhance both cooperation and efficiency. The model does not include explicit punishment or sanctioning, but norm compliance is enforced via conformity in the utility function.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that, in public-goods-like environments with norm-based conformity (but no explicit punishment), efficiency is maximized at intermediate levels of norm strength (tightness) under severe shocks, and at low tightness under mild shocks. The absence of explicit punishment means the results are not directly transferable to settings where punishment is enabled or disabled, but the findings are highly informative for predicting efficiency as a function of group size, benefit structure, and norm strength. The model's structure and results are most relevant for public goods games with strong norm compliance or conformity-based incentives, and less so for games where punishment is the main enforcement mechanism. The model also shows that increasing group size reduces efficiency unless benefits scale up, and that synergy between collective actions increases the range of conditions for high efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Group size N is a model parameter; increasing N reduces cooperation and efficiency unless benefits scale up.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Simulations run for 500 time steps; shocks last T=10 rounds. No direct analysis of round number effects on efficiency.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution to collective actions (coordinated infrastructure and cooperative subsistence) is binary (0 or 1); effort to independent subsistence is continuous.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is determined by benefit parameters (R^max, M^max) and group size N; increasing these parameters increases cooperation and efficiency.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents observe average group behavior (for conformity term in utility), but not explicit summaries of payoffs or contributions.

## Important Limitations
- No explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism; norm compliance is modeled via conformity, not costly punishment.
- Model is theoretical and agent-based; no empirical or experimental data are presented.
- Findings may not generalize to settings where punishment is the main enforcement mechanism.
- Group size effects are only robust if benefit parameters scale with group size.
- Model does not include leadership, political institutions, or explicit intergroup dynamics.
- Food sharing is modeled as a group-level parameter, not individual-level heterogeneity.
- No communication, chat, or explicit information-sharing between agents.
