## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in public goods and threshold punishment games with realistic hunter-gatherer population structure, the efficiency gains from punishment or contingent cooperation depend critically on group size (player count), relatedness (assortment), and the threshold for cooperation or punishment. When groups are small (e.g., n=10) and relatedness is moderate to high (r_e > 0.05), contingent cooperation and punishment can increase expected fitness and invade, especially if the threshold for cooperation/punishment is low (i.e., only a minority need to cooperate/punish). In larger groups (e.g., n=40) or with low relatedness (r_e ~ 0.01), efficiency gains are much harder to achieve, requiring implausibly high repetition or relatedness. The model shows that punishment can support widespread cooperation and higher efficiency if a minority of punishers is sufficient to induce cooperation, but contingent cooperation alone is unlikely to sustain high efficiency in large, low-relatedness groups. Equilibrium outcomes are typically mixed, with both cooperators and non-cooperators present, and the equilibrium frequency of cooperators (and thus efficiency) depends on the tolerance threshold.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical guidance for predicting the efficiency impact of punishment in public-goods-game-like environments as a function of player count, relatedness (assortment), and the threshold for cooperation or punishment. It suggests that enabling punishment can increase efficiency, but only under specific conditions: small group size, high relatedness, and low thresholds for action. In large groups or with low relatedness, punishment is unlikely to improve efficiency unless the punishment mechanism is highly effective and requires only a minority to act. The model also highlights that equilibrium efficiency may be limited by the coexistence of cooperators and non-cooperators, especially for tolerant strategies. For prediction tasks, this paper implies that the effect of punishment on efficiency is highly contingent on these structural parameters, and that simply enabling punishment is not sufficient for efficiency gains in all settings.

## Important Limitations
- The model is purely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- Assumes weak selection and random band formation, which may not fully capture real-world dynamics.
- Assumes no communication, reputation, or explicit norm enforcement mechanisms beyond contingent strategies.
- Does not model reward mechanisms, only punishment and cooperation.
- Results are highly sensitive to parameter choices (group size, relatedness, threshold), limiting generalizability.
- Equilibrium outcomes are mixed and may not correspond to full cooperation or maximum efficiency.
