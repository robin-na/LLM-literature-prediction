# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Exploring cyclic dominance in rock-paper-scissor strategies within the Optional Prisoner's Dilemma: A quantitative analysis with environmental feedback
Authors: Shuvo, M. F. R., & Kabir, K. M. A.
Journal: Applied Mathematics and Computation
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- net benefit of punishment (Study 2, Section 3.2.2)
- cost of punishment
- benefit of increased cooperation
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution level
- expectation of others' contributions
- punishment delivered
Outcome notes:
The primary dependent variable is the amount contributed (cooperation level). Some calculations of net benefit and cost-effectiveness of punishment are reported, but efficiency (as a ratio of group payoff to maximum possible) is not directly analyzed. Group payoff is not the main outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Punishment increases cooperation in one-shot, three-person PD games, both when imposed exogenously (costless) and endogenously (costly to the punisher). The effect of punishment is decomposed into a direct effect (altering incentives for the punished individual) and an indirect effect (raising expectations that others will cooperate, thus encouraging conditional cooperators). Both effects are of similar magnitude. In the endogenous punishment condition, the cost of punishment is small relative to the benefit from increased cooperation, resulting in a net positive benefit. However, the main outcome is contribution, not group efficiency or total payoff, though some calculations of net benefit are provided.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling punishment (even when costly to the punisher) increases cooperation in public-goods-like environments, and that the effect is due to both direct and indirect mechanisms. While efficiency (as defined by group payoff relative to maximum possible) is not directly reported, the paper's calculations of net benefit and cost-effectiveness suggest that punishment increases group welfare. The design is a one-shot, three-player, continuous-contribution PD with and without punishment, and with both exogenous and endogenous punishment. For prediction tasks, the findings support a more positive effect of punishment on efficiency, especially when both direct and indirect effects are present, and when punishment is not prohibitively costly. However, the lack of direct efficiency reporting means that predictions must be inferred from contribution and net benefit data.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played with three players per group.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. One-shot game; each group plays a single round.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication allowed; participants are escorted to individual rooms and do not see or talk to each other.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: participants choose any portion of their endowment to contribute.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each yen contributed benefits the other two players by 1 yen each; MPCR = 2/3 (since each yen is split between two others in a 3-person group).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Study 1: punishment is exogenous and costless to the punisher. Study 2: punishment is endogenous and costs 1 yen to reduce the target's earnings by 2 yen.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Study 1: punishment is a 50% fine on the amount kept (if punished). Study 2: each yen spent reduces the target's earnings by 2 yen.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game is one-shot (single round).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After the experiment, participants are informed of how much each member contributed and whether they were punished.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is contribution (cooperation), not group efficiency or total payoff; efficiency is not directly reported.
- The game is a one-shot, three-person PD, which is a close but not exact match to standard public goods games.
- Punishment is either exogenous and costless (Study 1) or endogenous and costly (Study 2), but only one punishment technology is used per study.
- The effect of punishment on efficiency must be inferred from contribution and net benefit calculations, not directly measured.
- The sample is limited to Japanese university freshmen, which may limit generalizability.
