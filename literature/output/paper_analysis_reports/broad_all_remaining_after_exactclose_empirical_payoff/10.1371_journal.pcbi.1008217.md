## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the CAPRI-n strategy achieves full cooperation and maximal efficiency in the iterated n-person public-goods game, even with implementation errors. When all players use CAPRI-n, the group achieves the cooperative payoff in the limit of small error probability. The strategy is defensible (cannot be exploited), efficient (achieves full cooperation), and distinguishable (can exploit unconditional cooperators). Evolutionary simulations show that CAPRI-n dominates the population across a wide range of benefit-to-cost ratios and population sizes, outperforming other memory-one strategies. The results are robust for n=2 and 3, and supported by Monte Carlo simulation for n=4.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and simulation-based evidence that, in repeated public-goods games with error, a well-designed punishment/reciprocity strategy (CAPRI-n) can achieve near-maximal efficiency, even as group size increases. The findings suggest that, when the game allows for memory-based contingent punishment and recovery, enabling such mechanisms can dramatically increase efficiency relative to baseline. The results are most directly applicable to settings with continuous contributions, no explicit punishment cost, and no external punishment/reward mechanism, but where strategies can implement contingent retaliation. The paper is highly informative for predicting efficiency in repeated PGGs with endogenous, strategy-based punishment/reciprocity, especially as group size increases.

## Important Limitations
- The model assumes infinite repetition (no endgame effects), which may not hold in real-world or experimental settings.
- Punishment is implemented as endogenous strategic retaliation, not as explicit costly punishment; results may not generalize to games with explicit punishment costs.
- The strategy requires players to track the full history of actions for 2n-1 rounds, which may be cognitively demanding for large n.
- Efficiency may decrease for large group sizes or higher error rates; the model's efficiency guarantee is strongest for small error and moderate n.
- No explicit consideration of communication, reward mechanisms, or institutional punishment.
