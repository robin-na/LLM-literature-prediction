## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Punishment and inspection mechanisms can promote cooperation and prevent overexploitation in a common-pool resource game, but their ability to improve group efficiency (resource sustainability) depends critically on the intrinsic growth rate of the resource. If the resource renews too slowly, even perfect cooperation enforced by strong punishment leads to resource depletion and low efficiency. At moderate growth rates, strong punishment and inspection can achieve both high cooperation and sustainable resource levels, but weak institutions result in mixed strategy populations and depleted resources. At high growth rates, the resource is always sustainable regardless of punishment, and punishment only affects the fraction of cooperators versus defectors. Thus, the efficiency gain from punishment is context-dependent and can be absent or even negative if resource dynamics are unfavorable.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and simulation evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-like common-pool resource games is highly contingent on the ecological context, especially the resource's intrinsic growth rate. When predicting the efficiency impact of punishment, it is crucial to consider not only the game design dimensions (such as punishment cost and effectiveness) but also the underlying dynamics of the resource. In settings where the resource cannot recover quickly, punishment may not improve efficiency even if it increases cooperation. In more favorable ecological conditions, punishment can have a strong positive effect on efficiency, but only if it is sufficiently effective (high detection probability and/or fine). Thus, the paper suggests that the efficiency effect of punishment is not universal and must be interpreted in light of resource dynamics.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are used.
- Punishment is modeled as a top-down, external institution (inspection and fine), not as peer punishment; thus, results may not generalize to peer-sanctioning settings.
- The model assumes a well-mixed population and global feedback; local or networked interactions are not considered.
- The cost of punishment to the punisher is not explicitly modeled; only the fine to the defector is considered.
- Resource dynamics are simplified (logistic growth); real-world resource systems may have more complex dynamics.
- No communication, reward, or other social mechanisms are included.
- The model assumes binary strategies (cooperate/defect) rather than continuous contribution levels.
