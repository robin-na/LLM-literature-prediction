Outcome notes:
The paper reports on the prevalence of different strategies (cooperation, pro-social punishment, defection, anti-social punishment) and the formation of cooperative clusters, but does not directly report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare. The closest outcome to efficiency is the prevalence of pro-social punishment and cooperation, which are interpreted as supporting the evolution of cooperation, but not measured in payoff terms.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that migration in spatially structured populations promotes the evolution and prevalence of pro-social punishment (altruistic punishment) and cooperation, especially when individuals migrate to maximize potential gains rather than avoid losses. There is an optimal population density for this effect. Migration can sustain pro-social punishment even when punishment efficiency (fine/cost ratio) is low, as long as the absolute amount of punishment is high. The model does not report efficiency or group payoff, but the prevalence of pro-social punishment and cooperation is interpreted as a positive outcome for the evolution of cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the idea that enabling migration or spatial self-organization can enhance the effectiveness of punishment in promoting cooperation, especially under certain migration preferences and population densities. However, because the model does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be used directly to predict efficiency outcomes in public goods games with or without punishment. The findings are most relevant for understanding the mechanisms by which migration and spatial structure interact with punishment, rather than for quantitative prediction of efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a lattice of size l x l (l=100), with population density rho=n/N varied. The effective number of players is n, which is manipulated via density.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for >50,000 Monte Carlo steps, with steady-state fractions averaged over the last 3,000 steps. The number of rounds is very large and fixed by simulation design.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperate (C) or defect (D) in a pairwise PD game.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost (gamma) is a key parameter, varied in the model (e.g., gamma=0.1, gamma>=0.42).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the fine-to-cost ratio (beta/gamma), varied in the model.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a simulation of a spatially structured pairwise Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare; outcomes are based on strategy prevalence, not payoffs.
- Migration is modeled as costless, which may not reflect real-world migration costs.
- The model does not include communication, reputation, or indirect reciprocity mechanisms.
- Network topology is limited to a lattice with periodic boundaries; more complex or realistic social networks are not considered.
