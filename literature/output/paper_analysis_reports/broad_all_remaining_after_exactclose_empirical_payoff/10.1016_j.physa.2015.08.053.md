## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in the meta-norms game, costly punishment and meta-punishment alone are insufficient to robustly maintain cooperation and high group efficiency, due to the vulnerability to second-order free-riding. However, the coercive introduction of a small number of always-defecting agents (the 'social vaccine') stabilizes the punishment system, prevents the drift toward non-punishing cooperators, and maintains a highly cooperative regime. This results in average payoffs for the group that are close to those of a fully cooperative group, even in parameter regions where cooperation would otherwise collapse. The effect is robust to changes in population size, temptation to defect, and punishment cost.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and simulation-based evidence that, in public-goods-like environments with meta-norms (second-order punishment), the presence of a small number of persistent defectors can dramatically increase the efficiency of the punishment regime, maintaining high group payoffs where they would otherwise collapse. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the stability and efficiency of punishment-enabled games may depend not only on the formal punishment parameters but also on the presence of persistent norm violators. In the absence of such 'social vaccine' agents, efficiency gains from punishment may be fragile or short-lived, especially in longer games or with low punishment costs. The findings are most directly relevant to games with meta-punishment and compulsory participation, and may be less directly transferable to standard linear PGGs without meta-norms.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Population size N is a key parameter, varied in simulations (N=20, 40, 60, 80, 100). Larger N without social vaccine increases cooperation, but with social vaccine, cooperation is robust for all N.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Number of generations (rounds) is varied (10^2, 10^3, 10^4, 10^5). Longer games without social vaccine are more likely to see collapse of cooperation; with social vaccine, cooperation is robust regardless of length.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost (E, ME) and punishment impact (P, MP) are varied. Lower punishment cost (higher absolute value of E) makes cooperation harder to sustain without social vaccine, but with social vaccine, cooperation is robust across a wide range.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (P, MP) is set and varied in the model. The effect of punishment parameters is explored, but the main result is that social vaccine dominates the effect of punishment tech on cooperation stability.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- The game is a meta-norms game (with second-order punishment), not a standard linear public goods game; results may not generalize to standard PGGs.
- The 'social vaccine' (persistent defectors) is exogenously introduced; the model does not address how such agents would be maintained or incentivized in real systems.
- No analysis of spatial structure, communication, or information sharing, which may affect real-world applicability.
- No reward mechanism is considered; only punishment and meta-punishment are modeled.
