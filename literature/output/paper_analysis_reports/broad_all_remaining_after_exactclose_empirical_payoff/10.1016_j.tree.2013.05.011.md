## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper argues that online social networks, if designed with evolutionary and social-psychological insights, can support mechanisms such as reputation, social rewards, and peer punishment to promote cooperation and pro-environmental behavior. It reviews evidence that such mechanisms are effective in both face-to-face and computer-mediated environments, and proposes testable hypotheses and design features (e.g., visible reputational displays, badges, leaderboards, group competition, and mechanisms for social rewards and punishment) for future field studies. However, it does not present new experimental or observational data, nor does it report efficiency or payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical and design-oriented guidance on how punishment, reward, and reputational mechanisms might be implemented in online public-goods-like environments to promote cooperation. It does not provide empirical estimates or direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff, nor does it report on the moderating effects of specific game design dimensions. Its value for prediction is in highlighting which mechanisms and features are hypothesized to matter, but it does not provide quantitative or outcome-based evidence for the prediction task.
