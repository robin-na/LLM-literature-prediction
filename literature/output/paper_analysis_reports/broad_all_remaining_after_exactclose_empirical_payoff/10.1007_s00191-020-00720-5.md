## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model finds that punishment (authoritarian leadership) can increase organizational performance relative to no control when workers are low-skilled or when monitoring is efficient, but is always less efficient than motivation (authoritative leadership) when workers are high-skilled and supervisors are charismatic. Multiple equilibria are possible: (1) 'Discipline' (universal cooperation with punishment), (2) 'Cooperation' (universal cooperation with motivation), (3) 'Conflict' (universal defection with punishment), and (4) mixed or cyclical regimes. The most efficient outcome is universal cooperation with motivation, but this requires high worker skill and supervisor charisma. Punishment can prevent the spread of shirking (the 'bad company' effect) by increasing procedural justice, but also reduces morale and is less efficient. Inefficient equilibria with widespread punishment and/or shirking can persist due to path dependence. The model provides explicit conditions for when each regime is stable and analyzes the welfare properties of each.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct theoretical evidence on how punishment affects efficiency in a public-goods-like team production setting, conditional on game design dimensions such as monitoring efficiency (punishment tech), team size (player count), and the presence/absence of reward (motivation/charisma). It shows that punishment can increase efficiency relative to no control when monitoring is effective and workers are not highly skilled, but is always less efficient than a well-functioning reward/motivation regime. The model gives explicit parameter boundaries for when punishment is effective, and highlights the importance of initial conditions and path dependence. For prediction, the paper suggests that enabling punishment will increase efficiency relative to no-punishment in some parameter regimes (especially with low worker skill and high monitoring efficiency), but that the maximum efficiency is achieved with motivation/charisma rather than punishment. The model is highly relevant for predicting treatment efficiency from design dimensions and control efficiency, especially in settings where reward/motivation is not available or not effective.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each supervisor oversees a team of two workers (team size = 3: 1 supervisor + 2 workers). The model is built around this fixed team size.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Workers have only two effort choices: cooperate (exert effort) or defect (shirk). All-or-nothing contribution.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Productivity parameters R^C and R^P convert effort into output; these play the role of MPCR, but are not labeled as such. The marginal return to cooperation depends on these parameters.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as wage reduction for defectors; the cost to the punisher is not explicit, but punishment is costly in terms of supervisor-worker relationship (reduced morale/utility).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Monitoring efficiency (probability p of catching a defector) is a key parameter; higher p increases the effectiveness of punishment in sustaining cooperation and efficiency.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Motivation/charisma is modeled as a reward mechanism (psychological utility B for cooperators with motivator supervisors). The model contrasts punishment and reward regimes.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is modeled as a psychological benefit (B) to workers; the cost to the supervisor is not explicit, but is implied in the need for charisma/effort.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Effectiveness of reward is parameterized by B (charisma); higher B increases the effectiveness of motivation in sustaining cooperation and efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental data.
- Team size is fixed at two workers per supervisor; results may not generalize to larger groups.
- No explicit round structure; continuous-time evolutionary dynamics may differ from discrete repeated games.
- Punishment and reward are modeled as supervisor strategies, not as peer-to-peer mechanisms.
- The cost of punishment and reward to the supervisor is not fully specified in monetary terms.
- Initial conditions and path dependence are critical; the model does not address how initial distributions arise.
- No explicit modeling of communication, information, or learning beyond imitation.
