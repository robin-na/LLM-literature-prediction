# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Communication, reputation, and punishment in sequential bargaining experiments
Authors: Brosig, J., Weimann, J., & Yang, C. L.
Journal: Journal of Institutional and Theoretical Economics-zeitschrift Fur Die Gesamte Staatswissenschaft
Year: 2004

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- proportion of first movers choosing non-equilibrium action (A)
- proportion of second movers punishing or rewarding
- frequency of equal splits
- frequency of punishment and reward
- frequency of agreements and their realization
Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on behavioral outcomes (choices, punishment, reward, agreement) rather than direct measurement of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings. Efficiency is discussed in the context of equal splits, but not directly measured as a ratio of realized to maximum possible group payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that, without communication, the efficiency of punishment and the opportunity cost for the first mover significantly affect the likelihood of punishment and the first mover's choices, respectively. However, almost all second movers exploit the first mover's cooperative move, and equal splits are rare. When preplay communication is allowed, especially face-to-face, there is a dramatic increase in the number of equal splits (efficient outcomes), and the influence of punishment efficiency and opportunity cost largely disappears. E-mail communication also increases equal splits, but less so than face-to-face. The effect of communication cannot be fully explained by reputation. The paper does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, but the behavioral shift toward equal splits with communication suggests higher efficiency in those treatments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that preplay communication (especially face-to-face) is a powerful moderator that increases the likelihood of efficient, equal-split outcomes in sequential bargaining games with punishment and reward options. In the absence of communication, the efficiency of punishment and the opportunity cost for the first mover are important determinants of behavior, but do not lead to efficient outcomes. The findings suggest that, for prediction tasks, the presence and type of communication should be considered a key dimension, and that enabling communication can substantially increase efficiency, even when punishment is available. However, the results are from a sequential bargaining game, not a standard public goods game, so direct quantitative predictions for PGGs should be made with caution.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are two-player (dyadic) sequential bargaining games.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are one-shot (single round).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Preplay communication is a main treatment variable: none, e-mail (anonymous), or video (face-to-face). Communication increases equal splits (efficient outcomes).
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/choice is binary: first mover chooses A or B; second mover chooses 1 or 2.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost to the second mover and punishment impact on the first mover are varied across games (H, M, L) via the payoff matrix. The ratio (punishment efficiency) is a key treatment variable.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment efficiency (impact per unit cost) is manipulated: high in game H, low in game M. Higher punishment efficiency increases punishment frequency in no-communication treatments, but effect disappears with communication.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Second mover can reward first mover by choosing the equal-split outcome after a cooperative move. Reward is available in all games.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward cost is embedded in the payoff matrix; second mover can choose to reward at a cost to themselves (foregoing higher personal payoff).
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is determined by the payoff structure; not varied independently but present in all games.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game is one-shot; instructions make this clear.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a sequential bargaining game, not a standard public goods game; results may not generalize directly to PGGs.
- Efficiency or group payoff is not directly measured or reported; conclusions about efficiency are inferred from behavioral outcomes (equal splits).
- All games are one-shot; repeated interaction effects are not studied.
- Player count is fixed at two; results may not generalize to larger groups.
- Communication treatments are limited to preplay (not during play), and only two forms (e-mail and video) are tested.
- Punishment and reward are binary choices embedded in the payoff matrix, not continuous or independently parameterized.
