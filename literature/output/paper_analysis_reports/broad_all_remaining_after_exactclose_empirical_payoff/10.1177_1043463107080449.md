# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The social exchange heuristic - Managing errors in social exchange
Authors: Yamagishi, T., Terai, S., Kiyonari, T., Mifune, N., & Kanazawa, S.
Journal: Rationality and Society
Year: 2007

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate (binary and continuous contribution)
- expectation of partner's cooperation
- preference for mutual cooperation vs. unilateral defection (subjective matrix transformation)
- role-taking/empathy scale
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are cooperation rates (binary in Experiment 1, continuous in Experiment 2), expectations of partner's cooperation, and subjective preference for mutual cooperation. No group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported or analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that cooperation in one-shot PDGs is significantly higher when participants perceive themselves to be in an ongoing social exchange relationship (i.e., when the SEH is activated) compared to when they do not. In Experiment 1, participants who deliberated about the game and their partner before being assigned to a specific exchange relationship (delayed-play) cooperated less than those who made decisions immediately after being assigned a partner (immediate-play). In Experiment 2, participants who made their contribution decision after being told they were matched with a specific partner (partner-specified) contributed more than those who made their decision before being matched (partner-unspecified). The study attributes these effects to the activation of the SEH, which frames the game as an Assurance Game and increases conditional cooperation. No punishment, sanctioning, or efficiency outcomes are studied.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. It does not manipulate or analyze punishment, sanctions, or group payoff/efficiency. However, it does provide informative evidence that cues of social exchange and perceived interdependence can substantially increase cooperation rates in dyadic social dilemmas. For prediction tasks, this suggests that psychological framing and the presence of cues indicating ongoing relationships may be important moderators of cooperation, but the paper does not address punishment or efficiency directly.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are dyadic (2 players per game).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are one-shot (single round).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players; participants are isolated in separate rooms.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Experiment 1: binary choice (all-or-nothing); Experiment 2: continuous contribution.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each yen given is doubled for the partner (MPCR = 2 for the recipient, but not a standard PGG structure).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told the game is one-shot; no uncertainty about the number of rounds.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses only dyadic Prisoner's Dilemma Games, not public goods games or n-person social dilemmas.
- No punishment, sanctioning, or reward mechanisms are present or analyzed.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates and expectations are measured.
- All participants are Japanese college freshmen; generalizability to other populations or cultures is untested.
- The findings are about psychological framing and social heuristics, not about institutional or structural interventions like punishment.
