# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: On the coevolution of retribution and trustworthiness:: An (indirect) evolutionary and experimental analysis
Authors: Güth, W., Kliemt, H., Levati, M. V., & von Wangenheim, G.
Journal: Journal of Institutional and Theoretical Economics-zeitschrift Fur Die Gesamte Staatswissenschaft
Year: 2007

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of effort exertion (trustworthiness)
- frequency of punishment (revenge/suing)
- correlation between untrustworthiness and punishment
- effect of monetary inducement on trustworthiness and punishment
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: whether player 1 exerts effort (trustworthiness) and whether player 2 punishes (revenge/suing). The paper does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare as primary outcomes. Payoff parameters are described, but aggregate payoff outcomes are not analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that most participants behave trustworthily (exert effort) and that a small but significant share of punishment (revenge/suing) persists, even among those without monetary inducement. The share of trustworthiness and punishment does not depend on the number of monetarily induced 'moral' players or the order of treatments. There is a weak positive correlation between observed untrustworthiness and subsequent punishment. Monetary inducement increases the likelihood of trustworthiness and punishment among targeted individuals, but not to the level predicted by rational choice. The main outcomes are behavioral; the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that, in a repeated trust game with costly punishment, the availability of punishment leads to persistent (though low) rates of punishment and high rates of trustworthiness, even without material incentives. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-like games. The behavioral findings suggest that punishment can support trustworthiness, but the lack of payoff analysis limits its direct utility for efficiency prediction tasks.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each matching group consists of 16 players; sessions have 32 participants split into two groups.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consists of 100 periods (two phases of 50 periods each).
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Effort choice is binary: exert effort or not (all-or-nothing).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (suing) costs 20 ECU to the punisher (player 2).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (suing) reduces the punished player's payoff to 0 ECU (if no effort), or to -20 ECU (if effort was exerted).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the total number of periods (100), as the session is divided into two phases of 50 periods each.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. In each period (except the first), participants receive information about the shares of no-effort and revenge choices in the previous period.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is not a public goods game or a direct variant; it is a repeated trust game with binary effort and punishment choices.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (effort and punishment rates), not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- No direct analysis of efficiency or total group earnings is reported.
- Punishment is only available as a costly option (suing) after failed investment; no positive reward mechanism exists.
- The design does not allow for direct mapping to standard PGG dimensions such as MPCR or continuous contribution.
