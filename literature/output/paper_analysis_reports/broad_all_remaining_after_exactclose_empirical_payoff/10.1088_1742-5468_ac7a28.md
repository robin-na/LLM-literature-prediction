## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that conditional punishment based on reputation (switching between fines and exclusion) is more effective at promoting cooperation than traditional punishment alone. Higher reputation thresholds (stricter punishment), higher fines, and lower exclusion costs all lead to higher frequencies of cooperation. The enhancement factor (analogous to MPCR) also strongly moderates the emergence and stability of cooperation. The model shows phase transitions between full defection and full cooperation, and regions where cooperation and defection coexist. However, the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes directly, focusing instead on behavioral outcomes (strategy frequencies).

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the design of punishment mechanisms (including conditionality based on reputation, the severity of fines, and the cost of exclusion) can substantially affect the frequency of cooperation in spatial PGGs. While the findings are highly relevant for predicting the impact of punishment on cooperation rates, they are only indirectly informative for predicting efficiency or group payoff, since these are not reported. The results suggest that, in similar spatial PGGs, enabling punishment (especially with flexible, reputation-based mechanisms) is likely to increase cooperation rates, which would generally be expected to improve efficiency, but the magnitude of efficiency gains cannot be directly inferred from this paper.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each player is on a 100x100 lattice (10,000 players), with each player participating in 5 overlapping groups of 5 (self + 4 neighbors). The group size is always 5.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for at least 10,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with averages taken over the last 500 MCS after stabilization.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: players either contribute 1 (cooperators, punishers) or 0 (defectors).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Enhancement factor r (1 < r < 5) is varied; higher r (higher MPCR) promotes cooperation.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punisher pays cost α per fine and γ per exclusion; lower exclusion cost γ increases cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is varied via fine β (for mild punishment) and exclusion (for severe punishment); higher β increases cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes directly; only behavioral outcomes (strategy frequencies) are analyzed.
- Findings may not generalize to non-spatial or non-lattice PGGs, or to real human subjects.
- No empirical or experimental data are presented; results are theoretical/model-based.
- No reward mechanisms are considered, so results may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
- The model assumes deterministic exclusion and does not consider probabilistic or partial exclusion.
