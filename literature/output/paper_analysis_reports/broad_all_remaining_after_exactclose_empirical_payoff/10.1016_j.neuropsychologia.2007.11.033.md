# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The neural correlates of the affective response to unreciprocated cooperation
Authors: Rilling, J. K., Goldsmith, D. R., Glenn, A. L., Jairam, M. R., Elfenbein, H. A., Dagenais, J. E., Murdock, C. D., & Pagnoni, G.
Journal: Neuropsychologia
Year: 2008

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- Neural activation (fMRI BOLD response) to unreciprocated vs. reciprocated cooperation
- Emotion ratings (anger, irritation, disappointment, happiness, camaraderie, trust, etc.) for each outcome
- Subsequent decision to cooperate or defect after unreciprocated cooperation
Outcome notes:
No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings. The focus is on neural and emotional responses to unreciprocated cooperation (partner defection) in the iterated Prisoner's Dilemma, and how these responses predict subsequent defection. No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present in the game.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that unreciprocated cooperation (being defected on after cooperating) in the iterated Prisoner's Dilemma elicits strong negative emotions and increased neural activity in regions associated with aversive and social pain (anterior insula, hippocampus, amygdala). These neural responses are stronger when the unreciprocated cooperation follows a cooperative move by the subject, suggesting sensitivity to perceived partner intent. Functional connectivity between the anterior insula and lateral orbitofrontal cortex predicts whether the subject will defect in the next round. The paper does not include any punishment or sanctioning mechanism, nor does it report efficiency or group payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff in public goods or Prisoner's Dilemma games. It does, however, provide evidence that unreciprocated cooperation (partner defection) triggers strong negative affect and neural responses that bias subjects toward defection in subsequent rounds. This suggests a psychological mechanism by which cooperation can unravel in the absence of punishment or sanctioning. For the downstream prediction task, this paper supports the idea that, without punishment, negative experiences with defectors can lead to reduced cooperation, but it does not provide quantitative or comparative data on efficiency or payoff, nor does it test the effect of introducing punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is two-player (dyadic) iterated Prisoner's Dilemma.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 rounds per session in both experiments.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication allowed; only a brief introduction with no conversation.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Choice is binary: cooperate or defect (all-or-nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Payoff matrix: CC = $2 each, CD = $0 (cooperator), $3 (defector), DC = $3 (defector), $0 (cooperator), DD = $1 each. This is a standard Prisoner's Dilemma, not a linear PGG; MPCR concept does not directly apply but payoffs are specified.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects were told they would play 20 rounds per session; number of rounds is known.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, subjects saw the partner's choice and the outcome; no mention of aggregate summaries.

## Important Limitations
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present in the game; the study does not test the effect of punishment on cooperation or efficiency.
- No direct measurement or analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings; outcomes are neural, emotional, and behavioral (cooperation/defection) only.
- Game is a two-player iterated Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game or a multi-player variant.
- Sample sizes are moderate (N=30 and N=20), and all subjects are from a university community.
- Findings are about neural and affective responses to unreciprocated cooperation, not about institutional or design interventions.
