Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are resource abundance (interpreted as group welfare/efficiency), and average payoffs for cooperators and defectors. The proportion of cooperators is also tracked, but the focus is on resource sustainability and group-level outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that both tax-funded punishment and reward mechanisms can promote cooperation and sustain public resources, but only if the natural growth rate of the resource pool is sufficiently high. When the resource growth rate is low, even universal cooperation (driven by strong punishment or reward) cannot prevent resource depletion, so efficiency remains low. For moderate or high growth rates, the system can reach stable states with high cooperation and sustainable resources, but the tax-punishment model can exhibit bistability, meaning the final outcome depends on initial conditions. The magnitude of the tax (which determines the strength of punishment or reward) also affects whether the system stabilizes at full cooperation, coexistence, or defection. The models provide explicit, parameterized predictions for group efficiency and resource sustainability as a function of player count, tax size, and resource growth rate.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct, parameterized theoretical predictions for the effect of punishment (and reward) on group efficiency and resource sustainability in public-goods-like resource management games. The models are not standard linear PGGs, but are close variants with dynamic, renewable resources and tax-based centralized punishment/reward. The findings show that the effect of punishment on efficiency is strongly moderated by the natural growth rate of the resource pool and the magnitude of the tax (punishment strength). For low growth rates, punishment does not improve efficiency; for moderate/high growth rates, punishment can improve efficiency, but outcomes may be bistable. The results are most applicable to settings where resource dynamics and centralized sanctioning are key features. The paper is highly informative for predicting efficiency outcomes from game design dimensions in such environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `unclear` under this dimension. Player count N is a key parameter in all models and simulations (e.g., N=1000 in all figures). The effect of varying N is not systematically analyzed, but the models are parameterized by N.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: each individual chooses to cooperate (abide by allocation rule) or defect (take more).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The resource allocation rule is parameterized by b_m, R_m, and N, which together determine the marginal return structure. Not a standard linear PGG, but analogous.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is funded by a tax δ paid by all; the total tax is distributed as punishment to defectors. The cost is thus δ per player per round.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by how the total tax is distributed among defectors. The model explicitly parameterizes this.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. The paper analyzes both tax-reward and tax-punishment models.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is funded by a tax δ paid by all; the total tax is distributed as reward to cooperators.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is determined by how the total tax is distributed among cooperators. The model explicitly parameterizes this.

## Important Limitations
- The models are theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- The resource management game is a close variant of the PGG, with dynamic, renewable resources and centralized, tax-based punishment/reward, not a standard linear PGG.
- The models assume perfectly rational agents and do not account for bounded rationality, learning, or other behavioral factors.
- No analysis of finite rounds, communication, or information feedback.
- Bistability in the punishment model means that outcomes can be highly sensitive to initial conditions, which may limit predictive power in real-world settings.
- No direct empirical validation with human subjects.
