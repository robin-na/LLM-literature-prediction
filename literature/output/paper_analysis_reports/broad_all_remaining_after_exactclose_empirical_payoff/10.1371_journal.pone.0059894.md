The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, welfare, or surplus directly, though the cooperation level is closely related to these outcomes in public goods games.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that the effectiveness of punishment strictness in promoting cooperation depends on the population structure. In spatially structured populations (lattice), strict punishment (high strictness parameter) leads to higher evolved cooperation levels than graduated punishment. In random-matching (well-mixed) populations, graduated punishment (low strictness parameter) is more effective at increasing cooperation. The results are robust across a range of parameters for public goods efficiency and punishment effectiveness. However, the paper does not report direct measures of group efficiency or total payoff; instead, it focuses on the average cooperation level as the main outcome.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and simulation evidence that the effect of punishment on cooperation (and by extension, efficiency) is moderated by the population structure: strict punishment is more effective in spatially structured settings, while graduated punishment is more effective in well-mixed settings. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the design dimension corresponding to population structure (e.g., fixed groups vs. random rematching) is a key moderator of punishment's effect. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, its findings should be interpreted as informative about contribution/cooperation rates, which are closely related but not identical to efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 4 players in the public goods game; the total population is 50x50=2500 individuals, but the game is always played in groups of 4.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The evolutionary simulations run for 1,000,000 generations, with each individual experiencing one birth-death event per generation. There is no fixed number of rounds in the sense of a repeated game; the model is evolutionary.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous, not all-or-nothing; each player's cooperation level is a continuous variable between 0 and 1.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is r/4, where r is the efficiency of the public good (varied in simulations, e.g., r=3, r=4).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost is a function of the punisher's severity parameter (f), the strictness parameter (a), and the threshold (u); cost is paid per punished opponent. The cost function is explicitly defined.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is parameterized by β (beta), which is the damage per unit cost; β > 1. β is varied in simulations.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report direct measures of group efficiency, total payoff, welfare, or surplus; all main outcomes are behavioral (evolved cooperation level, punishment traits).
- The results are based on evolutionary simulations and mathematical models, not laboratory or field experiments with human subjects.
- The model assumes continuous traits and infinite evolutionary time, which may not map directly onto finite repeated games or experimental settings.
- No reward mechanism is considered, so findings may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
- The model does not include communication, reputation, or other social mechanisms beyond spatial structure and punishment.
