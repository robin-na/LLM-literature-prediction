# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Cooperation and assortativity with dynamic partner updating
Authors: Wang, J., Suri, S., & Watts, D. J.
Journal: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America
Year: 2012

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average payoff
- player earnings
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- assortativity
- link addition/deletion behavior
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are cooperation rate and assortativity, but average payoffs and player earnings are also reported and analyzed, especially in relation to the effect of dynamic partner updating.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that dynamic partner updating—where players can choose to make or break ties with others—substantially increases both cooperation rates and average payoffs in iterated prisoner's dilemma games, compared to static networks. Even low rates of partner updating are sufficient to produce large positive effects. The effect is concave: initial increases in update rate have large effects, but further increases yield smaller gains. When the payoff matrix is modified so that maintaining ties with defectors is more costly, cooperation is even higher and more sustained, as players are more willing to sever ties with defectors. The main mechanism is assortative mixing: cooperators preferentially connect with other cooperators and avoid defectors, rather than punishing defectors directly. The results suggest that dynamic partner selection can substitute for explicit punishment or reward mechanisms in promoting cooperation and increasing group payoffs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling dynamic partner updating (i.e., endogenous, bilateral partner choice and unilateral link deletion) in repeated social dilemma games can dramatically increase group efficiency (average payoff relative to the cooperative optimum), even in the absence of explicit punishment or reward mechanisms. The effect is robust across a range of update rates and is further enhanced when the cost of interacting with defectors is increased. For prediction tasks, the presence of dynamic partner updating should be treated as a strong positive moderator of efficiency, especially when players can freely choose partners and sever ties. However, the game is an iterated prisoner's dilemma on a dynamic network, not a standard public goods game, and the 'punishment' is implicit (via partner selection) rather than explicit. Thus, while the findings are highly informative for environments with endogenous partner choice, they are only adjacent to explicit punishment in PGGs. The results suggest that, in settings where partner choice is possible, explicit punishment may be less necessary for achieving high efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All experiments used groups of 24 participants.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games comprised 12 strategy update rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate or defect each round.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players knew the game comprised 12 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players were shown the identities (anonymous IDs) and strategy choices for up to the previous five rounds for all players, as well as their own and others' current and previous payoffs.

## Important Limitations
- The game is an iterated prisoner's dilemma on a dynamic network, not a standard public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGGs.
- There is no explicit punishment or reward mechanism; all 'punishment' is implicit via partner selection (link deletion).
- Payoff structure and network dynamics differ from standard PGGs, so marginal per-capita return and other PGG-specific parameters are not defined.
- The study uses online, anonymous subjects (Mechanical Turk), which may limit generalizability to other populations.
- The effect of dynamic partner updating is only tested in the context of endogenous, bilateral partner choice and unilateral link deletion; other forms of partner updating may yield different results.
