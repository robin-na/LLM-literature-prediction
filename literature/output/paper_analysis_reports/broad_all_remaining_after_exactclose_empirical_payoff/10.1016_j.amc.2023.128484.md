Outcome notes:
The main outcome is the frequency of cooperation (proportion of cooperators) in the population. No direct analysis of group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings is reported. The model is theoretical and simulation-based, focusing on behavioral dynamics rather than explicit payoffs or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that incorporating local loyalty to both cooperation and defection strategies in a spatial prisoner's dilemma game can promote the emergence and stability of cooperation, especially when the strength index of local loyalty is positive and the weighting between payoff and loyalty is balanced. There are optimal parameter values that maximize cooperation frequency, and the system can reach full cooperation under these conditions. However, the paper does not report on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, and all results are based on simulation of behavioral outcomes (cooperation rates and loyalty patterns).

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-game-like environments. While it explores mechanisms (loyalty, reputation) that are conceptually adjacent to punishment and cooperation, it does not manipulate or analyze punishment, nor does it report efficiency or payoff-based outcomes. Its findings may be contextually informative for understanding how social mechanisms can promote cooperation, but it does not provide quantitative or qualitative evidence for the efficiency prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a spatial lattice of 10,000 individuals (100x100 grid), each with 4 neighbors. The player count is fixed in simulations.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 100,000 Monte Carlo steps (rounds), with outcomes averaged over the last 3,000 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate or defect (binary, all-or-nothing).

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are used.
- The main outcome is cooperation frequency, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game or direct variant.
- No explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism is modeled; loyalty is not equivalent to punishment.
- Findings may not generalize to public goods games or to efficiency outcomes relevant for the prediction task.
