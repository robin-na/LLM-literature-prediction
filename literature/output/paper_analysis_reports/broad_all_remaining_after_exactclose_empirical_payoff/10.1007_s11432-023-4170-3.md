The main outcomes are the stationary fractions of each strategy (altruistic punishers, non-punishing cooperators, defectors, exiters) in the population under different parameter regimes. No explicit efficiency or welfare ratio is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that adding an exit option to a prisoner's dilemma with altruistic punishment changes the evolutionary dynamics. In well-mixed populations, exiters replace defectors as the exit payoff increases, but altruistic punishers do not become dominant. In networked populations, exiters can support the coexistence of altruistic punishers, defectors, and exiters via cyclic dominance, but this effect is unstable and depends on the exit payoff. Sometimes, non-punishing cooperators replace altruistic punishers, and the system can exhibit bi-stability between these outcomes. The effect of exiters on cooperation and punishment is nuanced and context-dependent. The paper does not provide direct predictions or measurements of group efficiency or welfare, but the results suggest that exit options can sometimes support, but also sometimes undermine, the mechanisms that sustain cooperation and punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about how the presence and incentive level of an exit option (voluntary participation) can affect the evolutionary stability of punishment and cooperation in social dilemmas. While the model is not a standard public goods game and does not report efficiency as a ratio to the fully cooperative outcome, it suggests that exit options can destabilize defection and, under some conditions in networked populations, support the coexistence of altruistic punishment. However, the effect is highly sensitive to the exit payoff and population structure, and exiters can also undermine punishment by enabling non-punishing cooperators to dominate. For prediction tasks, this paper suggests that the presence of an exit option and its incentive level are important moderators of punishment's effect, especially in structured populations, but does not provide quantitative efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Finite population simulations use N=100 for well-mixed, and networked populations use regular lattices of size 200x200 to 800x800. Infinite population replicator dynamics are also analyzed.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Evolutionary dynamics are run for up to 50,000 Monte Carlo steps in networked populations; stationary distributions are analyzed. The model is not round-based in the experimental sense but uses repeated evolutionary updating.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are discrete: cooperate (with or without punishment), defect, or exit. No continuous contribution levels.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost to the punisher is fixed at γ=0.1 in all simulations.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (fine to defector) is fixed at β=0.3. The ratio of fine to cost is not varied.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a two-player prisoner's dilemma with punishment and exit, not a standard public goods game; results may not transfer directly to multiplayer PGGs.
- No explicit efficiency or group payoff ratio is reported; outcomes are in terms of stationary fractions of strategies.
- All results are theoretical/simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- Network structure is limited to regular lattices; real social networks may differ in important ways.
- Punishment parameters (cost and effectiveness) are fixed and not varied systematically.
- No reward mechanism is included; only punishment and exit are modeled.
- No human behavioral data; predictions may not match real-world or experimental outcomes.
