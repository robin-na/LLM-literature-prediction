## Source
Title: Exchange in the Absence of Legal Enforcement: Reputation and Multilateral Punishment under Uncertainty
Authors: Hajikhameneh, A., & Rubin, J.
Journal: Journal of Law Economics & Organization
Year: 2019

empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- total welfare (sum of principal and agent earnings)
- mean earnings per period by role
- group surplus
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of send (principal's trust)
- frequency of divide (agent's cooperation)
- frequency of inform (punishment)
- effect of reputation on behavior
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is total welfare (sum of principal and agent earnings), with detailed reporting of mean earnings by role and treatment. Behavioral outcomes (send, divide, inform) are also analyzed, but efficiency/welfare is the primary focus.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated trust game with 12 principals and 12 agents per session, a multilateral punishment institution allows principals to assign a permanent bad reputation to agents at a cost (C=0 or C=5). The experiment varies the probability that the principal's return is destroyed by nature (θ=0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.30). Total welfare (sum of principal and agent earnings) is the main outcome. When punishment is costless, welfare decreases monotonically with uncertainty. When punishment is costly, welfare is not significantly different between no uncertainty and modest uncertainty (θ=0.05), but falls at higher uncertainty. The cost of punishment reduces the frequency of punishment and can lead to more 'benefit of the doubt' given to agents with bad reputations, which can offset welfare losses from uncertainty at low levels. However, at high uncertainty, welfare declines regardless of punishment cost. The design does not allow for endogenous institution formation or communication between players.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence on how the cost of punishment and the presence of outcome uncertainty moderate the effect of multilateral punishment on efficiency in a trust-game-like environment. For prediction tasks, it suggests that punishment can sustain high efficiency when uncertainty is low and punishment is not too costly, but that the effect is attenuated or reversed as uncertainty increases or punishment becomes more costly. The results are most relevant for trust games with binary reputation and costly, permanent punishment, and less directly transferable to standard PGGs. The findings support the importance of including punishment cost and environmental uncertainty as moderators when predicting the efficiency impact of punishment mechanisms.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session has 12 principals and 12 agents (24 players total), matched in pairs each round.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session lasts 12 periods (rounds).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (send) and division (split) decisions are binary (all-or-nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. If principal sends, group endowment doubles (from 20 to 40 francs); if agent splits, each gets 20. MPCR for principal is 1 (if agent splits and nature does not intervene), but the structure is not a standard linear PGG.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment (informing) cost is either 0 or 5 francs (25% of max principal period earnings). Higher cost reduces frequency of punishment and can moderate negative welfare effects of uncertainty.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is permanent and binary: one report gives agent a bad reputation for all future rounds, visible to all principals. No gradation or recovery.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players do not know the exact number of rounds in advance; lab reserved for longer than needed to prevent endgame effects.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Principals see whether the agent they are matched with has a bad or good reputation. After each period, both participants see their own earnings and whether the principal sent, received francs, and reported (when applicable).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous in the sense that agents do not know which principal reported them, but all principals see the agent's reputation status in future rounds.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a modified trust game, not a standard public goods game; results may not generalize directly to PGGs.
- Punishment is permanent and binary (bad reputation), not continuous or proportional.
- No reward mechanism is present; only punishment is studied.
- No communication or endogenous institution formation is allowed.
- The effect of punishment is confounded with the effect of reputation and exclusion; not all forms of punishment are captured.
- The environment includes outcome uncertainty (nature can destroy returns), which is not typical in standard PGGs.
- The sample is from a university lab population; external validity may be limited.
