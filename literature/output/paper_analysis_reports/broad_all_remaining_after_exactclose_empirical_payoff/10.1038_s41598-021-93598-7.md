## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the stability of cooperation in a continuous donation game with indirect reciprocity depends on the sensitivity of the assessment and behavioral rules (social norms). It derives explicit conditions (in terms of derivatives of the assessment and behavioral functions) under which a cooperative norm is stable against local mutants, and shows that the benefit-to-cost ratio required for stability increases if reputation is insensitive to observed behavior or if cooperation is insensitive to recipient reputation. The analysis is supported by numerical simulations, which confirm the theoretical predictions about payoff differences between resident and mutant norms. The model does not include explicit punishment or reward mechanisms as in standard PGGs, but reputation loss functions as an indirect sanction. The main outcome is the expected payoff (efficiency) of the group under different norms and parameter settings.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical and simulation evidence about how the structure of social norms (in terms of sensitivity to behavior and reputation) affects the efficiency (payoff) of cooperation in a continuous donation game with indirect reciprocity. While not a direct PGG or explicit punishment game, it is relevant for predicting efficiency in environments where cooperation is enforced by reputation-based indirect sanctions rather than direct punishment. The findings suggest that the efficiency of cooperation depends on the responsiveness of the reputation system and the behavioral rule, and that certain parameter regimes (high benefit-to-cost ratio, high sensitivity) are required for stable, efficient cooperation. The results are most applicable to settings where indirect reciprocity is the main enforcement mechanism, and less so to direct punishment or reward treatments in PGGs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Player count N is a parameter in the model and simulations (e.g., N=50, N=100), but the main theoretical results are in the large-N limit. No direct analysis of how N moderates efficiency.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Simulations are run for a fixed number of rounds (M), e.g., M=10^4, M=5x10^4. Theoretical analysis focuses on stationary states as M→∞.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (cooperation) is continuous between 0 and 1, not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is b/c, with b=2 and c=1 in most simulations. The benefit-to-cost ratio is a key parameter in the stability condition.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Players have access to reputation information about others, but only probabilistically (observation probability q).

## Important Limitations
- The model is not a public goods game or a direct punishment game; it is a continuous donation game with indirect reciprocity and reputation-based enforcement.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanisms are modeled; only indirect sanctions via reputation loss.
- Theoretical results focus on local stability against small mutations, not global evolutionary stability against all possible mutants.
- Simulations are limited to moderate population sizes and specific parameter regimes.
- The model assumes continuous reputation and behavior, which may not fully capture cognitive or institutional constraints in real-world settings.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are theoretical or simulation-based.
