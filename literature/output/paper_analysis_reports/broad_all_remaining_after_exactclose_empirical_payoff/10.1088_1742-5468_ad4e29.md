Non-payoff outcomes:

Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are expected payoffs and stationary frequencies of strategies, which directly relate to group efficiency. Behavioral outcomes (strategy frequencies) are also reported, but payoff-based outcomes are central to the analysis.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that global exclusion (a centralized exclusion mechanism) in the public goods game leads to higher group efficiency and can robustly promote cooperation, even in the presence of unbiased mutation. Theoretical analysis and agent-based simulations show that, for reasonable exclusion costs, the stationary level of global excluders (and thus group payoff) is higher than in standard PGGs. Mutation (strategy exploration) can synergistically interact with global exclusion, further increasing the emergent level of cooperation and efficiency, with an optimal mutation rate maximizing this effect. The model also identifies bifurcation structures and regions of parameter space where global exclusion is most effective.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that enabling a centralized exclusion mechanism (a form of punishment) in a PGG-like environment increases group efficiency, especially when the cost of exclusion is not too high. The effect is robust to the presence of unbiased mutation (strategy exploration), and there is an optimal mutation rate that maximizes efficiency. For prediction tasks, the findings suggest that, given a control game without exclusion/punishment, enabling a global exclusion mechanism (with reasonable cost) will increase average efficiency, and that the effect is further enhanced by moderate levels of mutation/exploration. The results are most directly applicable to infinitely well-mixed, large-population, one-shot or non-repeated PGGs with centralized exclusion and no second-order free-riding. The model does not address peer punishment, reward, or repeated interactions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Group size N is a parameter in the model (e.g., N=5 in simulations), but the main analysis is in the infinite population limit. The group size affects the payoff structure but is not varied in a way that allows direct inference about its moderating effect on punishment efficiency.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is one-shot (single-round) in structure, as repeated interactions are explicitly excluded to avoid second-order free-riding. Each group interaction is a single PGG.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: players either contribute a fixed amount (cooperate/exclude) or defect (contribute nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is r/N, where r is the enhancement factor and N is group size. r is a parameter in the model.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Exclusion cost (c_GE) is the cost to the excluder for constructing the exclusion institution. Lower c_GE leads to higher efficiency gains from exclusion; high c_GE reduces or eliminates the benefit.
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is modeled as exclusion from the public good, implemented by a centralized institution. The effectiveness is not parameterized separately from the exclusion cost, but the exclusion is assumed to be complete if the institution is constructed.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is one-shot; the structure is fully known to all players (in the model).

## Important Limitations
- The model only considers global exclusion (centralized, institutional exclusion) and does not address peer punishment, pool punishment, or other forms of sanctions.
- The analysis is restricted to the first-order free-riding problem (defection vs. exclusion) and does not include second-order free-riding (cooperators who do not pay for exclusion).
- The model assumes an infinitely well-mixed population and does not consider structured populations or repeated interactions.
- No reward mechanisms or positive incentives are modeled.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with peer punishment, reward, or more complex strategy sets.
