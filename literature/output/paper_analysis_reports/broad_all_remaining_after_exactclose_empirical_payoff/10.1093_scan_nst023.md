# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Diminishing parochialism in intergroup conflict by disrupting the right temporo-parietal junction
Authors: Baumgartner, T., Schiller, B., Rieskamp, J., Gianotti, L. R., & Knoch, D.
Journal: Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience
Year: 2014

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment points assigned by third-party
- parochial punishment pattern (difference in punishment of outgroup vs ingroup perpetrators)
- retaliation motive
- anger
- justice motivation
- improvement of future behavior motivation
- perspective-taking (mentalizing bias)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the number of punishment points assigned by a third-party to norm violators, with a focus on the difference in punishment between outgroup and ingroup perpetrators (parochial punishment). No group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported. The study also measures self-reported motives and emotions related to punishment.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that third-party punishment is more severe toward outgroup norm violators than ingroup violators (parochial punishment), especially in cases where the outgroup member defects against an ingroup member. Disrupting the right TPJ with rTMS reduces this parochial punishment, making third-parties treat outgroup and ingroup violators more similarly. This effect is mediated by a reduction in the motive to retaliate and a reduction in mentalizing bias (the tendency to take the perspective of ingroup more than outgroup members). The manipulation does not affect punishment in intragroup constellations or in non-defection cases. The study does not measure or report group efficiency or total payoffs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence about the psychological and neural mechanisms underlying parochial punishment in third-party punishment games, but does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. The design is adjacent to public goods games, as it uses a Prisoner's Dilemma for norm violation and a third-party punishment stage, but does not involve repeated public goods contributions or measure group efficiency. The findings are relevant for understanding when and why punishment is more severe toward outgroup members, and how neural interventions can reduce this bias. However, the results cannot be directly used to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as no efficiency or payoff data are reported. The punishment technology (1 point spent reduces target's payoff by 3) is specified, but the main outcomes are behavioral (punishment assigned) and motivational (retaliation, perspective-taking), not payoff-based.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment decision involves three players: Player A (potential violator), Player B (other PDG player), and Player C (third-party punisher).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each Player C made 30 punishment decisions (trials), each with a different preselected PDG outcome. No repeated interaction with the same individuals; all trials are one-shot and independent.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players; all interactions are anonymous and one-shot.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/defection in the PDG is all-or-nothing: each player chooses to keep all 20 points or pass all 20 points.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the PDG, points passed are doubled (multiplier = 2), and there are two players, so MPCR = 1.0 for the recipient (all-or-nothing transfer).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher (Player C) pays 1 point to reduce the punished player's payoff by 3 points.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3:1 (1 point spent reduces target's payoff by 3 points).
- reward availability: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No reward mechanism is available in the punishment stage; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of punishment trials (30) and that all are one-shot and independent.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Player C is shown the group affiliations and choices of Players A and B for each trial.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; all interactions are conducted in complete anonymity to exclude reputation effects.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes; all main outcomes are behavioral (punishment assigned) or motivational (retaliation, perspective-taking).
- The experimental design is a third-party punishment paradigm following a Prisoner's Dilemma, not a standard public goods game; thus, findings may not fully generalize to PGG settings.
- No data are provided on how punishment affects group efficiency or cooperation rates in repeated or multi-player settings.
- Sample size is relatively small (N=36, with 3 between-subjects conditions).
- All subjects are male soccer fans from Switzerland, which may limit generalizability.
