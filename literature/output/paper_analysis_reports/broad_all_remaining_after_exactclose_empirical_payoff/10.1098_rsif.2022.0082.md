The main dependent variables are the abundance of strategy types (cooperators, punishers, defectors) and the conditions under which cooperation is favored. Payoff expressions are derived and used to determine evolutionary stability, but group efficiency (as a ratio of realized to maximum possible group payoff) is not directly reported. The model is not a standard PGG but a lending game with joint liability and punishment.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that joint liability, when combined with costly punishment, strongly promotes the evolution of cooperation in a lending game. Punishment alone is insufficient to sustain cooperation, but joint liability provides incentives for individuals to punish defectors, leading to high levels of cooperation and low levels of defection. The condition for cooperation to evolve is relaxed from β−γ>4c (without joint liability) to β>γ (with joint liability and low tolerability for defection). The effect is robust to model extensions, including intragroup donation games (Prisoner's Dilemma). The abundance of cooperators and punishers is maximized at intermediate values of selection intensity, population size, and mutation rate. The model is adjacent to public goods games but differs in structure and outcome measures.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that the combination of joint liability and costly punishment can greatly increase cooperation in group settings similar to, but not identical with, public goods games. The findings suggest that punishment is much more effective at promoting cooperation when joint liability is present, and that the threshold for punishment to be effective is lower. However, because the model is not a standard PGG and does not report efficiency as a ratio of realized to maximum group payoff, the results should be transferred to PGG efficiency predictions with caution. The paper is most informative for understanding the mechanisms by which punishment and group-level liability interact, and for predicting that punishment will be more effective in environments with joint liability or similar group-level consequences.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Population size N is a key parameter, varied in both finite and infinite population analyses. Intermediate N is most beneficial for cooperation; very large N reduces cooperation.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: borrowers either repay (cooperate/punish) or default (defect).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost γ is a key parameter. Lower γ (relative to β) makes cooperation easier to evolve. The threshold for cooperation is β > γ (with joint liability).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (fine β per cost γ) is central. Higher β (for fixed γ) increases cooperation. The key threshold is β > γ (with joint liability).

## Important Limitations
- The model is not a standard public goods game; it is a lending game with joint liability and punishment, so direct transfer to PGG efficiency predictions is limited.
- Efficiency (group payoff as a fraction of maximum possible) is not directly reported; only abundance of types and expected payoffs are analyzed.
- Theoretical and simulation results may not capture all behavioral or institutional complexities of real-world group cooperation.
- No empirical or experimental data are provided; all results are theoretical or simulation-based.
- No analysis of communication, reward, or information structure effects.
