## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing a punishment strategy (punishers who pay a cost to fine defectors) in a spatial multigame environment (mix of PD and SD) increases the fraction of cooperation (cooperators and punishers), especially when the sucker's payoff is large or when more players play the Snowdrift game. Punishers form compact clusters that help protect against defectors. The average payoff is shown to recover as cooperation increases, but efficiency (as a ratio to full cooperation) is not directly analyzed. The findings are based on agent-based simulations, not experiments or real human subjects.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the idea that punishment can increase cooperation and average payoff in spatial social dilemmas, especially when there is heterogeneity in game parameters. However, because the model is not a public goods game and does not report efficiency as defined for the prediction task, its value for predicting treatment efficiency in PGG-like environments is limited. The main outcomes are behavioral (cooperation rates), and the only payoff-related outcome is average payoff, which is not benchmarked to full cooperation. The punishment mechanism is explicit and parameterized, but the results are not directly transferable to PGG efficiency prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a square lattice with N=4×10^4 players (200x200 grid), each player interacts with 4 neighbors.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 3×10^5 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with results averaged over the last 3×10^4 runs.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Strategy choice is discrete: cooperate, defect, or punish (no continuous contribution).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays cost γ=0.1 per punishment; fixed in main simulations.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment imposes fine β=0.3 on defectors per punishment; fixed in main simulations.

## Important Limitations
- The study is a theoretical agent-based simulation, not an experiment with human subjects.
- The game is not a public goods game; it is a spatial mix of Prisoner's Dilemma and Snowdrift games.
- Efficiency, as defined by group payoff relative to full cooperation, is not reported or analyzed.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (cooperation rates), not payoff-based efficiency.
- Parameter values for punishment are fixed and not systematically varied.
- Results may not generalize to PGG-like environments or to human behavior.
