## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study shows that time-varying reputation mechanisms driven by environmental feedback can promote and sustain higher levels of cooperation in a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma game. The effectiveness of different reputation update rules depends on the temptation to defect (b): some mechanisms are more effective under low social dilemma intensity, while others are better under high intensity. Increasing the maximum possible reputation reward/punishment (ΔR_max) and the environmental sensitivity parameter (β) can further enhance cooperation. The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not group efficiency or payoff. No direct analysis of group payoff, welfare, or efficiency is provided.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff in public goods games or their close variants. The model is a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game, and the outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency. The reputation mechanism is analogous to a form of social sanctioning, but it is not implemented as a costly punishment with explicit payoff consequences. Therefore, while the findings suggest that adaptive, environment-dependent sanctioning can promote cooperation, they do not provide quantitative or directly transferable evidence for the efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like environments. The results may be useful for mechanism design intuition, but not for direct prediction of efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a square lattice of size L x L, with L=200 as default (N=40,000), and robustness checks for L=100, 200, 300, 400. All players interact with their neighbors.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS) to equilibrium, with averages over the last 3,000 steps. Each MCS is a round of updates.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate (C) or defect (D). No continuous contribution.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reputation rewards for cooperation are implemented, but not as direct material rewards.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents have access to global environmental feedback (proportion of cooperators/defectors) for reputation updates. Reputation is visible for imitation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial weak Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game or direct variant.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate (fraction of cooperators), not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare.
- Reputation rewards and penalties are not implemented as costly punishment or reward with explicit payoff consequences.
- No direct analysis of efficiency or group payoff is provided.
- Findings may not transfer quantitatively to PGG-like environments with explicit punishment costs and payoff-based efficiency.
