## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a 60-round repeated prisoner's dilemma, human subjects played against computer opponents using either extortionate or generous zero-determinant (ZD) strategies. Extortionate strategies succeeded in obtaining higher payoffs than their human co-players, but both parties earned less in absolute terms than in the generosity treatments. Human players punished extortion by reducing cooperation, even at a cost to themselves, leading to lower overall efficiency. Generous strategies, in contrast, elicited higher cooperation and resulted in higher payoffs for both parties. The study demonstrates that implicit costly punishment (withholding cooperation) can limit the success of exploitative strategies and reduce group efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in repeated dyadic prisoner's dilemma games without explicit punishment mechanisms, the presence of exploitative (extortionate) strategies leads to lower efficiency than more cooperative (generous) strategies. Human players respond to perceived unfairness by reducing cooperation, which acts as an implicit costly punishment and reduces total payoffs. For prediction tasks, this suggests that enabling mechanisms that allow for punishment (even implicit, via withholding cooperation) can reduce efficiency if they are used in response to exploitation. The findings are most directly relevant to two-player, repeated, all-or-nothing contribution games with hidden horizon and no explicit communication or punishment options. The results highlight the importance of fairness concerns and the potential for costly punishment to reduce group welfare, even in the absence of formal punishment institutions.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a two-player repeated prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game or multi-player environment.
- Punishment is only implicit (withholding cooperation), not an explicit institutional mechanism.
- All games are against computer opponents using fixed strategies, not against other humans.
- Payoff structure details (exact R, S, T, P values) are not fully specified.
- No explicit manipulation of group size, communication, or other institutional features beyond the ZD strategy class.
