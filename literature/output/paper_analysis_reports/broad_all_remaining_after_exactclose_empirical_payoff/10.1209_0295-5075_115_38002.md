Title: Cooperation induced by wise incentive allocation in spontaneous institution
Authors: Cong, R., Li, K., Wang, L., & Zhao, Q. C.
Journal: Epl
Year: 2016

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- stationary distribution of strategies (fraction of time population spends in each pure state, which reflects average group payoff and cooperation level)
- expected payoff of each strategy
- fixation probability of strategies
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of cooperation
- frequency of defection
- institution membership (IC, ID, C, D)
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are theoretical predictions of the long-run prevalence of each strategy, which directly reflect group efficiency and welfare. The model explicitly computes payoffs for each strategy and analyzes how institutional punishment and reward affect the evolutionary outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a public goods game with optional institution membership, the optimal regime for cooperation and group efficiency is achieved when the institution balances punishment and reward. Pure punishment or pure reward is suboptimal: too much punishment collapses the institution, while too much reward allows institutional defectors to dominate. The efficiency of punishment and reward (i.e., their impact per unit cost) increases the range of conditions under which cooperation prevails. The model provides explicit conditions for when institutional cooperators outcompete defectors, and shows that the stationary distribution of strategies (and thus group efficiency) is maximized at intermediate values of the punishment/reward split.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support for the prediction that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games depends critically on the balance between punishment and reward, as well as the effectiveness of each. It suggests that enabling punishment alone may not always increase efficiency; the presence and strength of reward, and the cost-effectiveness of both, are crucial moderators. For prediction tasks, the model implies that intermediate punishment (with some reward) is optimal for efficiency, and that both the cost and impact of punishment and reward should be considered. The findings are directly relevant for settings where institutional incentives are endogenous and both punishment and reward are possible.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is entirely theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental validation.
- The model assumes well-mixed populations and does not consider spatial or network structure.
- Institutional incentives are endogenous but simplified; real-world institutions may have more complex rules and enforcement mechanisms.
- No explicit modeling of behavioral noise, learning, or bounded rationality beyond mutation and selection.
- No analysis of communication, reputation, or information feedback effects.
