# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Emergence of communities and diversity in social networks
Authors: Han, X., Cao, S. N., Shen, Z. S., Zhang, B. Y., Wang, W. X., Cressman, R., & Stanley, H. E.
Journal: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America
Year: 2017

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- proposer offer (p)
- responder minimum acceptance level (q)
- standard deviation of offers (SD(p))
- standard deviation of acceptance levels (SD(q))
- formation of local communities
- spatiotemporal patterns of offers
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: proposer offers, responder minimum acceptance levels, and their diversity (SD). The study focuses on the emergence of communities and diversity in offers, not on efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings. Payoff is discussed as part of the ultimatum game structure, but not as a primary outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in static network structures (both regular and random bipartite networks), proposers form stable local communities with diverse offers, while in randomly rewired networks, proposers become homogeneous. The diversity in offers (SD of p) is much higher in structured networks. Responders' behavior is heterogeneous but not affected by network structure. The mechanism is that rational proposers best-respond to their local responder 'leaders,' whose heterogeneous minimum acceptance levels drive local agreement. The study does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or surplus, and does not include a public goods game or explicit costly punishment mechanism.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. The experiment uses the ultimatum game, where 'punishment' is the rejection of offers, not a costly sanctioning mechanism. The main outcomes are behavioral (offer diversity, community formation), not payoff-based. Therefore, this paper should not be used as primary evidence for predicting efficiency changes due to punishment in PGG-like games. It may provide some indirect context about how local interaction structure and heterogeneity can affect behavioral diversity, but not efficiency or payoff outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each experimental group had 50 participants, split evenly between proposers and responders.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each experiment included 60-70 rounds; participants were not told the total number to avoid endgame effects.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (offer) is continuous: proposers choose p in [0,100], responders set minimum acceptance q in [0,100]. Not all-or-nothing.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were not told the number of rounds to prevent endgame effects.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each round, players were shown their own behavior and payoff, and the behavior of their neighbors (not linked to specific identities).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Feedback on neighbor behavior was provided, but not linked to specific neighbors; thus, punishment/rejection was anonymous.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses the ultimatum game, not a public goods game or a direct variant.
- Punishment is operationalized as rejection of offers, not as a costly sanctioning mechanism as in PGG or PD games.
- No efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes are reported or analyzed.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (offer diversity, community formation), not payoff-based.
- Findings about network structure and diversity may not generalize to efficiency or payoff effects in PGG-like environments with punishment.
