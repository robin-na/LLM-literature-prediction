## Outcomes Measured

Non-payoff outcomes:

- propensity to cooperate (unconditional cooperation)
- level of cooperation in the population
Outcome notes:
The model tracks the evolution of judgement bias, discrimination, and cooperation, but does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare. Payoff is used as a selection mechanism, but efficiency or group payoff is not a reported outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that judgement bias—weighting bad acts more heavily than good acts in reputation updating—can evolve in indirect reciprocity models, especially when the benefit-to-cost ratio is low. Under 'standing' reputation rules, negative judgement bias is favored over a wider range of parameters than under 'scoring.' Allowing judgement bias to evolve increases the level of cooperation in the population. The model does not include explicit costly punishment, but negative judgement bias acts as a form of social sanctioning by reducing the likelihood of future help to defectors. The model does not report group efficiency or total payoff as an outcome.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency in public goods games. However, it suggests that mechanisms that increase the reputational cost of defection (such as negative judgement bias) can promote cooperation, especially when the cost of cooperation is high. The findings are most relevant for settings where reputation-based indirect reciprocity is the main mechanism, rather than explicit costly punishment. The model does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot be used directly for quantitative prediction of efficiency outcomes in PGGs with or without punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size is 250 in the main simulations; robustness checks with 25 players showed qualitatively similar results.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Main simulations use 25 rounds per generation; robustness checks with 5 and 50 rounds showed similar results.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Cooperation is a binary choice: help (cooperate) or not help (defect).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of rounds per generation is fixed and known in the simulation.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. All acts are observed by all individuals in the main model; robustness checks with limited observation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is not a public goods game but an indirect reciprocity game with pairwise interactions.
- There is no explicit costly punishment; negative judgement bias acts as a reputational sanction, not a direct cost-imposing mechanism.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare are not reported as outcomes; only cooperation rates and trait evolution are tracked.
- Findings may not transfer directly to PGGs with explicit punishment or to settings where payoff-based efficiency is the main outcome of interest.
- Theoretical/simulation results may not generalize to empirical human behavior without further validation.
