# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Letting off Steam! Experimental Evidence on Displaced Aggression
Authors: Wiederhold, E., & Bühren, C.
Journal: Review of Behavioral Economics
Year: 2025

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment behavior (points given up to reduce others' payoffs)
- target of punishment (who is punished: B or C)
- differences in punishment across treatments (intentional vs. chance unfairness)
- differences in punishment across subject pools
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are behavioral: how much and whom participants punish under different allocation scenarios. There is no direct analysis of group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff as a dependent variable. Payoff calculations are described in the instructions and theory, but not as a main outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment studies how participants in the role of 'Player C' choose to punish others after allocations that may be unfair (intentional or by chance) or fair. Punishment is costly: for each point given up, 5 points are deducted from the target. The main findings (from the main paper, not this appendix) concern when and whom participants punish, and how this varies by the intentionality of the unfairness and by subject pool. Theoretical predictions using the Fehr-Schmidt model suggest punishment should only occur in unfair allocations, and only against those with higher payoffs. The appendix does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides detailed evidence on punishment behavior in a non-PGG allocation game, with a strong manipulation of intentionality and target of punishment. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. The punishment technology (cost and impact) is clearly specified, which may be useful for mapping to other studies. The design is adjacent to PGGs but not a direct variant.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 4 players: 1 A, 1 B, 2 C.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Single-shot (one allocation and punishment decision per participant).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication is strictly forbidden.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/punishment choices are discrete (0-5 points), not all-or-nothing but limited integer choices.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher gives up 1 point to deduct 5 points from the target.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 5:1 (5 points deducted per 1 point spent).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the structure is one-shot; all information about the game is provided in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are informed of the allocations and can see the payoffs of all players before making punishment decisions.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; identities are never revealed.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is not a public goods game or a direct variant; it is an allocation game with punishment.
- No efficiency, welfare, or group payoff outcomes are reported as dependent variables.
- The design is single-shot, not repeated.
- No reward mechanism is present.
- Findings on punishment behavior may not generalize to PGG-like environments with endogenous contribution decisions.
