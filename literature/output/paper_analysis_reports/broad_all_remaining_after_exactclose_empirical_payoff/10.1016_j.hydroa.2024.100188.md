Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are resource quality (salt concentration in groundwater) and agent energy (a modeled economic proxy), but not group payoff or efficiency in the public goods sense. The model tracks institution emergence, compliance, and behavioral adaptation, not direct payoffs as in PGG experiments.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The agent-based model demonstrates that self-organized institutions with sanctions (fines for defectors, monitoring) can emerge and stabilize resource use, keeping salt concentrations below harmful thresholds when defection rates are low and innovation rates are moderate to high. High defection rates undermine institution effectiveness, leading to resource degradation. The model highlights the importance of cooperation, monitoring, and sanctions in sustaining common-pool resources, but does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or group payoff as in public goods games. The findings are most relevant for understanding the emergence and stability of sanctioning institutions in resource dilemmas, rather than for predicting efficiency outcomes in PGG-like experiments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, conceptual support for the idea that punishment and monitoring can improve resource outcomes in social dilemmas, especially when defection is not too prevalent and agents can innovate or imitate successful strategies. However, it does not provide quantitative or empirical data on efficiency or group payoff, nor does it manipulate standard PGG design dimensions. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency in PGG-like games, this paper offers contextual and mechanistic insights but not direct predictive evidence. Its main value is in illustrating how sanctions and institutional emergence can stabilize cooperation and resource quality in agent-based models of common-pool resource dilemmas.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of agents (players) is set at 100 in the main case study; parameter is explicit and could be varied in future work.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Model runs for 21 years of daily time steps (over 8500 days); rounds are not discrete as in PGG but are daily decisions.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (salt application) is continuous, not all-or-nothing; agents choose willingness from 0.0 to 1.0.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (fine) for defectors is a model parameter (agent fine belief, F_i, and institution fine F(t)); monitoring also has a cost (monitoring fee).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is modeled via the fine amount and monitoring intensity; higher fines and more monitoring increase deterrence.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents complying with the institution can receive an incentive (energy gain) from a pool funded by monitoring fees.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward/incentive is funded from monitoring fees; cost is implicit in the redistribution of collected fees.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is determined by the size of the incentive pool and the fraction of agents rewarded.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents observe the energy and willingness of their social network peers; they do not see global summaries.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and agent-based, not empirical or experimental; results are simulation-based and not validated with real-world data.
- Outcomes are not reported in terms of efficiency or group payoff as in public goods games; main outcomes are resource quality (salt concentration) and agent 'energy' (a modeled economic proxy).
- The model does not manipulate or systematically analyze standard PGG design dimensions such as MPCR, player count, or round structure.
- Agents are homogeneous property owners; the model does not include heterogeneous stakeholders or broader sources of salinization.
- Social network structure is simple and static; more complex or dynamic networks are not explored.
- Sanctioning parameters (fine, monitoring) are not updated by agents in response to feedback, unlike salt application willingness.
- No direct communication (chat) or identification of punishers is modeled.
- The model assumes agents know the strategies and defection decisions of their social network peers, which may not reflect real-world information constraints.
