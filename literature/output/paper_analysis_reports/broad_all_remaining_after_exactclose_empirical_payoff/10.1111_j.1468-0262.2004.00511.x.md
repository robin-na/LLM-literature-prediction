# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Relational contracts and the nature of market interactions
Authors: Brown, M., Falk, A., & Fehr, E.
Journal: Econometrica
Year: 2004

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- firm profit
- worker earnings
- total surplus
- distribution of surplus
- efficiency (relative to maximum possible surplus)
- rent sharing
Non-payoff outcomes:
- effort level
- frequency and duration of long-term relationships
- frequency of private vs public offers
- contract renewal probability
- wage offers
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are payoff-based: firm profit, worker earnings, and surplus/efficiency. Effort level is a key behavioral variable, but the paper directly analyzes efficiency and surplus as well.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The absence of third-party enforcement leads to the endogenous formation of long-term firm-worker relationships, where contract renewal is contingent on high effort. This informal punishment mechanism (firing/termination) disciplines workers and results in higher effort, higher wages, and much higher efficiency and surplus compared to settings where such relationships are not possible. The effect is robust: both firms and workers earn more in long-term relationships, and the distribution of surplus is more equal. When long-term relationships are ruled out (random rematching), effort and efficiency drop sharply, and minimal effort becomes common. When third-party enforcement is available, the market behaves competitively, with low wages, low rent sharing, and little incentive for long-term relationships. The findings show that informal, endogenous punishment (via contract renewal/firing) can substitute for formal enforcement and substantially improve efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in repeated market-like environments with incomplete contracts, the ability to endogenously form long-term relationships and use contract renewal/firing as a sanction leads to much higher efficiency and surplus than settings where such informal punishment is not possible. For prediction tasks, the presence of repeated interactions, the ability to condition future play on past behavior, and the absence of third-party enforcement are key moderators of punishment's positive effect on efficiency. The findings suggest that, in environments where informal punishment via relationship termination is feasible, efficiency can approach the level seen with formal enforcement, provided that agents can build reputations and relationships. The results are most directly relevant to labor market and relational contract settings, but the underlying mechanism is adjacent to public goods games with endogenous partner choice and repeated play.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session had 10 workers and 7 firms (17 players per market).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 15 trading periods in each session.
- punishment cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented as contract termination (firing), which imposes a cost on both parties (loss of future surplus/rent). The cost is endogenous, not a fixed parameter.
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is high: firing/termination strongly disciplines effort and increases efficiency. The 'technology' is the ability to end the relationship.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. All participants knew the experiment would end after period 15 (finite horizon, common knowledge).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each participant was informed about their own and their partner's payoffs and effort after each period. Firms were informed about public offers of competitors; workers saw all public offers and private offers addressed to them.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players had fixed IDs in the ICF and C conditions, so identity was known and contract renewal/termination could be targeted. In the ICR condition, IDs were randomized each period, preventing identification.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is not a public goods game or direct variant; it is a repeated labor market with incomplete contracts and endogenous long-term relationships.
- Punishment is implemented as contract termination (firing), not as costly peer punishment or direct monetary sanctions.
- The setting is bilateral (firm-worker) rather than group-based, so group-level dynamics may differ from standard PGGs.
- The cost and effectiveness of punishment are endogenous and context-dependent, not exogenously manipulated.
- No explicit reward mechanism is present beyond wage offers.
- The findings may not generalize to one-shot or anonymous group settings where reputation and relationship termination are not feasible.
- The experiment uses a finite horizon (15 periods), which may limit the strength of relational contracts compared to infinite or indefinite horizons.
