# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: An experimental investigation of designated punishment behavior in public goods
Authors: Hoeft, L., & Mill, W.
Journal: Economics Letters
Year: 2017

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual contribution levels (punisher vs non-punisher)
- difference in contributions over time
- punishment assigned
- imposed contribution norm
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are contribution levels and punishment behavior. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, or welfare, nor does it analyze total earnings or surplus. The focus is on behavioral outcomes (contribution, punishment) rather than payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a repeated public goods game with a single designated punisher, the punisher contributes significantly less than non-punishers but enforces higher contributions from others through punishment. This double standard grows over time. The results are robust to a full-information treatment where all contributions are public. The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, so it is unclear whether the presence of a designated punisher increases, decreases, or leaves unchanged the overall efficiency of the group.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence about the behavioral effects of designated punishment in public goods games, specifically that punishers can enforce higher contributions from others while themselves contributing less. However, because the paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, or welfare, it does not directly inform the prediction of efficiency outcomes when punishment is enabled. The design details (player count, rounds, MPCR, punishment cost/tech, information structure) are well specified and may be useful for matching to other studies, but the lack of efficiency or payoff data limits the direct predictive value for the downstream efficiency prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 participants (1 punisher, 3 non-punishers) in all sessions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 30 rounds per session.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: participants allocate any number of 20 tokens between private and public account each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.5 (each token contributed to the public account returns 0.5 to each group member).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is costless to the punisher: each punishment token deducts 1 token from the target, but unused tokens are not added to the punisher's payoff.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment token deducts 1 token from the target's payoff (1:1 punishment effectiveness).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know there are 30 rounds in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Only the punisher sees the contributions of all group members; non-punishers do not see others' contributions or punishment assigned to others. In the full-information treatment, all contributions are public.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is assigned by a designated punisher whose identity is known by role assignment, but the assignment of punishment to specific individuals is not made public to the group.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, welfare, or surplus, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (contribution, punishment) rather than payoff-based.
- Punishment is costless to the punisher, which is not typical of most real-world or standard experimental public goods games.
- Only a designated punisher can assign punishment, which differs from standard peer punishment designs.
- No control condition without punishment is reported, so the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency or contributions relative to a no-punishment baseline is not available.
