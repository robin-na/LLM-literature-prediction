# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Co-Evolution of Complex Network Public Goods Game under the Edges Rules
Authors: Sun, X. P., Li, Y. B., Kang, H. W., Shen, Y., Peng, J., Wang, H. Y., & Chen, Q. Y.
Journal: Entropy
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperation
- network topology (degree, excess average degree, entropy)
- strategy choices (cooperate/defect)
- network reconnection/breaking behavior
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the fraction of cooperators. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. Payoff is discussed in the model, but not as a reported outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that allowing players to break unsatisfactory connections and form new ones (edge rules) in a public goods game on a network leads to higher and more stable cooperation, especially at low gain factors (r=2). Both simulation and a 30-person lab experiment show that the fraction of cooperators increases and stabilizes under these rules, compared to a static network. The mechanism is interpreted as self-organized punishment (breaking ties with defectors) and reward (forming ties with cooperators), but there is no explicit costly punishment. The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators; efficiency or group payoff is not reported.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that endogenous network adaptation (breaking and forming ties based on satisfaction) can promote cooperation in public goods games, even at low MPCR. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of explicit punishment on efficiency. The 'punishment' here is not a costly sanction but the breaking of ties, which is structurally different from standard punishment mechanisms. The findings may be useful for understanding the effects of network fluidity or endogenous partner choice, but not for predicting efficiency changes from adding explicit punishment to a fixed-group PGG.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use 1000 nodes; lab experiment uses 30 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 100 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), repeated 1000 times; lab experiment has 30 rounds.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Gain factor r is varied (e.g., r=2, 4, 6); MPCR = r / group size. Group size is degree+1 (e.g., degree 4, so group size 5).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the lab experiment, players are informed of their own payoff, the strategy and payoff of each neighbor, and the dominant nodes in the previous round.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or total earnings as outcomes; only the fraction of cooperators and network structure are analyzed.
- There is no explicit costly punishment mechanism; edge breaking is interpreted as endogenous punishment but is not parameterized or analyzed as such.
- Findings may not generalize to standard PGGs with fixed groups and explicit punishment options.
- The main experimental evidence is for endogenous network adaptation, not for the effect of adding punishment to a fixed game.
