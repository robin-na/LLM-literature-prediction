# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Productive and Unproductive Effort Choices in Groups and Sharing: An Experimental Study
Authors: Büyükboyaci, M., Karagözoglu, E., & Küçüksenel, S.
Journal: Managerial and Decision Economics
Year: 2025

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- joint surplus produced (size of surplus)
- dictator game giving (share of surplus allocated to other)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- resource allocation choice (productive vs unproductive investment)
- frequency of recognition-oriented (unproductive) choice
- punishment via lower giving in distribution stage
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are resource allocation choices (productive vs unproductive), and dictator game giving (how much of the surplus is given to the other). The size of the surplus is determined endogenously by the allocation choices, but the main focus is on behavioral responses (giving, punishment) rather than efficiency or total group payoff. Efficiency is not explicitly calculated or reported, but the surplus size is a direct function of choices.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that increasing the opportunity cost of unproductive investment (i.e., making productive investment more attractive) does not significantly increase productive investment rates. Subjects' own allocation choices do not significantly affect their giving in the distribution stage, but they give less to human partners who chose unproductive investment, indicating a punishment mechanism. This punishment is not applied when the unproductive choice is made by a computer. The size of the surplus (group payoff) is determined by the sum of productive investments, but the paper does not directly analyze efficiency or compare group payoffs across treatments. The main behavioral outcome is the use of distributive punishment in response to unproductive choices by humans.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence relevant to predicting efficiency in public-goods-like games with punishment. The design is adjacent to a PGG: resource allocation between productive and unproductive uses, with endogenous surplus and a contest for control. There is no explicit punishment stage, but distributive punishment (giving less in the dictator game) serves a similar function. The main implication is that increasing the marginal return to productive investment (analogous to MPCR) does not necessarily increase productive investment or efficiency. Punishment (via lower giving) is selectively applied to human partners who make unproductive choices, but this does not feed back to increase productive investment rates. Thus, the presence of a punishment-like mechanism does not guarantee higher efficiency in this context. For prediction, this suggests that punishment may not always improve efficiency, especially if it is not structured as a direct, costly sanction and if it does not change initial contribution behavior.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in pairs (2 players per group).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject plays two blocks (each with two stages), but only one block is randomly selected for payment. No repeated interaction with the same partner.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: Option A (surplus-oriented) or Option B (recognition-oriented), not continuous.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `unclear` under this dimension. The multiplier on productive investment (Account 2) is either 1 (low opportunity cost) or 3 (high opportunity cost), analogous to MPCR. Varying this did not significantly affect productive investment rates.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the structure: two blocks, one randomly selected for payment.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. No feedback is given about others' choices or payoffs during the experiment.

## Important Limitations
- The design is not a standard public goods game; it is a two-stage game with endogenous surplus and a contest for control, so results may not generalize directly to PGGs.
- There is no explicit, costly punishment mechanism; punishment is implemented via lower giving in a dictator game, which may not have the same behavioral or efficiency effects as standard punishment.
- Efficiency or group payoff is not directly analyzed or reported as a main outcome; the focus is on behavioral responses (giving, punishment) rather than on total welfare.
- The sample is limited to undergraduate students at a single university, which may limit external validity.
- No continuous contribution or punishment choices; all-or-nothing design may limit generalizability to settings with more granular decisions.
