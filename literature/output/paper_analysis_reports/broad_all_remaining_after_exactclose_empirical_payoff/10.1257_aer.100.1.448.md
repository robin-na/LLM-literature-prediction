## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model predicts that, in repeated relationships with incomplete information about how to cooperate, initial stages are characterized by inefficient punishment (exit) due to imperfect monitoring and the need to incentivize information revelation. As players learn and routines are established, the need for punishment disappears and efficiency increases. However, the cost of inducing further information revelation can outweigh the benefits, leading to path dependence and persistent inefficiency: partnerships may settle into suboptimal routines even when more efficient cooperation is possible. The extent of inefficiency depends on the magnitude of incentive conflicts and the cost of aligning incentives. The model provides qualitative predictions about the efficiency costs of punishment and the conditions under which punishment is required or can be avoided.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support for the idea that punishment (or inefficient exit) in repeated games with incomplete information can reduce efficiency, especially in the early stages of a relationship. The efficiency cost of punishment is highest when players are still learning about how to cooperate, and disappears once routines are established and monitoring becomes perfect. The model suggests that the efficiency gain from enabling punishment depends on the information structure and the stage of the relationship: punishment is necessary to induce information revelation but is always inefficient compared to the complete information benchmark. The findings imply that, in environments similar to public goods games with incomplete information and learning, enabling punishment may initially reduce efficiency, but its necessity diminishes as learning occurs. The model also highlights the importance of path dependence and the possibility of persistent inefficiency due to early random events. For prediction tasks, this paper suggests that the efficiency effect of punishment is context-dependent and may be negative or mixed, especially when learning is costly and incomplete.

## Important Limitations
- The model is purely theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental evidence.
- The setting is a two-player repeated game with asymmetric information, which is only adjacent to standard public goods games.
- Punishment is modeled as inefficient exit rather than as a targeted sanction or fine, which may differ from standard PGG punishment mechanisms.
- The model assumes infinite horizon and does not address finite repeated games.
- No explicit marginal per-capita return (mpcr) or group size variation is modeled.
- No communication, reward, or more complex punishment technologies are considered.
- Efficiency outcomes are path-dependent and may not generalize to all repeated cooperation settings.
