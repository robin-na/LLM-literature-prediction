Outcome notes:
The paper reports on the frequencies and spatial distributions of strategies (cooperation, defection, extortion) in an evolutionary game simulation. No explicit group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported. Outcomes are behavioral (strategy prevalence), not payoff-based.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing peer pressure (punishment) into a spatial donation/extortion game can promote the prevalence of cooperation, especially at moderate punishment intensities. When punishment is too strong, extortioners dominate. The effect of punishment is non-monotonic: moderate punishment helps cooperation, but high punishment harms it. The main reported outcomes are the frequencies of each strategy, not group payoff or efficiency. The findings are based on simulation, not empirical data.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, mechanistic evidence that punishment can promote cooperation in spatial evolutionary games with extortion strategies, but only within a certain range of punishment intensity. However, because the outcomes are frequencies of strategies rather than group payoff or efficiency, and because the model is a spatial donation/extortion game (not a standard public goods game), the results are only adjacent to the prediction task. The paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency changes due to punishment in public goods games, but it does suggest that the effect of punishment on cooperation (and by extension, possibly efficiency) is non-monotonic and context-dependent.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a square lattice of 800x800 individuals (640,000 players), each interacting with 4 neighbors. The player count is fixed and very large.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 30,000 Monte Carlo steps, with results averaged over the last 3,000 steps. The number of rounds is fixed and very large.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Strategy choices are discrete: unconditional cooperation, unconditional defection, or extortion. No continuous contribution.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The benefit-to-cost ratio (b and c) is varied; b is the main parameter (1 < b ≤ 2), c is set so that b-c=1. This is not a standard MPCR but is analogous.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment is parameterized by alpha (α), which controls the loss imposed for holding a different strategy. The cost is not separated for punisher and punished, but α is the punishment intensity. The effect of increasing α is non-monotonic: moderate α promotes cooperation, high α leads to extortioner dominance.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is directly controlled by α. The impact per unit cost is not separated, but α determines the strength of punishment. The effect is non-monotonic.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial evolutionary simulation, not an empirical or experimental study.
- The game is a donation/extortion game with three discrete strategies, not a standard public goods game.
- Outcomes are frequencies of strategies (cooperation, defection, extortion), not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare.
- Punishment is modeled as a peer pressure parameter (α) that penalizes strategy difference, not as a voluntary or targeted punishment action.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or total group payoff.
- No human subjects or real-world data; all results are from agent-based simulation.
