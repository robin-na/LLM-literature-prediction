Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are behavioral: fraction of cooperative acts, strategy parameters (p, q), and robustness of cooperation. Payoff is discussed in terms of evolutionary fitness and strategy success, but group efficiency or total payoff is not directly reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that allowing players to use reactive strategies (punishing defectors and rewarding cooperators based on memory of past actions) leads to high and robust levels of cooperation in scale-free networks, even when participation costs are present. The effect is much stronger than for unconditional strategies. The main outcome is the fraction of cooperative acts, not group efficiency or total payoff. The model is based on the prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game, but the mechanisms of punishment/retaliation and reward are closely related. The findings suggest that cognitive strategies can overcome the negative effects of participation costs and defector invasion on cooperation, but do not provide direct evidence about efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that punishment/retaliation mechanisms (as part of cognitive/reactive strategies) can sustain high cooperation rates in networked social dilemmas, even with participation costs and frequent defector invasion. However, because the model is a prisoner's dilemma (not a public goods game) and does not report efficiency or group payoff, its direct value for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like environments is limited. The main signal is that punishment/retaliation can be highly effective in maintaining cooperation under adverse conditions, especially when players can condition their behavior on others' past actions. The findings are most relevant for settings with network structure, memory, and the possibility of both punishment and reward.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use 1000 players (nodes) in the network.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000–100,000 generations after a transient period; each generation consists of many pairwise interactions.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each interaction is a binary choice: cooperate or defect.
- reward availability: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Reward is present in the sense that players can reciprocate cooperation (high p parameter), but not as a separate costly reward action.

## Important Limitations
- The model is based on the prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG-like environments.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or total welfare are not directly measured or reported; only behavioral outcomes (cooperation rates) are analyzed.
- Punishment and reward are implemented as reactive strategies (conditional cooperation/defection), not as explicit costly actions.
- The model is entirely simulation-based and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- Network structure (scale-free) is assumed; results may differ in other interaction structures.
