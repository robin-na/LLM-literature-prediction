## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that group effort and efficiency in public goods games and group contests depend strongly on group size, within-group heterogeneity, and the nonlinearity of cost and benefit functions. In linear models, group effort typically decreases with group size and increases with heterogeneity, while in nonlinear models, these patterns can reverse. The largest contributors are those with the highest stake or lowest cost, but under some conditions, these individuals may end up with lower net payoffs than free-riders. The review also summarizes experimental findings, noting that punishment can increase contributions but may not always increase total earnings or efficiency, especially in heterogeneous groups. Theoretical models predict that group selection, between-group competition, and synergicity can all increase group effort and efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and synthetic evidence on how game design dimensions such as player count, group heterogeneity, cost/benefit structure, and group selection affect efficiency in public goods games. However, it does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment mechanisms on efficiency, as punishment is only discussed in the context of prior literature and experiments. For prediction tasks involving the effect of punishment on efficiency, this paper is mainly useful for understanding baseline efficiency and the moderating role of group structure, but not for estimating the incremental effect of punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is a theoretical review and does not provide new empirical or experimental data.
- Punishment is not modeled directly; discussion of punishment is limited to review of prior experimental findings.
- No direct analysis of the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff outcomes.
- Some experimental results reviewed are inconsistent or context-dependent, limiting generalizability.
- Repeated games, communication, and other institutional features are not modeled.
