The main outcomes are group and individual payoffs, with explicit focus on efficiency and Pareto improvements. There is no empirical or experimental data.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper develops mathematical models of a self-governed collective where individual effort generates aggregate income, distributed by pre-negotiated shares. In the baseline, self-interested behavior leads to an inefficient Nash equilibrium. When a coalition forms and coordinates effort, the coalition's and its members' payoffs increase. If the coalition offers contracts to non-members (who then forego their share in exchange for a reward per effort), the organization transforms into a hierarchical structure, and under certain conditions, all agents' payoffs increase, reaching the communal optimum. The paper provides theorems showing that expanding the coalition always increases the sum of payoffs for coalition members and the new joiner, and can also increase payoffs for non-coalition members. The analysis is entirely theoretical, with no empirical or experimental results.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical support for the idea that mechanisms enabling coordination, selective incentives, or contracts (analogous to institutionalized punishment or reward) can increase efficiency in public-goods-like environments. While it does not model explicit punishment, it analyzes how selective incentives and contracts can overcome inefficiency. For prediction tasks, the results suggest that introducing mechanisms that align individual and group incentives (such as contracts or selective rewards) can increase group efficiency, even in the absence of explicit punishment. The findings are most relevant for settings where coalition formation or contracting is possible, and less directly informative for standard PGGs with simple punishment options.

## Important Limitations
- The analysis is entirely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental validation.
- No explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism is modeled; the focus is on coalition formation and contracts (selective incentives).
