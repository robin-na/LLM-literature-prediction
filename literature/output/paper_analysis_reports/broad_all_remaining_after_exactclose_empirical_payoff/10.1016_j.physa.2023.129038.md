## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that a confidence mechanism—where confidence in a strategy is updated based on the difference between payoff and aspiration—can promote and stabilize cooperation in a spatial prisoner's dilemma game. The effect of the confidence increment parameter (alpha) is non-monotonic: smaller alpha can lead to higher cooperation under some conditions, but too small an alpha or high temptation to defect undermines cooperation. Common aspirations in the group support higher cooperation than heterogeneous aspirations. There is a threshold effect for aspiration: below the threshold, increasing aspiration increases cooperation, but above it, cooperation collapses. However, all findings are in terms of cooperation rates, not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. It does not manipulate or analyze punishment, sanctions, or payoff-based outcomes. Its findings on confidence and aspiration mechanisms in spatial prisoner's dilemma games may be of limited indirect relevance for understanding how psychological or updating mechanisms affect cooperation, but not for the efficiency prediction task as defined. The absence of payoff or efficiency outcomes and the lack of punishment or reward mechanisms mean this paper should not be weighted for the core prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a 100x100 lattice (10,000 players), each interacting with 4 neighbors (von Neumann neighborhood).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 100,000 steps, with outcomes averaged over the last 5,000 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (strategy) is binary: cooperate (C) or defect (D).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of rounds (steps) is fixed and known in the simulation setup.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game or direct variant.
- No punishment, sanction, or reward mechanisms are present or analyzed.
- No efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates are analyzed.
- Findings on confidence and aspiration may not generalize to environments with explicit punishment or public goods structures.
- Simulation-based results may not translate to empirical or experimental settings.
