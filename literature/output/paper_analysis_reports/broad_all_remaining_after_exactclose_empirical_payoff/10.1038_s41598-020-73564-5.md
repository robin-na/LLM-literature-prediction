## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in indirect reciprocity models with private reputation assessment, punishment can increase group efficiency and maintain stable cooperation if assessment norms are designed to (1) detect free riders, (2) justify punishment of bad actors, and (3) generously assess cooperation toward badly reputed recipients. Two main solutions are identified: one based on recipient-focused justified punishment (with generosity toward bad recipients), and another based on doer-focused justified punishment (with high cooperation efficiency required). The stability and efficiency of cooperation depend on the benefit-to-cost ratio (b/c), with more generous norms required at lower efficiency. The presence of punishment, when justified and properly assessed, leads to higher average payoffs and stable cooperative regimes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-like games depends critically on the assessment norm and the efficiency of cooperation (b/c). If the punishment system is designed so that justified punishment is recognized and not misinterpreted (i.e., the intention is correctly communicated), and if generosity is applied in assessing cooperation toward bad recipients, then enabling punishment will increase efficiency relative to the control. The benefit-to-cost ratio (mpcr analog) is a key moderator: at higher efficiency, more norms can sustain cooperation. The findings suggest that, for prediction, the presence and design of punishment, the assessment norm, and the efficiency parameter are crucial for forecasting the efficiency gain from punishment.

- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Time is discrete and the game is repeated indefinitely (infinite horizon); not a fixed number of rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate (pay cost, give benefit) or defect (do nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Efficiency of cooperation is parameterized by benefit (b) and cost (c); higher b/c ratio allows more norms to sustain cooperation and efficiency.
- punishment cost: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is modeled as defection against bad recipients, which costs the punisher the opportunity to cooperate (i.e., forgoes the cost of cooperation). No explicit cost per se, but the opportunity cost is present.
- punishment technology: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the assessment norm: only justified punishment (defection against bad) is effective in maintaining cooperation and efficiency.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The model distinguishes between public and private assessment schemes. In the private scheme, only some players observe and update reputations; in the public scheme, all observe. The private scheme is key to the dilemma of punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- Assumes infinite population and well-mixed interactions; does not address finite group size or network structure.
- Punishment is modeled as defection against bad recipients, not as a separate costly action.
- No explicit modeling of communication, chat, or identification of punishers.
- Only three strategies (ALLC, ALLD, DISC) are considered; more complex strategy sets are not analyzed.
- Replicator dynamics are solved numerically, not analytically.
- No explicit public goods game; the model is adjacent to PGG but focuses on indirect reciprocity with binary actions.
