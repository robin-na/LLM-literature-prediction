# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Indirect Punishment and Generosity Toward Strangers
Authors: Ule, A., Schram, A., Riedl, A., & Cason, T. N.
Journal: Science
Year: 2009

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average payoff per round (francs)
- relative payoff of strategies
- cohort average earnings
Non-payoff outcomes:
- helping rate
- punishment frequency
- strategy classification (rewarder, punisher, defector, etc.)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is average payoff per round, both overall and by strategy. Helping rates and punishment frequencies are also reported, but the central analysis is on payoff outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment compares two treatments of an indirect helping game: one where indirect punishment is only symbolic (no material effect on the recipient), and one where punishment is harmful (recipient loses 250 francs). In both, helping is costly and beneficial, and donors can observe recipients' recent behavior. The main finding is that, although the frequency of helping does not differ significantly between treatments, the availability of harmful punishment reduces the payoffs of defectors and increases the payoffs of rewarders and cautious defectors. In the control, defectors earn most; with harmful punishment, rewarders and cautious defectors earn more than defectors. Harmful punishment is rare but targeted at defectors, and this is sufficient to change the payoff structure in favor of cooperation-supporting strategies.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that enabling costly, effective punishment in an indirect reciprocity environment (adjacent to PGG) increases the average payoff of cooperative strategies relative to defectors, even if overall cooperation rates do not rise. For prediction, this suggests that, in games where punishment is enabled and is materially costly to the recipient, group efficiency or average payoff can increase relative to a control with only symbolic or no punishment, primarily by reducing defectors' payoffs. The effect is not driven by increased cooperation rates but by the deterrent effect of punishment on defectors' earnings. The design is not a standard PGG but is closely related in its focus on cooperation, punishment, and payoff outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each cohort consists of 10 participants, randomly matched into donor-recipient pairs each round.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 100 rounds per session; analysis focuses on the first 90 rounds due to endgame effects.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/helping choice is binary: donor can help, pass, or hurt (no continuous contribution).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Helping costs the donor 200 francs and benefits the recipient by 250 francs; marginal per-capita return is 1.25 (250/200).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishing (hurting) costs the donor 50 francs per use in both treatments.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In HP, punishment removes 250 francs from the recipient per 50 francs spent by the punisher (ratio 5:1); in SP, punishment has no material effect.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Rewarding/helping is always available as an option.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Helping costs the donor 200 francs per use.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Helping gives the recipient 250 francs per 200 francs spent by the helper (ratio 1.25:1).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Donors observe the recipient's three most recent actions and can pay to access additional information about the recipient's observed history.

## Important Limitations
- The game is an indirect reciprocity helping game, not a standard public goods game; results may not fully generalize to PGG settings.
- Punishment is rare in this design, and the effect is primarily on the payoff structure rather than on increasing cooperation rates.
- The experiment uses fixed group sizes (cohorts of 10), and the effect of varying group size is not tested.
- Analysis focuses on the first 90 rounds due to endgame effects; long-term dynamics beyond this are not observed.
- No communication or reputation outside the observed history; real-world indirect reciprocity may involve richer information flows.
