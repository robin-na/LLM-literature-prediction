Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are the long-run average frequencies of cooperation, defection, and nonparticipation. The model is evolutionary and tracks population shares of strategies, not group payoff, efficiency, or total welfare. Payoff functions are specified and drive dynamics, but aggregate efficiency or group payoff is not directly reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the effect of voluntary participation on the evolution of cooperation and altruistic punishment is asymmetric: in large groups and for a moderate range of nonparticipant payoffs, voluntary participation can increase the long-run frequency of cooperation compared to compulsory participation. If the nonparticipant payoff is too low, voluntary participation has little effect; if too high, it sharply reduces cooperation. In small groups, voluntary participation does not increase cooperation for any nonparticipant payoff. The model tracks frequencies of strategies, not group efficiency or total payoff, so direct implications for efficiency are not reported.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task. It models a public goods game with punishment and voluntary participation, showing that the effect of enabling voluntary participation (an 'exit' option) depends on group size and the outside option's payoff. While the model does not report efficiency or group payoff, higher frequencies of cooperation (especially in large groups with moderate outside options) suggest that efficiency could be higher in those cases. However, because efficiency is not directly measured, the results should be used as informative indirect evidence: enabling voluntary participation may increase efficiency only in large groups and only if the outside option is not too attractive or too poor. The model's structure and parameterization are relevant for mapping to game design dimensions, but the lack of direct efficiency outcomes limits its predictive value for the downstream task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Group size (n) is a key manipulated parameter, varied from 20 to 120. The effect of voluntary participation on cooperation is positive in large groups (n=80,100,120) for moderate outside options, but not in small groups (n=20,40,60).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 2,000 time periods, with long-run averages taken over the last 1,000 periods. Not a repeated game in the experimental sense, but evolutionary time steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: agents either contribute or do not (defect).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is determined by b (benefit if everyone cooperates, b=0.5) and group size n. The payoff function is 1 + b*x - c for contributors.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Cost to punisher per punished defector is k=0.2 (Table 1). Sensitivity analysis shows that when punishment is absent or less costly to defectors, cooperation decreases.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical/simulation-based and does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total welfare directly; only frequencies of strategies are tracked.
- Findings about cooperation frequency may not translate directly to efficiency or payoff outcomes, especially when nonparticipation is high.
- No empirical or experimental data are presented; all results are from agent-based simulations.
- No reward mechanisms or communication are modeled, limiting generalizability to settings with those features.
- The model assumes fixed payoffs for nonparticipants and does not allow for endogenous or context-dependent outside options.
