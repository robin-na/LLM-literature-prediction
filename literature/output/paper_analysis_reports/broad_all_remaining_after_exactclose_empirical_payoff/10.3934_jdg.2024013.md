## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that, without intervention, individual incentives lead to inefficient outcomes (universal burning of crop residue), analogous to the tragedy of the commons. Social pressure can sometimes induce cooperation, but is often not strong enough. The introduction of government punishment (penalty per burned plot) and/or reward (per non-burned plot) can shift the Nash equilibrium to full cooperation (no burning), maximizing group payoff and efficiency. The model provides explicit conditions for when this shift occurs: when the sum of penalty and reward per plot exceeds the net private benefit of burning minus the greener alternative and pollution cost. Simulations confirm these theoretical results, showing that moderate levels of punishment and reward are sufficient to achieve efficient, cooperative outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and simulation-based evidence that introducing punishment (and/or reward) in a public-goods-like environment with negative externalities can shift the equilibrium from inefficient (all defect) to efficient (all cooperate), provided the policy parameters are set so that the penalty plus reward per unit exceeds the net private benefit of defection. The model is directly relevant for predicting the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency: if the punishment is sufficiently strong (relative to the private incentive to defect), efficiency will increase dramatically, potentially reaching full efficiency. The findings are most applicable to settings where punishment is implemented as a per-action cost, is known to all, and is not so severe as to cause practical or political infeasibility. The model does not address empirical implementation issues, but provides clear guidance on the direction and mechanism of efficiency change.

The model is not framed in terms of MPCR, but the payoff structure is analogous: the pollution cost c is shared across all, and the benefit of burning is private. The effective 'MPCR' is negative, as burning imposes a cost on all.

## Important Limitations
- The model is entirely theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- Assumes perfect implementation and enforcement of punishment and reward; does not address practical, political, or administrative constraints.
- Assumes homogeneous plot sizes and ignores heterogeneity among farmers.
- Does not model repeated game incentives in the standard sense; uses replicator dynamics rather than strategic repeated play.
- Does not address information, communication, or learning effects.
- Does not consider possible unintended consequences or resistance to punishment/reward policies.
