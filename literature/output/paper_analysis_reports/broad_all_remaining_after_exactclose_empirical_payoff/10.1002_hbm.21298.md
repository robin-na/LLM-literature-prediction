# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The mentalizing network orchestrates the impact of parochial altruism on social norm enforcement
Authors: Baumgartner, T., Götte, L., Gügler, R., & Fehr, E.
Journal: Human Brain Mapping
Year: 2012

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment assigned by third-party
- neural activation patterns (fMRI)
- functional connectivity between brain regions
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the amount of punishment assigned by third-party subjects to norm violators (defectors) in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma context, with a focus on group membership (ingroup vs outgroup). No group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported. The study is primarily about the neural mechanisms and behavioral patterns of parochial punishment, not about efficiency or group earnings.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that third-party punishment is strongly modulated by group membership: outgroup members who defect are punished much more than ingroup members who commit the same norm violation. This parochial punishment pattern is supported by increased activation and connectivity in a neural network associated with punishment-related decision processes (right orbitofrontal gyrus, right lateral prefrontal cortex, right dorsal caudatus) for outgroup punishment, and increased activation/connectivity in the mentalizing network (dorsomedial prefrontal cortex, bilateral temporo-parietal junction) for lenient ingroup punishment. The study provides evidence for neural mechanisms underlying parochial altruism in norm enforcement, but does not analyze or report efficiency or group payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the presence of a punishment option leads to parochial (group-biased) punishment behavior in a third-party punishment context, with outgroup norm violators punished more than ingroup violators. However, it does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-game-like environments. The design is a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma with third-party punishment, which is adjacent to but not the same as a repeated public goods game. The main value for prediction is in understanding that punishment can be highly group-biased, and that the behavioral effect of punishment may depend on group context, but the paper does not provide quantitative or qualitative evidence about efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game involves three players: Player A, Player B (PDG players), and Player C (third-party punisher).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All interactions are one-shot; no repeated rounds. Each third-party punisher makes 30 separate punishment decisions, each on a different pair's outcome.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players; all interactions are anonymous and one-shot.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice in the PDG is all-or-nothing: each player chooses to keep all points (defect) or transfer all points (cooperate).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the PDG, transferred points are doubled. Each player has 20 points; if transferred, the other player receives 40. MPCR for the recipient is 2.0, but for the group as a whole, the structure is a standard PDG, not a linear PGG.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is costly: 1 point spent by Player C reduces the punished player's income by 3 points.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3:1 (1 point spent by punisher reduces target's payoff by 3 points).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know all interactions are one-shot and that there are no repeated interactions.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Player C is shown the choices (cooperate/defect) of Players A and B, as well as their group affiliations, before making punishment decisions.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; there is no indication that punished players know the identity of the punisher (Player C).

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report on efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes; only punishment behavior and neural activation are analyzed.
- The experimental design is a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma with third-party punishment, not a repeated public goods game; thus, findings may not generalize to standard PGG environments.
- The sample size is small (16 subjects in the fMRI experiment), all male, and drawn from Swiss Army officer candidates, which may limit generalizability.
- No analysis of how punishment affects overall group outcomes or cooperation rates; the focus is on parochial punishment patterns and neural mechanisms.
- No communication or chat is allowed, and all interactions are anonymous and one-shot, which may not reflect real-world social norm enforcement contexts.
