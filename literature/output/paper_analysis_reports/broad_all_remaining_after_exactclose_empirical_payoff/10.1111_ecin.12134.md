# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: INCOMPLETE INFORMATION STRENGTHENS THE EFFECTIVENESS OF SOCIAL APPROVAL
Authors: Greiff, M., & Paetzel, F.
Journal: Economic Inquiry
Year: 2015

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate
- relative contribution
- social approval assigned (stars)
- correlation between contributions and approval
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is contribution rate (absolute and relative). No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings is reported. Social approval (stars) is also analyzed as a behavioral outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that enabling subjects to express social approval (assigning 0-10 stars to each group member after observing contributions) significantly increases contributions to the public good compared to a control with no approval. This effect is stronger when subjects have only partial information (see only others' contributions) than when they have full information (see both contributions and endowments). The study isolates the consumption value of social approval by using stranger matching and anonymity, ruling out reputation effects. No direct measures of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings are reported; all main results are in terms of contribution rates.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that non-monetary, symbolic social approval can increase contributions in public good games, but the effect is sensitive to the information structure. However, because the study does not include monetary punishment or analyze efficiency or group payoff, it is only indirectly informative for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency. The design is otherwise closely matched to standard PGGs, so the behavioral findings may be relevant for understanding how non-monetary incentives (like approval) interact with information, but cannot be directly used to predict efficiency outcomes from punishment interventions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 3 players per round; fixed throughout all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 8 rounds per session in all treatments.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication allowed; all interactions are anonymous.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is continuous: 0 ≤ c_i ≤ e_i.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per capita return (MPCR) is 0.5 in all treatments.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Symbolic, non-monetary reward (social approval via stars) is present in Treatments 1 and 2, absent in Control.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Assigning stars (approval) is costless to the assigner.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is symbolic: 0-10 stars per group member, no monetary value.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know there are 8 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, subjects see the absolute contributions of their group members in all treatments. In Treatment 2, they also see endowments.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Approval (stars) is assigned anonymously; identities are never revealed. Subjects see how many stars they received, but not from whom.

## Important Limitations
- No monetary punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present; only symbolic, non-monetary social approval is studied.
- No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings; all main outcomes are contribution rates.
- Findings on social approval may not generalize to monetary punishment or to efficiency outcomes.
- The effect of social approval is only tested in the context of symbolic, costless rewards, not costly or material incentives.
- The study uses a fixed group size (n=3) and a specific MPCR (0.5), which may limit generalizability to other parameterizations.
