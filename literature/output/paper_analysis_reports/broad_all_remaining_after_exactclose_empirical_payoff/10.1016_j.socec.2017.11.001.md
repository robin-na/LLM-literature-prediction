Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is average fitness, which is the average payoff per player per round, directly analogous to efficiency. The paper also analyzes the distribution and clustering of average fitness, and the emergence of different behavioral regimes (e.g., full cooperation, partial cooperation, wallflower). Non-payoff outcomes are discussed as mechanisms or correlates.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The evolutionary IPD with choice and refusal of partners (IPD/CR) produces higher average fitness (group efficiency) than random partner matching, with most runs evolving to near full cooperation. The average fitness clusters around a few regions, with the highest corresponding to mutual cooperation. However, if the refusal payoff is high or players are highly intolerant of defection, populations can become trapped in low-efficiency wallflower states. Sensitivity analyses show that the refusal payoff, minimum tolerance level, and memory weight are key moderators: high refusal payoffs or high intolerance increase the risk of social isolation and lower efficiency. Increasing population size reduces the risk of wallflower ecologies. The findings are robust across different strategy representations and parameter sweeps.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling partner selection (choice and refusal) in repeated social dilemmas can increase group efficiency compared to random matching, especially when refusal payoffs and intolerance are not too high. For prediction tasks, the presence of a partner selection mechanism (even in the absence of explicit costly punishment) can be expected to increase efficiency, but only if the cost/benefit structure of refusal does not incentivize social isolation. The results are most directly relevant to settings where agents can avoid defectors, but less so to standard public goods games with centralized punishment. The findings suggest that game design dimensions such as the cost of refusal, tolerance thresholds, and population size are critical moderators of the efficiency effect.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Main simulations use N=30; some experiments double to N=60. Increasing player count reduces the risk of wallflower (low-efficiency) outcomes and increases the chance of cooperation.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each tournament has I=150 iterations (rounds); some experiments use I=400. Number of rounds is fixed and known to the experimenter, but not discussed as a moderator of efficiency.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each move is a binary choice: cooperate or defect (standard PD).

## Important Limitations
- The model is an evolutionary simulation, not a human experiment; results may not generalize to human behavior.
- The game is an iterated Prisoner's Dilemma with partner selection, not a public goods game; findings are adjacent but not directly transferable to PGG settings.
- No explicit costly punishment or reward mechanism is present; partner selection (refusal) is not parameterized as a punishment cost.
- Small population sizes and strong elitism in the genetic algorithm may affect the diversity and stability of outcomes.
- The effect of the number of rounds (iterations) is not systematically explored as a moderator.
- Players do not communicate or observe global summaries; only direct payoff history is used for partner selection.
- The model assumes deterministic strategies encoded as finite automata, which may not capture the full range of human or agent behaviors.
