## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that jealousy-motivated sanctions (where agents punish neighbors with higher payoffs, regardless of strategy) can promote cooperation in spatial prisoner's dilemma games. The effect depends on the network structure: in lattice networks, higher sanction efficiency (penalty per cost) promotes cooperation, while in scale-free networks, lower efficiency (higher cost per penalty) can be more effective. However, the paper notes that even when cooperation is achieved, the continued use of jealous sanctions (due to persistent payoff inequalities) can reduce total social payoff, potentially lowering efficiency. The main reported outcome is the cooperation fraction, not direct group efficiency or payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-like environments, but the model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game. The main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency. The findings suggest that punishment can increase cooperation, but may not always improve efficiency due to the cost of sanctions, especially when punishment is motivated by jealousy and can be directed at cooperators as well as defectors. The effect of punishment on efficiency is likely to depend on network structure and the cost-effectiveness of punishment. For prediction tasks, this paper suggests that punishment may increase cooperation but could reduce efficiency if sanctioning is costly and not well-targeted.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- The main outcome is cooperation fraction, not direct group efficiency or payoff; efficiency is discussed qualitatively but not measured.
- Sanctions can be directed at cooperators as well as defectors, which differs from standard punishment models in PGGs.
- The effect of punishment on efficiency is ambiguous: while cooperation can increase, total social payoff may decrease due to sanction costs.
- Results are based on agent-based simulations and may depend on specific model assumptions (e.g., network structure, update rule).
