Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the order parameter (fraction of cooperators minus fraction of defectors), not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. The paper discusses phase transitions in cooperation, not direct payoff or efficiency outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a one-dimensional PGG with three players per group, cooperation only emerges when the synergy parameter r exceeds the group size (r > 3). Introducing punishment (with a fine for defectors) shifts the critical point, allowing cooperation to emerge at lower r. The effect of punishment depends on the fine imposed on defectors, not on the cost to punishers. The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not group efficiency or total payoff. The results are derived analytically and confirmed by agent-based simulations, but neither approach reports group efficiency or welfare directly.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and simulation evidence that punishment increases the fraction of cooperators in a PGG-like environment and lowers the threshold for cooperation. However, it does not report or analyze group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare. The findings are useful for predicting when cooperation will emerge as a function of game parameters (e.g., punishment fine, group size, synergy), but do not directly inform the prediction of efficiency outcomes. The results suggest that, under the studied conditions, enabling punishment is likely to increase cooperation rates, which may be associated with higher efficiency in standard PGGs, but this link is not explicitly demonstrated or quantified in the paper.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The main model and simulations use groups of 3 players (k=2, so k+1=3).
- number of rounds: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Simulations run for 2 million updates, but the game is modeled as an evolutionary process, not as a repeated finite-round PGG.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: players either cooperate (contribute) or defect (do not contribute).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is r/(k+1); with k=2, so MPCR = r/3. The synergy parameter r is varied.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost to punisher is parameterized as gamma (γ), varied in analysis.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is parameterized as epsilon (ε), the fine imposed on defectors. The critical point for cooperation depends on ε, not γ.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- No direct analysis or reporting of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes; results on cooperation cannot be directly translated to efficiency effects.
- The model is limited to one-dimensional, spatially structured populations with three-player groups; results may not generalize to other group sizes or structures.
- No communication, reward, or information-sharing mechanisms are modeled.
- Punishment is modeled as a strategy (moralist/immoralist), not as a targeted action with identity or intent.
- The evolutionary process is ongoing and not structured as a finite repeated game.
