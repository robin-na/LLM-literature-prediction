Outcome notes:
The paper analyzes the evolutionary dynamics of cooperation and defection in public goods games with institutional punishment and bribery. The main outcomes are the frequency of cooperation and the stability of equilibria, not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. Payoff functions are derived and used to determine evolutionary stability, but aggregate efficiency or group payoff is not directly reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that: (1) Corruption (bribery) reduces the level of cooperation when cooperators are more inclined to bribe, undermining the effectiveness of institutional punishment. (2) Both stronger leaders (higher punishment multiplier) and richer economic potential (higher pool multiplier) enhance cooperation, but their effects interact with the bribery tendencies of group members. (3) When defectors are more likely to bribe, strong leaders can sustain cooperation under poor economic potential, but in rich economic environments, weaker leaders may be more beneficial. The analysis is based on replicator dynamics and the stability of cooperation/defection equilibria, not on direct efficiency or group payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical insight into how punishment, corruption, and economic potential interact to affect cooperation in public goods games. While it does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, it shows that punishment's effectiveness in promoting cooperation is reduced by corruption, especially when cooperators are more likely to bribe. Stronger punishment and higher economic potential generally expand the basin of attraction for cooperation, but the effect can reverse depending on who bribes more. For prediction tasks, the paper suggests that the presence of corruption and the relative strength of punishment and economic potential are key moderators of punishment's effect on cooperation, but direct translation to efficiency outcomes requires caution.

- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each individual decides whether or not to contribute a fixed amount c; binary (all-or-nothing) contribution choice.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Pool multiplier F is used; MPCR = F/N. F is varied in the analysis.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is funded by a fixed tax τ paid by all; cost to punisher is not individual but institutional (tax-based).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment multiplier r_p represents the effectiveness of punishment per unit tax. Higher r_p (stronger leader) increases cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not report direct efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; all results are in terms of cooperation frequency and evolutionary stability.
- The analysis assumes infinite population and continuous-time replicator dynamics, which may not capture all features of finite, repeated public goods games.
- Corruption is modeled as bribery with specific parameters for the probability and amount of bribes, which may not generalize to all forms of corruption.
- No reward mechanism is considered; only punishment and bribery are modeled.
- No empirical or experimental data are presented; findings are theoretical and numerical.
