Outcome notes:
The paper's main dependent variable is the relative cooperation ratio (fraction of cooperators among non-exiters). Payoff is discussed in terms of the game structure and as a mechanism, but not as a reported outcome. No group payoff, efficiency, or welfare is directly measured or reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that granting exit rights to high-degree (leader) nodes in a spatial prisoner's dilemma can either suppress or promote cooperation, depending on the strength of the social dilemma and the network's assortativity. In weak dilemmas, leader-priority exit reduces cooperation, while in strong dilemmas, it can promote cooperation by isolating defectors and allowing cooperative clusters to survive among low-degree nodes. Random and fringe-priority exit have weaker or negligible effects. The main outcome is the relative cooperation ratio among non-exiters. The model does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, and is not a public goods game. The exit option is not a punishment mechanism per se, but can act as a hard sanction by denying payoffs to others.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is not directly informative for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. The game is a spatial prisoner's dilemma with an exit option, not a PGG, and the main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency or group payoff. The exit option is not a punishment mechanism, though it can function as a hard sanction. The findings may be relevant for understanding how voluntary exclusion or exit options affect cooperation in networked dilemmas, but do not provide direct evidence for the efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like environments. Use this paper only for very indirect, mechanistic insights about how exclusion or exit options might interact with network structure and dilemma strength.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use scale-free networks of size N=100^2 (10,000 nodes), but the main focus is on network structure and node degree, not group size per se.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10^5 Monte Carlo steps, with outcomes averaged over the last 1000 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are discrete: cooperate, defect, or exit (no continuous contribution).

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma with an exit option, not a public goods game.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanism is present; exit is not equivalent to punishment.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- Findings are based on agent-based simulations, not empirical or experimental data.
- Results may not generalize to PGGs or to real-world group efficiency outcomes.
- Network structure and node degree are central, but group size and round information are not varied in a way directly relevant to PGG prediction tasks.
- No analysis of payoff-based efficiency or surplus is reported.
