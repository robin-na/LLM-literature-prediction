# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Evolution of cooperation through adaptive interaction in a spatial prisoner's dilemma game
Authors: Pan, Q. H., Liu, X. S., Bao, H. L., Su, Y., & He, M. F.
Journal: Physica A-statistical Mechanics and Its Applications
Year: 2018

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- individual payoff per round (sum of payoffs from games with neighbors)
- group payoff (implied, not directly reported as efficiency or total group earnings)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperators
- cooperator/defector extinction or coexistence
- disconnection rate
Outcome notes:
The main reported outcomes are the fraction of cooperators and the system's evolutionary state (cooperator/defector extinction or coexistence). Payoff is discussed in terms of the game's payoff matrix and is used to explain mechanisms, but efficiency or total group payoff is not directly reported as a primary outcome. Some payoff data are collected and referenced, but the main focus is on cooperation rates.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that introducing and increasing the probability of disconnection (β) after defection leads to a significant increase in the fraction of cooperators in both simulation and human lab experiments. The effect is nonlinear: at low β, the effect is modest, but at higher β, cooperation increases sharply. The mechanism is interpreted as a punishment for defection, as defectors are excluded from interactions and thus lose opportunities for payoff. The system can transition between states of all-defector, all-cooperator, or coexistence depending on the parameters (temptation to defect, cost, and β). While payoffs are discussed and collected, the main reported outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not efficiency or total group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that a punishment-like mechanism (disconnection after defection) can substantially increase cooperation rates in a spatial prisoner's dilemma environment. However, it does not directly report efficiency or group payoff as a primary outcome, so its value for predicting efficiency is indirect. The findings suggest that enabling a punishment mechanism that reduces defectors' ability to interact (and thus earn payoff) can increase cooperation, which is likely to improve efficiency in similar environments, especially when the punishment is strong (high β). The design is adjacent to public goods games, so transfer to PGG-like settings should be cautious. The main predictive signal is that punishment mechanisms that exclude defectors can be effective, especially when the exclusion probability is high.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each experimental group had 24 participants; simulations used 200x200=40,000 players. The effect of player count is not analyzed as a moderator.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Experiments ran for 20 rounds. Simulations ran for 100 steps. No analysis of round number as a moderator.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication was explicitly disallowed in the experiment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperate or defect (C or D).
- punishment cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Punishment is implemented as probabilistic exclusion (disconnection) after defection. The cost to the punisher is not explicit; both parties lose the opportunity for payoff in the disconnected round.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the probability β that a defector is excluded from play with a neighbor. Higher β increases the impact of punishment and increases cooperation.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants knew the experiment would last 20 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each round, participants saw their own payoff, their neighbors' payoffs, and their neighbors' strategies.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not efficiency or total group payoff; efficiency is not directly measured or reported.
- The game is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG-like environments.
- Punishment is implemented as probabilistic exclusion (disconnection), not as a costly fine or deduction; the cost structure differs from standard punishment in PGGs.
- No analysis of how the punishment mechanism affects efficiency or group payoff, only cooperation rates.
- No reward mechanism is present, so no evidence on reward-punishment interactions.
- The effect of player count, number of rounds, or other design dimensions as moderators is not analyzed.
