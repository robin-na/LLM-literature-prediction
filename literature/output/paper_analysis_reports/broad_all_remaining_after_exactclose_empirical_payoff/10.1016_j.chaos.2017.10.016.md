## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study demonstrates that introducing and increasing the probability of social punishment (punishers who pay a cost to penalize defectors) in a spatial prisoner's dilemma model leads to a higher steady-state density of cooperators. When the willingness to punish is high, defectors are eliminated and cooperation dominates. The mechanism is shown to be robust across a range of temptation-to-defect values. However, the paper does not report on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, so its findings are limited to behavioral outcomes (cooperation rates and spatial patterns).

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that punishment increases cooperation rates in spatial prisoner's dilemma settings, but does not provide direct evidence about efficiency or payoff-based outcomes. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, the results are only indirectly informative: higher cooperation rates may imply higher efficiency, but the cost of punishment and its effect on total group payoff are not analyzed. The model is also not a public goods game, but an adjacent two-player game with spatial structure. Use this paper as indirect support for the behavioral effect of punishment, but not for quantitative efficiency predictions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each player interacts with 4 neighbors on a 100x100 lattice; local group size is 2 (pairwise PD), but the population is 10,000.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 50,000 Monte Carlo steps, with outcomes averaged over the last 5,000 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperate, defect, or punish (punishers act as cooperators except when punishing).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishers pay cost gamma (e.g., gamma=0.1) to punish defectors.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment imposes fine beta (e.g., beta=0.5) on defectors per act of punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial evolutionary prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates and spatial patterns are analyzed.
- Punishment costs and fines are parameterized, but their net effect on group payoff is not evaluated.
- Simulation-based results may not generalize to human behavior or experimental settings.
- No analysis of the tradeoff between increased cooperation and the cost of punishment in terms of total group welfare.
