## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing dynamic, group-level environmental feedback (bonuses or penalties based on collective contribution thresholds) in a spatial public goods game strongly promotes cooperation, especially when the synergy factor is low. The effect is not due to individual punishment or reward, but to a group-level adjustment of payoffs. The main reported outcomes are cooperation rates and the distribution of strategies and incentives. The model shows that higher expectation thresholds for group contributions can, counterintuitively, make cooperation more likely, and that the system transitions from defection to cooperation as the dynamic incentive increases. However, the paper does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it does not include a punishment mechanism. Instead, it models a group-level dynamic incentive (environmental feedback) that is not targeted at individuals and is not costly to assign. The findings may be indirectly relevant for understanding how group-level feedback mechanisms (distinct from punishment) can promote cooperation, but they do not inform the prediction of efficiency outcomes when punishment is enabled versus disabled. The results are most relevant for adjacent mechanisms (dynamic group-level incentives) rather than direct punishment or sanctions.

## Important Limitations
- The model does not include individual-level punishment or reward mechanisms; only group-level environmental feedback is present.
- Outcomes are reported in terms of cooperation rates and incentive distributions, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- The findings are based on theoretical modeling and simulation, not empirical or experimental data.
- The environmental feedback mechanism is not costly to assign and is not targeted at individuals, limiting comparability to standard punishment treatments.
- No direct evidence is provided for the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff outcomes.
