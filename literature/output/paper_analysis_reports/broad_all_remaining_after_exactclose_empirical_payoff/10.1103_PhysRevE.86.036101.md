## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study shows that risk-driven migration, where players move in response to the risk of collective failure, can substantially increase the fraction of cooperators in a collective-risk social dilemma. Cooperation is promoted most effectively at intermediate population densities and when the collective target is high and the risk function is not too flat. The mechanism outperforms random migration, which can actually inhibit cooperation. The results highlight the importance of spatial structure and self-organized mobility for sustaining cooperation, but do not provide direct evidence on efficiency or group payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is relevant for understanding how migration and risk of collective loss affect cooperation in public-goods-like environments, but it does not directly address the effect of punishment or sanctions on efficiency or group payoff. The model does not include explicit costly punishment or reward mechanisms; instead, it uses a collective-risk loss as a sanction for group underperformance. Therefore, while the findings may inform predictions about the role of endogenous migration and risk in promoting cooperation, they do not provide direct evidence for the effect of punishment on efficiency as required by the downstream prediction task. Use this paper for contextual or mechanistic insight, not for quantitative prediction of efficiency changes due to punishment.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are played on a lattice with local groups of up to 9 (Moore neighborhood), but the total population size is varied via lattice size and density.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for many Monte Carlo steps until equilibrium; not a fixed number of rounds as in lab experiments.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The 'synergy factor' is not explicitly named, but the model uses a collective target and a donation ratio c/b; the marginal return is implicit in the group payoff structure.

## Important Limitations
- No explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism; group loss is not equivalent to targeted punishment.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes.
- Simulation/theory only; no empirical or experimental data.
- Outcomes focus on cooperation rates and spatial patterns, not on payoff-based efficiency.
- Migration is modeled as risk-driven, not as a treatment variable in the sense of enabling or disabling punishment.
