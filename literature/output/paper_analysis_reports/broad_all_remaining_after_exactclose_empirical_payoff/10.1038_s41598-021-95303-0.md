## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing a peer-pressure-like punishment mechanism—where players with inconsistent strategies across interdependent network layers have their payoffs reduced—can promote cooperation in a spatial prisoner's dilemma. The effect of punishment is non-monotonic: moderate punishment maximizes cooperation, while too little or too much punishment is less effective. The effect is robust to changes in network structure (lattice, small-world, random) and the degree of payoff interdependence between layers. However, the paper does not report on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare; the main focus is on cooperation rates and the spatial distribution of strategies.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that punishment mechanisms can increase cooperation rates in social dilemma settings, especially when the punishment is moderate. However, because the model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma (not a public goods game or direct variant), and because the main outcome is cooperation rate rather than efficiency or group payoff, the findings should be transferred to efficiency prediction with caution. The results suggest that moderate punishment is likely to be most effective for promoting cooperation, but do not provide direct evidence about the effect on group efficiency or total payoff. The design dimensions most relevant here are the punishment cost (modeled as a reduction in payoff for inconsistent pairs) and the degree of interdependence between players' payoffs. The absence of direct efficiency or payoff outcomes limits the paper's direct utility for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Each network layer is an L x L lattice (e.g., L=200), so each layer has 40,000 players; each player is paired with one partner in the other layer. The large group size is fixed and not varied.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Simulations run for 50,000 Monte Carlo steps, with results averaged over the last 5,000 steps. The number of rounds is fixed and not varied.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete actions: cooperate (C) or defect (D).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a reduction in payoff for both players in an inconsistent pair, parameterized by u (0 ≤ u ≤ 1). The cost is not paid by a third party but is a direct reduction in both players' payoffs.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is controlled by the parameter u, which determines the fraction of payoff lost by inconsistent pairs. The effect on cooperation is non-monotonic: moderate u maximizes cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma on interdependent networks, not a public goods game or direct variant.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- Punishment is implemented as an automatic reduction in payoff for inconsistent pairs, not as a costly action by a third party.
- No empirical or experimental data; results are from agent-based simulations.
- No analysis of how punishment affects group efficiency or surplus, limiting direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
