## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in two-person Bayesian games with a single informed player, the existence of a uniform punishment strategy (UPS) for the uninformed player guarantees the existence of feasible, individually rational (i.e., efficient/welfare-maximizing) solutions. These solutions can be implemented as Nash equilibria of appropriately designed noncooperative games. The set of such payoffs coincides with the set of joint plan equilibrium payoffs in the infinitely repeated game. When UPS does not hold, feasible, individually rational solutions may not exist. The results generalize to cases where both players have private information, under certain conditions. The paper provides theoretical foundations for how punishment strategies can support efficient outcomes in games with incomplete information, but does not provide empirical estimates or experimental results.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical support for the idea that the ability to punish (specifically, the existence of a uniform punishment strategy) is crucial for achieving efficient, individually rational outcomes in two-person Bayesian games with incomplete information. For the downstream prediction task, the main implication is that the presence and effectiveness of punishment mechanisms (punishment cost, punishment tech, and the ability to credibly threaten punishment) are key moderators of whether efficiency gains from punishment are possible. However, the paper does not provide quantitative predictions or empirical estimates, and its results are not specific to public goods games or experimental designs. It is most useful for understanding the theoretical boundary conditions under which punishment can support efficiency, rather than for direct prediction of efficiency changes in experimental PGG-like environments.

- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Both one-shot and infinitely repeated games are analyzed. The main results concern the one-shot game, with connections to the repeated game.
- punishment cost: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Punishment is modeled as a strategy available to the uninformed player (uniform punishment strategy), but explicit costs of punishment are not parameterized or analyzed.
- punishment technology: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is captured by the ability to hold the informed player to their minmax individually rational level (UPS), but not as a quantitative parameter.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental evidence.
- The results are for two-person Bayesian games and may not generalize directly to larger groups or standard public goods games.
- Punishment is modeled as a strategic capability (uniform punishment strategy), not as an explicit costly action with parameterized cost or effectiveness.
- No quantitative predictions or effect sizes are provided for efficiency changes due to punishment.
- The analysis is not specific to public goods games, and public goods are only mentioned as an application domain.
- No discussion of communication, chat, or other behavioral interventions beyond signaling structures.
