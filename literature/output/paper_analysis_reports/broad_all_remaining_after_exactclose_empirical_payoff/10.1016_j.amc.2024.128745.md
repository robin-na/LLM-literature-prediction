## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that introducing a conditional strategy—where agents can switch between excluding defectors and opting out as loners—substantially increases the frequency of cooperation in a spatial public goods game. Lower tolerance for defectors (i.e., agents become loners more readily), lower exclusion costs, and higher exclusion probability all lead to higher cooperation levels. However, the main outcome is the frequency of cooperative strategies, not group efficiency or total payoff. The study does not report efficiency as a fraction of the social optimum, nor does it provide direct evidence on how these mechanisms affect group payoff or welfare.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that conditional exclusion and voluntary opt-out mechanisms can increase the frequency of cooperation in spatial public goods games, especially when exclusion is cheap and effective. However, because the study does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment or exclusion on efficiency. The results are most useful for predicting changes in cooperation rates, not payoffs. For the downstream prediction task, this paper suggests that punishment-like mechanisms (social exclusion) are more effective when costs are low and effectiveness is high, but the translation to efficiency outcomes remains uncertain.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a 100x100 lattice (N=10,000), with each player in groups of 5 (self + 4 neighbors). Group size is fixed at 5.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000–20,000 Monte Carlo steps, but the game is modeled as an indefinitely repeated evolutionary process, not a fixed finite number of rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: cooperators contribute a fixed cost c, defectors contribute nothing, loners do not participate.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is r/5, where r is the gain coefficient (varied in simulations), and group size is 5.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Exclusion cost (alpha) is the cost to the excluder per excluded defector; lower alpha leads to higher cooperation. This is analogous to punishment cost.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Exclusion probability (beta) is the effectiveness of exclusion per attempt; higher beta leads to higher cooperation. This is analogous to punishment effectiveness.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is cooperation level (frequency of cooperators and excluders), not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency as a fraction of the social optimum.
- The model is a simulation, not an empirical or experimental study.
- The exclusion mechanism is modeled as social exclusion (removal from the group), which is analogous but not identical to standard punishment in PGGs.
- No reward or positive incentive mechanisms are considered.
- Results may not generalize to non-spatial or non-evolutionary settings.
