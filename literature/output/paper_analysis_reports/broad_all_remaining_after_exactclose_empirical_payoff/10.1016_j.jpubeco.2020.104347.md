# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Sanctioning and trustworthiness across ethnic groups: Experimental evidence from Afghanistan
Authors: Bartos, V., & Levely, I.
Journal: Journal of Public Economics
Year: 2021

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- willingness to punish (minimum acceptable offer, MAO, in TPPG)
- actual punishment assigned
- amount shared in dictator game (DG)
- amount shared in third-party punishment game (TPPG)
- beliefs about others' sharing and punishment
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are behavioral: willingness to punish norm violations (MAO), actual punishment, and sharing behavior. No group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare/surplus outcomes are reported or analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that during periods of scarcity (lean season), participants are significantly less willing to engage in costly third-party punishment to enforce sharing norms, as measured by a lower minimum acceptable offer (MAO) in the TPPG. However, the amount shared by dictators (Player A) in both the DG and TPPG remains stable across seasons, indicating that sharing behavior is robust to scarcity even as enforcement weakens. The results are robust to various controls and alternative explanations. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is provided.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the willingness to punish norm violations (i.e., enforce sharing) is sensitive to resource scarcity, but actual sharing behavior is not. However, because the design is a one-shot dictator/TPPG (not a repeated public goods game), and because no group efficiency or total payoff outcomes are reported, the findings are only indirectly informative for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in repeated PGG-like environments. The main implication is that punishment may become less frequent or intense under scarcity, but this does not necessarily reduce sharing in the short run. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper suggests that the effect of punishment on efficiency may be context-dependent and potentially weaker under scarcity, but does not provide direct quantitative evidence for efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game is played in groups of 3 (Player A, B, C).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game is one-shot (single round), but the same participants play in two different seasons (lean and post-harvest).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication was not allowed in any round of the experiment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (sharing) choices are continuous: Player A can transfer any amount from 0 to 10 ECUs in increments of 1.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher (Player C) can pay 1 ECU to reduce A's payoff by 3 ECUs, or 2 ECUs to reduce A's payoff by 6 ECUs.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is fixed: 1 ECU cost for 3 ECU impact, 2 ECU cost for 6 ECU impact.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game is one-shot (single round) and are told the structure in advance.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a one-shot dictator and third-party punishment game, not a repeated public goods game, so results may not generalize to repeated or group-based cooperation settings.
- No group efficiency, total payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes are reported or analyzed; only individual-level sharing and punishment behaviors are measured.
- The sample is limited to male Afghan farmers in a specific region, which may limit generalizability.
- The study does not manipulate or analyze the effect of punishment on efficiency directly, so implications for efficiency in PGG-like environments are indirect.
- Punishment is only available as third-party punishment, not peer punishment or other forms.
