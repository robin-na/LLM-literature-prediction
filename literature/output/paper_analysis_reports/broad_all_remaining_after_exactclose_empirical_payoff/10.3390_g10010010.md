## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that linkage strategies, which exclude defectors from indirect social exchanges (ISEs), can maintain high levels of cooperation and group payoff in n-person social dilemmas, even in the presence of perception and implementation errors. Among the norms tested, the Kandori norm is the most robust: linkage strategies using Kandori maintain a payoff advantage over non-linkage strategies and are not invaded by defectors or non-linkage cooperators, even when errors are present. This means that, in agent-based simulations, the average group payoff (efficiency) is higher and more stable with Kandori-based linkage than with other norms or with no linkage. The results generalize across different ISE game structures (giving game vs. mutual aid game). The findings suggest that costless exclusion (linkage) can substitute for costly punishment in sustaining cooperation and efficiency, provided the right norm is used.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in public-goods-like environments, costless exclusion of defectors from indirect social exchanges (linkage) can achieve and sustain high efficiency, especially when the Kandori norm is used for reputation. The effect is robust to perception and implementation errors, which often undermine other mechanisms. For prediction tasks, this suggests that enabling a Kandori-based linkage mechanism (a form of costless, reputation-based exclusion) will increase efficiency relative to control (no linkage or no punishment), and that the effect is robust to errors and does not require costly punishment. The findings are most directly relevant to settings with large groups, repeated interactions, and full information about others' histories. The results are less directly applicable to standard costly punishment treatments, but they show that costless, norm-based exclusion can be a powerful alternative for sustaining efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The study is based on agent-based simulations, not empirical experiments with human subjects.
- The linkage mechanism is costless exclusion from indirect social exchanges, not costly punishment; results may not generalize to standard punishment treatments.
- Simulations assume full information about all players' histories, which may not hold in real-world or experimental settings.
- Only one type of action rule is considered (help if recipient is good, refuse otherwise); more complex or mixed strategies are not analyzed.
- Evolutionary dynamics between different norms (e.g., linkage vs. non-linkage with different norms) are not considered.
- The SD game is one-shot per generation, which differs from repeated public goods games commonly used in experiments.
- The model assumes large group size (n=100); results may differ for smaller groups.
