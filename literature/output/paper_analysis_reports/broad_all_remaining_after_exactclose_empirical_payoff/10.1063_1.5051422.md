The main dependent variable is the average payoff of the population, which is directly modeled and analyzed. The paper also reports on the frequencies of different strategies and their evolutionary stability, but these are secondary to the payoff-based analysis.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a well-mixed infinite population PGG, both peer-based and pool-based punishment and exclusion mechanisms can promote cooperation and increase average group payoff (efficiency) compared to defection. When individuals use a switching strategy—punishing defectors when they are few and excluding them when they are many—there is a stable coexistence of cooperators, defectors, and sanctioning strategists, leading to higher average payoffs. The efficiency gains are maximized at intermediate switching thresholds. Pure punishment (high threshold) leads to dominance of defectors and low efficiency, while pure exclusion (low threshold) can lead to oscillatory coexistence but still supports higher efficiency than no sanctions. The model explicitly tracks average payoff as the key outcome.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct theoretical evidence that enabling punishment (and/or exclusion) in a standard PGG increases group efficiency (average payoff) compared to the control (no sanctions), especially when the sanctioning mechanism is flexible (switching between punishment and exclusion based on the number of defectors). The effect is sensitive to the threshold for switching, the costs and effectiveness of punishment/exclusion, and the marginal per-capita return (mpcr). The model is fully specified for N=5, r=3, c=1, and various punishment/exclusion costs, and can be used to predict efficiency under different game design dimensions. The findings are highly relevant for predicting treatment efficiency from control efficiency and design parameters in PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The main model uses N=5 players per group, but the model is general for arbitrary N.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperate (contribute c) or defect (contribute nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. mpcr = r/N; main parameterization uses r=3, N=5, so mpcr=0.6.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Peer punishment cost per punished defector is gamma (e.g., gamma=0.4); pool punishment cost is G (e.g., G=0.4).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is beta (peer, e.g., beta=0.4) or B (pool, e.g., B=0.4) per unit cost.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is entirely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The model assumes infinite well-mixed populations and continuous-time replicator dynamics, which may not capture all features of finite, discrete, or real-world groups.
- No stochasticity, learning, or bounded rationality is modeled.
- No communication, reputation, or network structure is included.
- No reward mechanisms are considered; only punishment and exclusion.
- Parameter values (e.g., costs, effectiveness) are chosen for illustration and may not map directly to real-world settings.
