## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that allowing cooperators to preferentially contribute more to groups with higher cooperation levels (high-quality groups) significantly promotes the evolution of cooperation in public goods games, both on regular lattices and scale-free networks. The mechanism works by enabling cooperators to cluster and avoid exploitation by defectors, and by increasing the wealth/payoff of cooperators for a given cooperation level. The effect is stronger as the preference parameter (alpha) increases. The paper interprets the reduction of contributions to low-quality groups as a form of indirect, costless group-level punishment, but does not model or analyze explicit costly punishment. Group efficiency or total group payoff is not directly reported.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is relevant for understanding how endogenous contribution patterns (where cooperators can direct more resources to cooperative groups) affect cooperation and the distribution of payoffs in public goods games. However, it does not provide direct evidence on the effect of explicit, costly punishment on efficiency or group payoff. The findings suggest that indirect, costless group-level punishment (via reduced contributions to low-quality groups) can promote cooperation and increase the payoffs of cooperators, but the implications for overall group efficiency are not quantified. For prediction tasks focused on explicit punishment interventions, this paper provides only adjacent evidence, mainly relevant for understanding alternative mechanisms to punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a 40x40 lattice (1600 players) and a scale-free network with 1600 nodes.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 5000 generations with data averaged over a time window of 1000 generations.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The renormalized enhancement factor delta = r/(z+1) is varied; this is equivalent to MPCR.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Players (agents) are assumed to know the quality (cooperation level) of each group they participate in, which is used to bias contributions.

## Important Limitations
- No explicit costly punishment mechanism is modeled; only indirect, costless group-level punishment via reduced contributions.
- Group efficiency or total group payoff is not directly reported or analyzed.
- Findings are based on theoretical modeling and numerical simulations, not empirical or experimental data.
- The main outcome is cooperation level (fraction of cooperators), not efficiency or welfare.
- Implications for explicit punishment interventions are only adjacent, not direct.
