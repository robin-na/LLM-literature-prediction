# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Separating individual and group-level cooperation in the Public Goods Game
Authors: Nielsen, Y. A., & Pfattheicher, S.
Journal: Pnas Nexus
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=observational; experimental subtype=N/A

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution level
- intraclass correlation (ICC) of contributions
- group convergence in cooperation
Outcome notes:
The primary outcome is the intraclass correlation (ICC) of contributions, which measures the proportion of variance in cooperation attributable to group-level convergence. The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or other direct payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that group-level convergence in cooperation (as measured by ICC) increases over the first five rounds of repeated PGGs and plateaus between 20% and 50% under standard conditions. The presence of punishment opportunities and detailed feedback (showing all group members' decisions) substantially increases group convergence, sometimes up to 70%. Small groups (≤4 players) and groups with homogenous social preferences also show higher convergence. The study does not report on efficiency or group payoff, so the direct effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined from this paper.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that punishment opportunities and detailed feedback increase group-level convergence in cooperation in repeated PGGs, especially in small and homogenous groups. However, because the primary outcome is ICC (convergence in contributions) rather than efficiency or payoff, the findings should be used with caution for predicting efficiency. The results suggest that punishment is likely to increase behavioral alignment within groups, which may be associated with higher or lower efficiency depending on the equilibrium reached, but the paper does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or group payoff.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Group size is a key moderator; small groups (≤4) show higher group convergence (ICC) than large groups. The effect is on behavioral convergence, not efficiency.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Number of rounds varies across datasets (7-50). ICC plateaus after about 5 rounds. No direct evidence on efficiency.
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. MPCR varies across datasets (0.3 to 0.75). The effect of MPCR on ICC is discussed but not conclusive due to confounds. No direct evidence on efficiency.
- reward availability: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. One dataset (Rand et al. 2018) includes a reward condition. Reward increases group convergence similarly to punishment, but no efficiency data.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Most datasets report whether endgame knowledge is present (see Table 1). Some have known number of rounds, some do not.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Feedback type (aggregate, detailed, none) is manipulated. Detailed feedback (showing all group members' decisions) increases group convergence (ICC).

## Important Limitations
- The primary outcome is group convergence in cooperation (ICC), not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- No direct analysis of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes; findings on punishment and feedback apply to behavioral convergence, not efficiency.
- Punishment cost and effectiveness are not analyzed as moderators.
- Some subgroup analyses are confounded by other variables (e.g., group size and MPCR).
- No information on communication (chat), default contribution framing, or punishment/reward identification.
