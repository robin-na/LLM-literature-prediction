# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Labor union members play an OLG repeated game
Authors: Kandori, M., & Obayashi, S.
Journal: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America
Year: 2014

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=observational; experimental subtype=N/A

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation/helping behavior
- member participation in union activities
- turnover/exit after dispute resolution
- mechanisms of reciprocity and conditional cooperation
Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the mechanisms sustaining cooperation (helping behavior) in a real-world overlapping generations (OLG) setting, specifically a Japanese community labor union. Outcomes are behavioral (who helps whom, under what conditions), not measured in terms of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare. Payoff structure is discussed in the theoretical model, but not measured empirically.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that cooperation (helping behavior) among union members is sustained not by explicit punishment, perfect monitoring, or reputation, but by a focal-point equilibrium where members help others in expectation of reciprocal help when they need it. Most members leave the union after their own disputes are resolved, indicating that helping is conditional and self-interested rather than purely altruistic. The equilibrium is supported by direct observation and mutual monitoring during joint activities, but not by formal sanctions or payoff-based incentives. The paper provides qualitative evidence and a theoretical model, but does not measure or report efficiency, group payoff, or related outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is not directly informative for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar laboratory environments. It provides qualitative, contextual evidence about how cooperation can be sustained in a real-world OLG setting with limited observability, but does not report or manipulate efficiency, payoff, or formal punishment mechanisms. The findings may be useful for understanding the range of mechanisms that can support cooperation in loosely knit groups, but do not provide quantitative or experimental evidence relevant to the prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Theoretical model specifies 6 players at a time (2 young, 2 middle-aged, 2 old), but the real union has ~231 members with variable participation. The OLG model is used for interpretation, not for experimental manipulation.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Theoretical model is infinite horizon; in practice, members stay for variable durations (most <2 years). No fixed number of rounds.
- chat: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Members communicate via email and in-person at meetings, but there is no structured or universal chat. Communication is limited and not systematically recorded.
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Theoretical model defines benefit per help (b) and cost per help (c), but no explicit MPCR as in standard PGGs. The ratio b/c > 8 is required for equilibrium.
- reward availability: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Reciprocal help functions as a reward; no formal reward mechanism.
- reward cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Cost of helping (c) is discussed in the theoretical model; helping is costly (e.g., taking a day off work).
- reward technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Effectiveness of help (b) is discussed in the model; benefit to recipient is large if many help.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Members are notified of upcoming activities and who needs help, but there is no systematic summary of others' past contributions or payoffs.

## Important Limitations
- The study is a single-case qualitative analysis and does not provide generalizable or quantitative evidence.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are measured or reported.
- No experimental manipulation of game design dimensions or punishment/reward mechanisms.
- Findings are based on interviews, observation, and theoretical modeling, not on controlled experiments.
- The mechanisms described may not transfer to standard public goods games or laboratory settings.
