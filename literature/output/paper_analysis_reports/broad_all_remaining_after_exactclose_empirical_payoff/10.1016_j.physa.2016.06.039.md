While payoffs are defined and used in the model, the primary focus is on behavioral dynamics and the maintenance of cooperation, not on group efficiency or total welfare in the sense of public goods games.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a discriminative cooperation strategy in a spatial multi-player donor-recipient game can maintain high levels of cooperation and prevent the dominance of defectors, especially when the cost-to-benefit ratio is low and the cost of information collection is small. Discriminators act as conditional cooperators who deny resources to defectors, which functions as a form of indirect punishment. The model shows that, under certain parameter regimes, all three strategies (cooperation, defection, discrimination) can coexist in a cyclic dominance pattern, and the ratio of donated to total resources can remain high. However, the model does not directly report group efficiency or total welfare in the sense of public goods games, and the structure is not a standard PGG.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that conditional, discriminative strategies (akin to targeted punishment or exclusion) can sustain cooperation and high resource donation in spatial multi-player settings, especially when the cost of such discrimination is low. However, because the model is not a standard public goods game and does not report group efficiency or total payoff, its findings should be transferred to PGG-like prediction tasks with caution. The main implication is that mechanisms which allow players to conditionally withhold benefits from defectors (even at a cost) can be effective in maintaining cooperation, but the quantitative effect on efficiency in PGGs is not directly established.

## Important Limitations
- The model is not a standard public goods game; it is a spatial multi-player donor-recipient game with different structure and payoff mechanisms.
- Outcomes are primarily behavioral (strategy frequencies, resource donation ratios), not direct measures of group efficiency or total payoff as in PGGs.
- Punishment is implemented as conditional withholding of resources (exclusion), not as explicit costly punishment or fines.
- No direct experimental or empirical data; results are from agent-based simulations and theoretical analysis.
- No explicit reporting of group efficiency, welfare, or surplus; only indirect measures such as the ratio of donated to total resources.
