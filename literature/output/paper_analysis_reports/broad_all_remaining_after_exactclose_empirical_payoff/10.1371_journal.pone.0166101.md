# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Women Tend to Defect in a Social Dilemma Game in Southwest China
Authors: Pansini, R., Shi, L., & Wang, R. W.
Journal: Plos One
Year: 2016

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- monetary payoff/earnings per subject
- earnings by strategy (cooperation, defection, punishment)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of cooperation
- frequency of defection
- frequency of punishment
- conditional strategies by gender
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: cooperation, defection, and punishment rates, especially by gender. Some analysis of monetary payoff is reported, but not as the primary focus. Efficiency or group-level payoff is not directly analyzed; individual earnings are discussed in relation to strategy and gender.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a repeated dyadic Prisoner's Dilemma with a punishment option, women in Southwest China defect more and cooperate less than men, a pattern that contrasts with previous findings from Western and other Asian samples. Both genders earned less when they punished, and there was no significant gender difference in overall monetary earnings. The use of punishment did not increase individual earnings, and the paper does not analyze group efficiency or total welfare. The main findings are about behavioral strategies and gender differences, not about the effect of punishment on efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that in a dyadic iterated Prisoner's Dilemma with a punishment option, punishment is used more frequently than in comparable Western samples, but its use is associated with lower individual earnings. The design is not a public goods game but a two-player PD, so direct prediction for PGG efficiency is limited. The main decision support is that punishment, as implemented here, does not increase individual payoffs and may reduce them. The paper does not report group efficiency or the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency, so its value for predicting efficiency outcomes in PGG-like environments is limited.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is played in dyads (2 players per game).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each dyad played a repeated game with a maximum of 9 rounds per interaction, but the number of rounds was uncertain to subjects (continuation probability 0.75 per round).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed; subjects interacted anonymously via computer.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Choices are discrete: cooperate, defect, or punish (no continuous contribution).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment costs the punisher 5 points (see Table 1: e.g., (Cooperation, Punishment) = (-5, 1)), and imposes a cost on the target (e.g., (Punishment, Defection) = (-2, -3)). Use of punishment is associated with lower individual earnings.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: the cost to the target varies (e.g., -3 or -5), but always less than or equal to the cost to the punisher. Punishment does not increase earnings.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward option is available; only cooperation, defection, or punishment.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects did not know the number of rounds in advance; continuation probability was 0.75 per round.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, choices and updated payoff scores were shown to both participants.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. In dyadic play, the identity of the punisher is always known to the other player.

## Important Limitations
- The game is an iterated dyadic Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not generalize to multi-player PGG environments.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (cooperation, defection, punishment rates), not efficiency or group payoff.
- No direct analysis of the effect of enabling punishment on group efficiency or total welfare.
- Cultural and gender effects may not generalize beyond the specific sample of Chinese university students.
- Punishment is only analyzed in terms of individual earnings, not group-level outcomes.
