# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The spiral of distrust: (Non-)cooperation in a repeated trust game is predicted by anger and individual differences in negative reciprocity orientation
Authors: Harth, N. S., & Regner, T.
Journal: International Journal of Psychology
Year: 2017

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- amount sent by sender (trust/investment)
- anger (state anger ratings)
- negative reciprocity orientation (individual difference)
- amount returned by trustee
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the amount sent by the sender in a repeated trust game, interpreted as willingness to cooperate. No group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings are analyzed as outcomes. Anger and negative reciprocity orientation are also measured and analyzed as predictors of cooperation.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that senders who receive feedback about low back transfers from trustees experience increased anger, which in turn leads to reduced cooperation (lower amounts sent) in subsequent rounds. Individuals with a high negative reciprocity orientation are more prone to anger and are less willing to cooperate, even in the absence of anger-eliciting feedback. The interaction between anger and negative reciprocity orientation further reduces cooperation. The absence of feedback results in lower anger and relatively higher cooperation, especially among those low in negative reciprocity orientation. The study does not analyze efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings as outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods or similar games, as it studies a repeated trust game without any punishment or sanctioning mechanism. The main outcomes are behavioral (amount sent/cooperation) and emotional (anger), not payoff-based. However, it provides indirect evidence that negative emotions and individual differences in reciprocity orientation can reduce cooperation in repeated interactions, especially when feedback about others' behavior is available. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency or payoff, this paper is only weakly informative and should not be weighted heavily unless the prediction context is specifically about trust games or the psychological mechanisms of cooperation.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each trust game round is played between two players (sender and trustee). The experiment involved 74 senders and 74 trustees, but each round is a dyadic interaction.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The trust game was played over six periods (rounds).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or visual interaction was allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (amount sent) is continuous between 0 and 10 ECU, not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Amount sent by sender is doubled (multiplied by 2) and added to trustee's endowment. For each ECU sent, 2 ECU are created for the trustee. The marginal per-capita return for the sender is not explicitly stated, but the structure is clear.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants knew that the game would be played over six periods.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the feedback condition, senders were informed about the trustee's back transfer at the end of each trial. In the no feedback condition, they were not.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a repeated trust game, not a public goods game or a direct variant, so findings may not generalize to PGG settings.
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present; the study focuses on emotional and dispositional predictors of cooperation, not institutional interventions.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings outcomes are analyzed; only individual behavioral outcomes (amount sent) are reported.
- Sample size is moderate (74 senders), and all participants are university students in a lab setting, which may limit external validity.
- The study does not manipulate or analyze any of the key game design dimensions related to punishment or reward.
