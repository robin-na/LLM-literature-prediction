## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The ABM shows that cooperation (publicly calling others to share a resource) is stable when social capital (reputation) is valued in the fitness function and when the visibility of actions (vision) is high, making it easier to detect defectors. When only material gain matters, defection dominates. The system tends to polarize into either high cooperation or high defection regimes, with intermediate states rare. The model highlights the importance of indirect reciprocity and the public observability of actions for sustaining cooperation. However, the model does not report group efficiency or total payoff as outcomes, focusing instead on behavioral regimes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, theoretical support for the idea that mechanisms increasing the visibility of actions and the value of social reputation can promote cooperation in public-goods-like dilemmas. It suggests that punishment via social exclusion or reputation loss (rather than explicit costly punishment) can sustain cooperation if actions are observable and social capital is valued. However, because the model does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. Its main value is in highlighting the importance of observability and social capital as moderators of cooperation, which may inform the interpretation of similar dimensions in empirical studies.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and agent-based, not empirical or experimental; results are illustrative, not predictive.
- Outcomes are behavioral (cooperation/defection), not group efficiency or total payoff; efficiency is not reported.
- Punishment is modeled as social exclusion and reputation loss, not as explicit costly punishment.
- The model is adjacent to, but not an exact instance of, public goods games or standard punishment experiments.
- Parameter values (e.g., player count, number of rounds) are not always specified or systematically varied in a way that maps directly to public goods game dimensions.
