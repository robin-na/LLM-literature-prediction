Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the evolution of cooperation, investment allocation between cooperation and punishment, and spatial patterns. It does not report group efficiency, total payoff, welfare, or surplus. Outcomes are primarily behavioral and strategy-based.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the effect of punishment on cooperation in a spatial public goods game depends on both the punishment intensity and the learning rule (PSO weighting coefficient). For low punishment intensity, low susceptibility to neighbors (low omega) promotes cooperation; for high punishment intensity, high omega is more beneficial. Intermediate omega values increase punishment input but can reduce cooperation. The enhancement factor (r) also moderates these effects. However, the study does not report efficiency or group payoff, so its findings are about cooperation rates and investment allocation, not direct welfare or efficiency outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about how punishment and learning rules affect cooperation rates and investment allocation in spatial PGGs with continuous strategies. It does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency or group payoff with or without punishment. For the prediction task, the main value is in understanding that the effect of punishment on cooperation is highly sensitive to punishment intensity and the learning rule, and that the allocation of resources between cooperation and punishment can affect the expansion of cooperative clusters. However, since efficiency is not reported, the paper's findings should be used only as indirect, mechanistic context rather than as direct quantitative evidence for efficiency prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each player is on a square lattice of size L x L, with L=100 in simulations. Each player has 4 neighbors (M=4) and participates in 5 games (self + neighbors).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for many Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with outcomes averaged over the last 500 MCS after transients.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players allocate a fixed endowment between cooperation and punishment, not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Enhancement factor r is varied (e.g., r=2.5, 3.5, 3.8, 4), with MPCR = r/(M+1) = r/5.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players allocate part of their fixed endowment (c=1) to punishment; cost is endogenous and determined by allocation. Punishment intensity beta determines the impact per unit cost.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is parameterized by beta (punishment intensity). Higher beta increases the impact of punishment per unit cost and promotes cooperation.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players observe the performance of their neighbors (public goods investment and payoffs) as part of the PSO learning rule.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes.
- Outcomes are limited to cooperation level and investment allocation, not direct measures of group benefit or efficiency.
- The model assumes homogeneous endowments and does not consider heterogeneity in player resources.
- No empirical or experimental data are provided; all results are theoretical/simulation.
- The spatial structure (square lattice) and continuous strategy space may limit generalizability to other PGG settings.
