# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Altruistic Punishment and Between-Group Competition Evidence from n-person Prisoner's Dilemmas
Authors: Rebers, S., & Koopmans, R.
Journal: Human Nature-an Interdisciplinary Biosocial Perspective
Year: 2012

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=other_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- amount spent on punishing defectors
- amount spent on punishing cooperators
- contribution decision (cooperate/defect)
- expectations about others' punishment
- number of cooperators/defectors
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the amount of money spent on punishing defectors and cooperators, and the contribution decision. The paper does not report group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare. Payoff tables are provided, but efficiency or group payoff is not analyzed as an outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that participants spend more resources punishing defectors in n-person prisoner's dilemma games when there is intergroup competition, compared to single-group conditions. However, this increase is explained by participants' expectations about others' punishment behavior, and the direct effect of competition becomes insignificant when controlling for these expectations. There is no effect of intergroup competition on the punishment of cooperators. The study uses a one-shot (single-round) design, so it does not assess the long-term effects of punishment on cooperation or efficiency. No direct efficiency or group payoff outcomes are reported.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that intergroup competition increases punishment of defectors, but does not affect punishment of cooperators, in a one-shot n-person prisoner's dilemma with a punishment option. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. The design is close to a public goods game, but not identical. The findings suggest that in similar one-shot games, punishment may be more frequent under intergroup competition, but the downstream effect on efficiency is not measured. The results are most useful for understanding behavioral mechanisms (punishment behavior) rather than efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 6 participants per group in all conditions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. One-shot (single round) for both contribution and punishment stages.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed; complete anonymity between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is all-or-nothing: each participant chooses to contribute their full endowment (€2.00) or nothing.
- default contribution framing: The paper directly informs this dimension. Default for dropouts is 'group account' for contribution and 'no punishment' for punishment, but these are excluded from analysis. No explicit default for active participants.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each €2.00 contributed to the group account yields €0.60 per group member (including the contributor), so MPCR = 0.60/2.00 = 0.3 per member, with 6 members.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost is €0.10 per increment; each €0.10 spent reduces the target's payoff by €0.30.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3:1 (each €0.10 spent reduces target's payoff by €0.30).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game is one-shot (single round for both contribution and punishment).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are informed about the cooperating and defecting behavior of group members before the punishment stage.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; participants do not know who punished them.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is one-shot (single round), so it does not assess the long-term or repeated effects of punishment on cooperation or efficiency.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only punishment and contribution behaviors are measured.
- The design is a close variant of a public goods game (n-person prisoner's dilemma), but not a standard linear PGG.
- The online setting and use of default decisions for dropouts may limit comparability to standard lab experiments.
- The effect of punishment on actual group outcomes (efficiency, total payoff) is not analyzed.
