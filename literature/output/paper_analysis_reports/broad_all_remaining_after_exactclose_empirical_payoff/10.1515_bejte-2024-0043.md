# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Voluntary Partnerships for Equally Sharing Contribution Costs
Authors: Buso, I. M., Di Cagno, D., Gueth, W., & Spadoni, L.
Journal: B E Journal of Theoretical Economics
Year: 2025

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- individual earnings
- group total contributions
- proportion of endowment contributed
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution amount
- willingness to join partnership
- partnership formation frequency
- role (partner/outsider) contributions
- beliefs about others' contributions
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are contribution levels and partnership formation. While payoffs are calculated and reported, the primary focus is on contribution behavior, not directly on efficiency or group payoff as a share of the social optimum. Efficiency is not explicitly calculated, but can be inferred from contribution data.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Voluntary cost-sharing partnerships in a PGG-like environment significantly increase average contributions and prevent the typical decay of cooperation over repeated rounds. Partners contribute more than outsiders, and the presence of the partnership mechanism (whether endogenous or exogenous) leads to higher group contributions and payoffs than the standard PGG. The effect is strongest for larger partnerships. The mechanism does not involve punishment or reward, but rather a self-commitment to cost-sharing. Efficiency (in terms of group payoff relative to the social optimum) is not directly reported, but the data show that average contributions are about 50% of the endowment with partnerships, compared to about 32% in the standard PGG. The voluntary nature of partnership formation has a modest additional effect, especially for small partnerships.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that institutional mechanisms allowing voluntary cost-sharing partnerships can substantially increase group contributions and payoffs in PGG-like settings, even in the absence of explicit punishment or reward. For prediction tasks, the presence of such a partnership mechanism should be expected to yield higher efficiency than standard PGGs with similar MPCR and group size. The effect is robust to whether partnership formation is endogenous or exogenous, except for very small partnerships. The findings are most directly relevant for settings where cost-sharing or coalition formation is possible, but do not directly inform about punishment effects. The results suggest that, in the absence of punishment, other institutional mechanisms can also enhance efficiency, and that the design dimensions (player count, MPCR, endogenous/exogenous group formation) interact with the mechanism's effectiveness.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All experimental conditions use groups of 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consists of 12 rounds.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Participants could contact experimenters via chat for clarification, but there is no evidence of communication between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are continuous (integer between 5 and 20), not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is fixed at 0.4 in all conditions.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are informed that there are 12 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants see their own payoff, their own contribution, and the total group contribution. Partners also see total partnership contribution.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not include any punishment or reward mechanism, so results do not directly inform about punishment effects.
- Efficiency is not explicitly calculated or reported as a share of the social optimum, though it can be inferred from contribution data.
- The findings are specific to a cost-sharing partnership mechanism and may not generalize to other institutional designs or to settings with punishment.
- All experiments use a fixed group size (n=4) and MPCR (0.4), limiting generalizability to other parameterizations.
- The primary outcomes are contribution rates and partnership formation, not direct measures of group payoff or efficiency.
