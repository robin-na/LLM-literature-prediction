## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper presents a formal evolutionary model of a voluntary public goods game with contributors, defectors, nonparticipants, and altruistic punishers. Without punishment, the population cycles between cooperation, defection, and nonparticipation, and cooperation is not stable. When altruistic punishment is introduced, punishers can invade and eventually dominate the population, leading to a stable equilibrium with high cooperation and higher group payoffs. The model shows that punishment can only enforce strategies that are payoff-improving relative to nonparticipation, and that the presence of punishment eliminates the previously observed cycles and leads to persistent cooperation and higher efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support that, in voluntary public goods games, enabling punishment leads to higher group efficiency and welfare, provided that the punishment is sufficiently strong and not excessively costly. The model predicts that punishment will eliminate cycles of cooperation and defection, stabilize cooperation, and increase average payoffs relative to the baseline without punishment. For prediction tasks, this suggests that, all else equal, enabling punishment in a voluntary PGG-like environment should increase efficiency, but only if the parameters (punishment impact, cost, and net benefit of cooperation) allow for a payoff advantage over nonparticipation. The model also highlights that punishment cannot enforce inefficient or welfare-reducing strategies.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental data.
- It assumes a large, well-mixed population and does not address small group or finite population effects.
- The model abstracts from implementation details such as monitoring, information structure, and the process of identifying defectors and nonpunishers.
- No explicit time horizon or round structure; results may not directly translate to finite repeated games.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanisms are considered.
- Assumes punishment is always possible and can be targeted as needed.
