## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a nested PGG environment with both local and global public goods, indirect reciprocity (reputation-based conditional aid) can stabilize cooperation at a single scale, but when multiple scales are present, local cooperation is more stable and can invade global cooperation. This occurs even when the global PGG has a higher theoretical maximum payoff. The model demonstrates that the presence of multiple, potentially conflicting reputations leads to a situation where local cooperation undermines global cooperation, reducing overall efficiency at the larger scale. The more fragmented the population (more local groups), the harder it is for global cooperation to be stable. Thus, indirect reciprocity, as typically modeled, cannot by itself sustain efficient, large-scale cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that the structure of group membership and the scale at which reputational incentives operate are critical moderators of the efficiency gains from indirect reciprocity-based 'punishment' (here, conditional aid/reward) in PGG-like environments. Specifically, if the game design allows for multiple, nested groups and reputations can be built at the local level, enabling local cooperation, then enabling indirect reciprocity (punishment/reward) may not increase, and can even decrease, efficiency at the global (large-group) level. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the presence of multiple group scales and the ability to build local reputations are key negative moderators of the efficiency effect of punishment/reward mechanisms. The findings are directly relevant for settings where the control (no punishment/reward) game is inefficient, but the addition of indirect reciprocity does not guarantee higher efficiency at the largest scale.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental data.
- The model assumes perfect information about others' actions and reputations, except for a small implementation error.
- The model does not include direct punishment (costly punishment that reduces others' payoffs), only conditional aid (reward) as the reputational mechanism.
- The model does not consider noisy or private reputational information, which may further complicate real-world dynamics.
- Findings may not generalize to settings where group structure, reputation mechanisms, or punishment/reward technologies differ substantially from the model.
