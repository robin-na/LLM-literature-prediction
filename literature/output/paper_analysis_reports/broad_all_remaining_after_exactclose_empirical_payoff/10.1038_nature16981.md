Journal: Nature

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:

- frequency of punishment/helping
- perceived trustworthiness
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are behavioral: whether third-party punishment is used as a signal, how it affects trust and trustworthiness in a subsequent trust game, and how the availability of helping as an alternative signal moderates these effects. No group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff outcomes are reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that TPP is used as a signal of trustworthiness when it is the only available signal, leading to greater trust and reciprocation in a subsequent trust game. When helping is also available as a signal, the use and signaling value of TPP are reduced, while helping remains a strong signal. The results support a reputational account of TPP, showing that people may punish to signal their character rather than solely to deter selfishness. However, the study does not report on group efficiency, welfare, or total payoffs in public goods or similar games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the signaling value and frequency of punishment depend on the availability and informativeness of alternative prosocial signals (like helping). However, it does not provide direct evidence about the effect of punishment on group efficiency or total payoffs in public-goods-game-like environments. Its main relevance is in highlighting that the context and available signals can moderate the behavioral use of punishment, which may indirectly affect efficiency in other designs. For direct prediction of efficiency outcomes, this paper is only indirectly informative.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The experimental design involves three roles (Helper, Recipient, Punisher) in the TPPG, and two roles (Signaller, Chooser) in the trust game. The TPPG is not a standard multi-player PGG but a three-person game. The player count is fixed by design.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Each participant plays a single round in each role per condition; the games are one-shot.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Punishment and helping are binary choices (punish or not, help or not).
- punishment cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Punishment is costly to the punisher, but the exact cost is not specified in the main text.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Helping is available as an alternative costly prosocial signal in some conditions, functioning as a reward mechanism.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Games are one-shot; participants know the structure in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Choosers observe the Signaller's punishment and/or helping decisions before making trust decisions.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment and helping decisions are attributed to the Signaller and observed by the Chooser.

## Important Limitations
- The experimental design is not a standard public goods game; it uses a third-party punishment game and a trust game, so results may not transfer directly to PGG efficiency.
- No group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff outcomes are reported; all main outcomes are behavioral (signaling, trust, trustworthiness).
- Punishment and helping are binary (all-or-nothing) rather than continuous.
- The cost and impact of punishment and helping are not specified in the main text.
- The study does not manipulate or report marginal per-capita return (MPCR) or other key PGG parameters.
- Theoretical model assumes individual differences in costs/benefits, but the experiment does not manipulate these parameters.
