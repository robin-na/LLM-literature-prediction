## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a spatial public goods game with continuous strategies, punishing the minority of lowest contributors (i.e., when the number of punished individuals is less than half the group) increases the average contribution level, while punishing the majority decreases it. Increasing the punishment fine increases cooperation when the punished are a minority, but decreases it when the punished are a majority. The main reported outcome is the average contribution (cooperation level), not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about how punishment design (number punished, fine size, cost structure) affects average contribution in a spatial continuous PGG. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot be used directly to predict efficiency outcomes. The findings suggest that punishment can backfire if the cost to punishers is too high relative to the fine, and that targeting a minority of low contributors is more effective for increasing cooperation. These insights may inform the qualitative direction of efficiency effects in similar designs, but quantitative prediction is not possible from this paper alone.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group has 5 players (focal individual + 4 neighbors); the lattice is 100x100 (10,000 individuals), but each group interaction is among 5.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 20,000 transient steps plus 1,000 steps for averaging; not a fixed number of rounds as in human experiments, but a long-run evolutionary process.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous between 0 and 1; not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Enhancement factor r is varied; MPCR is r/5 (group size 5).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost per punisher is \beta = L\alpha/(5-L); cost structure is central to the findings.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment fine \alpha is varied; effectiveness depends on number punished and cost structure.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is average contribution (cooperation level), not efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes.
- Results are from agent-based simulations, not human experiments.
- The spatial structure and continuous strategy space may limit generalizability to standard lab PGGs.
- No analysis of stochasticity or robustness to parameter changes beyond those reported.
