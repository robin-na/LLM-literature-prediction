## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that group selection can, under certain conditions, support the evolution of cooperation and altruism in public-goods-like and Prisoner's Dilemma games. Three main approaches are discussed: fixation (requiring isolated pure groups), assortative group formation (e.g., via conformity or signalling), and reproductive externalities (where group-level payoffs affect evolutionary success). The paper notes that group selection is more likely to support cooperation when migration is low, groups are small, or positive externalities can be internalized. It also reviews how cultural mechanisms (like conformity) and group competition can foster social preferences. However, the paper does not provide direct evidence or predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff, and only discusses punishment as a possible mechanism in the context of conformity and social preferences.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical context for how group structure, migration, and mechanisms like conformity or signalling can affect the evolution of cooperation in public-goods-like games. It is useful for understanding the broader conditions under which cooperation and social preferences may arise, but it does not provide direct evidence or quantitative predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff. For the downstream prediction task, this paper suggests that group structure and the ability to internalize externalities are important moderators, but it does not inform the specific effect of punishment interventions in experimental public goods games.
