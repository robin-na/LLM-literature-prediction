Non-payoff outcomes:

Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are payoff functions for both cooperative members and the cooperative itself, with explicit modeling of how punishment affects these payoffs. The paper also analyzes the stability of full cooperation (all members faithful) as an equilibrium under different punishment strengths.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that effective punishment (penalty parameter β > 1) stabilizes full cooperation in a marketing cooperative, leading to the group achieving the maximum possible profit (i.e., efficiency). When punishment is limited (β = 1), cooperation is unstable and members tend to defect, reducing group profit. The model is dynamic, with payoffs and strategies evolving over time, but the key result is that sufficiently strong punishment ensures convergence to the efficient outcome. The findings are demonstrated both analytically and through simulation examples.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support for the prediction that enabling sufficiently strong punishment in a public-goods-like environment (here, a marketing cooperative) will increase efficiency, moving the group toward the maximum possible collective payoff. The model is not a standard PGG but is structurally similar: members choose how much to contribute to the group (by selling through the cooperative), and the group can punish defectors. The explicit analysis of payoff functions and the demonstration that efficiency is achieved only when punishment is strong enough (β > 1) are directly relevant for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency, especially in settings where the punishment technology is effective and the cost/impact ratio is favorable. However, the model is theoretical and does not provide empirical effect sizes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is defined for n players (cooperative members), with examples using n=3. The results are general for any n.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is dynamic and continuous-time, with both real-time (continuous) and discrete-time (periodic) delivery analyzed. There is no fixed number of rounds, but the time horizon can be partitioned into periods.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (x_i) is continuous in [0,1], not all-or-nothing. Players can choose any fraction of their production to sell through the cooperative.
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is not explicitly defined as in standard PGGs, but the payoff structure is analogous: the group profit depends on collective contribution, and the market price is a function of total output. The model uses a Cournot-type price function.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is parameterized by β (penalty rate). The cost to the punisher (the cooperative) is not explicitly modeled as a resource cost, but the penalty is proportional to the extra revenue of defectors. The effect of increasing β is directly analyzed.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is captured by the penalty parameter β. The model shows that only when β > 1 (i.e., punishment is strong/effective) does efficiency result.

## Important Limitations
- The model is purely theoretical and does not provide empirical effect sizes or real-world data.
- The setting is not a standard public goods game but a marketing cooperative with a specific payoff and punishment structure; results may not generalize to all PGGs.
- Punishment is modeled as an institutional penalty, not as peer-to-peer punishment.
- The cost to the punisher (the cooperative) is not explicitly modeled as a resource or utility cost, only as a penalty imposed on defectors.
- No analysis of stochasticity, noise, or bounded rationality; all players are assumed to adapt strategies according to payoff gradients.
- No consideration of communication, reputation, or other social mechanisms beyond punishment.
