# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: On approval and disapproval: Theory and experiments
Authors: López-Pérez, R., & Vorsatz, M.
Journal: Journal of Economic Psychology
Year: 2010

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- approval/disapproval expectations
- approval/disapproval messages sent
- willingness to pay for feedback
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the cooperation rate in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma. The study focuses on how the possibility of non-material (costly) approval/disapproval feedback affects cooperation. No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings is reported; all outcome analysis is at the level of individual choices (cooperate/defect) and feedback behavior.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment compares three treatments: (1) control (standard PD, no feedback), (2) expectations (subjects state what they expect their co-player will think of their choice), and (3) feedback (after the PD, subjects can pay to send an approval/disapproval message to their co-player). The cooperation rate is highest in the feedback treatment (38.3%), intermediate in the expectations treatment (29.0%), and lowest in the control (22.4%), but only the difference between feedback and control is statistically significant. The study finds that the possibility of non-material sanctions (approval/disapproval) increases cooperation, but does not analyze or report efficiency or group payoff. The effect is interpreted as arising from increased awareness of others' opinions and aversion to receiving negative feedback. The feedback mechanism is costly (10 ECU per message), but the messages have no material effect on payoffs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that the opportunity for non-material, costly approval/disapproval feedback can increase cooperation in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma, but does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or group payoff. The findings are adjacent to public goods games with punishment, as the game is a two-player PD and the 'punishment' is non-material and costly, with no direct effect on payoffs. For prediction tasks, the main implication is that enabling even non-material, costly feedback can increase cooperation rates, but the effect on efficiency is not measured. The design dimensions most relevant here are the presence of a feedback/punishment stage, the cost of feedback, and the fact that feedback is non-material. The results suggest that both the psychological salience of being evaluated and the aversion to negative feedback are important for increasing cooperation, but the transferability to efficiency in PGGs is limited by the absence of group payoff data.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are two-player (dyadic) prisoner's dilemmas.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject plays a single round of the PD in each treatment (one-shot game).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication is allowed except for the structured feedback messages in the feedback treatment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate (X) or defect (Y).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the PD, mutual cooperation yields 180 ECU each, mutual defection 100 ECU each, unilateral cooperation yields 80 ECU (cooperator) and 260 ECU (defector). Not a standard PGG, but the marginal return to cooperation is negative for the individual, positive for the pair.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sending a feedback message (approval/disapproval/neutral) costs 10 ECU to the sender.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment/approval is non-material: messages have no effect on the recipient's payoff, only on their emotional state.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Approval (positive feedback) is possible as a non-material reward, at the same cost as disapproval.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sending an approval (reward) message costs 10 ECU, same as disapproval.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Approval messages have no material effect on recipient's payoff, only emotional/psychological.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the PD is a single round (one-shot).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Feedback messages are sent directly from one player to their paired co-player; the recipient knows the message is from their co-player.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a two-player prisoner's dilemma, not a multi-player public goods game; results may not generalize to larger groups or standard PGGs.
- The feedback mechanism is non-material and costly, with no direct effect on recipient payoffs; this differs from standard punishment in PGGs, which is typically material.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates and feedback behavior are analyzed.
- The study is limited to one-shot interactions; repeated game effects are not examined.
- The psychological mechanisms (awareness, negative information avoidance) are inferred from treatment comparisons, not directly measured.
