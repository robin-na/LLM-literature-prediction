## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in the survivor's dilemma, cooperation can be evolutionarily stable if the benefit-to-cost ratio is high and/or environmental volatility is low. In these cases, mutual cooperation leads to higher expected lifespan (efficiency) for all. However, if the benefit-to-cost ratio is low or volatility is high, defection dominates and efficiency is lower. The model does not include explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanisms; instead, indirect punishment occurs because defectors lose partners and thus reduce their own survival prospects. The findings are presented for both 2- and 3-player games, with detailed analysis of parameter regions where cooperation or defection is stable. The results are directly about efficiency (expected lifespan) and its dependence on game parameters, but do not address the effect of enabling or disabling explicit punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence about how efficiency (expected lifespan) in repeated social dilemmas depends on group size, benefit-to-cost ratio, and environmental volatility. It does not directly address the effect of enabling or disabling punishment, as no explicit punishment mechanism is present. However, it does show that indirect forms of 'punishment' (loss of cooperative partners) can sustain cooperation and efficiency under certain conditions. For prediction tasks involving explicit punishment in public goods games, this paper is only indirectly informative: it suggests that efficiency gains from cooperation depend strongly on the benefit-to-cost ratio and the likelihood of future interaction (volatility), and that even without explicit punishment, cooperation can sometimes be stable. The model is adjacent to PGGs and does not manipulate or analyze punishment as a treatment.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- No explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present; only indirect punishment via loss of cooperative partners.
- The game is not a public goods game but an iterated prisoner's dilemma variant with survival-based payoffs.
- Results are limited to small group sizes (N=2, N=3); scalability to larger groups is not analyzed.
- No communication, reputation, or history-dependent strategies (e.g., tit-for-tat) are modeled.
- No analysis of the effect of enabling or disabling explicit punishment on efficiency.
