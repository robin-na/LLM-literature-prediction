# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Can job rotation eliminate the Ratchet effect: Experimental evidence
Authors: Wei, C.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=field_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution level
- conditional cooperation (response to co-ethnic vs. non-co-ethnic contributions)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the amount contributed to the public good per round. No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings is reported. The paper discusses efficiency and public goods provision in the abstract and discussion, but all quantitative results are about contribution behavior.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that ethnic dominance (caste hierarchy within groups) has a large negative effect on contributions in a public goods game, while ethnic diversity alone does not. Individuals display strong in-group bias, increasing their contributions in response to co-ethnic peers but not to others. The experiment did not include punishment or reward options, and all quantitative results are about contribution levels, not payoffs or efficiency. The authors discuss antisocial punishment and discrimination as real-world mechanisms but these are not present in the experimental design.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as no punishment or reward mechanisms were present in the experiment. It does, however, provide informative context about how group composition (especially ethnic dominance) can affect baseline cooperation rates, which may be relevant for interpreting control group efficiency in similar environments. For the downstream prediction task, this paper is not directly informative about the effect of enabling punishment, but it does highlight that group structure (e.g., ethnic dominance) can strongly affect baseline cooperation, which could interact with punishment effects in other studies.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups consisted of 6 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group played 10 rounds (plus a 4-round practice game).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players was allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution was continuous: each player could choose how much of 10 INR to contribute each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The common fund was doubled and divided evenly among all 6 players. MPCR = 2/6 = 0.333.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players were told the number of rounds in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players received complete information about all group members' contributions, shares, income, earnings, and caste every round.

## Important Limitations
- No punishment or reward mechanisms were present in the experiment, so the paper does not provide direct evidence about the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff.
- All outcome measures are based on contribution behavior, not efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings.
- The findings are specific to a rural Indian context with strong caste-based social structure; generalizability to other settings may be limited.
- The experiment only manipulated group composition (ethnic diversity and dominance), not institutional features like punishment or communication.
