No explicit analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total welfare is provided. All outcomes are behavioral (strategy frequencies), not payoff-based.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that when defectors are forced by survival pressure to reward cooperators, the frequency of cooperation in the population can increase, especially when the survival pressure threshold is low (players are more sensitive to pressure), the reward cost is low, and the reward amount is sufficiently high. The effect of these parameters is non-linear and depends on their combination with the temptation to defect. The model shows that rewarding behavior by defectors acts as a transitional strategy that can facilitate the emergence and maintenance of cooperation, but does not analyze efficiency or group payoff. The results are based on agent-based simulations on a lattice network.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency or group payoff in public goods games with or without punishment. Its findings are limited to the effect of a specific form of reward (not punishment) on cooperation rates in a spatial prisoner's dilemma. The model and results are adjacent to the target prediction task, as they do not include payoff-based outcomes or public goods game structures, nor do they manipulate or analyze punishment. The main relevance is in showing that certain forms of reward can increase cooperation rates under specific parameter regimes, but this cannot be directly translated into efficiency predictions for PGGs with punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not generalize to PGGs.
- No punishment or sanction mechanism is included; only reward is modeled.
- Outcomes are limited to cooperation rates and strategy frequencies; no efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported.
- All results are from agent-based simulations; no empirical or experimental data.
- Simulations are limited to lattice networks of fixed size; no analysis of other network structures or scalability.
- No direct comparison to other mechanisms (e.g., punishment, tax-based incentives) is provided.
