## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that enhancing retributive (punishment) tendencies in social dilemma games (including public goods and prisoner's dilemma paradigms) is likely to lower group efficiency, even if it increases cooperation rates. The authors review theoretical models and cite experimental evidence indicating that harsh punishment can make groups poorer, and that those who punish often gain less than those who do not. The effect of punishment on efficiency is sensitive to the cost/benefit ratio but relatively insensitive to punishment severity. The paper also notes that anti-social punishment (punishing cooperators) can be highly destructive to cooperation and efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and literature-based evidence that enabling or increasing punishment in public-goods-game-like environments is likely to reduce group efficiency, especially when punishment is harsh or misapplied. For prediction tasks, this suggests that, all else equal, adding or intensifying punishment mechanisms may not increase and may even decrease efficiency, despite possible increases in cooperation rates. The effect is particularly negative when punishment is severe or when anti-social punishment is possible. The paper is less informative about the precise quantitative effect sizes but is clear about the direction of the effect on efficiency.
