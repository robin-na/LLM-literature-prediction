## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that allowing cooperators to rewire their links away from defectors (link rewiring) in a CPR game can prevent the collapse of cooperation and resource depletion, especially in sparsely connected networks and when resource inflow is high. The model uses a costless social ostracism mechanism (reputation-based exclusion) rather than costly punishment. The main reported outcomes are the fraction of cooperators and resource abundance, not group efficiency or total payoff. The results suggest that adaptive network structure (partner switching) can be a powerful mechanism for sustaining cooperation, but the effects depend on network density and ecological parameters.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for the effect of costly punishment on efficiency in public goods or CPR games. Instead, it analyzes a costless ostracism/reputation mechanism and the impact of adaptive network rewiring. While the findings are relevant for understanding how social structure and reputation can support cooperation, they do not inform predictions about the efficiency effects of enabling costly punishment. The model and results are adjacent to the prediction task, offering indirect insight into the role of network adaptation and costless sanctions, but not the direct impact of costly punishment on efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=100 agents, justified as realistic for CPR settings.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for at least 10^6 time steps to equilibrium; not structured as discrete rounds but as time steps in a dynamic process.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (extraction) is not all-or-nothing; agents choose between two effort levels (cooperator: efficient effort, defector: Nash equilibrium effort).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Social ostracism is costless for cooperators; there is no costly punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Ostracism reduces defectors' utility via a nonlinear function of the number of cooperator neighbors; effectiveness is parameterized but not directly compared to costly punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The model uses costless social ostracism (reputation-based exclusion) rather than costly punishment, so results do not generalize to costly punishment interventions.
- Outcomes focus on cooperation fraction and resource abundance, not on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- Theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data.
- Network adaptation is limited to cooperator-initiated rewiring; other forms of network change are not explored.
- The model does not address asynchrony between structural, ecological, and strategic dynamics.
- Findings may not generalize to more heterogeneous or realistic social networks.
