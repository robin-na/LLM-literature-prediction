## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a spatial public goods game with punishment (both pool and peer), time-dependent effects such as oscillations in the enhancement factor (r) and delays in punishment delivery reduce the prevalence of cooperation and increase defection. Peer punishment is more effective than pool punishment at promoting cooperation, but both are less effective when r oscillates with high amplitude or when punishment is delayed. The main outcomes are the frequencies of cooperators, defectors, and punishers, not group efficiency or total payoff. The results suggest that instability in game parameters and delays in sanctioning undermine the effectiveness of punishment in sustaining cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task. It confirms that punishment can promote cooperation in public goods games, especially when punishment is immediate and the enhancement factor is stable. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot be used to directly predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings suggest that in environments with time-varying productivity or delayed punishment, the positive effect of punishment on cooperation (and by extension, likely on efficiency) is weakened. For prediction, this paper supports the idea that punishment is less effective in unstable or delayed settings, but does not provide quantitative efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a square grid of size L x L, typically L=300 (N=90,000), but sometimes L=100 (N=10,000) for computational reasons. Each player is a cell in the grid.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for thousands of Monte Carlo Steps (MCS), e.g., 5000 MCS. Each MCS is a round in which every player has a chance to update their strategy.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: cooperators and punishers contribute 1, defectors contribute 0.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is r/G, where r is the enhancement factor (varied, e.g., r0=3.6, r0=4.0, etc.), and G=5 (group size).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost (gamma) is set to 0.0125 per act of punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment impact (fine) is beta=0.125 per act. Peer punishment is more severe than pool punishment because each punisher punishes each defector individually.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcomes are frequencies of strategies (cooperators, defectors, punishers), not group efficiency or total payoff.
- Results are from agent-based simulations, not laboratory or field experiments with human subjects.
- The model assumes spatial structure (square grid) and evolutionary updating, which may not generalize to all PGG settings.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency, welfare, or group payoff.
- No reward mechanism is considered; only punishment is studied.
