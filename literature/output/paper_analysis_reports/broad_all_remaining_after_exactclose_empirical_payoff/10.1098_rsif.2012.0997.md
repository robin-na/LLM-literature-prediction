Outcome notes:
The review focuses on cooperation rates, strategy dynamics, and pattern formation in public goods games and their variants on structured populations. While payoffs are discussed as part of the game mechanics, the main reported outcomes are behavioral (e.g., prevalence of cooperation, dominance of certain strategies, emergence of clusters), not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The review summarizes that structured populations (lattices, networks, bipartite graphs) and coevolutionary rules can strongly affect the evolution of cooperation in public goods games. Punishment and reward mechanisms, as well as strategic complexity (e.g., conditional cooperation, pool/peer punishment, rewarding), can promote cooperation and solve second-order free-rider problems in structured settings. The review highlights that the effectiveness and stability of punishment and reward depend on the interplay between game parameters (e.g., group size, benefit functions, network structure) and evolutionary dynamics. However, the main outcomes discussed are cooperation rates and the survival/extinction of strategies, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This review provides high-level theoretical and simulation-based context for how punishment and other mechanisms affect cooperation in public goods games on structured populations. It is useful for understanding which game design dimensions (e.g., group size, network structure, strategic complexity) are likely to moderate the effect of punishment on cooperation. However, it does not provide direct quantitative evidence or predictions about efficiency or group payoff, nor does it report control vs. punishment-enabled efficiency rates. For the downstream prediction task, this review can inform which dimensions are likely to be important moderators, but cannot be used for direct quantitative prediction of efficiency outcomes.

## Important Limitations
- This is a theoretical review and does not report new empirical or simulation results.
- The main outcomes discussed are cooperation rates and strategy dynamics, not group efficiency or total payoff.
- No direct quantitative evidence is provided for the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff-based outcomes.
- Game design dimensions are discussed in a qualitative or contextual manner, not as manipulated variables with reported effects on efficiency.
- The review synthesizes findings from many studies, but does not provide a systematic or quantitative meta-analysis.
