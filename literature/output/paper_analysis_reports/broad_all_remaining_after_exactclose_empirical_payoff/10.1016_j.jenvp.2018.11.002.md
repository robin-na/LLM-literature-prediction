# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: From cash to crickets: The non-monetary value of a resource can promote human cooperation
Authors: Bastian, B., Brewer, M., Duffy, J., & Van Lange, P. A. M.
Journal: Journal of Environmental Psychology
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- total points taken (personal consumption)
- motivation ratings (financial gain, concern for others, concern for crickets)
- punishment assigned (amount paid to fine another player)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the total number of points taken by each participant, interpreted as a measure of cooperation (lower consumption = higher cooperation). No direct group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported. Punishment is measured as the amount participants are willing to pay to fine a greedy player.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that highlighting the non-monetary value of a resource (e.g., the moral significance of not harming crickets) leads to reduced personal consumption in a resource dilemma game, indicating increased cooperation. This effect is robust across two studies and is not moderated by individual differences in empathy or moral expansiveness. When a punishment mechanism is introduced (participants can pay to fine a greedy player), there is no significant difference in punishment behavior between the moral-value and control conditions. The main outcome is behavioral (points taken), not direct group efficiency or payoff, but the results suggest that moral framing can promote cooperation even in the presence of defection and punishment opportunities.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that moral or non-monetary framing of the resource can increase cooperation (reduce consumption) in resource dilemma games, which may translate to higher group efficiency if mapped to payoff outcomes. However, the study does not directly report efficiency or group payoff, and the punishment mechanism (costly punishment of defectors) did not significantly affect behavior. For prediction tasks, the findings suggest that game design features that increase the perceived moral value of the resource may enhance cooperation, but the effect of punishment per se on efficiency cannot be determined from this paper. The design is close to a public goods/common pool resource game, but not a standard PGG, and the main outcomes are not efficiency or payoff-based.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games were run in groups of 5 participants per session, but participants played individually with simulated others.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game lasted 20 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players was allowed; participants were in separate cubicles and did not interact.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (points taken) was a continuous choice from 0 up to 30 points per round.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. In Study 2, participants could pay $1, $2, or $3 to fine another player, with each $1 costing the target $3.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness was 3:1 (each $1 paid by punisher cost the target $3).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The round number was displayed on the screen; participants knew the game would end after 20 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. In Study 2, after each round, participants saw which player took the most points and how many points that player took.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is behavioral (points taken), not direct group efficiency or payoff; efficiency is not reported.
- The punishment mechanism did not significantly affect behavior, and the study does not analyze its effect on efficiency.
- The resource dilemma is not a standard PGG; it is a common pool resource game with continuous extraction.
- The moral framing (cricket death) is a strong manipulation and may not generalize to subtler interventions.
- No real interaction between participants; all other players and feedback were simulated.
- Potential for demand characteristics, as experimenters could not be blind to condition.
