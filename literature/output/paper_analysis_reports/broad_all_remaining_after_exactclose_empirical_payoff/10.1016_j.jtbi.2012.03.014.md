## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in the Traveler's Dilemma, evolutionary dynamics favor higher (more cooperative) claims when the reward/penalty R is small relative to the maximum claim M, and lower (less cooperative) claims when R is large. The model reproduces the empirical finding that average claims decrease as R increases. The analysis is focused on the distribution of claims, not on group efficiency or total payoff, and the Traveler's Dilemma is structurally different from public goods games: it is a two-player, competitive undercutting game with a specific bonus/penalty mechanism, not a multi-player contribution game. The findings are relevant for understanding how incentive structures (like R) shape cooperative behavior, but do not provide direct evidence on efficiency or punishment effects in public goods games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, adjacent evidence for the prediction task. It shows that in a two-player social dilemma with a reward/penalty for undercutting (Traveler's Dilemma), increasing the size of the penalty/reward (R) leads to less cooperative behavior (lower claims). However, the game structure is not a public goods game, and the outcomes are not group efficiency or total payoff, but rather the average claim. There is no direct manipulation or analysis of punishment in a PGG or direct efficiency outcomes. Thus, the paper may inform general intuitions about how incentive structures affect cooperation, but should not be used as direct evidence for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in public goods games.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Traveler's Dilemma is a two-player game by construction; all analysis is for N=2 in the game, though evolutionary population size N is varied in the model.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (claim) is a discrete choice between R and M, but not all-or-nothing; players choose any integer value in the range.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. The 'punishment' is the penalty R imposed on the higher claimant; cost is built into the payoff structure, but not as a voluntary punishment choice.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by R; the penalty/reward is exactly R per unit undercut.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is the bonus R given to the lower claimant; both penalty and reward are symmetric and automatic.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is built into the payoff structure; not a voluntary cost, but an automatic mechanism.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is R per unit undercut; symmetric with penalty.

## Important Limitations
- The paper studies the Traveler's Dilemma, not the public goods game or its direct variants.
- Outcomes are behavioral (claim size), not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- Punishment and reward are automatic, not voluntary or strategic as in PGG punishment treatments.
- Theoretical and simulation-based; no new experimental data are reported.
- Findings may not generalize to multi-player, contribution-based public goods games.
