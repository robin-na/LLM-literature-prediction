# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The demand for complete and incomplete punishment institutions to promote cooperation
Authors: Bühren, C., Dannenberg, A., & Händel, P.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2025

## Study Type

Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the implemented punishment rate and the contribution rate to the public good. Group or individual payoffs are not directly reported as outcome variables, but are discussed as the theoretical basis for efficiency and as the rationale for the design. Efficiency is not directly measured or reported, but can be inferred from the combination of contribution rates and the structure of the public goods game.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a public goods game with endogenous, costless, formal punishment institutions, complete punishment institutions (where all group members are covered) reliably lead to the implementation of deterrent punishment when cooperation is profitable, resulting in high contributions and, by implication, high efficiency. In contrast, incomplete punishment institutions (where only a subgroup is covered, or where subgroups can independently implement punishment) lead to less consistent implementation of deterrent punishment, lower average punishment rates, and lower contributions, even when cooperation is profitable. The marginal group return (MGR) is a strong predictor of punishment demand and contributions in complete institutions, but much less so in incomplete institutions, where individual cooperativeness and subgroup coordination become more important. Coordination failures between subgroups often prevent the realization of efficient outcomes in incomplete institutions.

## Prediction Guidance
For predicting the efficiency effect of punishment in public goods games, this paper provides strong evidence that the structure of the punishment institution (complete vs. incomplete) is a critical moderator. When punishment is costless and covers all players, enabling punishment leads to near-maximal efficiency if cooperation is profitable. When punishment is incomplete (applies only to a subgroup or is implemented separately by subgroups), enabling punishment leads to less reliable efficiency gains, with outcomes depending more on group composition and coordination. The marginal per-capita return (mpcr) is a strong predictor of efficiency gains from punishment in complete institutions, but much weaker in incomplete ones. Control efficiency (without punishment) is not directly reported, but the design implies low efficiency in the absence of punishment when cooperation is not profitable. The findings suggest that, for prediction, the presence of a complete, costless, formal punishment institution is a strong positive moderator of efficiency, while incomplete institutions introduce substantial uncertainty and lower average efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use groups of 6 players, divided into two subgroups of 3 (red and blue).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The main public goods game is played over 24 rounds, divided into 6 phases of 4 rounds each.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed; only private chat with experimenter for clarification.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are continuous from 0 to 250 tokens (not all-or-nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. MPCR is 1/3 (alpha = 1/3) in all treatments; marginal group return (MGR) is varied (0.75, 1.5, 2.5) by changing alpha.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is costless to the punisher; deductions are not redistributed.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a percentage deduction (0-100%) from the amount not contributed; effectiveness is direct and deterministic.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of rounds and phases in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, individual contributions, deductions, and payoffs of all red and blue players are displayed to all group members.

## Important Limitations
- Efficiency and group payoff are not directly reported as outcome variables; must be inferred from contribution rates and game structure.
- Punishment is costless in all treatments, which may not generalize to settings with costly punishment.
- The experiment uses only groups of 6 players and two subgroups; results may not generalize to other group sizes or more complex subgroup structures.
- No communication is allowed between players, which may limit coordination compared to real-world settings.
- The study does not report control (no-punishment) efficiency directly, so absolute efficiency gains from punishment must be inferred.
- The findings are based on a student sample from two German universities, which may limit external validity.
