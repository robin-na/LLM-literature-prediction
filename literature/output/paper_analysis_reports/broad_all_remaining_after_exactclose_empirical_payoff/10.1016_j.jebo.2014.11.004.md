# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Do buyer groups facilitate collusion?
Authors: Normann, H. T., Rösch, J., & Schultz, L. M.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2015

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- industry output (inverse proxy for collusion/efficiency)
- firm profits
- welfare (sum of consumer and producer surplus)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of exclusion
- communication content (agreements)
- buyer group participation
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is industry output, which is inversely related to collusion and directly related to welfare/efficiency. The paper also reports profits and welfare calculations. Non-payoff outcomes include exclusion behavior and communication patterns.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In repeated Cournot triopoly experiments, buyer groups that can exclude members (closed buyer groups) enable targeted punishment of high-output (deviating) firms, which facilitates collusion and reduces industry output toward the joint-profit maximizing (collusive) level. This reduces efficiency and welfare compared to baseline or open buyer groups (which cannot exclude). Communication, especially when used for explicit quantitative agreements, further increases collusion and reduces output. Open buyer groups without exclusion do not significantly differ from baseline in terms of output or efficiency. The exclusion mechanism is used mainly against high-output firms, consistent with punishment of deviations. Overall, mechanisms that enable punishment (exclusion) or communication (especially explicit agreements) reduce efficiency by facilitating collusion.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling targeted punishment (via exclusion from a buyer group) in a repeated Cournot game reduces efficiency by facilitating collusion, as measured by lower industry output and welfare. The effect is more pronounced when communication is allowed, especially with explicit quantitative agreements. In the absence of exclusion or communication, buyer groups do not significantly affect efficiency. For prediction tasks, the presence of a punishment mechanism (exclusion), communication, and the structure of the game (repeated, triopoly, continuous output) are key moderators of the efficiency impact of punishment. The findings are directly relevant for settings where punishment is implemented as exclusion or similar targeted sanctions, and less so for standard PGGs with costly punishment tokens.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use 3 players (triopoly) per group.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. At least 20 periods after 7 initial rounds; random stopping rule from period 21 onward (actual number 21-28).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Communication (chat) allowed in Talk treatments; leads to more collusion and lower efficiency.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/output choice is continuous (Cournot output in [0,50]), not all-or-nothing.
- punishment cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented as exclusion from the buyer group, which imposes a cost on the excluded (higher input cost) but does not have a direct cost to the punishers except for possible future retaliation or group dynamics.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is highly effective: exclusion raises the target's cost and reduces their profit/output. The mechanism is more severe than standard Nash reversion.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know there are at least 20 rounds after the initial 7; from round 21 onward, a random stopping rule is used (continuation probability 2/3), so the exact end is not known.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects are informed each period about buyer group participation, exclusion, quantity choices, and profits.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a Cournot triopoly, not a standard public goods game; results may not generalize to PGGs with standard costly punishment.
- Punishment is implemented as exclusion (raising costs), not as a direct costly punishment token; the mechanism differs from standard PGG punishment.
- The group size is fixed at 3; effects may differ with larger groups.
- The output choice is continuous, not binary or all-or-nothing.
- The random stopping rule means the exact number of rounds is not known in advance, which may affect strategies.
- Communication is group-wide and not private; effects of private communication are not tested.
- No reward mechanism is present, so interaction with positive incentives is not studied.
