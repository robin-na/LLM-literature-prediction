# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Mutual monitoring in multi-period teams: an experimental investigation
Authors: Nikias, A., & Sy, A.
Journal: Team Performance Management
Year: 2021

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- expected utility (payoff) for each strategy profile
- aggregate output (implied in payoff structure)
- total points earned (used for payment)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- percentage of working (cooperation) in each period
- frequency of punishment (not working in second period as punishment)
- coordination on socially efficient strategies
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: working (cooperation) rates in each period and frequency of punishment. Payoff tables are provided and used to motivate the design, but the main statistical analyses focus on working and punishment rates, not directly on group efficiency or total payoff. However, the link between working and group payoff is direct and explicit in the design.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that lowering the cost of punishment in a two-period team production game (with payoffs analogous to a prisoner's dilemma) increases both the frequency of punishment and the rate of working (cooperation) in both periods. When punishment is costless, participants are more likely to punish non-cooperators, and this threat leads to higher cooperation. The design ensures that higher working rates directly translate to higher group output and expected utility, so efficiency is higher when punishment is less costly. The effect is statistically significant for both periods. The study isolates the effect of punishment cost by holding other factors constant and using a one-shot horizon to rule out future gains from cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a two-player, two-period team production game with mutual monitoring and contingent punishment, reducing the cost of punishment increases cooperation and thus group efficiency. The design is closely related to public goods and prisoner's dilemma games, and the mapping from working to group payoff is explicit. For prediction tasks, the results suggest that lower punishment cost (punishment_cost dimension) will lead to higher efficiency when punishment is enabled, holding other design features constant. The findings are most directly applicable to small, short-horizon, mutual monitoring settings without communication or rewards.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in pairs (2 players per group).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each experimental session consists of 15 rounds, each round is a two-period game (i.e., 2 periods per round, 15 rounds total).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (working) is binary: each player chooses to work or not work in each period.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is embedded in the payoff tables: when both work, both get 80 (first period), if one works and one does not, the worker gets 20, shirker gets 90. The mapping is not a standard linear PGG but is functionally similar.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The main treatment variable: punishment is either 'costless' (punisher does not lose utility by punishing) or 'costly' (punisher incurs same cost as punished). Lower punishment cost leads to more punishment and higher cooperation/efficiency.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness per unit cost is higher in the 'costless' condition. The impact of punishment is greater when it is less costly.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know each round is a two-period game; the number of rounds (15) is known in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, actions and outcomes are displayed to both players, including total points.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is mutual and direct (not working in second period); both players know who did what.

## Important Limitations
- Small sample size (22 participants) limits statistical power and generalizability.
- Game is limited to two players and two periods per round; results may not generalize to larger groups or longer horizons.
- No direct measurement or statistical analysis of group efficiency or total payoff; efficiency is inferred from working rates and payoff structure.
- No communication or reward mechanisms; findings may not extend to settings with these features.
- Participants are graduate business students, which may limit external validity.
