## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper claims that strong reciprocity and altruistic punishment are key to human cooperation, but that their effectiveness is limited by cognitive constraints, especially as group size increases. As groups grow, the costs and logistical difficulties of monitoring and punishing free riders rise, reducing the effectiveness of informal punishment and thus limiting group size. The emergence of large-scale societies required the development of institutions that formalize and distribute the responsibility for sanctioning (the 'social division of sanction'), which in turn enabled larger, more complex societies. The paper references experimental evidence (e.g., Isaac et al. 1994; Carpenter 2007; Keser and van Winden 2000) to support claims about group size, punishment, and cooperation, but does not itself report new data or analyze efficiency directly.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical context for how punishment and cooperation interact with group size and institutional structure, but does not provide direct empirical evidence for predicting efficiency outcomes in public goods games with or without punishment. It suggests that as group size increases, the effectiveness of punishment in sustaining cooperation may decrease due to cognitive and monitoring constraints, unless institutional mechanisms are introduced. For prediction tasks, the paper supports the idea that group size and the presence or absence of formal sanctioning institutions are important moderators of punishment's effect, but does not provide quantitative or directly predictive evidence for efficiency outcomes.
