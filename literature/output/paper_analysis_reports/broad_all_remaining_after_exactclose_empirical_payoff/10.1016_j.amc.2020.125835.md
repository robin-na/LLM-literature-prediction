Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the evolution of cooperation and the prevalence of different strategies (cooperators, defectors, excluders) in a spatial public goods game with pool exclusion. It does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare directly. Outcomes are primarily behavioral (strategy frequencies, cooperation level), not payoff-based.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing pool exclusion, where excluders pay a cost to exclude defectors from the public good, can significantly increase the level of cooperation in a spatial public goods game. The effectiveness of exclusion depends on parameters such as the enhancement factor (r), the probability and effectiveness of exclusion, and the contribution rate to the exclusion pool. Cooperation is more likely to prevail when exclusion is effective and not overinvested. However, the main reported outcome is the fraction of cooperators and excluders, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the idea that exclusionary sanctions (pool exclusion) can increase cooperation in spatial public goods games, especially under certain parameter regimes. However, because the main outcome is cooperation level (not efficiency or group payoff), and because the sanction is exclusion rather than classic costly punishment, the findings should be used with caution for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in PGGs. The results suggest that effective, not overly costly exclusion can help sustain cooperation, which may translate to higher efficiency, but this is not directly demonstrated.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a square lattice of 100x100 individuals (N=10,000), with each player participating in 5 overlapping groups (von Neumann neighborhood).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 20,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCSs), with outcomes averaged over the last 1,000 steps.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The enhancement factor r is varied (e.g., r=2.5, 2.7, 2.9, 3.1, 3.2). MPCR is r/G, with G=5.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Excluders pay a cost to the exclusion pool, modeled as a function of payoff and parameter θ (contribution rate). This is not classic punishment cost, but cost of exclusion.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Effectiveness of exclusion is determined by the probability μ, which depends on exclusion cost and parameters σ (fixed probability) and τ (adjustment factor).

## Important Limitations
- The model studies pool exclusion (social exclusion) rather than classic costly punishment; results may not generalize to punishment-based PGGs.
- The main outcome is cooperation level (fraction of cooperators and excluders), not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes; implications for efficiency are indirect.
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- No communication, reward, or identification mechanisms are included, limiting generalizability to richer PGG environments.
