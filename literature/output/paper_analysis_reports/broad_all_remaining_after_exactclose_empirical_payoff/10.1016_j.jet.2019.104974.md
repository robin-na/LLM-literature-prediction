Outcome notes:
The paper's main dependent variables are network efficiency, group/individual payoffs, and the structural properties of networks that support cooperation under different monitoring and punishment regimes. Behavioral outcomes like contribution or punishment frequency are not directly modeled, but the focus is on the payoff consequences of network and punishment structure.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that punishment mechanisms can sustain efficient cooperation in networked repeated games, but the effectiveness of punishment—and thus the efficiency of equilibrium outcomes—depends critically on the monitoring structure. With perfect or partial monitoring (players can observe at least their neighbors' neighbors), punishment can be immediate and efficient networks are possible. With local (private) monitoring (players only observe their own ties), punishment is delayed and only highly clustered networks (with triadic closure) can robustly support cooperation. The paper provides formal incentive constraints (PIC, LIC, RIC) that characterize when efficient or near-efficient networks can be sustained. Efficiency is maximized when monitoring allows for rapid punishment, and is reduced when monitoring is local and punishment is delayed or less credible.

## Prediction Guidance
For the downstream prediction task, this paper provides strong theoretical guidance on how the monitoring structure (analogous to 'show_other_summaries' and 'show_punishment_id'), network clustering, and the immediacy/effectiveness of punishment affect the efficiency of public-goods-like games. If punishment is possible and monitoring is sufficiently rich (players can observe others' actions beyond their own ties), then enabling punishment is likely to increase efficiency or at least sustain cooperation. If monitoring is strictly local, only highly clustered (triadically closed) networks can sustain cooperation, and efficiency gains from punishment are limited. The paper also shows that the cost and effectiveness of punishment (punishment_cost, punishment_tech) interact with network structure to determine whether efficient outcomes are possible. The results are most directly applicable to networked repeated games with bilateral ties, but the logic extends to public goods games with networked or local interaction structures.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. The model allows for arbitrary finite player count; results are derived for general I. Some results (e.g., complete networks) become more feasible as player count increases. The effect of player count on efficiency is parameter-dependent: larger networks can support more pooling, but local monitoring makes long cycles less sustainable.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The model is an infinitely repeated game (discounted), which is essential for sustaining cooperation via punishment. Finite horizon would undermine punishment and reduce efficiency.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/cooperation is binary (cooperate or defect) on each tie in each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The benefit of cooperation per tie is normalized to 1, and the cost is c_ij; the effective 'mpcr' is 1-c_ij per tie. Higher benefit-to-cost ratios make cooperation more sustainable and efficient networks more likely.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment is endogenous: the cost is the loss of future cooperation (ties are severed after defection). The cost to the punisher is the loss of the tie; the effectiveness depends on network structure and monitoring. Lower cost/more effective punishment supports higher efficiency.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented by severing ties (ending cooperation). The immediacy and reach of punishment (how quickly and widely it can be applied) is crucial: more immediate/effective punishment (as in partial/perfect monitoring) increases efficiency.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The game is infinitely repeated (players know the game continues with discounting), which is necessary for punishment to be credible and for efficiency to be sustained.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The radius of information (monitoring structure) is a key parameter: when players can observe others' actions beyond their own ties (rho ≥ 2), punishment is more effective and efficiency is higher. When monitoring is local (rho = 1), efficiency is lower.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Players can identify who defected on their own ties, but not necessarily who punished whom beyond their own neighborhood. The ability to identify defectors (and thus punish) is more complete with higher radius of information.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not provide empirical estimates of efficiency or punishment effects.
- The setting is a networked repeated game with bilateral ties, not a standard public goods game; results are adjacent but not directly transferable to standard PGGs.
- Punishment is implemented by severing ties (ending cooperation), not by costly punishment actions as in some PGG experiments.
- No explicit reward mechanism is modeled.
- The model assumes infinite repetition (discounting); results may not generalize to finite-horizon games.
- No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- The model does not address stochastic or noisy monitoring, only deterministic local/partial/perfect monitoring.
