## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the introduction of trembles (errors) in repeated prisoner's dilemma games can support the evolutionary stability of conditional cooperation (punishment-based strategies), but only when the benefit-to-cost ratio of cooperation is not too high. Specifically, if the benefit of cooperation is less than or equal to twice the cost (b ≤ 2c), then the ALLT (all trigger) equilibrium is asymptotically stable for sufficiently high continuation probability and low error rate. If the benefit is too high (b > 2c), then the equilibrium becomes unstable, and unconditional cooperators can invade, undermining the effectiveness of punishment. The analysis is entirely theoretical, using explicit payoff calculations and stability conditions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that the effectiveness of punishment in supporting cooperation (and thus efficiency) in repeated prisoner's dilemma-like environments depends critically on the benefit-to-cost ratio of cooperation and the presence of errors. For prediction tasks, it suggests that punishment will only improve efficiency when the benefit-to-cost ratio is not too high and when errors are present but not too frequent. The results are most directly applicable to two-player repeated games with trigger strategies, but the logic may extend to public goods games with similar strategic structure. The findings highlight that increasing the benefit of cooperation does not always make punishment more effective; in fact, it can undermine stability if too large. The paper does not provide direct evidence for multi-player public goods games, but the mechanisms discussed are relevant for adjacent settings.

## Important Limitations
- The analysis is entirely theoretical and limited to two-player repeated prisoner's dilemma games; results may not generalize directly to multi-player public goods games.
- Punishment is implemented via strategy (trigger/Grim), not as a separate costly action; no explicit punishment cost parameter.
- No empirical or experimental data are provided.
- No consideration of communication, group feedback, or identification of punishers.
- The model assumes infinite or indefinite repetition with known continuation probability, which may differ from finite-round experimental designs.
