# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Normative conflict and the limits of self-governance in heterogeneous populations
Authors: Gangadharan, L., Nikiforakis, N., & Villeval, M. C.
Journal: European Economic Review
Year: 2017

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- group earnings
- individual earnings
- efficiency (earnings as % of maximum possible)
- inequality (earnings ratio, standard deviation)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution levels
- reward points assigned/received
- agreement on contribution rules
- normative preferences (survey responses)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group and individual earnings (efficiency), with detailed analysis of contributions and reward behavior. No punishment mechanism is present; only rewards are available.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Allowing communication and rewards in a public goods game leads to near-maximal efficiency in homogeneous groups, but only partial efficiency in heterogeneous groups where returns from the public good differ. In homogeneous groups, communication increases group earnings to 94% of the maximum, while in heterogeneous groups, the increase is only 80% of the maximum. The main reason is that most heterogeneous groups prioritize equality or compromise between equality and efficiency, rather than maximizing group earnings. Rewards are not systematically used to offset inequality. The loss in efficiency in heterogeneous groups is due both to lower contributions and to under-use of rewards. The study demonstrates that normative conflict can limit the effectiveness of self-governance mechanisms in promoting efficiency in heterogeneous populations.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the efficiency gains from enabling communication and rewards in public goods games depend critically on group homogeneity. In homogeneous groups, such mechanisms can achieve near-maximal efficiency. In heterogeneous groups, even with communication and rewards, efficiency gains are much smaller due to normative conflict between equality and efficiency. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the presence of heterogeneity in returns (mpcr differences) is a strong moderator that limits the efficiency benefit of enabling communication and rewards. The absence of punishment means the results are most directly applicable to reward-based or communication-based interventions, but the findings on normative conflict are likely relevant for predicting the limits of punishment as well, especially in heterogeneous settings.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 6 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 periods (rounds) per session.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Communication (chat) is a treatment variable: present in HOM-C and HET-C, absent in HOM-NC and HET-NC. Communication increases efficiency, but the effect is much smaller in heterogeneous groups.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: subjects allocate any amount (0-20 ECU) to the public account.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Homogeneous: all players have mpcr=0.3. Heterogeneous: half have mpcr=0.4, half have mpcr=0.2. Average mpcr is 0.3 in all treatments.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward mechanism (costly, 1:3 technology) is present in all treatments. Rewards increase efficiency, especially with communication.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward cost: 0.5 ECU per point assigned; recipient gains 1.5 ECU per point.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward technology: 1:3 (sender pays 0.5 ECU, receiver gets 1.5 ECU per point).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Subjects are told the game has 20 periods; endgame effects are discussed.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, subjects see each group member's contribution, reward points received from each member, identification number, and return rate.

## Important Limitations
- No punishment mechanism is present; only rewards are studied, so direct implications for punishment are limited.
- The study focuses on communication and reward mechanisms; findings may not generalize to settings with punishment or other institutional features.
- Heterogeneity is operationalized as differences in returns from the public good, not endowments or other forms of heterogeneity.
- All groups are of fixed size (6 players) and play for 20 rounds; results may differ with other group sizes or time horizons.
- Laboratory setting with student subjects may limit external validity.
- The reward mechanism is capped and has a specific 1:3 cost/benefit ratio, which may affect generalizability to other reward technologies.
