Outcome notes:
The paper reports on the frequency of cooperation (f_c) as the main outcome, which is a behavioral measure. There is no direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, welfare, or surplus. Payoff is used as an individual-level variable for updating strategies, but not as a reported group outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that periodic update rules with Q-learning and a punishment mechanism can substantially increase the frequency of cooperation in a spatial prisoner's dilemma game. Lower punishment costs and shorter update intervals are especially effective at promoting cooperation. The model is robust across different network structures. However, the main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not group efficiency or total payoff, and the results are based on agent-based simulations rather than empirical experiments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that punishment mechanisms, when combined with learning and periodic update rules, can increase cooperation rates in spatial games. However, because the main outcome is cooperation frequency rather than efficiency or group payoff, the findings should be used with caution for predicting efficiency effects. The results suggest that lower punishment costs and more frequent updating are likely to make punishment more effective at promoting cooperation, which may translate to higher efficiency in related models, but this is not directly demonstrated.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a square lattice of size L x L, with L=100 or L=200 in examples (i.e., 10,000 or 40,000 players). Player count is fixed per simulation.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 100,000 time steps (rounds), with outcomes averaged over the last 5,000 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: agents choose to cooperate (C) or defect (D) in each round.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost (rho) is a key parameter; lower punishment cost increases cooperation frequency, higher cost suppresses cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment impact per unit cost is set by parameter beta (punishment magnitude); beta=0.8 in most simulations.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- The main outcome is cooperation frequency, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- The game is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game or direct variant.
- No direct analysis of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes is provided.
- Findings may not generalize to non-spatial or non-binary contribution games.
- No human or field data; all results are from agent-based simulations.
