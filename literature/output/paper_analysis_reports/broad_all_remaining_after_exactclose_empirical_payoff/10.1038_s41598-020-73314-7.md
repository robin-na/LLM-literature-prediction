# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Heterogeneous groups cooperate in public good problems despite normative disagreements about individual contribution levels
Authors: Otten, K., Buskens, V., Przepiorka, W., & Ellemers, N.
Journal: Scientific Reports
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution level
- punishment assigned
- punishment received
- normative views
- normative expectations
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is contribution level (i.e., cooperation), not efficiency or group payoff. Punishment behavior and normative views are also analyzed. The paper does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare as a primary outcome, though it discusses these concepts in interpretation.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that, in a linear public goods game with peer punishment and heterogeneous returns, experimentally induced normative disagreement within groups does not reduce cooperation (measured by average contributions) or increase punishment. Instead, the average normative view within a group predicts the group's contribution level. The study does not directly report efficiency or group payoff, but notes that joint payoffs are maximized at full contribution, and that observed contribution levels are lower than this maximum, consistent with underprovision. The presence of punishment maintains cooperation at moderate levels, but does not eliminate the effect of group-mean norms. The study does not include a no-punishment control, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be directly assessed.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides high-quality evidence on the effect of normative disagreement in a linear PGG with peer punishment, but does not directly report efficiency or group payoff, nor does it include a no-punishment control. For prediction tasks, it shows that, in a 3-player, 10-round, continuous-contribution PGG with punishment (cost 1, impact 3), with full information about others' contributions and payoffs but anonymous punishment, group-mean normative views are a strong predictor of contribution levels. The presence of punishment maintains moderate cooperation, but the paper does not allow direct estimation of the efficiency gain from enabling punishment versus disabling it. The results suggest that, under these parameters, punishment supports cooperation even in the presence of normative disagreement, but the efficiency effect relative to no-punishment is not measured.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 3 players per game, fixed for 10 rounds.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 10 rounds per group in the analyzed part of the experiment.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed; participants were in individual cubicles and could not communicate.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is continuous: 0-20 MUs per round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is 0.75 for one player and 0.5 for the other two; total multiplier is 1.75.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point costs 1 MU to the punisher.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the punished player's payoff by 3 MUs.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism; only punishment is available.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants knew there would be 10 rounds in the first part.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, contributions and payoffs of each player are communicated to all group members.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; individuals do not see who punished them.

## Important Limitations
- No no-punishment control: cannot estimate the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency or payoff.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings are not directly reported as outcomes; only contribution levels are analyzed.
- Sample is university students in the Netherlands; generalizability may be limited.
- Only one level of heterogeneity (returns 0.75 vs 0.5) is tested; effects may differ with greater heterogeneity.
- Participants are not informed about others' normative views or group formation method, which may limit the salience of disagreement.
- Reward mechanisms are not studied; only punishment is available.
