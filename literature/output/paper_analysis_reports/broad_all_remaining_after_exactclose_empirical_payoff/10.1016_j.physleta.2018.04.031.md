## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:

Outcome notes:
The main reported outcome is the average cooperation level (mean investment), not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. Payoff is defined in the model, but simulation results focus on cooperation rates.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that both self-punishment (modeled as a fitness penalty for contributing less than peers) and peer punishment (costly punishment of low contributors by others) substantially increase the average cooperation level in a continuous spatial PGG. Lower tolerance for low contributions and higher punishment intensity both promote higher cooperation. The effect is robust across a range of memory and imitation parameters. However, the paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, or welfare, so it is unclear whether the increased cooperation translates into higher group efficiency, especially given the costs of punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that both self-punishment and peer punishment mechanisms can increase average cooperation rates in continuous spatial PGGs, especially when tolerance is low and punishment intensity is high. However, because the main outcome is cooperation rate rather than efficiency or group payoff, the results should be used with caution for predicting efficiency effects. The cost of punishment is modeled and subtracted from individual fitness, but the aggregate effect on group payoff or efficiency is not reported. Thus, while the paper supports the idea that punishment increases cooperation, it does not directly inform whether punishment increases or decreases efficiency in these settings.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a 100x100 lattice (N=10,000 agents), all with the same degree (homogeneous network).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 50,000+ generations, with outcomes averaged over the last 500 generations.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous between 0 and 1, not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Investment amplification factor r is varied (1 < r < group size). MPCR = r/(group size).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Peer punishment cost per act is parameter alpha (e.g., alpha=0.1). Self-punishment is a fitness penalty (gamma).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment impact per act is parameter beta (peer punishment) or gamma (self-punishment).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Agents can observe neighbors' strategies and fitness for imitation and memory, but not explicit summaries.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is average cooperation level, not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare.
- The cost of punishment is modeled and subtracted from individual fitness, but aggregate group payoff or efficiency is not reported.
- Results are from agent-based simulations, not empirical experiments or real human subjects.
- The model uses a large, regular lattice (N=10,000), which may not generalize to smaller or more heterogeneous groups.
- No direct analysis of the tradeoff between increased cooperation and punishment costs in terms of efficiency.
