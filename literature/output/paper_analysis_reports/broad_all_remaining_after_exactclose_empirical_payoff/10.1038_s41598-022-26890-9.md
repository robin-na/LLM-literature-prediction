## Outcomes Measured

Non-payoff outcomes:

Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the evolution of cooperation rate and strategy stability in a spatial prisoner's dilemma model with memory and weighted historical strategy information. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is reported. Outcomes are behavioral (cooperation, strategy retention) rather than payoff-based.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that increasing the length of historical memory (M) and the weight of memory information (β) in a spatial prisoner's dilemma model leads to higher cooperation rates and more stable cooperative clusters. The effect saturates at high memory lengths, and excessive memory does not further promote cooperation. The model does not include explicit punishment or reward mechanisms, and all outcomes are reported in terms of cooperation rate and strategy stability, not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or their close variants. It models a spatial prisoner's dilemma with memory-based strategy updating, focusing on how memory and its weight affect cooperation rates. There is no manipulation or analysis of punishment, reward, or efficiency outcomes. Thus, its relevance to the downstream prediction task is limited to providing indirect, adjacent evidence that memory and historical information can promote cooperation, but it does not inform the effect of punishment on efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run on a 200x200 grid (N=40,000), with each player having 4 neighbors. The player count is fixed and not varied for effect analysis.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 10,000 iterations to reach stability. Number of rounds is fixed for all runs.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Strategy choice is binary: cooperate (C) or defect (D).

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game or direct variant.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanisms are included; the study focuses on memory and historical strategy information.
- All outcomes are behavioral (cooperation rate, strategy stability), with no analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- Findings are based on numerical simulations, not empirical or experimental data.
- Results may not generalize to public goods games or to settings with explicit punishment/reward mechanisms.
