## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that in a dynamic conflict game with opportunities for punishment-like behavior (stealing more than equilibrium), most players engage in mutually destructive cycles, resulting in very low payoffs for both themselves and their co-players. Only a small minority (Meeks) avoid this pattern and achieve higher payoffs. The study identifies four behavioral types and finds that the majority (Sharks) consistently play greedily, while others (Educators, Gamesmen) respond to greed with punishment or equilibrium play. The prevalence of punishment and counter-punishment leads to highly inefficient outcomes, with over 70% of games ending with both players having minimal expected payoffs. The findings are robust across treatments and are related to similar behavioral types in public goods and repeated prisoner's dilemma games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that in dynamic conflict games structurally similar to public goods games, the availability and use of punishment-like actions (stealing more than equilibrium) leads to lower efficiency and group payoffs, as most players engage in mutually destructive cycles. The findings suggest that when punishment is possible and frequently used, efficiency is likely to decrease relative to the cooperative equilibrium. For prediction tasks, this implies that enabling punishment in similar dynamic games may reduce efficiency, especially if the punishment mechanism is easily accessible and not costly enough to deter overuse. The behavioral heterogeneity observed (with only a small minority avoiding destructive cycles) should be considered when predicting treatment efficiency from game design dimensions.

## Important Limitations
- The Vendetta Game is not a standard public goods game; it is a dynamic two-player conflict game with alternating moves and a different payoff structure, so direct generalization to standard PGGs may be limited.
- Efficiency is not directly measured as a ratio to a fully cooperative benchmark, but as average payoffs and frequency of minimal-payoff outcomes.
- Punishment is not implemented as a separate stage or explicit sanctioning mechanism, but as stealing more than equilibrium in the main game dynamic.
- The number of rounds is endogenous and varies across games, which may affect comparability to fixed-round PGGs.
- No reward mechanism is present, so findings are limited to punishment-like dynamics.
- Behavioral types are inferred via cluster analysis, which may be sensitive to methodological choices.
