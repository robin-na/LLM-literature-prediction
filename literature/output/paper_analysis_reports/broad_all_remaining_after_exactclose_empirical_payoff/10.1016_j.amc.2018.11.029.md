The main reported outcome is the stationary fraction of cooperators (cooperation rate). The paper discusses group benefit and resource levels in spatial patterns, but does not report group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings as a primary outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of central governance based on monitoring and reporting, with top-down punishment of defectors, can dramatically increase the fraction of cooperators in a spatial collective-risk social dilemma. There are threshold effects: small increases in monitoring probability, punishment fine, or decreases in reporting cost can cause a sudden jump from low to high cooperation. The effect is stronger under harsh conditions (low synergy factor). However, the study focuses on cooperation rates, not on efficiency or group payoff, so direct implications for efficiency are not reported.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that top-down punishment based on monitoring and reporting can increase cooperation rates in spatial collective-risk dilemmas. However, because the main outcome is the fraction of cooperators rather than efficiency or group payoff, the results should be used with caution for predicting efficiency. The findings suggest that, in similar spatial PGG-like environments, enabling punishment via monitoring/reporting is likely to increase cooperation, which may translate to higher efficiency if contributions are productive, but the paper does not quantify this. The model's details (e.g., spatial structure, overlapping groups, no explicit cost of punishment to individuals) should be considered when generalizing.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group has 5 players (G=5), but the population is a large LxL lattice (L=100 to 2000), with overlapping groups.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for many time steps until a stationary state is reached; not a fixed number of rounds as in lab experiments.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperators contribute a fixed amount c=1, defectors contribute nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor (alpha) is analogous to MPCR; varied in simulations (e.g., alpha=3, 5, 15).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost is not paid by individuals but by the institution; the fine (lambda) is imposed on defectors. Reporting cost (epsilon) is paid by cooperators who report.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the fine (lambda); higher fines increase cooperation rates.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency or group payoff; direct effects on efficiency are not reported.
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- Punishment costs are not borne by individuals but by an external institution, which may not generalize to settings where punishment is costly for group members.
- Spatial structure and overlapping groups may limit generalizability to well-mixed or non-spatial environments.
- No explicit analysis of group welfare, surplus, or total earnings.
