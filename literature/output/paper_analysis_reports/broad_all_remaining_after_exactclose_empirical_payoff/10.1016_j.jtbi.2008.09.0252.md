## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The presence of empty sites in a lattice-structured population (spatial game) can promote the evolution of cooperation and does not reduce the effect of costly punishment, especially when using a score-dependent viability updating rule. In well-mixed populations, empty sites do not promote cooperation by punishment. The evolutionary dynamics with empty sites resemble those of a fertility (death-birth) model. The effect of punishment depends on both the cost of being punished and the benefit from cooperation in the presence of empty sites. The paper does not report efficiency or payoff outcomes, but rather which strategies dominate under different conditions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for how spatial structure, population density (empty sites), and updating rules may moderate the effect of punishment on cooperation. However, it does not report efficiency or payoff outcomes, so it cannot be used directly to predict efficiency changes due to punishment. It is most useful for understanding mechanisms and for contextualizing the role of spatial structure and population density in evolutionary games with punishment. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper is only indirectly informative.

## Important Limitations

- Outcomes are reported as evolutionary success of strategies, not as group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff.
- The model is not a standard public goods game but an evolutionary game with four strategy types and spatial structure.
- No explicit mapping to marginal per-capita return (mpcr) or standard PGG payoff structure.
- No direct analysis of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes; results are not directly transferable to efficiency prediction tasks.
