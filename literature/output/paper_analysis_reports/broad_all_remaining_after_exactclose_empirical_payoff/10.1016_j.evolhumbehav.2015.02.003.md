## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The main findings are: (1) In populations of SLA agents without memory, those with low penalty sensitivity dominate, leading to high defection rates and low average payoffs. (2) SLA strategies are not stable against invasion by TFT or ALLD; TFT can dominate and lead to higher population payoffs. (3) Adding memory to SLA agents enables them to learn to cooperate with TFT, increasing cooperation and average payoffs, but this effect is limited to interactions with TFT; among themselves, SLA agents still defect. (4) The penalty parameter (analogous to punishment sensitivity) is a key moderator: higher penalty sensitivity leads to more cooperation and higher payoffs, but is outcompeted by less sensitive types in evolutionary dynamics. (5) The model predicts that, absent memory or external cooperative strategies, populations of simple learning agents will evolve toward defection and low efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical and simulation evidence that, in repeated Prisoner's Dilemma environments without explicit punishment mechanisms, agents with low sensitivity to penalties (i.e., low punishment cost or low punishment effectiveness) evolve toward defection and low efficiency. The findings suggest that, in analogous public goods games, the presence and effectiveness of punishment (or penalty sensitivity) are critical for sustaining cooperation and high efficiency. However, the model does not include explicit peer punishment as a game design feature, so direct quantitative predictions for the effect of enabling punishment in public goods games are limited. The results do support the qualitative prediction that increasing the effectiveness or salience of punishment (or penalty) responses can increase efficiency, but such strategies may be evolutionarily unstable without additional mechanisms (e.g., memory, reputation, or external enforcement).

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model analyzes both two-player (dyadic) interactions and large populations (effectively infinite, well-mixed). Most results are for pairwise repeated games, but population dynamics are also modeled.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are repeated for a large (effectively infinite) number of rounds per generation, allowing equilibrium to be reached. Memory length (n) is also varied in some models.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Action space is binary: cooperate (C) or defect (D), i.e., all-or-nothing choices.

## Important Limitations
- The model is based on the repeated Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- There is no explicit peer punishment or sanctioning mechanism; the penalty parameter is a learning sensitivity, not a cost paid by a punisher.
- All results are theoretical or simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are presented.
- The model assumes large (effectively infinite) populations and rounds, which may not reflect real-world or experimental settings.
- Memory-based cooperation requires large memory and/or a training species (e.g., TFT); such mechanisms may be unrealistic or rare in nature.
- No analysis of the effect of enabling or disabling explicit punishment as a game design feature.
