# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Self-Policing Through Norm Internalization: A Cognitive Solution to the Tragedy of the Digital Commons in Social Networks
Authors: Villatoro, D., Andrighetto, G., Conte, R., & Sabater-Mir, J.
Journal: Jasss-the Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation
Year: 2015

## Study Type
theory; empirical subtype=N/A; experimental subtype=N/A

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- number of successful transactions
- cooperation rate (proxied by norm compliance and successful service sharing)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- norm internalization
- norm salience
- adaptation to environmental change
- proportion of agents internalizing each norm
- flexibility in norm compliance
- emergence and coexistence of norms
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the number of successful transactions (service exchanges meeting quality requirements), which is a proxy for cooperation but not a direct measure of group efficiency as defined in public goods games. The paper does not report group payoff, total welfare, or efficiency ratios. Outcomes focus on norm internalization, norm salience, and adaptation, with some attention to cooperation and successful service provision.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that agents capable of norm internalization (EMIL-I-A) achieve higher levels of cooperation (as measured by successful service transactions) than purely self-interested agents (IUMA) in a digital commons scenario. Internalizers flexibly adapt to environmental changes, internalizing and de-internalizing norms as conditions shift. The presence of punishment and sanction mechanisms supports norm salience and internalization, but the main driver of improved cooperation is internalization rather than external punishment alone. The model demonstrates that norm internalization can enable self-policing and robust cooperation in dynamic, decentralized environments. However, the outcomes are not reported in terms of group efficiency or total payoff as in standard public goods games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that the presence of punishment and especially norm internalization mechanisms can increase cooperation and successful collective action in social dilemma-like environments. However, because the scenario is not a standard public goods game and does not report efficiency or group payoff, the findings are only adjacent for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in PGGs. The results suggest that when agents internalize norms (potentially supported by punishment/sanction), cooperation is more robust and adaptive, but the direct effect of punishment on efficiency is not isolated or quantified. The model's focus on norm salience and internalization may inform predictions about environments where norm-based motivations are strong, but it does not provide direct quantitative guidance for the efficiency impact of punishment in PGGs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use 50 agents (Section 5.43, 5.57), and one experiment uses 60 agents (Section 5.66). Player count is fixed in each run but varied across some experiments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 20,000 time steps (Section 5.24).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost is fixed at 1:4 (punisher pays 1, target loses 4) (Section 5.38). The cost to the punisher is 1 unit per punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 1:4 (punisher pays 1, target loses 4) (Section 5.38).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents observe the actions (compliance, violation, punishment, sanction) of their direct neighbors in the social network (Section 5.24, 5.41).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment and sanction are observed by neighbors; agents know who punished whom (Section 4.11, Table 1, Section 5.24).

## Important Limitations
- The scenario is not a standard public goods game; it is a digital commons/service sharing environment with different structure and payoff logic.
- Outcomes are not reported as group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare; the main outcome is the number of successful transactions, which is only a proxy for cooperation.
- The effect of punishment on efficiency is not isolated; the main focus is on norm internalization and salience.
- The model is entirely simulation-based and does not include empirical (human subject) data.
- Parameter values (e.g., punishment cost, network structure) are fixed by design and may not generalize to other settings.
- No analysis of marginal per-capita return or direct mapping to PGG parameters.
- No reward mechanism is present, so results do not inform about reward effects.
