Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are resource sustainability and the stability of equilibria with high cooperation and non-depleted resources, which are direct analogs to efficiency and group payoff. The model tracks both the fraction of cooperators and the resource level, with explicit analysis of when the system achieves full cooperation and sustainable resources.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a feedback-evolving common resource game (a close variant of the public goods game), the introduction of punishment (or a hybrid of reward and punishment) by a third-party institution significantly increases the likelihood of achieving both full cooperation and sustainable resource levels, which are direct analogs to high efficiency. Pure reward is less effective than punishment or hybrid schemes, especially when the resource growth rate is moderate. Increasing the per capita incentive (tax) and allocating more of the incentive budget to punishment (lower w) both make the ideal outcome (full cooperation and resource sustainability) more likely. When the resource growth rate is too low, no incentive scheme is effective. The results are robust across a range of parameter values and are supported by both analytical and numerical phase diagrams.

## Prediction Guidance
For predicting the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency in public-goods-like games, this paper provides strong theoretical evidence that, in environments where resource renewal is not too slow, adding punishment (or a hybrid of reward and punishment) increases the likelihood of achieving high efficiency (full cooperation and resource sustainability) compared to reward alone or no incentives. The effect is stronger when the per capita incentive is higher and when more of the incentive budget is allocated to punishment. The model is deterministic and infinite-population, but the qualitative insights about the superiority of punishment (or hybrid) over pure reward for efficiency are likely to generalize to similar institutional public goods settings. The paper does not address stochasticity, finite groups, or experimental data, but its results are directly relevant for theoretical prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is for an infinite well-mixed population, but the game is parameterized by group size N (e.g., N=1000 in most figures, N=10 in some). The effect of N is not a focus, but its value is always specified.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: individuals choose between cooperation (rule-abiding extraction) or defection (overexploitation).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is implicit in the resource extraction and renewal equations; b_m and resource growth rate r play analogous roles.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented via a tax; the cost to punish is the same as the fine imposed on each defector (punishment intensity 1). The per capita incentive (tax) delta is varied.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 1: each unit of tax allocated to punishment is used as a fine of equal size. This is fixed in the model.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is present in all model variants; the allocation between reward and punishment is controlled by parameter w.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is funded from the tax; each unit of tax allocated to reward is distributed to cooperators. Cost per unit reward is 1.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is 1: each unit of tax allocated to reward is distributed as a reward of equal size. This is fixed in the model.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and uses infinite well-mixed populations; results may not generalize to finite or structured populations.
- No empirical or experimental data are provided.
- The model assumes binary choices (cooperate/defect) and does not allow for continuous contribution levels.
- Resource growth rate is exogenous and not affected by individual behavior beyond extraction.
- No stochasticity or noise is modeled.
- No communication, reputation, or identification of punishers/rewarders is included.
- The model does not address the effect of group size N in detail, though it is parameterized.
