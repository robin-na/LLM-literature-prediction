## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing a modified pool punishment mechanism and aspiration-driven updating in a multi-heterogeneity public goods game on a lattice can promote cooperation under certain conditions. Specifically, moderate punishment and low aspiration factor are most effective at increasing the fraction of cooperators. Too high punishment or aspiration factor leads to instability or loss of cooperation. The results are robust to different lattice sizes and initial conditions. While average payoffs for cooperators and defectors are reported, the main focus is on cooperation rates, not group efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the prediction task by showing that punishment mechanisms can increase cooperation rates in public goods games with multiple heterogeneities, but only under moderate punishment and low aspiration factor. However, because the main outcome is cooperation rate and not group efficiency or total payoff, the findings should be used with caution for predicting efficiency. The results suggest that the effect of punishment on efficiency may be non-monotonic: moderate punishment may improve outcomes, but excessive punishment can reduce both cooperation and payoffs. The lattice structure and aspiration-driven updating are important contextual factors.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a 100x100 lattice (10,000 players), with robustness checks at 80x80 and 200x200. The player count is fixed in each simulation.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each simulation runs for 10,000 rounds, with cooperation rate measured over the last 1,000 rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between cooperation (invest) and defection (no invest); investment amount for cooperators is adaptive but binary at the strategy level.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Enhancement factor r (1 < r < 5) is varied; higher r increases cooperation rate.

- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment impact per unit cost is determined by omega and group composition; moderate effectiveness is optimal for cooperation.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. The number of rounds is fixed and known in simulation, but there is no explicit discussion of player knowledge about the game horizon.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not group efficiency or total payoff; efficiency is not systematically analyzed.
- The model is simulation-based and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- Payoff outcomes are reported at the individual level (cooperators and defectors), not at the group or population level.
- The lattice structure and aspiration-driven updating may limit generalizability to other game structures or updating rules.
- No reward or positive incentive mechanisms are considered.
