Title: The evolution of cooperation in spatial public goods games under a risk-transfer mechanism
Authors: Ding, R., Wang, X. J., Zhao, J. H., Gu, C. L., & Wang, T.
Journal: Chaos Solitons & Fractals
Year: 2023

theory; empirical subtype=N/A; experimental subtype=N/A

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average group payoff
- stationary fractions of cooperators and defectors
- phase diagrams of cooperation level as a function of model parameters
Non-payoff outcomes:
- strategy frequencies (CC, CD, TC, TD)
- spatial patterns of strategies
- dynamics of cluster formation and invasion
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the stationary fractions of each strategy (coalitional cooperator, coalitional defector, traditional cooperator, traditional defector) and the resulting group-level cooperation and payoff. The paper explicitly analyzes how these relate to total group payoff and the efficiency of cooperation, using phase diagrams and time series. No experimental or observational data are reported; all results are from agent-based simulations of the spatial PGG model.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that introducing a risk-transfer (coalition) mechanism into a spatial public goods game can enhance or reduce cooperation and group efficiency, depending on the synergy coefficient (r) and the coalition closeness (alpha). In two-strategy systems, coalitional cooperators are more competitive than traditional cooperators, and coalitional defectors are less competitive than traditional defectors. In four-strategy systems, increasing coalition closeness (alpha) generally increases cooperation, but can also lead to non-monotonic effects where too strong coalitions reduce cooperation due to the breakdown of indirect territorial competition. The mechanism acts as a wealth redistribution system, strengthening cooperators at the boundary of clusters but sometimes making them vulnerable to invasion by other types. The model does not include punishment or reward, but the coalition mechanism is compared to these in the discussion.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not directly inform the prediction of punishment effects on efficiency in PGGs, as it does not include a punishment or sanctioning mechanism. However, it provides informative theoretical evidence about how payoff-redistribution mechanisms (such as coalitions or risk-sharing) can affect group efficiency and cooperation in spatial PGGs. The findings suggest that mechanisms which redistribute payoffs among players can have complex, sometimes non-monotonic effects on efficiency, depending on the structure of the game (e.g., synergy coefficient, spatial structure, coalition closeness). For prediction tasks, this paper suggests that the effect of any payoff-redistribution mechanism (including punishment, if it were present) may depend strongly on the details of the mechanism and the spatial or network structure. However, direct quantitative prediction of punishment effects is not possible from this paper.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a square lattice of size L=200, so each simulation has 40,000 players. Each player interacts with its four first-order neighbors in overlapping five-person PGGs.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run for up to 10^8 rounds to reach stationary states. Snapshots and time series are shown at various time points.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: players either invest a cost c or not (cooperate or defect).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is r/5, where r is the synergy coefficient (varied in simulations).

## Important Limitations
- The model is entirely theoretical and simulation-based; no experimental or observational data are provided.
- No punishment or reward mechanism is present; the coalition mechanism is a form of risk-sharing/wealth redistribution, not sanctioning.
- The spatial structure is fixed (square lattice); results may not generalize to well-mixed or other network structures.
- Only binary contribution choices (cooperate/defect) are modeled; no continuous contribution levels.
- Coalitions are only between immediate neighbors and are symmetric; real-world coalitions may be more complex.
- The model does not address how the coalition mechanism would interact with punishment or reward if both were present.
