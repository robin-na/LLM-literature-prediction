# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Excludability: A laboratory study on forced ranking in team production
Authors: Croson, R., Fatas, E., Neugebauer, T., & Morales, A. J.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2015

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- efficiency (team payoff / max possible payoff)
- team production (output as % of max possible)
- earnings
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate
- reaction to exclusion (behavioral adjustment)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are efficiency (payoff-based), team production, and contribution rates. Behavioral responses to exclusion are also analyzed, but efficiency is a primary focus.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that excludability (the ability to exclude the lowest contributor from group benefits) dramatically increases both contributions and efficiency in linear public goods (VCM) and best-shot (BSM) games, but not in weakest-link (WLM) games. In the VCM, efficiency rises from 65% (baseline) to 83% (excludability) and 99% (excludability with redistribution). In the BSM, efficiency rises from 81% (baseline) to 86% (excludability), but redistribution reduces efficiency to 75%. In the WLM, excludability alone does not improve efficiency (52% in both baseline and excludability), but redistribution increases efficiency to 72%. Thus, the effect of excludability on efficiency is highly dependent on the production function, and redistribution is necessary for efficiency gains in environments with strong complementarities (WLM).

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment-like exclusion mechanisms on efficiency depends critically on the production function. In standard linear public goods games (VCM), enabling exclusion of the lowest contributor increases efficiency substantially, and redistribution of the excluded share further boosts efficiency. In weakest-link games, exclusion alone does not improve efficiency, but redistribution is necessary for efficiency gains. In best-shot games, exclusion increases efficiency, but redistribution actually reduces it. For prediction tasks, the production function (average, minimum, or maximum) is a key moderator of the efficiency effect of punishment/exclusion. Control efficiency is a strong predictor, but the treatment effect of exclusion is highly context-dependent.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games use teams of 4 players (N=4).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consists of 10 rounds plus a surprise restart (another 10 rounds), for a total of 20 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players was allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are continuous (not all-or-nothing); players allocate endowment between private and group project.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. In VCM, MPCR = 0.5 (each unit contributed yields 0.5 to each of 4 players, as multiplier is 2).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented as exclusion of the lowest contributor from group benefits; effectiveness is high in VCM and BSM, low in WLM unless redistribution is added.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of rounds in advance (10 rounds, then a surprise restart).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, players see all individual contributions in increasing order (anonymized), and their own earnings (total and by source).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Exclusion is determined by lowest contribution, but individual contributions are not identified; only the ordering is shown, not the identity.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses only symmetric settings (identical endowments and payoffs); results may not generalize to heterogeneous groups.
- Excludability is implemented as an automatic rule, not as a costly or voluntary punishment action.
- No explicit punishment or reward costs are present; exclusion is not player-initiated.
- The experiment is conducted in a lab with student subjects, which may limit external validity.
- The effect of excludability may differ in settings with communication, repeated interactions with changing partners, or more complex group structures.
