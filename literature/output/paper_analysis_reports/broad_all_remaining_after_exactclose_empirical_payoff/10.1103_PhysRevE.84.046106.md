Outcome notes:
The paper reports on the stationary frequencies of strategies (D, C, E, O) and phase transitions between different strategy compositions. It does not directly report group payoff, efficiency, or welfare, but discusses the elimination of defectors and the maintenance of cooperation as proxies for resolving the 'tragedy of the commons'.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in spatial public goods games with both peer and pool punishment, the prevailing strategy depends sensitively on the synergy factor (MPCR analog), punishment cost, and fine. Peer punishers often dominate and are more effective at eliminating defectors, especially at lower synergy factors where cooperation would otherwise collapse. However, when both punishment types are present, their competition can sometimes undermine the effectiveness of punishment, leading to the reemergence of defectors. The paper provides detailed phase diagrams mapping out the conditions under which each strategy or combination of strategies prevails. The findings are based on the frequencies of strategies and do not directly quantify group efficiency or payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence about how the presence and type of punishment (peer vs pool), as well as the synergy factor (MPCR), punishment cost, and fine, affect the prevalence of cooperation and defection in spatial PGGs. However, because the outcomes are reported as strategy frequencies and not as group efficiency or payoff, the results must be interpreted as indirect evidence for efficiency. The elimination of defectors and the dominance of cooperators or punishers can be taken as a proxy for higher efficiency, but the paper does not provide quantitative efficiency values. The detailed mapping of parameter regimes is useful for predicting when punishment will be effective, but direct prediction of efficiency outcomes requires caution.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 5 players (G=5), arranged on a square lattice with overlapping groups. Each player participates in 5 groups.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for a large number of Monte Carlo steps (MCS) until stationary state is reached; not a fixed number of discrete rounds as in human experiments.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: players either contribute a fixed amount (1) or nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Synergy factor r (analogous to MPCR) is varied (r=2, 3.5, 3.8). Higher r supports cooperation; lower r makes cooperation harder. The effect of punishment depends on r.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost (gamma) is varied for both peer and pool punishment. The cost structure differs between peer and pool punishers. The effect of punishment depends on cost.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (fine per cost) is varied via the fine parameter (beta) and the cost parameter (gamma), and by comparing peer vs pool punishment. The impact of punishment on defectors and the resulting strategy frequencies are mapped.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare directly; all results are in terms of strategy frequencies and phase diagrams.
- Findings are based on agent-based simulations, not on human experiments or real-world data.
- The model assumes spatial structure (square lattice) and overlapping groups, which may not generalize to well-mixed or other network structures.
- No reward mechanisms or communication are included.
- Interpretation of efficiency or payoff outcomes must be inferred indirectly from the prevalence of cooperation and defection.
