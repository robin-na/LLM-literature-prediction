The main dependent variables are expected payoffs and the stability of cooperative states, which are directly related to efficiency and welfare. The model tracks the replicator dynamics of strategies and analyzes when cooperation (paying for costly assessment) is stable.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in indirect reciprocity models with costly assessment, cooperation is not stable unless there is a mechanism to sanction or exclude those who evade the cost of assessment. By introducing a pre-assessment stage that labels non-payers as 'nasty' and excludes them from receiving help, the model shows that a cooperative equilibrium (where everyone pays for assessment and helps others) can be locally stable if the benefit of helping exceeds the cost of assessment. This mechanism is analogous to pool punishment in public goods games and is effective under leading social norms. The findings are theoretical but directly address efficiency and the conditions for stable cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support for the idea that institutional mechanisms to sanction or exclude cost-evaders (such as pre-assessment or pool punishment) can significantly improve efficiency in social dilemma games with costly monitoring or punishment. For prediction tasks, the presence of a pre-assessment or institutionalized punishment mechanism is likely to shift efficiency from low (collapse of cooperation) to high (stable cooperation), provided the net benefit of cooperation is positive. The results are robust across different social norms and suggest that the design of the punishment/sanctioning mechanism is a key moderator of efficiency outcomes.

- punishment cost: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Assessment cost k is analogous to punishment cost; paying discriminators incur cost k per round.
- punishment technology: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Punishment is implemented via exclusion (labeling as 'nasty' and denying help); effectiveness is determined by the assessment and exclusion mechanism.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Players have access to a shared list of image scores (reputation) for all individuals, provided by the assessment system.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is entirely theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental evidence.
- The model is based on indirect reciprocity (giving game) rather than a standard public goods game, so direct quantitative transfer to PGGs may be limited.
- Population is assumed infinite and well-mixed; structured populations or finite groups may yield different results.
- The model assumes perfect assessment in the pre-assessment stage; real-world errors may reduce effectiveness.
- No explicit modeling of communication, reward, or other institutional features beyond pre-assessment.
