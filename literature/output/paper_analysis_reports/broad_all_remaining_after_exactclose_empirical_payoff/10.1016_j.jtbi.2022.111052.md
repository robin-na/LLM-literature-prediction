## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:

- frequency of cooperation

Outcome notes:
The paper is a theoretical evolutionary game theory analysis. Outcomes are frequencies of behavioral strategies (spite, cooperation, conditional strategies) at equilibrium under different parameter regimes. Payoff tables are specified, but the main results are about evolutionary stability and prevalence of behaviors, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the introduction of observable tags and the possibility of conditioning behavior on tags (e.g., only harming out-group members) fundamentally changes the evolutionary dynamics of costly harming (spite) in the Prisoner's Delight game. Tags make pure (unconditional) spite less likely to evolve even when anti-correlation would otherwise favor it, but make conditional (tag-based) spite more likely in some regimes. Several stable equilibria are identified, including cooperative polymorphisms (mostly cooperation, little spite), pure spite, and a novel 'treacherous polymorphism' where spite is directed within groups. The presence of tags generally reduces the prevalence of widespread spiteful behavior, even under conditions that would otherwise favor it. The results are framed in terms of evolutionary stability and behavioral frequencies, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or their close variants. The model is a two-player game (Prisoner's Delight) with costly harming, not a multi-player public goods game. The main outcomes are the evolutionary frequencies of behavioral types, not group efficiency or total payoff. However, the analysis is relevant for understanding how the structure of punishment-like mechanisms (costly harming, conditional on observable tags) and population structure (correlated/anti-correlated interactions) can affect the prevalence of cooperation and spite. The findings suggest that mechanisms allowing for conditional punishment (e.g., based on group identity) can both promote and suppress harmful behavior, depending on the context. For the downstream prediction task, this paper provides indirect theoretical context about how punishment-like behaviors may interact with population structure and signaling, but does not provide quantitative or empirical evidence for efficiency outcomes in PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The base model is a two-player game (Prisoner's Delight). Some results are extended to n > 2 tags, but the game remains pairwise.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (harming or not) is a binary choice: pay cost c to harm (spite) or not.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Cost to the punisher (c) is a key parameter, varied in the analysis.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment impact per unit cost is parameterized as harm h per cost c; both are varied.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Tags are mutually observed before play; players can condition behavior on the tag of their counterpart.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental data.
- The game analyzed is a two-player Prisoner's Delight, not a multi-player public goods game.
- Outcomes are frequencies of behavioral strategies, not group efficiency or total payoff.
- Assumes infinite populations, frequency-independent assortment, costless and perfectly recognized tags.
- The exogenous correlation parameter is abstract and may not map directly to real-world mechanisms.
- No direct analysis of efficiency, welfare, or group payoff outcomes.
