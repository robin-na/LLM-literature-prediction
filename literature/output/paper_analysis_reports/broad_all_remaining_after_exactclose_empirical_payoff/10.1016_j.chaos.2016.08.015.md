Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the evolution of cooperation (number of cooperators, conversion rates) in a threshold public goods game with mechanisms such as publishing the list of cooperators and the presence of lobbyists. Payoff is discussed as a driver of imitation, but group efficiency or total payoff is not directly analyzed or reported as an outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that both publishing the list of cooperators and the presence of lobbyists can significantly increase the number of cooperators in a threshold public goods game, especially when the synergy factor is not too high. The effectiveness of these mechanisms depends on the population structure (proportion of unconditional cooperators vs. defectors), the synergy factor, and emotional influence parameters. When the synergy factor is high, publishing the list is always beneficial and the role of lobbyists becomes negligible. When the synergy factor is low, the decision to publish the list should consider the relative numbers of cooperators and defectors and the strength of emotional inhibition. The paper does not report on efficiency or group payoff, so its findings are only indirectly relevant to predicting efficiency outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about how social recognition (publishing the list) and persuasive agents (lobbyists) can increase cooperation rates in threshold public goods games, especially under certain parameter regimes. However, because it does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment or similar mechanisms on efficiency. The findings may be useful for understanding the behavioral pathways by which social mechanisms affect cooperation, but should be used with caution for efficiency prediction tasks.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Simulations are run on an L x L lattice (L=199), with each player interacting with their von Neumann neighborhood (5 players per group). The total number of players is large, but the effect of group size is not directly analyzed.
- number of rounds: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. The model is evolutionary and proceeds in repeated stages until equilibrium, but the number of rounds is not fixed or systematically varied.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: UCs contribute 1, UDs and SRs contribute 0.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r (1 < r < N) is analogous to MPCR; it is systematically varied in simulations.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Publishing the list of cooperators is a central mechanism; it increases cooperation rates under certain conditions.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is simulation-based and does not report empirical or experimental data.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or total welfare are not directly analyzed or reported; only cooperation rates and behavioral outcomes are studied.
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present; the mechanisms studied (publishing the list, lobbyists) are forms of social recognition and persuasion.
- Findings may not generalize to standard public goods games with punishment or to real-world settings without further empirical validation.
- The model assumes all-or-nothing contributions and a specific population structure, which may limit applicability to other game designs.
