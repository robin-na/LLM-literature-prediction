Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the probability of the system selecting different stable states (e.g., all cooperation/punishment, all defection, all non-participation) as a function of game parameters. While these states are associated with different group payoffs, the paper does not directly report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare as explicit outcome variables. The analysis is primarily about the prevalence of cooperative or defecting equilibria under different parameter regimes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that introducing punishment and non-participation strategies into a stochastic evolutionary public goods game changes the evolutionary dynamics and the likelihood of the system reaching cooperative, defecting, or non-participation equilibria. Higher punishment probability (alpha) and lower punishment cost (gamma) both increase the parameter regions where the system is likely to reach cooperative/punishing (C+P) equilibria. However, the paper does not directly report efficiency or group payoff; instead, it reports the probability of the system selecting each type of equilibrium as a function of the parameters. The results suggest that punishment can promote cooperation under a wide range of conditions, but the effect is moderated by the cost and probability of punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task: it shows that the likelihood of cooperative/punishing equilibria increases with higher punishment probability and lower punishment cost, and that these effects are sensitive to the marginal per-capita return (r/N) and the fixed income for non-participants. However, because the paper does not directly report efficiency or group payoff, its findings must be mapped from the probability of cooperative equilibria to expected efficiency. The results suggest that, in parameter regions where the C+P equilibrium is likely, efficiency would be higher than in regions dominated by defection or non-participation. Thus, the paper supports the idea that punishment can increase efficiency, especially when punishment is effective and not too costly, but does not provide quantitative efficiency outcomes.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size M is a model parameter (e.g., M=20 in numerical experiments); group size N=4 for each public goods game. The model allows for arbitrary M and N.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is r/N, with r varied in simulations (e.g., 1.1 ≤ r < N, N=4).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost (gamma) is varied; higher cost reduces the parameter region for cooperation and increases defection.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the ratio of penalty to cost (beta/gamma); higher punishment probability (alpha) and lower cost (gamma) increase the likelihood of cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is entirely theoretical and does not report empirical or experimental results.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare are not directly reported; only the probability of reaching different equilibria is analyzed.
- Mapping from equilibrium selection probabilities to expected efficiency is indirect and may not capture all relevant payoff dynamics.
- The model assumes well-mixed populations and does not consider network or spatial structure.
- No reward mechanisms, communication, or other social features are included.
