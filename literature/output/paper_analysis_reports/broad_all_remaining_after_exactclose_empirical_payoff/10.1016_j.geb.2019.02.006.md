# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The market for talent: Competition for resources and self-governance in teams
Authors: Ramalingam, A., Stoddard, B. V., & Walker, J. M.
Journal: Games and Economic Behavior
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual contribution levels
- group contribution levels
- contribution convergence/divergence
- ostracism/voting behavior
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are individual and group contributions to the public good, and ostracism/voting behavior. The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings as primary outcomes, though these could be calculated from the contribution data.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Competition for a common-member's contributions creates 'winners' and 'losers': groups that start with higher contributions retain the common-member's loyalty and sustain higher contributions, while low-contribution groups decline further. Providing dedicated-members with more information about the common-member's contributions increases the common-member's total contributions and reduces discrimination between groups, but does not significantly raise dedicated-members' contributions in low-performing groups. Allowing ostracism (costless exclusion by majority vote) leads to higher and more equal contributions across groups, as both common- and dedicated-members increase contributions and the threat of exclusion disciplines low contributors. Ostracism is targeted at low contributors and rarely used against high contributors. However, the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, so the effect on efficiency is not directly measured.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about the effects of punishment-like mechanisms (ostracism) and information on contribution behavior in public goods games with divided loyalties. While it does not report efficiency or group payoff, the findings suggest that ostracism (a form of endogenous, costless group-level punishment) can increase and equalize contributions across groups, especially in settings where initial disparities would otherwise persist. The results also show that information about others' contributions can increase contributions by pivotal members. For prediction tasks, the paper suggests that enabling ostracism or increasing transparency may improve group outcomes in public goods games, but the lack of direct efficiency or payoff data limits quantitative prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups are of size 3 (2 dedicated-members + 1 common-member shared between two groups), so each paired-group has 5 players. Control (No-CM) groups have 3 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use 20 repeated rounds, known in advance.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication is allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are continuous from 0 to 20 tokens (not all-or-nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.6 in all treatments.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Ostracism (exclusion) is costless to the voter; there is no explicit costly punishment mechanism.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Ostracism is binary (excluded for one round, lose group earnings for that round). No gradation or cost scaling.
- reward availability: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Rewards are discussed in the introduction/literature review but not implemented in any treatment.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The total number of rounds (20) is public information before the first round.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are shown individual contributions and group totals for their own group. In CM-Info, dedicated-members also see the common-member's contributions in both groups.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Player IDs are fixed and known within groups; ostracism votes are anonymous, but the number of votes received by each member is shown.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings as primary outcomes, limiting direct inference about efficiency effects.
- Ostracism is costless and binary (exclusion for one round), which differs from standard costly punishment mechanisms in many PGG studies.
- The 'divided loyalties' structure (one common-member per pair of groups) is not a standard PGG or PGG with punishment design, so results may not generalize to standard settings.
- All players have equal endowments and productivity; effects may differ with heterogeneous resources.
- No reward mechanism is implemented, so no evidence on reward effects.
