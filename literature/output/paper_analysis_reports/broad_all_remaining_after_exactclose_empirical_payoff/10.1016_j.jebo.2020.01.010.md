## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that allocating taxes and rewards based on memory reputation generally increases the cooperation rate in spatial public goods games, especially in environments with high taxes or high synergy factors. In low-tax, low-synergy environments (favorable to defectors), equal allocation of rewards and taxes is more effective at sustaining cooperation. The model shows that the effectiveness of reward mechanisms depends on the balance between tax and reward costs, memory length, and the synergy factor. However, the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, and does not include punishment or sanctioning mechanisms.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it does not include punishment or efficiency outcomes. Its findings are only indirectly relevant: it suggests that the structure of incentive mechanisms (tax-funded rewards, reputation-based allocation) can affect cooperation rates, and that the effectiveness of such mechanisms depends on environmental parameters. For the downstream prediction task, this paper is not directly informative, but it may provide contextual insight into how non-punitive incentive mechanisms interact with game design dimensions.

- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperators and rewarders contribute a fixed amount (typically 1), defectors contribute nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r is varied (typically r > 1), and MPCR = r / group size (group size = 5).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward mechanism is central to the model; rewarders pay a cost to reward cooperators.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Reward cost G is varied; higher G can both help and hinder cooperation depending on other parameters.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Reward effectiveness is determined by how G is distributed among cooperators and rewarders, either equally or by reputation.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is entirely simulation-based and does not report any empirical or experimental data.
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is included; only reward and tax mechanisms are studied.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- Findings may not generalize to real human behavior or to settings with punishment.
- The model assumes a spatial lattice and local interactions, which may not reflect all public goods environments.
