# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The effect of sleep on public good contributions and punishment: Experimental evidence
Authors: Clark, J., & Dickinson, D. L.
Journal: Plos One
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution level
- punishment sent
- response to punishment
- pro-social and anti-social punishment
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are contribution levels and punishment behavior. The paper does not report group efficiency, total earnings, welfare, or surplus. Some monetary outcomes (e.g., average VCM earnings) are reported descriptively, but not as primary outcomes or in relation to punishment treatment.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that, in a standard VCM with 3-person groups and 10 rounds per treatment, the introduction of peer punishment increases contributions for sleep-restricted subjects but not for well-rested subjects. Without punishment, contributions do not differ by sleep condition. There is no significant difference in punishment behavior between sleep-restricted and well-rested subjects. The study does not report efficiency or group payoff as a function of punishment, so it does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings are robust to various compliance and attrition corrections, but some results are sensitive to the sleep deprivation measure used.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides high-quality evidence on the effect of punishment on contribution behavior in a standard VCM, but does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. For the downstream prediction task, the paper confirms that, in a 3-player, 10-round VCM with standard punishment (linear cost, known identities), the introduction of punishment increases contributions, especially for sleep-restricted subjects. However, because efficiency or group payoff is not analyzed, the paper cannot directly inform predictions about efficiency changes due to punishment. The design dimensions are well specified, and the behavioral results are likely to correlate with efficiency, but the lack of direct payoff analysis is a limitation.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 3 players per VCM group; fixed throughout.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 10 rounds per treatment (with and without punishment).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication allowed; interactions were anonymous over computer network.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is continuous: each subject chooses how many of 15 tokens to contribute (0-15).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.625 (each token contributed yields $0.05 to each of 3 players; endowment is 15 tokens).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point sent costs the punisher $0.10.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point received reduces the recipient's earnings by 10% of that round's earnings (up to 100% cap).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward mechanism does not exist; only punishment is available.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects knew the number of rounds in advance (10 rounds per treatment).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, subjects were informed of each individual's contribution to the group account.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects were informed of the ID number of the person sending punishment points.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus as a function of punishment; only contribution and punishment behavior are analyzed.
- Sample size is moderate (N=109 compliant), limiting power to detect small effects.
- Some findings are sensitive to the measure of sleep deprivation used (binary assignment vs. continuous self-assessed).
- The experiment uses student subjects in a lab setting, which may limit generalizability to real-world environments.
- No reward mechanism is studied; only punishment is available.
- The study does not vary or analyze the effect of changing punishment cost or effectiveness beyond the standard setting.
