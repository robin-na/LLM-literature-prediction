# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Personality, Punishment and Public Goods: Strategic Shifts Towards Cooperation as a Matter of Dispositional Honesty-Humility
Authors: Hilbig, B. E., Zettler, I., & Heydasch, T.
Journal: European Journal of Personality
Year: 2012

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- simulated group gain (sum of individual pay-offs minus initial endowments) as a function of group composition (Honesty-Humility) in the no-punishment condition
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual contribution to the public good (out of 100 points) in hypothetical PGGs with and without punishment
- difference in contribution between punishment and no-punishment conditions
- correlations between HEXACO personality factors and PGG contributions
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the number of points contributed to the public good in hypothetical PGGs, with and without punishment. No real monetary incentives were used. Efficiency or group payoff is only addressed in a simulation based on observed contributions, not as a primary experimental outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The presence of punishment in a PGG increases individual contributions to the public good, replicating prior findings. This effect is moderated by the Honesty-Humility personality trait: individuals low in Honesty-Humility increase their contributions substantially when punishment is possible, while those high in Honesty-Humility contribute at a high level regardless of punishment. The study uses hypothetical (non-incentivized) PGGs, and the main outcome is contribution behavior, not actual efficiency or group payoff. A simulation based on observed contributions suggests that group efficiency (total group gain) increases with the number of high Honesty-Humility members, but this is not an experimental outcome. The findings are robust across two experiments, including one with a large, heterogeneous sample and a substantial time lag between personality and PGG measurement.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the presence of punishment increases contributions in PGGs, especially among individuals low in Honesty-Humility. However, because the experiments are hypothetical and do not use real monetary incentives, and because efficiency or group payoff is not directly measured as an experimental outcome, the findings are most useful for predicting changes in contribution rates rather than efficiency per se. The simulation of group efficiency based on observed contributions provides indirect evidence that punishment could increase efficiency, but this is not a direct experimental result. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper offers informative indirect evidence that punishment increases contributions (and thus likely efficiency), but the lack of real incentives and direct efficiency measurement are important limitations.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each PGG is described as involving 5 players (the participant and four strangers).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each PGG is a one-shot (single round) decision; participants make a single contribution decision per condition.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed; all decisions are made individually and anonymously.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: participants choose any amount from 0 to 100 points.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (MPCR) is 0.3 (public good multiplier is 1.5, divided among 5 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. For each point invested in punishment, the punished group member loses three points (punishment cost to punisher is 1 per unit, impact is 3 per unit).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3:1 (each point spent on punishment causes 3 points loss to the target).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told the game is a one-shot (single round) decision; the structure is clear.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the no-punishment condition, only the total group contribution is revealed. In the punishment condition, individual contributions are visible to all group members.

## Important Limitations
- The PGGs are hypothetical and do not involve real monetary incentives, which may limit generalizability to incentivized settings.
- Efficiency or group payoff is not directly measured as an experimental outcome; only individual contributions are measured, and efficiency is addressed via simulation.
- The main outcome is contribution behavior, not actual group welfare or surplus.
- Potential for social desirability bias in hypothetical, non-incentivized games.
- No analysis of repeated or multi-round PGGs; only one-shot decisions are studied.
