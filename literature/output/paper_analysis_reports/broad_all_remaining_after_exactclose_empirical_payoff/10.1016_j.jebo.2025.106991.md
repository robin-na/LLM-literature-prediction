Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is contribution behavior (average contribution, rate of full contribution, etc.), not efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings. While payoffs are determined by contributions, the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff as a primary outcome, nor does it analyze treatment effects on these directly.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that both identification and attribution of individual contributions increase average contributions in a finitely repeated public goods game. The effect is strongest when both are present, but even imprecise identifiers (numbers, avatars) and partial attribution (not perfectly linking actions to identifiers) increase contributions relative to baseline. The effect is primarily behavioral: more subjects contribute their full endowment when individual contributions are revealed. There is no punishment or reward mechanism in the game, and the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff as an outcome. Theoretical analysis considers the possibility of punishment outside the game, but the experimental data do not support a punishment-based explanation for the observed effects.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is highly informative for understanding how identification and attribution affect contribution behavior in public goods games, but it does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff outcomes. Since no punishment or reward mechanism is present, the findings are not directly transferable to the prediction of efficiency with punishment enabled. However, the results do suggest that increasing transparency and reducing anonymity can increase contributions, which may interact with the effects of punishment in other designs. For the downstream prediction task, this paper provides strong evidence on the effects of information and identification, but not on punishment or efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 4 players (N=4) in all conditions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups play 32 rounds (T=32) in all conditions.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: subjects choose any integer amount from 0 to 20.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.5 (multiplier m=2, N=4).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game is finitely repeated for 32 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. All subjects see the aggregate group contribution and their own earnings after each round. In treatment conditions, individual contributions are also revealed (with varying attribution).

## Important Limitations
- No punishment or reward mechanism is present in the experiment; thus, the paper does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (contribution rates), not efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings.
- Theoretical discussion of punishment outside the game is not tested experimentally.
- Findings on identification and attribution may not generalize to settings with explicit punishment or reward mechanisms.
- All groups are of fixed size (N=4) and play 32 rounds; results may not generalize to other group sizes or time horizons.
