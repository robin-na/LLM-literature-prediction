# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Can Reminders of Rules Induce Compliance? Experimental Evidence from a Common Pool Resource Setting
Authors: Eisenbarth, S., Graham, L., & Rigterink, A. S.
Journal: Environmental & Resource Economics
Year: 2021

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=field_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- knowledge of rules
- attentiveness to rules
- scrutiny of others
- willingness to sanction
- perceived probability of sanctions
- compliance with rules (non-compliance index)
- forest use (self-reported and satellite-based)
- participation in governance bodies
- satisfaction with rules
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are behavioral: knowledge, attentiveness, compliance, and forest use. Forest use is a proxy for resource extraction, not a direct measure of group payoff or efficiency. No direct group payoff, welfare, or efficiency measures are reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The SMS reminder intervention increased self-reported knowledge of forest use rules and the perceived probability of sanctions for rule-breaking, but did not increase actual scrutiny or sanctioning of others. There was no significant effect on compliance with forest use rules or on forest use (measured both by self-report and satellite data). The intervention did not systematically reduce resource extraction or improve compliance, and thus did not provide evidence that reminders of rules (and increased perceived sanctions) improve efficiency or group welfare in this common pool resource context.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides little direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment mechanisms on efficiency in public goods or common pool resource games, as it does not manipulate or directly measure actual punishment, nor does it report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. The intervention only increased perceived probability of sanctions, not actual sanctioning or compliance, and did not reduce resource use. Thus, the findings suggest that increasing perceived sanctions via reminders alone may not be sufficient to improve efficiency or reduce overuse in common pool resource settings. For prediction tasks, this paper suggests that behavioral nudges increasing perceived sanctions (without changing actual punishment mechanisms or costs) are unlikely to have strong effects on efficiency or group payoff.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Each village is a community of forest users; household-level and village-level outcomes are reported. Villages had 10-20 surveyed households, but actual group size is larger and variable.
- punishment cost: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Sanctions exist in the real-world setting (fines, confiscation, etc.), but the intervention did not manipulate punishment cost. Some informal sanctions (e.g., scolding) are low-cost.
- punishment technology: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is discussed (formal and informal sanctions), but not manipulated or measured directly.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Community monitoring treatment included public reporting of forest use via posters and meetings, but not as part of the SMS intervention.

## Important Limitations
- No direct measure of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare; only behavioral proxies (compliance, forest use) are reported.
- The intervention increased perceived probability of sanctions but did not manipulate actual punishment, punishment cost, or effectiveness.
- All SMS reminder villages also received a community monitoring treatment, so effects of SMS alone are not isolated.
- Sample size for SMS treatment is small (10 villages), limiting statistical power.
- Context is a real-world common pool resource, not a stylized public goods game; results may not generalize to laboratory PGGs.
- No manipulation or measurement of key game design dimensions such as MPCR, player count, or punishment parameters.
