Journal: Physica A-statistical Mechanics and Its Applications

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of cooperators
- fraction of runs ending with 100% cooperators
- network heterogeneity (degree variance)
- degree-degree correlation (assortativity)
- fraction of C-C, C-D, D-D links
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are the prevalence of cooperation (fraction of cooperators) and network structural properties. No explicit analysis of group payoff, efficiency, or welfare is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that allowing agents to adapt their social ties by breaking links with the most frequent defectors (after a multi-round PDG) and rewiring to others can promote the emergence and dominance of cooperation in the population. The interplay between network structure adaptation and strategy evolution is key: there is a critical rate of network adaptation above which cooperators can dominate. The model explores how parameters such as temptation to defect, number of rounds, average connectivity, and selection intensity affect the prevalence of cooperation. However, the outcomes are reported in terms of cooperation rates and network properties, not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. Its model is adjacent: it studies a networked PDG with social tie breaking as a form of sanction, but does not include explicit costly punishment or measure efficiency/welfare. The findings suggest that adaptive social structure (link breaking as a response to defection) can promote cooperation, but the results are not directly transferable to standard PGGs with explicit punishment and efficiency outcomes. Use this paper as weak, indirect support for the idea that social sanctions (even non-costly ones) can promote cooperation, but do not use it for quantitative prediction of efficiency effects.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=1000 or N=10,000; population size is a parameter, but no systematic analysis of its effect is reported.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Number of rounds n between network updates is varied; higher n can suppress cooperation promoted by network adaptation.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. PDG is binary: cooperate or defect each round.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents observe the strategies and aggregate payoffs of their neighbors each round.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents know which neighbor defected most and break that tie; identification is local and direct.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a theoretical simulation, not an empirical or experimental study.
- The game is a networked Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly.
- Punishment is implemented as social tie breaking (link dismissal), not as costly punishment or sanctions.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates and network properties.
- No explicit cost to the punisher for breaking ties is modeled.
- No analysis of payoff-based outcomes, so the effect on efficiency is unknown.
