## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of conditional peer exclusion in a spatial public goods game model can greatly promote the emergence and stability of cooperation, especially when the tolerance for defectors is low (i.e., excluders act against defectors even when there are few of them), the cost of exclusion is low, and the probability of exclusion success is high. Even with moderate or low exclusion success probability, conditional exclusion can still promote cooperation if the synergy factor (equivalent to MPCR) is not too low. The main reported outcome is the cooperation level, not efficiency or group payoff. The model does not include a direct control (no-exclusion) efficiency comparison, but does reference the threshold for cooperation in the standard SPGG. The findings suggest that exclusion-based punishment can be a powerful mechanism for sustaining cooperation, but the paper does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that conditional exclusion (a form of punishment) can increase cooperation rates in spatial public goods games, especially under low tolerance, low cost, and high effectiveness. However, because the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings are most useful for predicting the effect of punishment on cooperation rates, and only indirectly suggest that efficiency may increase when cooperation is high. For the downstream prediction task (treatment efficiency from design dimensions plus control efficiency), this paper provides only indirect, mechanistic support, not quantitative or direct evidence.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 5 players (focal player plus 4 neighbors on a square lattice). The total population is 10,000 (100x100 lattice).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 to 20,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with each MCS allowing every player a chance to update. The game is iterated until a stable state is reached.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: cooperators and excluders contribute 1 unit, defectors contribute 0.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r is varied (e.g., r=1.58, 2.00, 2.50, 3.00, 3.72, etc.). MPCR = r / group size (5).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Exclusion cost c is varied (e.g., c=0.1, 0.5, 0.9). Lower c leads to higher cooperation levels at lower r. High c makes exclusion less effective.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Probability of exclusion success (β) is varied (e.g., β=0.2, 0.5, 0.8). Higher β increases cooperation at lower r.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is cooperation level, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare. The paper does not report or analyze efficiency or total payoff.
- The model is a simulation, not an experiment or empirical study; results may not generalize to real human behavior.
- No direct comparison of group efficiency with and without exclusion; only indirect reference to cooperation thresholds in standard SPGG.
- No reward mechanism is considered; only exclusion (punishment) is modeled.
- The model assumes all-or-nothing contributions and does not explore continuous contribution choices.
- No communication, reputation, or identification of punishers is modeled.
