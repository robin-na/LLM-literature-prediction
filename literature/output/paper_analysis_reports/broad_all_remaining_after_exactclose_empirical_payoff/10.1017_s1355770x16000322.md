# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Democracy and cooperation in commons management: experimental evidence of representative and direct democracy from community forests in Ethiopia
Authors: Gatiso, T. T., & Vollan, B.
Journal: Environment and Development Economics
Year: 2017

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- harvest ratio (HR): individual harvest as a proportion of maximum allowed
- compliance with regulation (harvesting no more than two trees)
- cooperation (lower HR interpreted as more cooperative)
- rule compliance rate
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the harvest ratio (HR), which is a behavioral measure of cooperation. The paper does not report group earnings, total payoff, welfare, or efficiency as a primary outcome. The design allows calculation of efficiency, but the paper focuses on behavioral outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Democratic procedures (either direct or representative) for rule or leader selection significantly increase cooperation in a dynamic CPR extraction game compared to imposed rules or leaders. The effect is not due to the presence of sanctions per se, but to the legitimacy conferred by democratic procedures. Sanctions only increase cooperation when democratically chosen. There is no significant difference in cooperation between direct and representative democracy. The main outcome is behavioral (harvest ratio), not efficiency or group payoff. The study does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or total group earnings, but the design allows for such calculation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that democratic procedures for rule or leader selection increase cooperation in CPR-like environments, and that sanctions only increase cooperation when democratically chosen. However, the main outcome is behavioral (harvest ratio), not efficiency or group payoff. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, the results are only indirectly informative: higher cooperation (lower HR) likely implies higher efficiency, but the paper does not quantify this. The game is a dynamic CPR extraction game, not a standard linear PGG, but is closely related. The findings suggest that the legitimacy of the process (democratic choice) is a key moderator of punishment/sanction effectiveness. The paper does not provide direct evidence on how punishment affects efficiency, so its value for efficiency prediction is indirect and should be weighted accordingly.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups had 5 players per session.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game lasted up to 10 rounds; two games per group (baseline and treatment).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication between participants was not allowed during the experiment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Harvest (contribution) choices were continuous within a bounded range (0 to max allowed per round), not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Not a standard linear PGG; each tree harvested yields a private payoff (ETB 0.5), and the remaining forest is shared equally at the end. The marginal per-capita return is not explicitly calculated, but the structure is described.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sanction: If caught harvesting more than two trees, twice the excess is deducted from the individual's account. Monitoring is random (1/5 chance per round). The cost is a deduction from the individual's payoff; there is no explicit cost to a punisher, as punishment is automatic and externally enforced.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is a deduction of twice the amount harvested above the allowed limit if monitored. The effectiveness is limited by the low probability of monitoring (1/5 per round) and the mildness of the sanction.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants knew the game would last up to 10 rounds, or until the resource was exhausted.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were informed about the group stock and the maximum allowed harvest per round. It is not explicitly stated if they saw others' individual harvests, but group-level information was available.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. All members knew who was inspected (monitored) each round, but it was not communicated whether the inspected person was actually sanctioned or not.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is behavioral (harvest ratio), not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare. The paper does not report efficiency or total group earnings as a primary outcome.
- The punishment mechanism is externally enforced and automatic, not peer-to-peer; there is no explicit cost to a punisher.
- Sanctions are intentionally mild and have a low probability of being applied, limiting their deterrent effect.
- The experiment is a dynamic CPR extraction game, not a standard linear PGG, though it is closely related.
- Small sample size (17 groups, 85 participants) may limit generalizability.
- No reward mechanism is present, so results do not inform about reward effects.
- The study population is Ethiopian forest users; results may not generalize to other contexts or populations.
- No direct analysis of efficiency or group payoff; findings on efficiency must be inferred indirectly from behavioral outcomes.
