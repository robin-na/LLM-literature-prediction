# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Time pressure and deliberation affect moral punishment
Authors: Philippsen, A., Mieth, L., Buchner, A., & Bell, R.
Journal: Scientific Reports
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- probability of cooperation
- probability of moral punishment
- probability of hypocritical punishment
- probability of antisocial punishment
- punishment bias
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are model-based probabilities of different types of punishment and cooperation, not group or individual payoffs, efficiency, or welfare. The paper does not report group efficiency or total earnings as a primary outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The main findings are that (1) time pressure reduces the probability of moral punishment, (2) deliberation increases the probability of moral punishment, and (3) the content of deliberation matters: deliberating fairness increases, while deliberating self-interest decreases, moral punishment. These effects are specific to moral punishment and do not consistently affect other types of punishment or cooperation. The study does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, so the direct impact of these manipulations on group efficiency is not assessed.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence about how cognitive constraints and deliberation affect the likelihood of moral punishment in a Prisoner's Dilemma with punishment, but does not provide direct evidence about efficiency or group payoff. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, the results suggest that manipulations increasing deliberation (e.g., removing time pressure, encouraging fairness-based reflection) are likely to increase the use of moral punishment, which in other literature is often associated with higher cooperation and potentially higher efficiency. However, because efficiency is not measured, the paper's findings should be used as indirect evidence about the behavioral mechanisms (punishment rates) rather than direct evidence about efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game is a two-player (dyadic) simultaneous Prisoner's Dilemma with punishment; each participant plays 20 one-shot games with different partners.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each participant plays 20 rounds (one-shot interactions with 20 different partners).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (cooperate/defect) is a binary choice; punishment is a discrete choice (0-5 units).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Payoff structure: mutual cooperation +10 each, mutual defection 0, unilateral cooperation: cooperator -10, defector +20. This is a standard Prisoner's Dilemma, not a linear PGG; MPCR is not directly reported but can be inferred as 0.5 for the cooperator (since they lose 10, partner gains 20).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost is 1-5 cents (or points) to deduct 10-50 cents (or points) from the partner; cost per unit is 1:10.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 10 units deducted per 1 unit spent (1:10 ratio).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know they will play 20 rounds (one with each partner).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants see a summary of their own and their partner's choices and payoffs.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know which partner they are punishing (face photo shown), but all partners are simulated and only interacted with once; no anonymity within the round.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare as outcomes; only behavioral probabilities (cooperation, punishment) are analyzed.
- The game is a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma with punishment, not a standard public goods game; findings may not generalize to multi-player or repeated PGGs.
- All partners are simulated (not real human partners), which may affect ecological validity.
- Participants interact with each partner only once, but play 20 rounds in total; possible learning or reputation effects across rounds cannot be fully ruled out.
- The social context is emphasized (face photos shown), which may differ from more abstract or anonymous economic games.
- No reward mechanism is present, so findings do not address interactions between punishment and reward.
