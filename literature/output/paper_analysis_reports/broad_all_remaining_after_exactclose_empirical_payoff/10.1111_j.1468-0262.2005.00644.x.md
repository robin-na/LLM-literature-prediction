# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Driving forces behind informal sanctions
Authors: Falk, A., Fehr, E., & Fischbacher, U.
Journal: Econometrica
Year: 2005

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- payoff reduction imposed by punishment
- cost of punishment to punisher
- distribution of payoffs under different punishment conditions
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of cooperation and defection
- frequency and targeting of punishment (who punishes whom)
- severity of punishment assigned
- motives for punishment (fairness, spite, retaliation)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: who punishes whom, how much, and under what conditions. Payoff-related outcomes are reported as the amount of payoff reduction imposed and the cost to the punisher, but group efficiency or total welfare is not directly analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a three-player one-shot PD with punishment, cooperators predominantly punish defectors, and this pattern is robust across different punishment effectiveness conditions and elicitation methods. Defectors also punish, but their punishment is more likely to be spiteful and disappears when punishment cannot increase payoff differences. The cost-effectiveness of punishment is manipulated, showing that cooperators are willing to spend more to punish defectors even when punishment is less effective. The results challenge fairness theories that focus only on group-level payoffs or inequality aversion, supporting instead a model where retaliation against unfairness is central. However, the paper does not report on group efficiency or total welfare outcomes, focusing instead on behavioral and motivational aspects of punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence on the behavioral patterns and motives of punishment in a three-player PD with punishment, including how punishment is targeted and how its cost-effectiveness affects behavior. However, it does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. It does suggest that punishment is frequently used by cooperators against defectors, and that the willingness to punish persists even when it is costly, which may imply potential for efficiency improvements in similar games, but this is not tested or quantified here. The design details (player count, one-shot, punishment cost/effectiveness) are well specified and can inform mechanistic expectations, but not direct efficiency predictions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All experiments use three-player groups.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All main experiments are one-shot (single round).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed; all interactions are anonymous.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate (invest) or defect (do not invest).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Implied by payoff table: if all cooperate, each earns 36; if all defect, each earns 20. The marginal return to cooperation can be calculated from Table I.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punisher pays 1 token per deduction point assigned. Two conditions: low-sanction (f=1), high-sanction (f=2.5 or 3.33).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness per token: f=1 (low), f=2.5 or 3.33 (high). Cooperators' willingness to punish persists even when punishment is less effective (f=1).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is one-shot; subjects know this.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After the cooperation stage, each subject is informed of the other players' individual decisions before punishment.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Interactions are anonymous; personal identities are not revealed. It is not explicitly stated whether the identity of the punisher is revealed, but anonymity is emphasized.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a one-shot (single round) design, so repeated game effects and long-term efficiency impacts are not studied.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (punishment patterns, motives), not group efficiency or total welfare.
- The game is a three-player prisoner's dilemma, not a standard public goods game; results may not generalize to larger groups or continuous contribution settings.
- No direct measurement or analysis of group efficiency, total earnings, or surplus is provided.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanism is included, so the interaction of punishment and reward is not addressed.
