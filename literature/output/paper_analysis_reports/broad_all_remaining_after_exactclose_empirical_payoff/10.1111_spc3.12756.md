## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The review finds that both social and nonsocial uncertainty can reduce prosocial behaviors by providing excuses for selfishness or increasing loss aversion, but in some contexts, uncertainty can also increase prosociality (e.g., by promoting forgiveness or overestimating one's impact on others). Coping strategies for social uncertainty include impression formation, impression updating, and compliance with social norms. Punishment and reward are discussed as norm enforcement tools, but the review does not analyze their effects on efficiency or group payoff. The review highlights the complexity and context-dependence of uncertainty's effects on cooperation and norm enforcement.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical and contextual background on how uncertainty, punishment, and social norms interact in social decision-making games, including public goods and related environments. However, it does not provide quantitative or direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff, nor does it systematically analyze how game design dimensions moderate these effects. Thus, it is not directly informative for predicting treatment efficiency from game design dimensions and control efficiency, but it may be useful for understanding mechanisms and generating hypotheses about the role of uncertainty and norm enforcement.

## Important Limitations
- This is a theoretical review and does not report new empirical data or meta-analytic effect sizes.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are not analyzed; the focus is on prosocial behavior, cooperation, and norm enforcement.
- Game design dimensions are not systematically varied or analyzed for their effects on punishment or efficiency.
