# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Quality versus quantity of social ties in experimental cooperative networks
Authors: Shirado, H., Fu, F., Fowler, J. H., & Christakis, N. A.
Journal: Nature Communications
Year: 2013

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- network density
- partner choice behavior (making/breaking ties)
- strategy switching (cooperation/defection)
- network centrality
Outcome notes:
The primary outcomes are cooperation rates and network/partner choice dynamics. Payoff is described in the game structure, but group efficiency or total earnings are not analyzed as dependent variables. The study focuses on behavioral outcomes (cooperation, tie formation/breaking) rather than explicit payoff or efficiency measures.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that allowing dynamic partner selection in a networked cooperation game leads to the highest levels of cooperation at intermediate rates of network rewiring. At low rewiring rates, players accumulate many ties, including to defectors, which undermines cooperation. At very high rewiring rates, defectors can re-attach to cooperators faster than cooperators can detach, also undermining cooperation. The ability to break ties with defectors (interpreted as a form of social punishment) helps maintain cooperation, but only when the rate of change is not too high or too low. The main outcome is the cooperation rate; efficiency or group payoff is not directly analyzed.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about the effects of dynamic partner selection (ostracism-like social punishment) on cooperation in networked public goods games. It suggests that enabling players to break ties with defectors can sustain cooperation, but only at intermediate rates of network change. However, because the study does not include explicit costly punishment, nor does it report efficiency or group payoff, its direct value for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in standard PGGs is limited. The findings are most relevant for settings where social exclusion or partner choice is the main sanctioning mechanism, rather than costly punishment. The results suggest that the design dimension of network dynamism (rewiring rate) can have a non-monotonic effect on cooperation, which may translate to efficiency if payoff outcomes were measured.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Average network size was 17 (s.d. = 4), with no significant difference among rewiring rates. Each session had 10-25 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session lasted 15 rounds, but participants were not told the number of rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players was allowed; interactions were anonymous.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice was all-or-nothing: each round, players chose to cooperate (with all neighbors) or defect (with all neighbors).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each cooperator pays 50 units per neighbor; each neighbor receives 100 units. MPCR = 100/50 = 2 per neighbor, but since each player can have multiple neighbors, the effective MPCR per group is not standard.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were not told the number of rounds; the game ending was sudden.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players were shown their neighbors and, after the first round, their neighbors' previous decisions. At the end of each turn, participants were informed about the decisions of their neighbors in that round, along with their own payoff.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. When a tie was broken or formed, the participant was told the number of others who chose to break or form links with them, but not the identities. Thus, partial information: players know they were ostracized but not by whom.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not include explicit costly punishment; only social exclusion via tie breaking is possible.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings are not analyzed as dependent variables; only cooperation rates and network dynamics are reported.
- The game is a networked public goods variant, not a standard PGG; each player interacts with neighbors, not the whole group.
- Partner choice is unilateral; results may differ with bilateral tie formation.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with explicit punishment or reward mechanisms.
