## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Adaptive rewarding in spatial public goods games can eliminate defectors and promote high levels of cooperation, outperforming steady (non-adaptive) rewarding. The effectiveness of adaptive rewarding depends on the synergy factor (r), the cost of rewarding (alpha), and the strength of the reward (Delta). When rewards are strong and not too costly, adaptive rewarding can lead to defector-free states, interpreted as maximal efficiency. However, adaptive punishment is even more effective: for the same cost, punishment achieves defector elimination at lower required intensity. The paper provides detailed phase diagrams showing under what parameter regimes each strategy dominates, coexists, or is eliminated. The main efficiency outcome is the prevalence of defector-free states, which is used as a proxy for group welfare.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that, in spatial public goods games, adaptive punishment is more effective than adaptive rewarding at achieving maximal efficiency (defector-free states), given the same cost-to-impact ratio. The results are highly informative for predicting the effect of introducing punishment (or reward) in spatial PGGs: punishment is more likely to yield higher efficiency than reward, especially when the cost of sanctioning is not prohibitive. The findings are most directly applicable to spatial, lattice-based PGGs with local interactions, and may be less directly transferable to well-mixed or non-spatial settings. The paper's phase diagrams and minimum required cost-to-impact ratios for defector elimination can inform quantitative predictions about efficiency under different game design parameters.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely theoretical/modeling and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The model is spatial (square lattice), which may limit generalizability to well-mixed or non-spatial populations.
- Payoff outcomes are inferred from stationary fractions of strategies and defector elimination, not from explicit group earnings or currency units.
- The model assumes binary contribution choices and does not consider continuous contribution levels.
- No communication, reputation, or identification mechanisms are included, which may affect real-world applicability.
