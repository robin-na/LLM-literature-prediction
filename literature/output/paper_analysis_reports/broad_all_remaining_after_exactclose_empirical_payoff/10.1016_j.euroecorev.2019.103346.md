# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Democracy and compliance in public goods games
Authors: Gallier, C.
Journal: European Economic Review
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution to public good
- voting behavior
- compliance with contribution rule
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the amount contributed to the public good (in LabDollar), both at the individual and group level. No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total welfare is reported. The design and results focus on contribution behavior as the main outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that a non-deterrent contribution rule in a public goods game increases contributions, regardless of whether it is imposed exogenously or chosen democratically. The aggregate increase in contributions is primarily due to the presence of the rule itself, not the democratic process. Democratic participation (voting) does not have a statistically significant effect on contributions at the aggregate level after accounting for the direct effect of the rule, self-selection, and information effects. At the individual level, participants who are overruled in the group vote tend to reduce their contributions, but this negative effect is counteracted by a small democracy premium if the rule is democratically chosen. The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a linear public goods game with a mild, non-deterrent automatic sanction, the introduction of the rule (punishment) increases contributions, but the effect is not due to democratic participation per se. The findings are directly relevant for predicting the effect of introducing a weak punishment mechanism on contribution rates, but not directly on efficiency or group payoff, as these are not reported. The game design dimensions (player count, rounds, MPCR, punishment cost/tech, no chat, continuous contribution, etc.) are clearly specified and can inform parameterization of prediction models. However, since efficiency is not reported, the paper's quantitative results cannot be directly used for efficiency prediction, but the qualitative pattern (punishment increases cooperation even when non-deterrent) is robust.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 3 players per session, fixed for the entire experiment.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 rounds total: 10 rounds Part I (baseline), 10 rounds Part II (treatment).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between players; matching is anonymous.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player chooses any amount from 0 to 20 LabDollar per round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per capita return (MPCR) is 0.5 (each LabDollar contributed returns 0.5 to each of 3 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is automatic: a fixed penalty of 4 LabDollar is imposed on any player who contributes less than 20. No cost to other players; cost is imposed by the rule.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is a fixed deduction of 4 LabDollar per non-compliant player; no variable or strategic punishment.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is present; only punishment for non-compliance.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the total number of rounds (20) in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are informed about the outcome of the voting stage and, in some treatments, about whether the majority/minority supported the rule. No explicit mention of showing others' contributions or payoffs per round.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is automatic and anonymous; no identification of who is punished or who imposed punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is contribution to the public good, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare. No direct analysis of efficiency is reported.
- Punishment is automatic and non-deterrent (not strong enough to make cooperation the dominant strategy), so results may not generalize to deterrent or strategic punishment settings.
- No communication or chat is allowed, so findings may not generalize to settings with deliberation or discussion.
- The experiment uses a fixed group size (3 players) and a specific MPCR (0.5), limiting generalizability to other parameterizations.
- No reward mechanism is present, so the interaction of punishment and reward is not addressed.
- The study is conducted in a lab setting with student participants, which may limit external validity.
