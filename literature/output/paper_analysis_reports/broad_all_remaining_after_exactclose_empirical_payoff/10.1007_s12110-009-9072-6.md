# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Hadza Cooperation - Second-Party Punishment, Yes; Third-Party Punishment, No
Authors: Marlowe, F. W.
Journal: Human Nature-an Interdisciplinary Biosocial Perspective
Year: 2009

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- offer size in Dictator Game (DG)
- offer size in Ultimatum Game (UG)
- offer size in Third-Party Punishment Game (TPPG)
- minimum acceptable offer (MAO) in UG and TPPG
- frequency of rejection (UG)
- frequency of punishment (TPPG)
Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on behavioral outcomes such as offers, rejections, and punishment decisions in DG, UG, and TPPG. Group earnings and expected income are discussed, but efficiency or group payoff is not the primary dependent variable and is only discussed in passing.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The Hadza, unlike participants from larger or more complex societies, rarely engage in costly third-party punishment in experimental games. Offers in all games are low, and Hadza proposers tend to maximize their own income. Second-party punishment (rejection of low offers in the UG) is frequent, but third-party punishment (punishing low offers to others in the TPPG) is infrequent. The paper finds that third-party punishment is more prevalent in larger societies, suggesting that the scale and complexity of society may moderate the use of third-party punishment. Group earnings are low due to frequent rejections and low offers, but efficiency or group payoff is not the main focus.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence relevant to the prediction task. While it does not use a public goods game or directly report efficiency, it shows that in a closely related allocation game (TPPG), the availability of third-party punishment does not increase offers or group earnings among the Hadza, because third-party punishment is rarely used. In small-scale societies with low third-party punishment, enabling punishment may not improve efficiency. The findings suggest that the effect of punishment on efficiency may depend on societal context and the willingness to use punishment, which may be low in small, egalitarian groups. The results are less informative for standard PGGs but suggest caution in assuming punishment will increase efficiency in all contexts.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. DG and UG are 2-player games; TPPG is a 3-player game (P1, P2, P3).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are one-shot (single round) for each participant.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed; games played privately in the Land Rover.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/offer choices are continuous in increments of 10% (0-100%).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. In TPPG, P3 can pay 20% of their endowment to subtract 3x that amount from P1. Cost to punisher is 20% of P3's endowment per punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: 1 unit paid by P3 removes 3 units from P1.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are one-shot; participants know the structure.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not use a public goods game; results are from Dictator, Ultimatum, and Third-Party Punishment Games, which are adjacent but not equivalent to PGGs.
- Efficiency or group payoff is not the primary outcome; the focus is on behavioral responses (offers, rejections, punishment).
- Findings may not generalize to standard PGGs or to societies with different social structures.
- Sample is limited to the Hadza, a small-scale forager society; results may not apply to larger or more complex societies.
- Punishment is only available as third-party punishment in the TPPG, not as peer punishment in a repeated PGG context.
