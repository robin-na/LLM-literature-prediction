Outcome notes:
The paper is a conceptual/theoretical review and synthesis. It discusses determinants of cooperation, punishment, and value orientation in natural resource management (NRM) dilemmas, referencing the prisoner's dilemma (PD) and game theory, but does not report empirical payoff or efficiency outcomes from experiments or field studies. It reviews case studies and literature but does not present new quantitative outcome data.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that Indonesia's NRM practices are primarily allocation-focused and legal-normative, which limits their effectiveness in promoting sustainable, cooperative behavior. By integrating the PD/game theory approach into a socio-ecological governance framework, the authors argue that institutions can be designed to foster trust, reciprocity, and cooperation. They highlight that social punishment (e.g., social disapproval) may be more effective than financial sanctions in promoting norm compliance and cooperation, and that non-utilitarian values (e.g., stewardship, feelings of guilt) should be incorporated into NRM incentive structures. However, the paper does not present new empirical data or analyze efficiency or payoff outcomes directly.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides conceptual and theoretical support for the idea that punishment (especially social punishment) can increase cooperation in NRM dilemmas, and that institutional and value-based design features are important moderators. However, it does not provide empirical estimates or quantitative predictions of efficiency or payoff changes due to punishment in PGG-like games. For downstream prediction tasks, this paper suggests that the effectiveness of punishment may depend on whether it is social or financial, the presence of trust and reciprocity, and the integration of non-utilitarian values, but it does not provide direct evidence to calibrate efficiency outcomes. Its main value is in highlighting relevant mechanisms and moderators for consideration in empirical synthesis.
