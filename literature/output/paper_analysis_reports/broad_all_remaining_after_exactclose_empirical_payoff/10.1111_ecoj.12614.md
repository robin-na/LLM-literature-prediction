# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: PUBLIC GOODS PROVISION WITH RENT-EXTRACTING ADMINISTRATORS
Authors: Cagala, T., Glogowsky, U., Grimm, V., & Rincke, J.
Journal: Economic Journal
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- mean group contribution rate
- mean group return rate
- group earnings
- efficiency (via MPCR)
- volatility of contributions
Non-payoff outcomes:
- beliefs about administrator return
- attitudes toward trust and cooperation
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are contribution rates, return rates (administrator's share returned), and group earnings, all of which are directly tied to efficiency. The paper also analyzes volatility and short-run dynamics, and explores heterogeneity by trust/cooperation attitudes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study introduces a 'public trust game' (PTG), a repeated public goods game with an administrator who can extract rents from the contribution pool. The main finding is that the presence of a rent-extracting administrator reduces contributions and efficiency, but this is entirely explained by the reduction in MPCR (i.e., the efficiency of the public good). When comparing the PTG to a standard PGG with the same (lowered) MPCR, there is no significant difference in contributions or efficiency. The dynamics show that rent-extraction shocks and cooperation shocks only have short-run effects; the system returns to its previous level of cooperation and efficiency. The volatility of contributions is mainly driven by contributors' own behavior, not by the administrator. Groups with higher trust and cooperative attitudes experience less short-run volatility in cooperation after shocks.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in public-goods-like environments where an administrator can extract rents, the negative effect on efficiency is fully mediated by the reduction in MPCR. If the control game (standard PGG) and the treatment game (with administrator) have the same MPCR, the presence of the administrator does not further reduce efficiency. Thus, for prediction, the key moderator is the effective MPCR: punishment or rent extraction that reduces MPCR will reduce efficiency accordingly, but the mere presence of an administrator or rent-extraction opportunity does not further depress efficiency beyond this. The paper does not study punishment in the standard sense (peer or centralized costly punishment), but its findings are highly informative for cases where punishment or corruption reduces the effective return from cooperation.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 5 players: 4 contributors and 1 administrator in the PTG; 4 players in the standard PGG.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 30 periods (rounds) per session.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contributions are continuous: each contributor chooses any amount from 0 to 10 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. MPCR is 0.75 in PGG3 (r=3, 4 players), 0.5 in PGG2 (r=2, 4 players). In PTG, effective MPCR is reduced by administrator's extraction: MPCR = (1 - extraction rate) * 0.75.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game lasts 30 periods.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each period, all agents are informed about group members' endowments, pool size, return, and their own profit.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not implement or analyze standard punishment mechanisms (peer or centralized costly punishment); the administrator's rent extraction is not a punishment but a form of efficiency loss/corruption.
- Findings may not generalize to settings where punishment is used to enforce cooperation, as opposed to settings where efficiency is reduced by rent extraction.
- The administrator role is exogenously assigned and not subject to group selection or competition.
- The experiment uses student subjects in a laboratory setting, which may limit external validity.
- The analysis focuses on efficiency and contribution rates, but does not explore welfare implications for different institutional designs beyond the administrator vs. no-administrator comparison.
