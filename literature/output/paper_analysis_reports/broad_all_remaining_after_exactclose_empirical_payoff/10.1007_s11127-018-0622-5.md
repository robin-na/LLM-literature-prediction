## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model shows that costly punishment (by Willing Punishers) is essential for maintaining high average welfare in a society vulnerable to free riding. If the proportion of punishers falls below a threshold, defectors proliferate and average welfare collapses. Timely recognition of the threat and sufficient support (redistributive rewards) for punishers can reverse this trend and restore high welfare. The model identifies threshold effects: there is a minimum level of support for punishment required to sustain cooperation and high efficiency. Delays in shifting support to punishers or insufficient rewards for them lead to welfare loss or collapse. The model also shows cyclical dynamics, with periods of high and low welfare depending on the political narrative and group composition.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that the presence and effectiveness of costly punishment mechanisms are critical moderators of efficiency in public-goods-like environments. The model suggests that enabling punishment can increase efficiency, but only if punishment is sufficiently incentivized and timely. The effect is not automatic: if support for punishers is too low or delayed, efficiency gains are not realized and may even be reversed. The findings support the prediction that punishment will increase efficiency when the game design ensures adequate support for punishers and rapid response to defection. The model also highlights the importance of migration/inflow of defectors and the political/institutional context (ingroup favouritism) as moderators. However, as a theoretical model, it does not provide empirical effect sizes or direct mapping to specific experimental game parameters.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical; results are not validated with experimental or field data.
- The model uses population proportions and evolutionary dynamics, not discrete games with fixed player counts or rounds.
- Key parameters (e.g., size of rewards, delay factor) are stylized and may not map directly to experimental game settings.
- The model assumes only three fixed types (Always Cooperator, Willing Punisher, Always Defector), which may not capture the full range of human strategies.
- Political and institutional context (ingroup favouritism, narrative shifts) is modeled abstractly and may not generalize to all public goods environments.
