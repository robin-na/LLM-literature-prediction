## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that allowing insiders to punish outsiders in a CPR setting increases efficiency by reducing poaching and aligning insider harvests with the social optimum. The effect of punishment is more pronounced when insiders have earned their property rights, as they coordinate more effectively to deter poaching through higher and more frequent punishment. However, even with punishment, insiders do not fully deter poaching, and some poaching persists. The presence of chat among insiders likely facilitates coordination. The study provides direct evidence on how punishment and the method of property rights allocation interact to affect group payoffs and efficiency in a CPR context with intergroup conflict.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling punishment in a CPR game with communication among insiders increases efficiency, especially when property rights are earned. The efficiency gain is due to reduced poaching and better coordination among insiders. The design is adjacent to a public goods game (PGG) but differs in the presence of outsiders and the poaching stage. The findings suggest that, in similar environments, enabling punishment will increase efficiency relative to the control (no punishment), with a larger effect if group identity is salient (e.g., earned rights). The effect is not absolute—some inefficiency remains due to incomplete deterrence. The results are most applicable to CPR games with insider/outsider structure, chat among insiders, and a punishment technology with a 1:3 cost-to-impact ratio.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 6 players: 4 insiders and 2 outsiders.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. There are 15 rounds per session.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Insiders can communicate via chat before making harvest decisions; outsiders cannot communicate.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/harvest and poaching choices are continuous (0-50 EDs), not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The CPR surplus function is quadratic: V(G) = aG - bG^2 with a=6, b=0.025, and 4 insiders. The marginal per-capita return varies with group harvest, but at the social optimum (G=100), MPCR = (a - 2bG)/n = (6 - 2*0.025*100)/4 = (6 - 5)/4 = 0.25.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost is 1 ED to reduce another's payoff by 3 EDs (1:3 ratio).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment technology is 1:3 (1 ED spent reduces target by 3 EDs).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the total number of rounds (15) in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Players see the value of the Account (aggregate harvest), total investment by insiders, and withdrawal by outsiders after each stage.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Subjects have fixed IDs throughout the session, and punishment is targeted by ID; players can identify who punished whom.

## Important Limitations
- The game is a CPR with insiders and outsiders, not a standard PGG; results may not fully generalize to standard PGGs.
- Punishment is only available to insiders and only after poaching; outsiders cannot punish or communicate.
- Communication is only allowed among insiders, not between insiders and outsiders.
- The experiment uses a fixed group size (4 insiders, 2 outsiders) and 15 rounds; effects may differ with other group sizes or time horizons.
- Punishment does not fully deter poaching; efficiency gains are incomplete.
- The salience of group identity (earned vs. assigned rights) may not be present in all real-world or PGG settings.
