# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Cooperation in Groups of Different Sizes: The Effects of Punishment and Reputation-Based Partner Choice
Authors: Wu, J. H., Balliet, D., Peperkoorn, L. S., Romano, A., & Van Lange, P. A. M.
Journal: Frontiers in Psychology
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate (binary: contribute all or nothing)
- expected others' cooperation
- perceived collective efficacy
- perceived conflict
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the cooperation rate (i.e., proportion of participants who contribute all tokens). No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings is reported. Payoff is only used as the incentive structure, not as an outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that both punishment and reputation-based partner choice significantly increase the rate of full cooperation in one-shot public goods games, regardless of group size (4, 20, or 40). The effect of punishment on cooperation is positive and robust, but there is no evidence that its effectiveness depends on group size. The main outcome is the binary cooperation rate, not efficiency or group payoff. The study does not report on efficiency, welfare, or total group earnings, so its findings are adjacent to the prediction task, which requires payoff-based outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that punishment increases cooperation rates in one-shot public goods games across a range of group sizes (4, 20, 40), with a fixed marginal group return (MGR = 1.6). However, because the outcome is cooperation rate (all-or-nothing contribution), not efficiency or group payoff, the findings are only indirectly informative for predicting efficiency. The design dimensions are well specified (player count, all-or-nothing, no chat, fixed MGR, known group size, etc.), so the paper is useful for understanding how punishment affects cooperation rates under these conditions, but not for directly predicting efficiency as defined by group payoff relative to the cooperative optimum.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Group size is directly manipulated (4, 20, 40). Punishment increases cooperation in all group sizes, but there is no significant interaction between group size and punishment effect.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are one-shot (single round).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. In Study 2, contribution is all-or-nothing (contribute all 20 tokens or none). In Study 1, contribution is continuous, but the main punishment results are from Study 2.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. In Study 2, MPCR varies with group size (MGR fixed at 1.6, so MPCR = 1.6/n). In Study 1, both fixed MGR and fixed MPCR conditions are used.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment costs 1 token to the punisher and reduces 3 tokens from the punished (1:3 ratio).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3 tokens deducted per 1 token spent.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game is one-shot (single round).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the punishment and reputation conditions, participants are told they will learn about 'some or all members' decisions' before assigning punishment or choosing partners. In the control, decisions are anonymous.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is cooperation rate (binary contribution), not efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings.
- The experiment is one-shot; repeated interactions and their effects on efficiency or welfare are not studied.
- No direct measurement or analysis of efficiency, welfare, or group surplus is reported.
- The study is conducted online with anonymous groups; ecological validity for real-world group interactions may be limited.
- Only three group sizes (4, 20, 40) are tested, and only one punishment cost/tech parameter is used.
- Potential selection bias due to dropouts in larger groups is acknowledged but not measured.
