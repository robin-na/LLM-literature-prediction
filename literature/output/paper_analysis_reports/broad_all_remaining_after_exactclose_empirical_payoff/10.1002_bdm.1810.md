# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: A Description-Experience Gap in Social Interactions: Information about Interdependence and Its Effects on Cooperation
Authors: Martin, J. M., Gonzalez, C., Juvina, I., & Lebiere, C.
Journal: Journal of Behavioral Decision Making
Year: 2014

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- pair performance (sum of points earned by both players over 200 rounds)
- joint satisfaction (sum of satisfaction ratings)
- mutual cooperation rate (as it directly determines group payoff in IPD)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual cooperation rate
- reciprocation (cooperation after cooperation)
- punishment (defection after defection)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is pair performance (joint payoff), which is a direct measure of efficiency in the IPD context. Satisfaction is also reported. Behavioral outcomes (cooperation, reciprocation, punishment) are analyzed as mechanisms.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that providing more information about interdependence in a two-player, 200-round IPD increases both mutual cooperation and joint payoffs (efficiency). Four information conditions were tested: No-Info (players unaware of the other), Min-Info (aware of other but cannot see their actions), Mid-Info (can see other's actions and outcomes), and Max-Info (can also see the full payoff matrix). Joint performance (sum of points) was lowest in No-Info and Min-Info, higher in Mid-Info, and highest in Max-Info. The effect of information on performance is fully mediated by mutual cooperation. Punishment (defection after defection) is high in all conditions and does not vary with information. The results suggest that awareness and transparency of interdependence are key to achieving higher efficiency in repeated social dilemmas.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in repeated dyadic social dilemmas structurally similar to the IPD, increasing the amount of information about interdependence (especially showing others' actions and the payoff matrix) leads to higher efficiency (joint payoff). For prediction, if a public-goods-like game allows players to see others' actions and/or the payoff structure, one should expect higher efficiency than in games where such information is hidden. The findings are most directly applicable to two-player, repeated, simultaneous-move games without explicit punishment or reward mechanisms, but the mechanisms (reciprocation, not punishment) may generalize to other settings. The paper does not manipulate punishment cost or technology, nor does it include explicit costly punishment or reward options, so its direct predictive value is limited for games with those features.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in pairs (2 players).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 200 rounds per game; number of rounds is not revealed to participants.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between players; all interaction is via choices.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Binary choice each round: Action A (defect) or Action B (cooperate).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Payoff matrix: mutual cooperation yields 1 point each; mutual defection yields -1 each; unilateral cooperation yields -10 (cooperator), 10 (defector). MPCR is not directly defined, but the structure is standard for IPD.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of rounds (200) is not revealed to participants; rounds are unnumbered.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In Mid-Info and Max-Info conditions, players see the other's actions and outcomes each round; in No-Info and Min-Info, they do not. Seeing others' actions/outcomes increases efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a two-player iterated prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not generalize to larger groups or to standard PGGs.
- No explicit costly punishment or reward mechanisms are present; punishment is operationalized as defection after defection, not as a separate action.
- All games are dyadic and simultaneous-move; findings may not apply to multi-player or sequential games.
- The effect of information is only tested in the absence of explicit punishment/reward options.
- Participants were not randomly assigned to conditions for logistical reasons, though blocks were dispersed to minimize confounds.
- The payoff structure is fixed and asymmetric (large negative for unilateral cooperation), which may not match all PGG-like environments.
