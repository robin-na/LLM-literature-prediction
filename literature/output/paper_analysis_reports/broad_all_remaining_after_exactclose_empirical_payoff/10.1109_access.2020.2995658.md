## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- strategy selection (knowledge input or not)
- evolutionary stability of cooperation
- proportion of input strategy over time
Outcome notes:
The paper models and simulates the dynamic evolution of cooperation (knowledge input) between two parties (state-owned and private enterprise TMTs) under different parameter regimes. Outcomes are primarily the stable strategies and the evolution of the probability of choosing input, not explicit group payoffs or efficiency ratios.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model shows that increasing knowledge flow, government reward, and government penalty (punishment) promotes the selection of the cooperative (knowledge input) strategy by TMTs, while higher input costs hinder cooperation. Under some parameter regimes, mutual cooperation (input, input) is the stable outcome; in others, only one party inputs knowledge. The effect of government penalty (punishment) is to increase the likelihood of cooperation, but the paper does not quantify efficiency or group payoff. The findings are based on theoretical analysis and numerical simulation of the evolutionary dynamics, not on empirical or experimental data.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, theoretical support for the idea that the presence and magnitude of punishment (government penalty) can increase the likelihood of cooperative behavior in a two-player, repeated interaction setting with knowledge input as the cooperative act. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, so it cannot be used directly to predict efficiency changes from punishment in public goods games. The model's structure and findings may be useful for mechanism or moderator reasoning, especially regarding the effect of punishment cost and effectiveness, but the lack of payoff-based outcomes limits its direct predictive value for efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is a two-player game (state-owned TMT and private TMT).
- number of rounds: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. The model is dynamic and continuous-time (evolutionary game), not a discrete finite-round game. The number of rounds is not specified, but the process is modeled as ongoing.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each player chooses between two strategies: input (cooperate) or not input (defect). This is an all-or-nothing contribution structure.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Government penalty (p1, p2) is a cost imposed on a party that terminates knowledge input. Increasing penalty increases cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is modeled as a direct penalty; effectiveness is not varied independently from cost, but higher penalty increases cooperation.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Government reward (r1, r2) is present and varied; higher reward increases cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are used.
- Outcomes are limited to strategy selection and evolutionary stability, not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare.
- The model is a two-player, asymmetric game, not a standard public goods game with multiple players.
- Punishment is implemented as an exogenous government penalty, not peer punishment.
- No explicit efficiency or group payoff outcomes are reported, limiting direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
