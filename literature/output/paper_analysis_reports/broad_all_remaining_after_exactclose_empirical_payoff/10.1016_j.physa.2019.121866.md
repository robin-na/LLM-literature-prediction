The paper analyzes the long-run frequencies of different strategy types (cooperation, defection, exclusion) and the probability of the system being in each phase, but does not directly report efficiency, group payoff, or total welfare. Payoff calculations are used to determine evolutionary dynamics, but efficiency or group payoff is not a reported outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the introduction of an exclusion-type punishment mechanism (social exclusion) in a PGG with increasing returns to scale can promote the emergence of cooperation, depending on the cost and effectiveness of exclusion, the returns to scale, and the public goods multiplier. Increasing the effectiveness of exclusion, lowering its cost, or increasing the public goods multiplier can shift the system from all-defection to coexistence of cooperation and exclusion. However, simply increasing returns to scale cannot eliminate defection entirely in the presence of exclusion; it only expands the parameter region where cooperation and exclusion coexist. In structured populations, increasing returns to scale can more directly promote cooperation. The model does not report efficiency or group payoff, so the findings are about the prevalence of cooperative strategies rather than direct payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task. It shows that the presence and effectiveness of an exclusion-based punishment mechanism can substantially increase the prevalence of cooperation in PGG-like environments, especially when the cost of exclusion is low and its effectiveness is high. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be used to directly predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The results suggest that, under certain parameter regimes, enabling punishment (via exclusion) is likely to increase efficiency, but this is inferred from the prevalence of cooperation rather than direct payoff data. The findings are most relevant for settings where punishment is implemented as social exclusion rather than direct fines.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size M is a model parameter; main results use M=30 (well-mixed) and N=5 (group size for each PGG). Structured population simulations use 100x100, 200x200, or 500x500 lattices.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is evolutionary and stochastic, with continuous updating; there is no fixed number of rounds, but the process is iterated until equilibrium. In simulations, time is measured in Monte Carlo steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: individuals choose to cooperate (contribute 1), defect (contribute 0), or exclude (contribute 1 and pay exclusion cost).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is determined by the amplification factor r and the increasing-returns-to-scale coefficient alpha. r is varied (e.g., r=2, 3.5, 4), and alpha is varied (e.g., alpha=1, 1.3, 1.5).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Unit exclusion cost c_E is a key parameter; lower c_E increases the prevalence of cooperation and exclusion. c_E is varied (e.g., c_E=0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is modeled as the probability of exclusion success (beta). Higher beta increases the prevalence of cooperation and exclusion.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are reported.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or total welfare are not directly reported as outcomes; only the prevalence of cooperation, defection, and exclusion strategies is analyzed.
- Punishment is modeled as social exclusion (removal from benefit sharing), not as direct fines or monetary punishment, which may limit generalizability to other punishment forms.
- The model assumes well-mixed or structured populations but does not address heterogeneity in player behavior or preferences.
- No analysis of communication, reward mechanisms, or other institutional features beyond exclusion.
