# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Image scoring and cooperation in a cleaner fish mutualism
Authors: Bshary, R., & Grutter, A. S.
Journal: Nature
Year: 2006

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- Time client fish spent near cooperative vs unknown-cooperation-level cleaner fish
- Proportion of less-preferred food (flakes) eaten by cleaner fish in image-scoring vs non-image-scoring scenarios
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are behavioral: client preference for cooperative cleaners (image scoring) and cleaner fish's willingness to feed against their preference (cooperation) in the presence of image-scoring bystanders. No direct measurement of group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study shows that client fish preferentially associate with cleaner fish that are observed to behave cooperatively, providing evidence for image scoring in a non-human animal. Cleaner fish, in turn, behave more cooperatively (feed against their preference) when their actions are being observed by a potential client (image-scoring scenario) compared to when they are not observed. This supports the hypothesis that reputation-based partner choice can promote cooperation. However, the study does not measure group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, and the experimental setup is not a public goods game but an asymmetric mutualism with partner choice.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that reputation mechanisms (image scoring) can increase cooperative behavior in asymmetric mutualisms, which may be relevant for understanding the effects of reputation or partner choice in public goods games. However, it does not provide direct evidence about the effect of punishment or sanctions on efficiency or payoff in public goods games or their close variants. The findings may inform predictions about the role of observation and reputation in promoting cooperation, but not about the quantitative effect of punishment on efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Experiments involve dyadic interactions (one client, one cleaner) or triads (one client, two cleaners), not group settings typical of public goods games.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Cleaner fish underwent 20 experimental rounds per scenario; not a repeated public goods game, but repeated trials for learning/behavioral measurement.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The key manipulation is whether the bystander (client) can observe the cleaner's behavior (image scoring scenario). Observation increases cooperative behavior.

## Important Limitations
- The experimental design is not a public goods game or a close variant; it is an asymmetric mutualism with dyadic or triadic interactions.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings; only behavioral outcomes are measured.
- No explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present; the study focuses on reputation/image scoring and partner choice.
- Findings may not generalize to human public goods games or to settings with explicit punishment or group-level cooperation.
