## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Rank-Order-VCM, which rewards higher contributors with a larger share of the public good (via a higher personal multiplier), leads to significantly higher and more sustained contributions than a control treatment with random assignment of multipliers. In Rank-Order-VCM, median contributions are at the maximum in almost all rounds, while in the control, contributions decline to near zero. The mechanism avoids the efficiency loss of fixed-prize contests by not subtracting a prize from the pool. The results suggest that introducing rank-based competition for access to an impure public good can mitigate free-riding and move outcomes closer to the social optimum, increasing group welfare and implied efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that introducing a rank-based competition mechanism (where higher contributors receive a larger share of the public good) can dramatically increase contributions and implied efficiency compared to a control with random assignment of benefits. While the main reported outcomes are contributions, the payoff structure ensures that higher contributions translate into higher group payoffs and efficiency, especially since no costly prize is subtracted from the pool. For prediction tasks, the findings suggest that mechanisms which create endogenous, contribution-dependent rewards (even without explicit punishment or costly rewards) can outperform standard VCMs and fixed-prize contests in terms of efficiency. However, the results are most directly applicable to impure public goods with preferential access, not to standard PGGs with explicit punishment or peer-sanctioning. The control efficiency (with random assignment) is much lower than in the treatment, so the predicted treatment efficiency should be substantially higher under similar design parameters.

## Important Limitations
- The main reported outcomes are contribution levels, not explicit efficiency or group payoff statistics; efficiency is discussed but not directly measured.
- The mechanism is tested only for impure public goods with preferential access, not for standard PGGs or settings with explicit punishment or peer-sanctioning.
- Group size is fixed at 4; results may not generalize to larger groups.
- No explicit punishment or costly reward mechanism is present, so findings may not transfer to settings where such mechanisms are central.
- Laboratory setting with student subjects; field generalizability is not established.
- No variation in key parameters such as group size, MPCR, or reward structure; only one parameterization is tested.
