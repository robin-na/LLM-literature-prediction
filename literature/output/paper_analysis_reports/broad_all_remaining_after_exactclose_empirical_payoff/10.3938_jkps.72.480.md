Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the fraction of cooperative strategies and the phase structure of the population. Payoff is used in the model's update rules, but aggregate efficiency, group payoff, or welfare are not directly reported or analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that allowing informed cooperators to rewire their network connections at a cost can substantially increase the prevalence of cooperation in public goods games on random networks. Higher rates of network rewiring (by informed cooperators) lower the threshold for cooperation to emerge, especially under adverse conditions (low synergy factor). The system exhibits distinct phases (full cooperation, full defection, coexistence, and segregated sub-networks), and the transition between these phases depends on the synergy factor and rewiring rate. However, the paper does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, and does not include explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanisms.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is not directly informative for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it does not implement or analyze punishment or sanctioning mechanisms. Instead, it studies the effect of costly information and network rewiring on cooperation rates. While the findings suggest that network adaptation can promote cooperation, they do not provide evidence about efficiency or payoff outcomes, nor about the effect of punishment. Thus, this paper should not be used as direct evidence for the prediction task focused on punishment and efficiency, though it may be contextually relevant for understanding alternative cooperation-promoting mechanisms.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size is set to N=10,000 in most simulations, with some results for N=50,000 and N=100,000 to check finite-size effects.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: each player either contributes 1 or 0 to the public good.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is r/N, where r is the synergy factor and N is the group size (number of players in a group). r is varied in the simulations.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not implement or analyze punishment or sanctioning mechanisms; it focuses on costly information and network rewiring.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are not reported or analyzed; only cooperation rates and phase structure are studied.
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with explicit punishment, reward, or communication mechanisms.
