## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that cultural group selection, enabled by human-specific social learning mechanisms, can explain the evolution and stability of large-scale cooperation and punishment in humans. It theorizes that punishment, when stabilized by conformist transmission, can support high levels of cooperation even in large, anonymous groups, and that these group-beneficial equilibria can be selected for at the group level. The paper does not provide empirical data or direct predictions about efficiency or payoff outcomes in public goods games, but it does argue that punishment and cooperation can be maintained at high frequency under plausible cultural evolutionary dynamics.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical support for the idea that punishment can stabilize cooperation and group-beneficial outcomes in large groups, especially when cultural transmission mechanisms are present. However, it does not provide empirical estimates or direct predictions about efficiency or payoff changes when punishment is enabled in public goods games or similar environments. Its main value for prediction is in highlighting the importance of group structure, cultural transmission, and norm enforcement (punishment) as moderators of cooperation and efficiency, but it does not specify how game design dimensions quantitatively affect efficiency outcomes.
