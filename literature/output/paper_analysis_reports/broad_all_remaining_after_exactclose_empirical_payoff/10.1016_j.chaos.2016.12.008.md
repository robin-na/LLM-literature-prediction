## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing a probabilistic peer-punishment mechanism—where the probability and severity of punishment are proportional to payoff differences—can increase both cooperation and the average payoff of all players in a spatial prisoner's dilemma game. The effect is robust across different network topologies (regular, random, scale-free) and for varying average degrees of connectivity, though the parameter range for positive effects narrows as connectivity increases. The model avoids antisocial punishment by design. The results suggest that, with appropriate parameterization, punishment can enhance group efficiency, not just cooperation rates.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that, in environments structurally similar to spatial prisoner's dilemma games, a well-designed probabilistic peer-punishment mechanism can increase group efficiency (average payoff), especially when the punishment is neither too weak nor too strong. The effect is sensitive to the network structure: as the average number of connections increases, the range of punishment parameters that yield efficiency gains becomes narrower. While the model is not a public goods game, the payoff-based outcome and the punishment mechanism are closely related, making the findings informative for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like settings, especially where network structure and punishment calibration are relevant.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not fully generalize to PGG settings.
- All results are from simulations/theory, not human experiments.
- The punishment mechanism is highly stylized and may not capture all relevant features of real-world or experimental punishment.
- No reward mechanism is modeled, so interaction effects between reward and punishment are not addressed.
- The effect of player count (N) is not varied; only network structure and punishment parameters are manipulated.
- No communication or information feedback is modeled.
