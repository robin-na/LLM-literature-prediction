The paper reports on average contribution levels, migration rates, and group satisfaction, but does not directly report efficiency, group payoff, or total welfare. All outcomes are based on simulated agent behavior in computational models.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study simulates spatial public goods games where agents can migrate between groups if unsatisfied. Four setups are compared: baseline (migration only), migration cost (punishment), rewarding scheme (loyalty reward), and combined. Migration costs (punishment) robustly increase average contribution, especially at low enhancement factors, and are more effective than rewards. The combined mechanism can further improve cooperation in some cases. The main reported outcome is average contribution, not efficiency or group payoff. The results suggest that institutional punishment (here, migration cost) is a stronger driver of cooperation than institutional rewards in this spatial setting.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that punishment-like mechanisms (here, migration costs) increase cooperation (contribution) more than rewards in spatial PGGs, especially at low enhancement factors. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be used directly to predict efficiency outcomes. The findings are most useful for understanding how migration costs and rewards affect contribution rates under different enhancement factors, group sizes, and migration probabilities. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper offers only adjacent, indirect support.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use 144 groups with 10 agents each (N=1440), and also test with 5 agents per group. Group size is varied in some robustness checks.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 800 or 2000 rounds, with outcomes measured at the end.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication is allowed; agents are in a 'blind model' with no information about others' contributions or payoffs.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is discrete but not all-or-nothing; agents choose from {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Enhancement factor r is varied from 1 to 3; MPCR is r divided by group size (n_i).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a migration cost c_m=0.2 paid by agents who migrate. Increasing migration cost increases cooperation (contribution).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the impact of migration cost per migration; higher cost increases cooperation. Only one value (c_m=0.2) is tested in detail.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Rewarding scheme is implemented as a loyalty reward (discount factor increases with group tenure). Rewards alone are less effective than punishment at increasing cooperation.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Reward is implemented as a discount factor on payoff, increasing with group tenure. The cost is not explicit, but the reward is not as effective as punishment.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Reward effectiveness is the increase in the discount factor per round (from 0.6 to 1.0). The effect is weaker than punishment.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of rounds is fixed and known in the simulation setup.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. No summary information about others is shown; agents are 'blind' to others' contributions and payoffs.

## Important Limitations
- The study is based entirely on computational simulations, not human experiments.
- The main outcome is average contribution, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- Punishment is implemented as a migration cost, which is not a standard PGG punishment mechanism.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or total group payoff.
- Agents have no information about others' actions or payoffs (blind model), which may limit generalizability to real-world or experimental PGGs.
- Reward and punishment mechanisms are institutional, not peer-to-peer.
