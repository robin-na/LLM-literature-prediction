# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The mixed impacts of peer punishments on common-pool resources: Multi-country experimental evidence
Authors: Angelsen, A., & Naime, J.
Journal: World Development
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=field_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- deforestation (resource extraction) per round
- frequency and type of punishment assigned
- change in deforestation after receiving punishment
- typology of first- and second-order cooperation
- prosocial vs. antisocial punishment rates
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: deforestation (extraction) choices, punishment assigned/received, and change in behavior after punishment. The paper does not report group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare directly, though it discusses how punishment changes the marginal incentives and payoffs for deforestation choices.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Peer punishment in a CPR extraction game increases cooperation (reduces deforestation) when it is prosocial (targeting above-average extractors), but reduces cooperation when it is antisocial (targeting cooperators). The frequency and impact of punishment vary by country and by group endowment inequality, with Indonesia showing much higher punishment rates and stronger norm enforcement. The experiment does not report group efficiency or total payoff, but shows that punishment changes the marginal incentives for extraction and that prosocial punishment can make deviation from the group norm unprofitable. Antisocial punishment, however, undermines cooperation. The findings highlight the importance of cultural and institutional context for the effectiveness of peer punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of peer punishment on cooperation in CPR-like games depends on the type of punishment (prosocial vs. antisocial), the cultural context, and group heterogeneity. While it does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, it shows that prosocial punishment increases cooperation and reduces extraction, which would be expected to increase efficiency in a standard PGG/CPR context. However, antisocial punishment can reduce cooperation and thus efficiency. The results suggest that the effectiveness of punishment for increasing efficiency is context-dependent and can be undermined by antisocial punishment. For prediction tasks, the paper supports the idea that punishment can improve efficiency when prosocial, but that the presence of antisocial punishment and cultural context are important moderators. The lack of direct efficiency or payoff data limits quantitative prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consisted of 6 participants in all sessions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each stage consisted of 6 rounds; the peer punishment treatment was one stage of 6 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat was allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (deforestation) was a discrete choice: participants chose how many plots (0 to max) to convert each round; not strictly all-or-nothing, but a discrete multi-level choice.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR (delta) was set at 0.4; each plot of forest gave 24 points to the group (4 per participant), each plot of agriculture gave 10 points to the individual.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Cost to punisher per punishment point is k (not numerically specified in main text, but payoff function shows cost structure).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment assigned reduces the punished player's payoff by 3k (i.e., punishment impact is 3x the cost to punisher).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants knew the number of rounds per stage (6 rounds per stage).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were shown others' deforestation choices (public monitoring) in the peer punishment stage.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment was anonymous; participants were identified by letters known only to themselves and the experimenter, and letters were changed each stage.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare outcomes directly; all main outcomes are behavioral (deforestation, punishment, cooperation).
- The experiment is a CPR extraction game, not a standard public goods game, though the structure is closely related.
- Punishment effects are analyzed in terms of behavioral change (deforestation reduction), not in terms of net group payoff or efficiency.
- The cost and impact of punishment are specified, but the net effect on group welfare (accounting for punishment costs) is not calculated.
- Cultural and contextual differences are strong, limiting generalizability.
- Antisocial punishment is substantial and undermines cooperation, which may limit the positive effect of punishment on efficiency in some contexts.
