# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Survey evidence on conditional norm enforcement
Authors: Traxler, C., & Winter, J.
Journal: European Journal of Political Economy
Year: 2012

## Study Type
theory; empirical subtype=N/A; experimental subtype=N/A

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- norm compliance rate
- probability of punishment
- reputation value
- critical mass of compliers
Outcome notes:
The model focuses on norm compliance, the probability of punishment, and the role of reputation, but does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes. The main dependent variable is the proportion of norm followers (compliance), not payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that both punishment and reputation can sustain norm compliance, but their roles differ depending on the distribution of social motivation in the population. When reputation effects are weak, higher levels of punishment are needed to enforce the norm. When reputation effects are strong, norms can be sustained with little or no punishment. The model shows two stable equilibria: one with high compliance (if initial compliance is above a critical threshold) and one with low compliance (if initial compliance is below the threshold). The probability of punishment increases as the benefit-cost ratio for punishers decreases (i.e., as the psychological cost of punishing decreases). The interaction of punishment and reputation provides a robust mechanism for norm stability.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical support for the idea that punishment can increase norm compliance, especially when reputation effects are weak. However, it does not provide quantitative predictions about efficiency or group payoff, nor does it specify the effects of game design dimensions such as MPCR, player count, or number of rounds. The model is adjacent to public goods games but is not directly parameterized in terms of efficiency. For prediction tasks, the main insight is that punishment is more effective at sustaining compliance when reputation incentives are weak, and that the two mechanisms can substitute for each other to some extent. The paper does not inform how punishment affects efficiency or payoff in public goods games with specific design features.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. The model assumes a continuum of agents, with two groups each comprising half the population. There is no fixed small player count as in typical PGGs.
- number of rounds: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. The model is dynamic and analyzes both short-run and long-run equilibria, but does not specify a finite number of rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (norm compliance) is binary: agents either comply (x=1) or not (x=0).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is costly for punishers (cost T > 0); the model analyzes how this cost affects the probability of punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is modeled as a fixed penalty C imposed on violators; the model analyzes how this penalty interacts with punishment probability.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Agents know the current proportion of norm followers (μ) in the population.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental evidence.
- The model is adjacent to public goods games but does not explicitly model group payoffs, efficiency, or welfare.
- Outcomes are behavioral (compliance, punishment probability), not payoff-based.
- No quantitative predictions about efficiency or the effect of specific game design dimensions such as MPCR, player count, or number of rounds.
- No consideration of communication, reward mechanisms, or identification of punishers.
