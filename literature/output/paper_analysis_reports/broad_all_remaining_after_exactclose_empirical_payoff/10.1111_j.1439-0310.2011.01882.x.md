Outcome notes:
The paper is a theoretical review and framework, not an empirical or modeling study. It does not report experimental or observational outcomes, but rather classifies and predicts which partner control mechanisms (including punishment) are likely to stabilize cooperation under different game structures. Payoff and efficiency are discussed conceptually, but not measured or modeled quantitatively.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper provides a decision-tree framework linking specific game structures (defined by investment, payoff structure, symmetry, repetition, and partner choice) to the partner control mechanisms that can stabilize cooperation. Punishment is predicted to be a viable control mechanism in repeated interactions without partner choice, especially when both players can invest and defect. The framework also highlights the roles of reward, sanctions, partner switching, and indirect reciprocity in other game structures. The review does not provide empirical or quantitative evidence on efficiency or payoff effects of punishment, but it does clarify the conditions under which punishment is theoretically expected to promote cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is useful for identifying which game structures are theoretically compatible with punishment as a mechanism for stabilizing cooperation, but it does not provide quantitative or empirical evidence on the efficiency or payoff effects of punishment in public goods or similar games. For the downstream prediction task, the framework can help map which design dimensions (e.g., repeated interactions, partner choice, symmetry) are necessary for punishment to be a relevant mechanism, but it cannot inform the magnitude or direction of efficiency changes. The paper is most useful for contextualizing when punishment is likely to be effective, not for predicting efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Framework is explicitly for pairwise (2-player) interactions; group size is fixed at 2.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of interactions (one-off vs repeated) is a key parameter in the framework; repeated interactions are necessary for punishment to be a relevant mechanism.
- punishment cost: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Punishment is defined as a behavior that reduces the immediate payoffs of both the actor and the target, implying a cost to the punisher, but no quantitative values or manipulations are discussed.
- reward availability: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Reward (positive reciprocity) is discussed as a control mechanism in some game structures, but not in a parameterized or manipulated way.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is a theoretical review and does not provide empirical or quantitative evidence on efficiency or payoff outcomes.
- The framework is limited to pairwise (2-player) interactions and does not address group public goods games directly.
- No quantitative or parameterized predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff are provided.
- Key game design dimensions relevant to public goods games (e.g., player count >2, mpcr, all-or-nothing vs continuous contribution) are not addressed.
- No discussion of how punishment cost or effectiveness quantitatively moderates outcomes.
