# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Social norms, political polarization, and vaccination attitudes: Evidence from a survey experiment in Turkey
Authors: Kaba, M., Koyuncu, M., Schneider, S. O., & Sutter, M.
Journal: European Economic Review
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- outgroup discrimination (allocation bias)
- amount allocated to ingroup/outgroup in allocation tasks
- change in outgroup bias after informational treatments
- predictors of anti-vaxxer identity
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is outgroup bias in allocation tasks (difference in money allocated to ingroup vs outgroup), interpreted as a measure of discrimination or 'punishment' but not as group efficiency or total payoff. No direct analysis of group efficiency, welfare, or total earnings is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that both pro- and anti-vaxxers discriminate against each other in allocation tasks, with pro-vaxxers showing stronger outgroup bias (interpreted as 'punishment' of anti-vaxxers for norm violation). Political polarization amplifies this discrimination. Randomized informational treatments (reminders of pandemic health/economic costs or the Russia-Ukraine war) reduce outgroup bias, especially the war treatment among pro-vaxxers. However, the main outcome is behavioral (allocation bias), not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. The allocation tasks and modified public goods game measure outgroup discrimination, not group efficiency or total earnings. The findings may be useful for understanding how social identity and polarization affect norm enforcement and discrimination, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency changes due to punishment mechanisms in PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Allocation tasks are between two players (or third-party allocation between two others); modified public goods game is two-player, each with 100 TL endowment.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each allocation task is a one-shot decision; no repeated rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between participants is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Allocation choices are in discrete steps (20 TL increments) between 0 and 100 TL; not strictly all-or-nothing, but not fully continuous.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the modified public goods game, the amount sent to the partner is doubled (MPCR = 2 for the recipient, but not pooled).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the structure: all tasks are one-shot; no repeated or hidden rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told the group identity (pro/anti-vaxxer) and income group of their partner, but not detailed summaries of others' behavior.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is outgroup discrimination (allocation bias), not group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff.
- No explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism with cost to punisher; 'punishment' is interpreted as allocation bias.
- The modified public goods game is not a standard PGG; contributions are sent directly to the partner and doubled, not pooled.
- No analysis of how discrimination or 'punishment' affects group efficiency or public good provision.
- Findings may not generalize to standard PGGs with explicit punishment/reward mechanisms.
