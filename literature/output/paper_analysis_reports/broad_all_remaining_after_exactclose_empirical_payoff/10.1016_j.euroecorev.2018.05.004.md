# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Cooperation, punishment and organized crime: a lab-in-the-field experiment in southern Italy
Authors: Nese, A., O'Higgins, N., Sbriglia, P., & Scudiero, M.
Journal: European Economic Review
Year: 2018

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- punishment frequency
- punishment amount
- attitudinal indices (cooperation, reciprocity, locus of control)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are cooperation rates in the Prisoner's Dilemma and Prisoner's Dilemma with third-party punishment, and the amount/frequency of punishment assigned. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that Camorra inmates are highly cooperative and strongly punish defectors, consistent with their internal codes of conduct. However, when external punishment (third-party punishment) is introduced, their cooperation decreases, indicating a rejection of external authority. Ordinary criminals, in contrast, increase cooperation when external punishment is possible, suggesting opportunism. Students' cooperation also decreases with TPP. The main outcomes are behavioral (cooperation and punishment), not efficiency or group payoff. The findings highlight the importance of group-specific social norms and attitudes in shaping responses to punishment opportunities.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence on how the introduction of punishment affects cooperation rates in a Prisoner's Dilemma setting, but does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. The design is adjacent to public goods games, as it uses a two-player PD and a three-player PD with third-party punishment, rather than a standard multi-player PGG. The results suggest that the effect of punishment on cooperation (and by extension, possibly on efficiency) is highly dependent on group norms and attitudes: punishment can reduce cooperation in some groups (students, Camorra) and increase it in others (ordinary criminals). However, because efficiency is not measured, the paper's direct value for predicting efficiency outcomes is limited. The evidence is most useful for understanding behavioral responses to punishment under different social norms, rather than for quantitative prediction of efficiency changes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. PD is 2-player; PD-TPP is 3-player (A, B, C).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are one-shot (single round).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is all-or-nothing: keep all tokens or send all tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. If a player sends 10 tokens, the partner receives 30 (tripled). For the sender, cost is 10, benefit to partner is 30. In 2-player PD, this is equivalent to a PGG with MPCR = 1.5 (30/20), but the structure is PD, not PGG.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. In PD-TPP, third party (C) spends 1 token to reduce A or B's payoff by 3 tokens.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3:1 (1 token spent by C reduces target's payoff by 3 tokens).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is not available; only punishment (deduction) is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is one-shot; participants know this.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed of the outcome structure and, after the experiment, results are posted, but during the game, no summary information about others' choices is provided.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; players do not know who punished whom.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma and a one-shot PD with third-party punishment, not a standard repeated or multi-player public goods game.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only behavioral outcomes (cooperation, punishment) are analyzed.
- The sample is specific to students, ordinary criminals, and Camorra inmates in Southern Italy, mostly in prison settings, which may limit generalizability.
- Punishment is only available as third-party punishment, not peer punishment or reward.
- The effect of punishment on actual group efficiency or surplus cannot be determined from the reported results.
