## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:

Non-payoff outcomes:
- steady-state frequencies of cooperators, defectors, and punishers
- total cooperation density (sum of cooperator and punisher frequencies)
- phase diagrams showing regions of full cooperation vs. mixed states
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are the steady-state frequencies of strategies (cooperation, defection, punishment) and the conditions for full cooperation or mixed states. Payoff functions are explicitly modeled, but the main results are reported in terms of behavioral frequencies and phase boundaries, not group efficiency or total payoff ratios.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that higher punishment severity, higher disguise cost, lower cost-to-benefit ratio, and larger group size all promote the extinction of defectors and the prevalence of cooperation (cooperators and punishers). When these parameters are less favorable, the system remains in a mixed state with both cooperators and defectors. The model shows that the system typically evolves to a state with only two strategies (either cooperators and punishers, or cooperators and defectors), and the boundaries between these regimes are mapped as functions of the key parameters. The results are robust to both deterministic replicator dynamics and stochastic mutation-selection dynamics in finite populations.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect but informative evidence about how punishment parameters (cost, effectiveness), group size, and cost-to-benefit ratio affect the prevalence of cooperation in an N-person social dilemma with punishment. While the model is for the snowdrift game (not a standard PGG), and the main outcomes are behavioral frequencies rather than efficiency, the results suggest that increasing punishment severity, increasing group size, and increasing the cost for defectors to avoid punishment all make punishment more effective at promoting cooperation. However, the paper does not report group efficiency or total payoff ratios, so direct prediction of efficiency outcomes is not possible. The findings are most useful for understanding qualitative moderators of punishment effectiveness in related social dilemmas.

## Important Limitations
- The model is for the N-person snowdrift game, not the standard public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- Main outcomes are steady-state frequencies of strategies, not group efficiency or total payoff ratios.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are theoretical and simulation-based.
- No explicit analysis of group efficiency or welfare; only indirect implications for efficiency can be drawn.
- No consideration of communication, reward, or other institutional features beyond peer punishment and disguise.
