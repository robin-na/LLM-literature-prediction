Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the frequencies of different strategies (cooperators, defectors, punishers) and the evolutionary dynamics under different punishment regimes. There is no direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total welfare. Some discussion of payoffs is present in the model description, but simulation results and figures report only strategy frequencies and spatial patterns.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that peer punishment leads to higher levels of cooperation than pool punishment in spatial public goods games. When the threshold for switching to pool punishment is high (i.e., more peer punishment), cooperation is more prevalent. The coexistence of both punishment types is explored, and the transition between regimes is mapped. The main findings are based on the frequencies of cooperators, defectors, and punishers, not on efficiency or group payoff. The ratio of fine to cost is higher for peer punishment, which is suggested as a reason for its greater effectiveness in promoting cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that peer punishment is more effective than pool punishment in increasing the prevalence of cooperation in spatial PGGs. However, because the outcomes are reported in terms of strategy frequencies rather than efficiency or group payoff, the results are only indirectly informative for predicting efficiency changes due to punishment. The model and parameterization are clearly described, so the paper can inform mechanistic expectations about how punishment type and its implementation affect cooperation, but not directly about efficiency or welfare outcomes.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 5 players (G=5), and the lattice size L varies from 300 to 1500, so total player count is L^2. Each player participates in 5 overlapping groups.

- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. For peer punishment, cost per punished defector is γ/(G-1) with γ=0.05. For pool punishment, each punisher pays γ/(G-1) per group.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes; all results are in terms of strategy frequencies.
- Findings are based on agent-based simulations, not empirical or experimental data.
- The model assumes spatial structure (lattice or small-world network), which may limit generalizability to well-mixed or real-world settings.
- No direct analysis of how punishment affects total group payoff or efficiency, so implications for efficiency must be inferred indirectly.
- No reward mechanisms or communication are considered.
