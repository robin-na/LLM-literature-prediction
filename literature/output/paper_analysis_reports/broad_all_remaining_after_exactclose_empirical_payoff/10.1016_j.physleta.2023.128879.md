Journal: Physics Letters A

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- average cooperation level (\eta_C)
- average punishment probability (P_f)
- average punishment cost (C_f)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the average cooperation level (fraction of cooperators). The paper also reports average punishment probability and average punishment cost, but does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. Payoff functions are specified, but aggregate payoff outcomes are not analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that sampling punishment can dramatically increase the average cooperation level in both linear (PGG) and nonlinear (CRD) social dilemmas, provided the punishment intensity is above a threshold. Low punishment thresholds and small sample sizes are optimal, as they maximize cooperation and minimize punishment cost. The effect of punishment intensity is nonlinear: below a threshold, it is ineffective, but above the threshold, further increases yield little additional benefit. These findings are robust across both PGG and CRD models. However, the paper does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, so the direct effect on efficiency cannot be determined.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that institutional punishment mechanisms—specifically, sampling punishment—can increase cooperation rates in PGG-like environments, especially when punishment is sufficiently intense, the threshold for triggering punishment is low, and the sample size is small. However, because the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings are most useful for predicting changes in cooperation rates, not payoffs. If the downstream prediction task assumes a strong link between cooperation and efficiency, these results may be indirectly informative, but caution is warranted.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size Z=100 and group size N=5 are specified in model and figures. The effect of varying player count is not analyzed.
- number of rounds: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. The model is evolutionary and does not have discrete rounds in the experimental sense, but simulates repeated interactions to stationary distribution.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: unconditional cooperation (contribute c) or defection (contribute nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r=3, group size N=5, so MPCR = r/N = 0.6 in PGG model.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a fine \beta imposed on defectors; the cost to the institution is tracked as average punishment cost C_f. The cost to the punisher is not paid by individuals but by an external institution.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the fine \beta per punished defector; \beta is varied in the analysis.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or total earnings; only cooperation rates and punishment-related quantities are analyzed.
- The model assumes an external institution pays the cost of punishment, which may not match experimental or real-world settings where individuals bear punishment costs.
- No empirical or experimental data are provided.
- No analysis of feedback, communication, or information structure is included.
- Findings on cooperation may not directly translate to efficiency or payoff outcomes, especially if increased cooperation does not always maximize group payoff (e.g., due to punishment costs).
