Outcome notes:
The main outcome is the steady-state fraction of cooperators (\rho_c) as a function of punishment fine (\alpha) and temptation to defect (r). No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare is reported. All results are in terms of cooperation rates, not payoffs.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that mutual punishment in the snowdrift game can either promote or inhibit cooperation depending on the temptation to defect and the punishment fine. For low temptation to defect, increasing punishment fine increases cooperation; for high temptation, it decreases cooperation; for intermediate temptation, there is a non-monotonic relationship. The main reported outcome is the steady-state fraction of cooperators, not efficiency or group payoff. The results are shown for both well-mixed and structured populations, with phase transitions in cooperation rates observed. The findings are relevant for understanding how mutual punishment (peer pressure) affects conformity and cooperation, but do not provide direct evidence on efficiency or payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency or group payoff in public-goods-game-like environments with punishment. Its results are based on the snowdrift game, which is adjacent but not equivalent to the PGG, and the main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency. The mutual punishment mechanism is novel and may inform mechanism design, but the lack of payoff or efficiency analysis limits its direct applicability to the prediction task. The findings may be useful for understanding how punishment parameters affect cooperation rates, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency changes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Structured population simulations use a 100x100 lattice (10,000 players); well-mixed population is analyzed theoretically for arbitrary size.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 50,000 time steps, with steady-state values averaged over the last 2,000 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: players choose to cooperate or defect (all-or-nothing).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment fine (\alpha) is the only punishment parameter; cost to punisher is absorbed into the fine, as punishment is mutual.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a fine \alpha per neighbor with a different strategy; both parties pay the fine.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses the snowdrift game, which is adjacent but not equivalent to the public goods game; results may not transfer directly.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- No direct analysis of payoff-based outcomes; implications for efficiency are not reported.
- The mutual punishment mechanism is stylized and may not correspond to standard punishment in PGG experiments.
- No human subjects or empirical data; results are theoretical and simulation-based.
