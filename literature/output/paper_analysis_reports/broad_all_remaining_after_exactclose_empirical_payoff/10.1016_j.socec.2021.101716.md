## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper reviews the literature on punishment in public goods games, emphasizing that while punishment can sustain cooperation, the presence of antisocial punishment (punishment of cooperators) can undermine this effect. It highlights the importance of reputation, group size, and social context in moderating the effectiveness of punishment. The paper does not present new data or direct analyses of efficiency or group payoff, but provides a conceptual overview of when and why punishment may or may not improve cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical and conceptual background on the mechanisms by which punishment can affect cooperation in public goods games, including the role of antisocial punishment and reputation. However, it does not provide quantitative or empirical evidence on efficiency or payoff outcomes, nor does it systematically analyze how game design dimensions affect the efficiency impact of punishment. Its value for prediction is mainly in highlighting potential moderators (e.g., group size, reputation systems, presence of antisocial punishment), but it does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency changes from design parameters.
