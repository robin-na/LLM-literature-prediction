The paper is theoretical and simulation-based, focusing on equilibrium actions, payoffs, and the coevolution of actions, norms, and beliefs. It does not report empirical data but provides analytical and simulation results for payoff-based outcomes in public goods and related games.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model predicts that, in repeated public goods and related games, the long-term average group action and payoff converge to the Nash equilibrium based on material payoffs when there is no external authority. Social and cognitive factors (such as conformity, cognitive dissonance, and beliefs about others) affect the distribution and variance of actions and beliefs, but do not shift the group average away from the material-payoff optimum in the absence of authority. When an external authority promotes a particular action, group behavior can shift toward the promoted standard, sometimes increasing or decreasing efficiency depending on the alignment of the promoted action with the material optimum. The model does not include explicit peer punishment or sanctioning as a treatment variable, so it does not directly inform the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides a theoretical baseline for predicting efficiency in public goods and related games without explicit punishment mechanisms. It shows that, absent external authority or explicit sanctions, efficiency is determined by material payoffs, and social/cognitive factors mainly affect variance and convergence speed. For the downstream prediction task, this paper suggests that, unless punishment is modeled as a material cost for non-conformity (as in the 'disapproval by peers' term), enabling punishment would require additional modeling or empirical evidence. The model is most useful for predicting control (no-punishment) efficiency and for understanding the role of soft normative pressures, but does not provide direct evidence for the effect of enabling explicit punishment on efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not report empirical or experimental data.
- No explicit peer punishment or sanctioning mechanism is modeled; only soft normative pressures (disapproval, conformity) are included.
- The model does not allow direct estimation of the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency.
- Findings are limited to long-term equilibria in repeated games with fixed group composition and continuous action spaces.
- External authority is modeled, but not peer-imposed sanctions or decentralized punishment.
- Transient (short-term) dynamics and networked or changing group structures are not addressed.
