## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in spatial public goods games with both prosocial and antisocial punishment, the negative effects of antisocial punishment on cooperation and efficiency are largely neutralized by second-order free-riding among defectors. When the synergy factor (multiplication factor) is high, cooperation is sustained by network reciprocity alone, and antisocial punishment is negligible. When the synergy factor is low, prosocial punishment can still restore cooperation and efficiency, because nonpunishing defectors (second-order free-riders) undermine antisocial punishers, allowing punishing cooperators to dominate. The result is that, across a wide range of parameters, the introduction of punishment increases group efficiency, even in the presence of antisocial punishment. The paper also identifies parameter regions with stable coexistence of strategies, but the main result is that prosocial punishment is robustly effective in structured populations.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support for the prediction that, in spatially structured public goods games, enabling punishment (even when antisocial punishment is possible) will increase group efficiency compared to the control (no punishment) condition, except in cases where punishment is extremely costly or the synergy factor is very low. The results suggest that the presence of antisocial punishment does not undermine the efficiency gains from prosocial punishment in structured populations, due to the protective effect of second-order free-riding. The findings are most directly applicable to games with local interactions, fixed group size (G=5), and continuous contribution choices. The mapping from strategy composition to efficiency is direct, so the phase diagrams and stationary states can be used to predict efficiency outcomes under different parameter settings (e.g., player count, mpcr, punishment cost, punishment effectiveness). The paper does not address chat, communication, or information dimensions, so predictions for those features are not informed.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are presented.
- The model assumes spatial structure (square lattice) and local interactions; results may not generalize to well-mixed or non-spatial populations.
- Only binary contribution choices (all-or-nothing) are modeled; continuous contribution levels are not considered.
- No communication, reputation, or information feedback mechanisms are included.
- No reward mechanisms are present; only punishment (prosocial and antisocial) is modeled.
- Efficiency outcomes are inferred from stationary strategy fractions, not from explicit group payoff calculations.
- The model does not address the effects of finite rounds, endgame effects, or explicit knowledge of game length.
