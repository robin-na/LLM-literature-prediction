## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The chapter argues that a wide variety of game-theoretic mechanisms—including punishment, sanctions, partner choice, and reciprocity—are necessary to explain the diversity of cooperative behaviors observed in nature. In n-player (public goods) games, the chapter discusses the tragedy of the commons and the theoretical role of punishment in stabilizing cooperation, especially in humans. It reviews empirical examples and theoretical models but does not provide quantitative data on efficiency or group payoff. The discussion of punishment is detailed, especially in the context of human experiments (citing Fehr & Gächter 2002 and others), but the chapter itself does not present new empirical results or efficiency measurements.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is useful for understanding the conceptual and mechanistic role of punishment in public goods and related games, and for mapping the diversity of game structures and partner control mechanisms. However, it does not provide direct quantitative evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff, nor does it report experimental manipulations of game design dimensions. For prediction tasks, this chapter can inform the expected direction and mechanisms by which punishment may affect cooperation, but not the magnitude or efficiency outcomes under specific game parameterizations.
