## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a social monitoring mechanism (SMM) that rewards judges based on the observed cooperation rate among participants leads to higher levels of cooperation and lower corruption in the simulated two-layer network. The effect is robust across different parameter settings, including reward/fine amounts, the social reward criterion, the presence of zealots, and the strength of the reward factor. SMM is more effective than simply increasing rewards/fines in the absence of SMM. The model is applied to both Prisoner's Dilemma and Snowdrift Game variants, showing similar qualitative results. However, the paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes, so the findings are limited to behavioral outcomes (cooperation/corruption rates).

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that mechanisms which reward third-party punishers based on group cooperation rates can increase cooperation and reduce corruption in environments structurally similar to the Prisoner's Dilemma and Snowdrift Game. However, because the outcomes are reported as cooperation rates rather than efficiency or group payoff, the results should be interpreted as informative about behavioral mechanisms rather than direct efficiency effects. The model is not a standard public goods game, but the two-layer structure and the focus on punishment/reward mechanisms are adjacent to PGG-like environments. The findings suggest that adding a social monitoring/reward mechanism for punishers may increase cooperation, but the effect on efficiency is not directly quantified.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are presented.
- Outcomes are reported as cooperation and corruption rates, not as group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- The game structure is a two-layer network with one-to-one participant-judge mapping, which is not a standard public goods game.
- Punishment and reward costs to judges are not explicitly modeled as deductions from judges' payoffs (except for bribes to corrupt judges).
- No explicit analysis of group efficiency or surplus is provided, limiting direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
- The model assumes infinite or very large populations (L=100), which may not generalize to small-group laboratory settings.
