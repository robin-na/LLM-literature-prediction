# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Obedience to the symbol of authority: Experimental evidence on the symbolic source of legitimate authority*
Authors: Xu, Z. C.
Journal: European Journal of Political Economy
Year: 2023

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- total profit (group and role-specific earnings, Table A7)
- group contribution (Table 1, Panel A)
- authority and citizen payoffs (Table A7)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- citizen support/hinder decisions (perceived legitimacy)
- authority enforcement/punishment frequency
- contribution rates
- targeting styles (resoluteness, perverse, predation)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is perceived legitimacy, operationalized as citizens' support/hinder decisions regarding authority's punishment. Contribution rates and enforcement frequency are also analyzed. Payoff-related outcomes (total profit, group earnings) are reported in the appendix and are not the main focus; the paper finds no significant effect of the symbolic treatment on payoffs or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that introducing a symbolic scepter for the authority significantly increases citizens' support for the authority's punishment decisions, regardless of the payment scheme (appropriation vs. fixed salary) or information transparency. However, the symbol does not affect the frequency or style of punishment by authorities, nor does it affect citizens' contribution rates or group/individual payoffs. The mechanism is psychological: the symbol increases intrinsic obedience and perceived legitimacy, not actual cooperation or efficiency. There is no evidence that the symbol improves efficiency, welfare, or total group earnings.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that symbolic manipulations of authority can increase perceived legitimacy (support for punishment) in public goods games with punishment, but do not affect efficiency, group payoffs, or contribution rates. For the downstream prediction task, the presence or absence of a symbolic authority is unlikely to affect the efficiency outcome when punishment is enabled, holding other game design dimensions and control efficiency constant. The main moderators (payment scheme, transparency) do not interact with the symbol to affect efficiency. Thus, symbolic authority should not be expected to improve efficiency in similar lab PGG environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 5 players: 1 authority and 4 citizens.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consists of 20 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players is allowed; all interactions are via computer.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: contribute all 20 tokens or keep all 20 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (public tokens are doubled and split among 5 players: 20 tokens contributed yields 40, divided by 5 = 8 per player; so each token contributed yields 0.4 per player).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher (authority) pays 2 tokens to attempt punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. If successful, punishment reduces target's payoff by 30 tokens; probability of success is 0.5 by default, modifiable by citizen support/hinder actions.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are told the game will last 20 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players see others' contribution choices and payoffs in the transparent information treatments; in limited information treatments, only total group contribution is shown.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Untargeted citizens observe which citizen is targeted for punishment (ID), and this is common knowledge.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is perceived legitimacy (support for authority), not efficiency or group payoff.
- No significant effect of the symbolic authority on efficiency, welfare, or total profit is found.
- External validity may be limited due to cultural context (Chinese undergraduate sample); results may not generalize to other cultures.
- Only one type of symbol (scepter) is tested; effects of other symbols are unknown.
- No reward mechanism is present; only punishment is studied.
