## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in spatial public goods games with reputation-based social norms, rewarding cooperation and punishing defection toward 'bad' individuals (low-reputation) can promote cooperation, especially when the reputation threshold for being considered 'good' is high. However, increasing the reward intensity for cooperating with 'bad' individuals does not further improve cooperation, while increasing punishment intensity for defecting against 'bad' individuals only helps in high-threshold environments. The main outcome is the frequency of cooperators; average payoff per strategy is reported but not the main focus. The model is robust to both lattice and well-mixed population structures.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the effect of punishment and reward mechanisms on cooperation in PGG-like environments, especially under varying reputation thresholds. However, because the main outcome is cooperation rate and not efficiency or group payoff, its direct value for predicting efficiency is limited. The reported average payoffs per strategy suggest that cooperators tend to have higher payoffs, but group-level efficiency (relative to full cooperation) is not analyzed. The model's manipulation of punishment and reward intensity, and the reporting of average payoffs, may inform qualitative expectations about the direction of efficiency effects, but quantitative prediction is not possible from this paper alone.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a lattice of size L=100 (10,000 individuals) and well-mixed population of N=10,000. Group size G is determined by lattice neighborhood (not varied).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 10,000 or 50,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with outcomes measured at evolutionary steady state.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: each cooperator contributes c=1, defectors contribute 0.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Enhancement factor r is varied; MPCR = r/G. r is a key parameter in all analyses.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment intensity (P2) is varied as the amount by which reputation is reduced for defecting against 'bad' individuals. Higher P2 increases the effect of punishment in high-threshold environments.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward for cooperation with 'bad' individuals (P1) is implemented as an increase in reputation. Its presence is manipulated.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `unclear` under this dimension. Reward intensity (P1) is varied as the amount by which reputation is increased for cooperating with 'bad' individuals. Increasing P1 above a certain level does not further improve cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not use empirical or experimental data.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not group efficiency or total payoff relative to full cooperation.
- Punishment and reward are implemented as changes in reputation, not as direct payoff costs or benefits.
- No direct analysis of group-level efficiency or welfare; average payoff per strategy is reported only in some cases.
- The model assumes all-or-nothing contributions and does not explore continuous contribution choices.
- No communication, default framing, or identification of punishers/rewarders is modeled.
