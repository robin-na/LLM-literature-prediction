Outcome notes:
The main reported outcomes are the fractions of different strategies (cooperators, defectors, excluders, loners) over time and at equilibrium. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings, but focuses on the prevalence of cooperation and exclusion as behavioral outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing a social exclusion mechanism (where excluders pay a cost to expel defectors from the public goods game) can significantly increase the level of cooperation in a networked public goods environment. The effect is stronger when the cost of exclusion is low and the probability of successful exclusion is high. Sparse network structures (low average degree) are more conducive to sustaining cooperation. Voluntary participation (allowing loners) also helps prevent defection from dominating. However, the paper does not report on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, focusing instead on the prevalence of cooperative strategies.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the idea that punishment-like mechanisms (here, social exclusion) can increase cooperation rates in public-goods-like environments, especially when the cost of exclusion is low and the network is sparse. However, because the model and results focus on behavioral outcomes (cooperation level) rather than efficiency or group payoff, the findings should be used with caution for predicting efficiency effects. The model's structure and parameter explorations may inform qualitative expectations about when punishment is more likely to be effective, but do not provide quantitative efficiency outcomes for the prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Simulations use N=10,000 nodes (enterprises) in the main results, but the model is general for large populations. The effect of group size is not directly analyzed for efficiency.
- number of rounds: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. The model is evolutionary and iterated over 10,000 Monte Carlo steps, but does not correspond to a fixed number of rounds as in lab experiments.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Contribution is modeled as a fixed investment per cooperator/excluder, not as a continuous or all-or-nothing choice. The act of contribution is binary (contribute or not), but the amount is fixed and divided among groups.
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The enhancement factor r is varied and is analogous to the MPCR. The effect of r on cooperation is analyzed, but not on efficiency.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The cost to excluders per defector excluded (omega) is a key parameter. Lower cost increases cooperation; higher cost reduces the effectiveness of exclusion.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The probability of successful exclusion (mu) is varied. Higher mu increases the effectiveness of exclusion in promoting cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are reported.
- The main outcome is cooperation level (fraction of cooperators/excluders), not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- The model assumes a fixed network structure and does not consider dynamic restructuring of clusters.
- The exclusion mechanism is modeled as social exclusion (expelling defectors), which is not identical to standard monetary punishment.
- The model does not include reward mechanisms or communication between players.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with different network structures, group sizes, or real-world complexities.
