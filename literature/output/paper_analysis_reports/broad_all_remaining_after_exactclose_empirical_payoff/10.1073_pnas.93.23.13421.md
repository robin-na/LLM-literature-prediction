Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of cooperation (left/right moves at key nodes)
- frequency of punishment (punishing moves at specific nodes)
- frequency of defection

Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable for the prediction task is efficiency, defined as the percentage of the cooperative [50,50] total payoff that is realized by all pairs. The paper also reports expected payoffs for specific strategies and frequencies of behavioral choices (cooperation, defection, punishment).

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiments show that allowing for direct punishment of defection (game 1) leads to higher cooperation and efficiency compared to when punishment is not available (game 2), though the difference is modest in single-play but more pronounced in repeated interactions. Efficiency (as a percentage of the cooperative joint payoff) is highest in repeated same-pair treatments with punishment (90.6%), and lower in random or single-play treatments (82.6%-86.9%). The presence of punishment increases the expected payoff for cooperative choices and reduces the profitability of defection. The probability of cooperation increases with the likelihood of repeated interaction with the same partner, and punishment is used at nontrivial rates even when costly. Overall, punishment and repeated interaction both have positive effects on efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that, in extensive form bargaining games with a punishment option, enabling punishment increases efficiency, especially when combined with repeated interaction with the same partner. The efficiency boost from punishment is present even in single-play, but is much larger in repeated games. The design dimensions most relevant for prediction are the presence of punishment, the matching protocol (same partner vs random), and the number of rounds. The efficiency values reported (Table 4) can be used as quantitative anchors for similar game designs. The findings support the prediction that, controlling for baseline efficiency, enabling punishment will increase efficiency, with the effect size moderated by the probability of repeated interaction and the cost/effectiveness of punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are two-player (dyadic) extensive form games.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Treatments include both single-play and repeated-play (20 rounds) conditions. Efficiency is higher in repeated-play, especially with same partner.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Choices at each node are discrete (move left/right/down), not continuous contributions.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is costly to the punisher (player 2) in game 1; the cost is determined by the structure of the game tree and payoffs (e.g., moving down at x5 yields [20,20] instead of [50,50] or [60,30]).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the difference in payoffs between cooperative and punishment outcomes (e.g., [50,50] vs [20,20]). The game design allows for direct, costly punishment that reduces the defector's payoff.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are not told the exact number of rounds in repeated-play treatments, to maintain uncertainty about the end; in single-play, the game is known to be one-shot.

## Important Limitations
- The game is not a standard public goods game; it is a two-person extensive form bargaining game, so findings may not fully generalize to multi-person PGGs.
- Punishment is implemented as a costly move in the game tree, not as a separate punishment stage or point assignment.
- All games are dyadic; effects of group size cannot be assessed.
- No communication is allowed, so results may differ in settings with chat or discussion.
- The cost and effectiveness of punishment are fixed by the game structure and not varied parametrically.
- Subjects are all undergraduates and matched with like individuals; results may not generalize to outgroup or more heterogeneous populations.
