# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: When charities compete: A laboratory experiment with simultaneous public goods
Authors: Krieg, J., & Samek, A.
Journal: Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Economics
Year: 2017

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate to public good (as % of endowment)
- sum of contributions to both public goods
- approval/disapproval points assigned (sanctions treatment)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the contribution rate (as % of endowment) in each game and treatment. The paper also reports the sum of contributions to both public goods, but does not directly report group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings as a fraction of the maximum possible. There is no direct calculation of efficiency or welfare, but the sum of contributions is closely related.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that monetary incentives (bonus) increase contributions to the treated public good and, in early rounds, to the untreated public good, resulting in a higher total sum of contributions (i.e., an expanded 'charitable pie'). Recognition and non-monetary sanctions do not significantly increase contributions in the treated game, and recognition leads to a decrease in contributions to the untreated game in later rounds, consistent with expenditure substitution. Sanctions are actively used but do not increase contributions. The findings suggest that monetary incentives are more effective than social incentives in increasing total contributions when multiple public goods are present. However, the study does not directly measure efficiency or group payoff, so implications for efficiency must be inferred from contribution rates.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides informative evidence about the effect of non-monetary sanctions (costless approval/disapproval) in a simultaneous public goods environment. In this design, punishment is costless and does not affect payoffs, and it does not increase contributions or the sum of contributions. Thus, in similar environments (simultaneous PGGs, costless non-monetary punishment, no chat, n=4, 20 rounds, MPCR=0.4), enabling this form of punishment is unlikely to increase efficiency or group payoff. The paper does not test costly punishment, so its results are most relevant for costless, non-monetary sanctions. The findings also suggest that the presence of alternative opportunities for prosocial behavior (multiple simultaneous PGGs) may reduce the effectiveness of social incentives. For prediction tasks, this paper supports the expectation that costless, non-monetary punishment will not improve efficiency in simultaneous PGGs with these parameters.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each public goods group has 4 players (n=4) in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consists of 20 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or free-form communication is allowed. The only communication is via costless approval/disapproval points in the sanctions treatment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: participants can allocate any integer amount from 0 to 80 francs.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each franc contributed returns 0.4 to each of 4 group members).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment (approval/disapproval points) is costless to assign and has no effect on the earnings of the recipient.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is non-monetary: approval/disapproval points are visible to all but do not affect payoffs. Maximum of 5 approval or 5 disapproval points per group member per round.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Approval points in the sanctions treatment serve as a non-monetary reward, but have no payoff impact.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Approval points (reward) are costless to assign and have no effect on payoffs.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Approval points are visible to all but have no monetary effect.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told in advance that there are 20 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants see the total group contribution and the individual contributions of all group members.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the sanctions treatment, the total number of approval/disapproval points assigned to each subject is displayed to the group, but the identity of the assigner is not revealed.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is contribution rate, not efficiency or group payoff; efficiency must be inferred indirectly.
- Punishment is costless and non-monetary; results may not generalize to costly or payoff-relevant punishment.
- The simultaneous play of two public goods games may reduce the effectiveness of social incentives compared to single-game settings.
- The experiment uses undergraduate students in a laboratory setting, which may limit external validity.
- No direct measurement or reporting of group payoff, efficiency, or welfare as a fraction of the maximum possible.
