Journal: Scientific Reports
Year: 2013

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperators and punishers at steady state
- cluster size and number of cooperative clusters
- distribution of strategies by node degree
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are the fraction of cooperators and punishers (strategy frequencies) and their spatial/structural organization. No explicit group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a punisher strategy (who pays a cost to reduce defectors' payoffs) in networked 2-person games (PD and SD) increases the fraction of cooperative strategies (cooperators and punishers) at equilibrium, across a range of network topologies (lattice, ER, scale-free) and punishment severities. The effect is robust: even small punishment fines can substantially increase cooperation. The paper analyzes the spatial and network organization of strategies, showing that punishers can form protective clusters that shield cooperators from defectors. However, the paper does not report on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, so the direct effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that social punishment increases the prevalence of cooperative strategies in 2-person networked games, but does not provide direct evidence about efficiency or group payoff. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency in public goods games, the results are only indirectly informative: they suggest that punishment can increase cooperation, which may translate to higher efficiency, but the actual payoff consequences (including the cost of punishment) are not analyzed. The game structure is not a public goods game but a 2-person PD/SD, so transfer to PGG-like settings should be cautious.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are 2-person (dyadic) interactions on networks; each node plays with all its neighbors, but each interaction is pairwise.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for at least 100,000 time steps to reach steady state; not a fixed number of rounds as in lab experiments, but evolutionary time steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are discrete: cooperate, defect, or punish (no continuous contribution).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost (gamma) is a parameter, typically set to 0.1. The effect of punishment is analyzed for different values of the fine (beta), but gamma is usually fixed.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the fine-to-cost ratio (beta/gamma); beta is varied (0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7), gamma is fixed (0.1). Higher beta (for fixed gamma) increases cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is not a public goods game but a 2-person (dyadic) evolutionary game (PD/SD) on networks; results may not transfer directly to PGG-like environments.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only strategy frequencies and spatial organization are analyzed.
- Punishment costs are not subtracted from group payoff in a way that allows direct efficiency calculation.
- Simulations are theoretical/numerical, not empirical or experimental.
- No human subjects or real-world data; all results are from agent-based simulations.
