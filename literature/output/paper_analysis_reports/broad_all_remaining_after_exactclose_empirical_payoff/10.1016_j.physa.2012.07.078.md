## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- strategy frequencies (fraction of cooperators, defectors, punishers)
- phase diagrams of population composition
- dynamics of strategy switching
Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the frequencies of different strategies (C, D, P) in the population and the resulting phase structure, not on group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. Payoff is used as a mechanism for strategy updating, but aggregate or average group payoff is not reported as an outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing a costly punishing strategy into the snowdrift game on a square lattice leads to richer population dynamics and phase structures than in well-mixed populations. For certain parameter ranges (low cost-to-benefit ratio r and sufficiently high punishment impact β), punishers can suppress defectors and coexist with cooperators, or even dominate. The spatial structure (lattice) allows for stable coexistence of all three strategies (C, D, P) in some regions, a phenomenon not observed in well-mixed populations. The pair approximation analytic method only qualitatively matches the simulation results and fails to capture some spatially-induced phases. The main reported outcomes are the steady-state frequencies of each strategy, not group efficiency or payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about the effects of punishment in structured populations, specifically in the context of the snowdrift game (which is adjacent to public goods games). It shows that costly punishment can promote the prevalence of cooperative strategies and suppress defectors in spatially structured settings, but does not quantify the effect on group efficiency or total payoff. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, the paper suggests that spatial structure and the presence of punishment can qualitatively enhance cooperation, but does not provide direct quantitative evidence for efficiency outcomes. The findings are most relevant for understanding how punishment and spatial structure interact to shape population composition, rather than for predicting efficiency changes in public goods games.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use square lattices of size 30x30, 60x60, and 90x90 (i.e., 900, 3600, 8100 players). The number of neighbors per player is fixed at 4 (lattice degree).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 5,000,000 time steps (Monte Carlo steps), with steady-state frequencies reported. The game is iterated until steady state.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are binary: cooperate (shovel) or defect (do not shovel).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The cost-to-benefit ratio r = c/(2b-c) is varied (0 < r < 1), which determines the payoffs. The marginal return is not directly called MPCR but is functionally equivalent.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishers pay a cost α to punish defectors. α is varied (e.g., α=0.01, α=0.1).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment impact per unit cost is β/α. β (punishment impact) is varied (e.g., β=0.05, β=0.5).

## Important Limitations
- The model is based on the snowdrift game, which is adjacent to but not the same as the public goods game.
- Outcomes are reported in terms of strategy frequencies and phase structure, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes; only indirect inference is possible.
- Theoretical and simulation-based; not an empirical or experimental study.
- The model assumes a fixed spatial structure (square lattice) and does not explore other network topologies in detail.
- No communication, reward, or identification mechanisms are included.
