## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper reviews and synthesizes sociological theories of norms, focusing on why and how people enforce norms (including through punishment and reward), and the implications for social order and inequality. It distinguishes between consequentialist (cooperation-focused), relational (coordination-focused), and agentic (contextual/individual-focused) approaches. The review highlights that sanctions (punishment and reward) can increase norm compliance, but also that enforcement can reinforce inequalities and sometimes undermine collective welfare, depending on the structure of interests and relationships. The paper does not report new empirical findings or analyze efficiency or group payoff in experimental games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides high-level theoretical context for understanding the role of punishment and reward in norm enforcement and social order, but does not provide empirical evidence or quantitative findings that can be directly used to predict efficiency outcomes in public goods games or similar experimental settings. It may inform the interpretation of why punishment can sometimes promote or undermine cooperation, but does not offer parameter-level guidance for the prediction task.
