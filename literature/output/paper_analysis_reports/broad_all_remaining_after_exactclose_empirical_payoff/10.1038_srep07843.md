# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Reputation drives cooperative behaviour and network formation in human groups
Authors: Cuesta, J. A., Gracia-Lázaro, C., Ferrer, A., Moreno, Y., & Sánchez, A.
Journal: Scientific Reports
Year: 2015

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperative actions
- average connectivity (fraction of links at the end)
- proposed links per round and player
- accepted links per round and player
- broken links per round and player
- mean lifetime of links
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are cooperation rate, network connectivity, and link dynamics. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is reported. Payoff structure is described, but outcomes are not aggregated or reported as efficiency or group earnings.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiments show that providing information about others' past actions (reputation/memory) leads to higher and more stable cooperation rates in a dynamic network Prisoner's Dilemma. When no memory is available, cooperation decays and the network becomes highly connected (as players have no reason to avoid defectors). With memory, players break links with defectors, leading to lower connectivity but higher cooperation. The effect is stronger as the memory window increases. The study does not report group efficiency or total payoff, so the direct effect on efficiency is not measured. The 'punishment' mechanism is the breaking of links, not a costly punishment as in standard PGG experiments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about the effect of reputation-based partner selection (link breaking) on cooperation in dynamic network games. While the setting is an iterated Prisoner's Dilemma (not a PGG), and the 'punishment' is not costly, the results suggest that allowing players to sanction defectors by breaking ties (when reputation is available) can sustain higher cooperation. However, because efficiency or group payoff is not measured, and the game is not a PGG, the findings are only adjacent to the prediction task. The main implication is that information about others' behavior (reputation) and the ability to avoid defectors can substitute for explicit costly punishment in sustaining cooperation, but the effect on efficiency is not quantified.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups ranged from 17 to 25 participants per session.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consisted of 25 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat was allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate (C) or defect (D).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Payoff per neighbor: if cooperate, 7 points per cooperating neighbor; if defect, 10 points per cooperating neighbor. This is a Prisoner's Dilemma, not a PGG, so MPCR is not directly defined.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants did not know the total number of rounds in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were informed of a certain number (m=0,1,3,5) of past actions of every participant (reputation/memory treatment).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players could identify who broke links with them, as link proposals and acceptances/rejections were explicit.

## Important Limitations
- The game is an iterated Prisoner's Dilemma on a dynamic network, not a public goods game or direct variant.
- Punishment is not explicit or costly; it is implemented via costless link breaking.
- No group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates and network properties are analyzed.
- The effect of punishment or reputation on efficiency cannot be directly inferred from the reported outcomes.
- The setting allows for endogenous network formation, which differs from standard fixed-group PGG designs.
