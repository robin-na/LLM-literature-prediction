# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Pairs of cooperating cleaner fish provide better service quality than singletons
Authors: Bshary, R., Grutter, A. S., Willener, A. S. T., & Leimar, O.
Journal: Nature
Year: 2008

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- service quality (proportion of ectoparasites removed per cheating bite)
- client jolt rates (proxy for cheating)
- ratio of preferred to non-preferred food items eaten
- aggression between partners
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are behavioral: service quality, cheating frequency, and food choice. No direct measurement of group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings is reported. Service quality is a behavioral proxy, not a direct payoff or efficiency measure.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Pairs of cleaner fish provide higher service quality to clients than singletons, mainly because females behave more cooperatively in pairs. Theoretical modeling shows that cooperation in pairs can yield higher service quality if there is added search efficiency, and empirical data (field and lab) support this. Aggression by males toward cheating females may function as a form of punishment, but this is not systematically manipulated or measured as a treatment. The outcomes are behavioral (cheating, cooperation, aggression), not direct measures of efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence relevant to the prediction task. It shows that, in a biological system with prisoner's dilemma-like structure, the presence of a partner (pair vs singleton) can increase cooperative behavior and service quality, especially when there is some mechanism for partner control (e.g., aggression/punishment). However, the study does not use a public goods game, does not manipulate punishment as a treatment, and does not report efficiency or group payoff. Thus, it is only adjacent to the prediction task and should be used as weak, indirect support for the idea that social context and partner control can moderate cooperation and service quality.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The main comparison is between singletons (1 cleaner) and pairs (2 cleaners) interacting with a client. Pairs show higher service quality (less cheating per unit service).
- number of rounds: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Interactions are repeated, but the number of rounds is not systematically manipulated or analyzed as a moderator.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not use a public goods game or a direct analog; it is a biological mutualism with some prisoner's dilemma features.
- No direct measurement or manipulation of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings; outcomes are behavioral proxies.
- Punishment (aggression) is observed but not systematically manipulated or parameterized.
- No direct manipulation of most game design dimensions relevant to the prediction task.
- Findings may not generalize to human or laboratory public goods games with explicit payoff structures.
