## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model shows that cooperation can emerge and be sustained in iterated Prisoner's Dilemma games when agents have memory and low mutation rates, with punishment and reciprocity as key mechanisms. Increasing memory increases cooperation, while higher mutation rates reduce it. When agents can discriminate kin, cooperation is still sustained, but the underlying heuristics shift from reciprocity to kin-based discrimination. The main reported outcomes are the fraction of cooperative actions and the distribution of strategies; efficiency or group payoff is not directly analyzed.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the importance of punishment and reciprocity in sustaining cooperation in repeated social dilemmas, especially when agents have memory and mutation rates are low. However, because the model is based on the Prisoner's Dilemma (not a public goods game or direct variant), and because efficiency or group payoff is not directly reported, the findings are only adjacent to the prediction task. The results suggest that enabling punishment and memory can increase cooperation rates, but do not provide quantitative predictions about efficiency or payoff outcomes in public-goods-game-like environments.

## Important Limitations
- The model is based on the iterated Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game or direct variant, so findings may not transfer directly to PGG-like environments.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are not directly reported; only behavioral outcomes (cooperation rates, strategy types) are analyzed.
- Punishment is modeled as a heuristic response, not as an explicit costly action with parameterized cost and impact.
- No empirical or experimental data; results are from agent-based simulations.
- No communication, reward, or explicit summary information mechanisms are modeled.
- Network structure is limited to regular lattices and random regular networks; results may not generalize to other topologies.
