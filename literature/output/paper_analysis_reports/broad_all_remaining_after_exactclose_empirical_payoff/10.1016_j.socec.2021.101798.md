# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: How to prevent destruction - On the malleability of anti-social behavior
Authors: Müller, J., Schwieren, C., & Spitzer, F.
Journal: Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Economics
Year: 2022

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- final group payoff (initial endowment minus points destroyed)
- average earnings per subject
Non-payoff outcomes:
- number of points destroyed (destruction rate)
- number of subjects destroying points
- distribution of destruction within groups
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the destruction rate (proportion of points destroyed), which is directly related to group payoff (since every point destroyed reduces all group members' payoffs). Efficiency is not explicitly calculated as a ratio to the fully cooperative outcome, but the destruction rate is a direct inverse proxy for efficiency. The study does not involve a public goods game or punishment of free riders, but rather a 'joy of destruction' game where subjects can destroy group resources at a cost to themselves and others.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
This paper demonstrates that anti-social destruction of group resources in a laboratory setting is highly sensitive to contextual factors. When the destruction opportunity is presented in a transactional, market-like framing and is the only available activity, subjects destroy a large share of the group's resources, sharply reducing group payoff. However, if the destruction is framed as harming jointly owned property, or if subjects have a parallel, meaningful activity (real-effort task) to occupy them, destruction is almost eliminated and group payoffs are preserved. The effect of these interventions is large and robust. The study does not use a public goods game or punishment of free riders, but the findings are relevant for understanding how institutional design and framing can prevent anti-social, payoff-reducing behaviors in group settings.

## Prediction Guidance
While this study does not directly test public goods games or punishment, it provides strong evidence that institutional context (such as framing and the presence of alternative activities) can dramatically affect the prevalence of anti-social, payoff-reducing behaviors. For prediction tasks involving efficiency in group games, the results suggest that interventions making the negative consequences of anti-social actions salient to all, or providing alternative meaningful activities, can greatly improve group efficiency. The findings are most directly applicable to settings where group resources can be destroyed at a cost, but the general principle—that context and framing can strongly moderate destructive or anti-social behavior—may inform predictions about punishment and efficiency in related environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 4 players. The design rationale for 4 players is discussed, but player count is not varied.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The destruction game is played in three phases/rounds, each lasting three minutes.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication between participants is allowed; this is explicitly stated in the instructions.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Destruction is continuous: subjects can destroy any number of points from 0 to 36.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each point destroyed reduces the payoff of all 4 group members by 1 ET, so the marginal per-capita return of destruction is -1 for self and -1 for each other (total -4 per point destroyed).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects are informed about the number of rounds/phases in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects can observe the immediate effect of destruction (points disappear from all screens), so group-level destruction is visible in real time.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment does not use a public goods game or a punishment mechanism; it studies anti-social destruction of group resources, which is adjacent but not identical to PGG with punishment.
- Efficiency is not explicitly calculated as a ratio to the fully cooperative outcome, but destruction rate is a direct proxy.
- The findings may not generalize to settings where destruction is used as targeted punishment of free riders or norm violators.
- The study does not vary player count, marginal per-capita return, or other key PGG parameters.
- The interventions tested (framing, parallel activity) may not be feasible or have the same effect in all real-world or PGG-like environments.
