## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The chapter synthesizes findings from experimental studies on social dilemmas, emphasizing that cooperation is more common than predicted by strong free-riding hypotheses, and that structural features such as group size, information, communication, and institutional rules (including punishment and reward) shape cooperation. Punishment is discussed as a mechanism that can increase cooperation, but its effectiveness depends on design features such as who can punish, the cost, and the identification of punishers and recipients. The chapter does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or group payoff outcomes, but notes that higher cooperation is often associated with institutional features like communication and targeted punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This chapter provides useful theoretical and qualitative evidence about the mechanisms by which punishment, communication, and information affect cooperation in public goods games and related social dilemmas. It does not provide quantitative or directly predictive evidence about efficiency or group payoff outcomes, nor does it report experimental results with efficiency as a dependent variable. For prediction tasks, the chapter supports the idea that punishment can increase cooperation (and thus potentially efficiency), but also warns that unconstrained punishment may reduce cooperation. The review is most useful for understanding which design dimensions are likely to moderate the effect of punishment, rather than for direct quantitative prediction.
