# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Cooperation in the name of God? Experimental evidence from Ghana and Tanzania
Authors: Hoffmann, L.
Journal: Journal of Economic Psychology
Year: 2022

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- beliefs about what is morally right
- expectations about others' behavior
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the rate of cooperation in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma. No group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings are reported or analyzed. The study also elicits beliefs and expectations, but these are not payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that religious reminders of punishment or reward decrease cooperation rates in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma among Christian and Muslim participants in Ghana and Tanzania. The punishment prime reduces cooperation by about 8 percentage points, and the reward prime by about 6 percentage points, relative to the control. There is no evidence that religious primes increase cooperation. The study does not report group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes, focusing instead on cooperation rates and beliefs/expectations.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency or payoff outcomes in public goods games with or without punishment. The design is a one-shot prisoner's dilemma (not a repeated PGG), and the 'punishment' and 'reward' treatments are religious primes, not institutionalized peer punishment or reward mechanisms. The main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency. Thus, the paper is only weakly informative for the downstream prediction task, except as a caution that not all forms of punishment/reward framing increase cooperation, and that context and mechanism matter.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each prisoner's dilemma is played by two players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is one-shot (single round).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players was allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate or defect.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Payoff matrices are provided. In Ghana: if both cooperate, each gets 12; if both defect, each gets 8; if one cooperates and one defects, cooperator gets 4, defector gets 16. In Tanzania: similar structure. This is a standard PD, not a PGG; MPCR is not directly defined but can be inferred from the matrix.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is one-shot; participants know this.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a one-shot prisoner's dilemma, not a repeated public goods game.
- No peer punishment or reward mechanism is implemented; 'punishment' and 'reward' are religious primes, not game features.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates and beliefs/expectations are analyzed.
- The findings may not generalize to institutional punishment/reward settings or to repeated games.
- Beliefs and expectations are elicited after choices, so causality is unclear.
- The study population is urban Christians and Muslims in Ghana and Tanzania; results may not generalize to other contexts.
