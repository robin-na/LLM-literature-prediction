## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that (1) static punishment (compensation) above a critical threshold leads to full cooperation, resource sustainability, and maximum group payoff; (2) dynamic punishment with resource feedback can promote cooperation and efficiency even when static punishment is insufficient, especially at low initial compensation intensity; (3) the relationship between punishment parameters and efficiency is non-monotonic and context-dependent, with oscillatory or convergent dynamics depending on parameter regions; (4) the proportion of fines used for ecological compensation (vs. other uses) should be tuned to maximize efficiency, with all fines used for compensation when feedback is high or initial intensity is high, and none when both are low.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that increasing punishment intensity (compensation) above a critical threshold sharply increases efficiency (group payoff) in common pool resource games. Dynamic, feedback-based punishment can further improve efficiency, especially when static punishment is weak. The results suggest that, for prediction, the presence and strength of punishment, as well as whether it is dynamically adjusted based on resource feedback, are key moderators of efficiency gains. The findings are directly relevant for predicting treatment efficiency from game design dimensions, especially for settings with resource feedback, variable punishment intensity, and allocation of fines to resource restoration.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- Population is well-mixed; no network or spatial structure is considered.
- Punishment is institutional and not peer-based; no individual-level targeting.
- No communication, reputation, or social learning beyond replicator dynamics.
- No reward mechanism is modeled, only punishment (compensation).
- The model assumes continuous effort and resource extraction, not discrete or all-or-nothing choices.
- The allocation of fines to ecological compensation vs. other uses is modeled as a parameter, but the real-world implementation may be more complex.
- No explicit time horizon or round number; results are based on convergence or steady-state analysis.
