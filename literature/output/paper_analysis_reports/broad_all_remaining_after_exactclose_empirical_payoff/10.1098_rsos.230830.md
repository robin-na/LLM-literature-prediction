## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a division-of-labour game on a tree network, without sanctions, the only stable equilibrium is full defection (premierD), resulting in low efficiency. Introducing a defector sanction system (where defectors are punished with probability and severity) can make full cooperation or mixed equilibria stable, depending on the punishment parameters and cost/loss structure. As the effective punishment (probability times fine) increases, the system transitions from defection to mixed states to full cooperation, thus increasing efficiency. The premier sanction system (punishing the root node for any defection) is less effective at promoting cooperation. The model provides explicit stability conditions for each equilibrium, showing how efficiency depends on the sanction system and network structure. Adding more levels to the network (making it deeper) hinders cooperation and efficiency, while adding more parallel branches has an indeterminate effect.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support that, in public-goods-like division-of-labour games on tree networks, enabling a defector-targeted punishment system increases group efficiency compared to the no-punishment baseline, provided the punishment is sufficiently strong (high probability and/or fine). The effect is mediated by the cost of cooperation, the loss from defection, and the network structure. The findings suggest that, for a given control efficiency (no punishment), introducing punishment will increase efficiency if the punishment is strong enough, but the effect may be partial (mixed equilibria) if punishment is moderate. The model also predicts that deeper (more levels) networks make it harder for punishment to achieve full cooperation. These insights can inform predictions of treatment efficiency from game design dimensions and control efficiency, especially in settings with hierarchical or supply-chain-like structure.

## Important Limitations
- The model is entirely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- Groups are assumed to be of infinite size, and only one player per group is selected per task, which may not match real-world group sizes.
- The model is adjacent to, but not an exact instance of, the standard public goods game; it focuses on division of labour and task completion on a tree network.
- Punishment cost to the punisher is not explicitly modeled; only the effect on the punished is considered.
- The model assumes perfect knowledge of payoffs and evolutionary updating, not bounded rationality or learning.
- No communication, reward, or reputation mechanisms are included.
- The effect of adding parallel branches to the network is indeterminate in the model.
- The benefit parameter (analogous to MPCR numerator) is shown to have no effect on cooperation or efficiency in this model, which may differ from standard PGGs.
