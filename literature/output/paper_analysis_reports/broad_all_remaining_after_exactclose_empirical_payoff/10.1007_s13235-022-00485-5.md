Outcome notes:
The paper's dependent variables are primarily the proportion of cooperation, defection, and loner actions, as well as measures of strategy complexity and allele use. There is no direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings. Payoff is used as a fitness function in the agent-based model, but aggregate efficiency or welfare is not reported as an outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in the OPGG, increasing the complexity of behavioral strategies (i.e., the ability to condition actions on more finely-grained reputational information) increases the proportion of cooperative actions, especially under social norms that assign different reputations to defectors and loners. However, this effect plateaus after a certain level of complexity. Cooperation is higher in smaller groups, and the benefit of strategy complexity is most pronounced in these settings. The model does not include punishment or reward mechanisms, and does not report efficiency or group payoff as outcomes. The results suggest that, in the absence of punishment, reputation-based mechanisms combined with complex behavioral strategies can sustain cooperation, but the findings are limited to behavioral outcomes rather than efficiency or welfare.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it does not include punishment or report efficiency or payoff outcomes. Its findings are relevant for understanding how reputation systems and behavioral strategy complexity affect cooperation rates in OPGG settings without punishment. For the downstream prediction task, this paper suggests that, in the absence of punishment, increasing behavioral strategy complexity and using social norms that distinguish defectors from loners can increase cooperation rates, especially in small groups. However, it does not inform how punishment would affect efficiency or payoff, nor does it provide control or treatment efficiency values.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Group size n is systematically varied (e.g., n=5, n=10), and its effect on cooperation is analyzed. Larger groups reduce cooperation, and the benefit of strategy complexity is most pronounced in small groups.
- number of rounds: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. The model involves repeated games within periods, with the number of rounds per period determined probabilistically by parameter Omega. The exact number of rounds is not a primary focus.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are discrete: cooperate (contribute), defect (do not contribute), or abstain (loner). No continuous contribution levels.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. The public good multiplier r is systematically varied in sensitivity analyses. For small r, cooperation is low regardless of strategy complexity; for larger r, more complex strategies increase cooperation.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players observe the average reputation of their group (excluding themselves) and condition their actions on this information.

## Important Limitations
- The model does not include punishment or sanctioning mechanisms, so it cannot inform the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are not reported; only behavioral outcomes (proportion of cooperation, defection, and loner actions) are analyzed.
- Findings are based on agent-based simulations and may not generalize to empirical or experimental settings.
- The model assumes perfect reputation assignment and observation in the baseline; errors are only explored in supplementary analyses.
- The results are specific to the OPGG with optional participation and may not transfer to standard PGGs or other social dilemma games.
