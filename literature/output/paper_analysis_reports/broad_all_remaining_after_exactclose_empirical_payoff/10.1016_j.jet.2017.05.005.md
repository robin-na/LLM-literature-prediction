## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the efficiency and welfare effects of punishment in repeated trust games depend critically on the structure of the social network and the speed of information flow. When information about defections spreads only to direct neighbors (slow information), cohesive networks (complete components) are optimal for sustaining cooperation and maximizing welfare, as they allow for coordinated punishment. When information spreads rapidly to all direct and indirect connections (fast information), minimally connected networks (e.g., trees) are optimal, as they maximize the number of players who can punish a defection. The paper also shows that the convexity or concavity of the welfare function determines whether stars or equally sized components maximize welfare. The analysis provides explicit characterizations of equilibrium payoffs and welfare under different network and monitoring regimes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical guidance on how network structure and information transmission technology moderate the efficiency gains from punishment in repeated trust-like games. It shows that the effect of punishment on efficiency is not uniform but depends on whether the network allows for coordinated punishment and how quickly information about defections spreads. For prediction tasks, the results suggest that game design dimensions related to network structure, monitoring technology, and information flow are critical moderators of punishment's efficiency effects. However, the model is not a standard public goods game and does not provide empirical effect sizes, so quantitative predictions should be made with caution and only in settings structurally similar to the model.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is defined for N investors plus one agent; N is a parameter throughout the analysis.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is an infinitely repeated game (discounted sum of payoffs), not a fixed number of rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/investment is continuous: investor chooses a non-negative real action a ∈ ℝ₊.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is endogenous: refusing to trade with the agent after a defection is the punishment; the cost is the foregone surplus from not trading.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the network structure and monitoring technology: how many players can observe and refuse to trade after a defection.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is infinitely repeated with discounting; all players know the structure.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players observe outcomes of interactions only if they are direct (or, in some variants, indirect) neighbors of the victim; information structure is a central focus.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know who defected and who is refusing to trade, but only within the limits of the network's information structure.

## Important Limitations
- The analysis is entirely theoretical; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- The model is a repeated trust game with one agent and N investors, not a standard public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGGs.
- Punishment is endogenous (refusing to trade), not an explicit costly punishment as in many PGG experiments.
- The model assumes perfect rationality and equilibrium play, which may not hold in practice.
- Quantitative predictions of efficiency effects are not provided; only qualitative and comparative statics results are available.
- The model assumes either infinitely repeated games or discounting, not finitely repeated games.
