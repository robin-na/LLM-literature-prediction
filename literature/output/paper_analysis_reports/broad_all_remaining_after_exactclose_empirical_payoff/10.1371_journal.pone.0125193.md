# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: To Punish or to Leave: Distinct Cognitive Processes Underlie Partner Control and Partner Choice Behaviors
Authors: Martin, J. W., & Cushman, F.
Journal: Plos One
Year: 2015

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment/reward decisions (amount added/subtracted to partner)
- partner switching (stay/switch decision)
- sensitivity to partner intent (die choice)
- sensitivity to outcome (allocation result)
- proportion of participants using each response strategy
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: whether and how much participants punish/reward, or choose to stay/switch partners, and their sensitivity to intent and outcome. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a dyadic allocation game with stochastic outcomes, punishment (partner control) is influenced by both the allocator's intent and the actual outcome, while partner choice (switching partners) is influenced almost exclusively by intent. The design allows for the separation of intent and outcome by having allocators choose between dice with different probabilities of fair/selfish outcomes. Responders in the punishment condition could add or subtract money (costlessly, with low probability of effect), while in the partner choice condition they could choose to stay or switch partners. The main dependent variables are the behavioral responses and their sensitivity to intent/outcome. No group-level efficiency or payoff outcomes are reported.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff in public goods or similar games. Its main contribution is to clarify the psychological determinants of punishment and partner choice behaviors, showing that punishment is sensitive to both intent and outcome, while partner choice is intent-driven. The game is not a standard public goods game, and efficiency or total payoff is not analyzed. Thus, the paper offers little direct support for predicting efficiency changes due to punishment in PGG-like environments, but may inform understanding of when punishment is likely to be used and what cues drive it.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4-12 participants, median group size 7; games played in dyads (1 Roller, 1 Responder per pair).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Partner Control: 1 round; Partner Choice: 2 rounds (to allow for switching).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed; participants separated by barriers and choices made privately.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is not all-or-nothing; allocation is determined by die choice (probabilistic split of $4), and punishment/reward is continuous (up to $2 added/subtracted).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment and reward are costless to the Responder (no cost to add/subtract up to $2).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment/reward action (add/subtract up to $2) is only implemented with 1/12 probability; otherwise, no effect. Maximum impact is $2 per round, but with low probability.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward (adding up to $2) is available in the Partner Control condition.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is costless to the Responder.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward (add up to $2) is only implemented with 1/12 probability.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were informed of the number of rounds: 1 in Partner Control, 2 in Partner Choice.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment/reward was anonymous; participants did not know the identity of their partner.

## Important Limitations
- The game is not a standard public goods game; it is a dyadic allocation game with stochastic outcomes and does not involve group contributions or marginal per-capita return.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported or analyzed; all main outcomes are behavioral (punishment/reward, switching, sensitivity to intent/outcome).
- Punishment and reward are costless and only probabilistically implemented, which differs from most PGG punishment designs.
- Sample is undergraduate students in a lab setting, which may limit generalizability.
- Partner control and partner choice are not directly compared in terms of their effect on cooperation rates or payoffs, only in terms of cognitive determinants.
