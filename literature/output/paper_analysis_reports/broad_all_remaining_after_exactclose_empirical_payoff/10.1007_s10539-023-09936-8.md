## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in agent-based models of the Prisoner's Dilemma with punishment (strong reciprocity), cooperation can be maintained at high levels when only overt defectors are present. However, when agents can mimic cooperators or defect covertly (evading detection and punishment), the population can be dominated by individuals who are not true cooperators, even though the observed rate of cooperation remains high. The models highlight that the opportunity to defect undetected undermines the effectiveness of punishment-based strategies in ensuring genuine prosociality. The paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, but raises concerns that high observed cooperation rates in experimental games may not indicate high group welfare or internalized prosocial preferences.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical caution for interpreting high cooperation rates in punishment-enabled games as evidence of increased efficiency or group welfare. It shows that punishment can maintain high cooperation in simple models, but that the introduction of undetectable defection strategies (mimicry, covert defection) can allow free-riding to persist without reducing the observed cooperation rate. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the presence of punishment may not always translate to higher efficiency, especially if the game design allows for undetected defection. However, the models are dyadic PDs, not public goods games, and do not report efficiency or group payoff, so direct quantitative prediction is not possible from this paper.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All models use N=100 agents in the population, paired randomly for dyadic interactions each round.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Models are run for 10,000 to 100,000 generations, with each generation consisting of one round of random dyadic pairings.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are binary: cooperate or defect in each dyadic interaction.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost to punisher is -0.1 per act; cost to punished is -0.4. These are fixed across all models.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is fixed: each punishment costs -0.1 to punisher and -0.4 to target. No variation or analysis of different punishment effectiveness.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of generations (rounds) is fixed and known in the simulation setup.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. In Models 1 and 2, punishment is always observable and attributed to the punisher. In Model 3, covert (silent) defectors can avoid detection and thus punishment is not always attributable.

## Important Limitations
- The models are dyadic Prisoner's Dilemma games, not public goods games; results may not transfer directly to multi-player PGGs.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only behavioral outcomes (cooperation rates, frequencies of types) are analyzed.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are from agent-based simulations.
- No partner choice, reputation, memory, or communication mechanisms are included, which are important in real-world cooperation.
- The models assume fixed punishment costs and effectiveness; no exploration of variation in these parameters.
- The models do not include reward mechanisms or positive incentives.
- The models do not account for migration, group structure, or indirect reciprocity.
