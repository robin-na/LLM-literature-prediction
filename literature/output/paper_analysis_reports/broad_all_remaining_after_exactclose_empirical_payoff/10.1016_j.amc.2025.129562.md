Outcome notes:
The main outcome is Average Cooperation Intensity (ACI), which measures the average level of investment (cooperation) in the public goods game. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings directly, but ACI is closely related to the level of cooperation.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study demonstrates that in a continuous SPGG with punishment, the use of exploration-based particle swarm optimization (EPSO) and its parameter optimization via Q-learning (QPSO) can rapidly drive the system to a state of full cooperation (ACI=1) under certain parameter regimes. Both low and high values of the individual learning coefficient (c1), higher inertia weight (omega), lower punishment intensity (eta), and higher gain intensity (r) promote higher cooperation intensity. The main reported outcome is ACI, not direct group payoff or efficiency. The findings are based on simulation experiments with large populations (L=200, i.e., 40,000 agents) on a square lattice, and the results are robust across a range of parameter values.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task: while it does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, it shows that punishment (with certain parameterizations) can lead to maximal cooperation intensity in a spatial PGG with continuous contributions. The results suggest that, in similar simulation/theoretical models, enabling punishment with low punishment intensity and high gain intensity can drive the system to near-full cooperation, which would likely correspond to high efficiency if mapped to payoff outcomes. However, the lack of direct efficiency or payoff reporting limits the direct applicability to the prediction task. The findings are most relevant for understanding how parameter settings (punishment intensity, gain intensity, learning coefficients) modulate the effect of punishment on cooperation, which may inform priors for efficiency outcomes in similar game designs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a square lattice of size L=200, i.e., 40,000 agents. The player count is fixed and very large.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 20,000 time steps (iterations), with results reported at various time points.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous between 0 and 1; all-or-nothing is not used.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The gain intensity coefficient r (1 < r < k+1, with k=4) determines the marginal per-capita return. For k=4, r=4 implies MPCR=0.8.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a direct deduction from defectors' payoffs, with intensity eta. Lower eta (punishment intensity) leads to higher cooperation intensity (ACI).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Rewards are given to full cooperators (s=1) as a direct addition to payoff, parameterized by eta.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is determined by the same parameter (eta) as punishment intensity.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of iterations (rounds) is fixed and known in the simulation setup.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents have access to their neighbors' strategies and payoffs for updating their own strategies.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not report empirical or experimental data.
- The main outcome is Average Cooperation Intensity (ACI), not direct efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings.
- Punishment and reward are implemented as direct payoff modifications, with no explicit cost to the punisher or rewarder.
- The model uses a very large, fixed population (L=200, 40,000 agents) on a square lattice, which may not generalize to smaller groups or other network structures.
- No analysis of stochasticity, robustness to noise, or real-world behavioral factors is provided.
- No direct mapping from ACI to efficiency or group payoff is given, limiting direct applicability to the prediction of efficiency outcomes.
