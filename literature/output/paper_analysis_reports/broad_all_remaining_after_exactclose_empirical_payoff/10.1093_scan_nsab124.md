# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Inter-brain synchronization is weakened by the introduction of external punishment
Authors: Li, J. B., Pan, J. J., Zhu, C. K., & Wang, Y. W.
Journal: Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience
Year: 2022

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- mean offer size (amount proposer offers to responder)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- inter-brain synchronization (PLV/PLS)
- rejection rate of responders
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is inter-brain synchronization (neural outcome). The only payoff-related behavioral outcome is the mean offer size; group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare are not directly analyzed. Rejection rates are reported but are not payoff outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of external punishment (i.e., making responder rejection payoff-relevant in the rrUG) leads to significantly higher mean offers from proposers compared to the rrDG (no punishment). However, the main focus is on neural outcomes: inter-brain synchronization is significantly lower in the punishment condition (rrUG) than in the no-punishment condition (rrDG). There is no significant effect of punishment on the rejection rate of responders. The study interprets the neural results as evidence that punishment crowds out moral motivation, reducing shared cognitive/emotional processing between players. No direct measures of group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare are reported.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that introducing punishment (via responder rejection in a two-player ultimatum game) increases proposer generosity (mean offer size), which could be associated with higher group payoffs if responders accept. However, the study does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare, so it cannot be used directly to predict efficiency outcomes. The design is adjacent to public goods games but is not a PGG or close variant. The main outcome is neural (inter-brain synchronization), not payoff-based. Thus, the paper offers only weak, indirect support for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in dyads (2 players: proposer and responder).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game (rrUG and rrDG) is played for 120 rounds per dyad.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed; participants are in separate rooms.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is not all-or-nothing; offers are discrete (2:8, 5:5, 8:2) but not binary.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. In rrUG, rejection by responder is costless to the responder but results in zero payoff for both players. In rrDG, rejection is non-payoff-relevant (no cost, no effect).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. In rrUG, punishment (rejection) is maximally effective: both players get zero if rejected. In rrDG, rejection has no effect.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game structure and number of rounds (120 per game).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, both players see the outcome (how much each earned).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (rejection) is always attributable to the responder; only two players per dyad.

## Important Limitations
- The study is not a public goods game or a close variant; it uses repeated ultimatum and dictator games.
- No direct measures or analyses of group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare are reported.
- The main outcome is neural (inter-brain synchronization), not payoff-based.
- All participants are male, limiting generalizability.
- The punishment mechanism is binary and maximally effective (rejection leads to zero for both), which differs from typical PGG punishment designs.
