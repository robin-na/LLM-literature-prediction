# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: To qualify as a social partner, humans hide severe punishment, although their observed cooperativeness is decisive
Authors: Rockenbach, B., & Milinski, M.
Journal: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America
Year: 2011

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average contribution per player per round
- payoff per round (points earned)
- costs incurred for hiding or observing
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency and pattern of hiding contributions, punishment, and giving
- frequency and pattern of observation and concealed observation
- partner selection decisions
- punishment assigned
- giving behavior
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: contribution levels, punishment/giving behavior, hiding/concealing actions, and partner selection. Payoff is tracked (points per round), but efficiency or group payoff is not the primary focus, nor is it explicitly analyzed as a main outcome. The effect of punishment on average contribution is reported, but not on efficiency per se.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that in a public goods game with punishment, players strategically hide low contributions and severe punishment from an observer who may select future partners. Observers focus on observed contributions when making partner choices, largely ignoring punishment behavior. Punishment is used mainly to discipline low contributors, and its presence increases average contributions compared to the no-punishment game. However, the paper does not report group efficiency or total payoff as a main outcome, nor does it analyze the effect of punishment on efficiency directly. The main findings are about behavioral strategies for hiding and observing, and the determinants of partner selection.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that punishment increases average contributions in a public goods game, but does not directly report on efficiency or group payoff. For prediction tasks, it supports the idea that punishment can raise cooperation, but the effect on efficiency must be inferred indirectly. The game design dimensions (player count, rounds, MPCR, punishment cost/tech, etc.) are reported and can be mapped, but the main outcomes are behavioral rather than payoff-based. The presence of strategic hiding and observation may moderate the effect of punishment, but the paper does not quantify this in terms of efficiency. Thus, the paper is informative for understanding behavioral mechanisms, but less directly useful for predicting efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 5 participants: 4 players and 1 observer per block.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each block has 15 rounds; two blocks per session (30 rounds total).
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No chat or communication between players is mentioned or allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are 0, 5, or 10 out of 10 points (discrete, not continuous, but not strictly all-or-nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Total group contribution is doubled and split evenly among 4 players. MPCR = 0.5.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishing costs 1 point to punisher per punishment point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point deducts 3 points from the punished player per 1 point cost to punisher (punishment effectiveness = 3).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of rounds (15 per block, 2 blocks).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed about individual contributions and payoffs after each round; observer can see up to 2 players' actions per phase.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Observer can see punishment decisions of up to 2 players per round, with player identification number; players can hide their punishment decisions.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcomes are behavioral (contribution, hiding, observation, partner selection), not efficiency or group payoff.
- Efficiency or total group payoff is not directly reported or analyzed as a main outcome.
- The presence of strategic hiding and observation may moderate the effect of punishment, but this is not quantified in terms of efficiency.
- Sample size is limited (8 groups per treatment), and the design is complex, which may limit generalizability.
- The observer mechanism and hiding options are not standard in most public goods experiments, which may affect comparability.
