## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that allowing individuals to reduce interaction intensity with neighbors who defect beyond a tolerance threshold increases the frequency of cooperation in a spatial prisoner's dilemma. However, if the tolerance threshold is too high (i.e., individuals are too forgiving), cooperation is undermined. The effect is stronger when more individuals in the population are 'environmental adaptors' who adjust their interaction intensity. The main outcome is the equilibrium frequency of cooperators, not group payoff or efficiency. The findings are robust to changes in network size and initial cooperator fraction.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about mechanisms that may promote cooperation in repeated social dilemmas, specifically through adaptive partner selection or ostracism based on tolerance for defection. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, and the game is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game. The results suggest that mechanisms allowing individuals to reduce interaction with defectors (after a tolerance threshold) can increase cooperation rates, but excessive tolerance undermines this effect. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency in public goods games with punishment, this paper is only weakly informative, as it does not analyze punishment costs, efficiency, or public goods structures.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a 2D lattice with L x L players (e.g., L=100, L=200, L=50-500). Each player has 4 neighbors.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with outcomes averaged over the last 3,000 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between cooperate (C) or defect (D) in each round; binary choice.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game, so results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- No explicit efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates are analyzed.
- The reduction in interaction intensity is not modeled as a costly punishment, so the mechanism differs from standard punishment in PGGs.
- Simulation-based results may not generalize to human behavior or experimental settings.
- The model does not include communication, explicit punishment/reward costs, or information about group structure beyond local neighborhoods.
