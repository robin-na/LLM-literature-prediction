## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that introducing dynamic elimination (removal of defectors) in a networked donation game greatly increases cooperation frequency, but at the cost of eliminating many players. Adding the ZD strategy further increases cooperation and allows more players to remain in the game. The combination of elimination and ZD strategy is robust to changes in game parameters and identification errors. Larger-degree nodes are more likely to survive, and the optimal initial strategy for survival depends on whether ZD is present. However, the study does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, focusing instead on behavioral and structural outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that mechanisms analogous to punishment (elimination of defectors) and sophisticated strategies (ZD) can increase cooperation and player retention in dynamic networked games. However, because it does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. The findings may be useful for understanding the behavioral and structural consequences of punishment-like mechanisms, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a scale-free network with 3000 nodes (players).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 2000 rounds, with analysis on the last 200 rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Donation game is binary: cooperate (C) or defect (D).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Players update intimacy based on observed neighbor strategies, implying some summary information is available.

## Important Limitations
- The study is simulation-based and does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus.
- The game is a donation game (prisoner's dilemma variant), not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- Elimination is not modeled as costly punishment; there is no explicit cost to the punisher.
- Outcomes are behavioral (cooperation frequency, retention) and structural (network properties), not payoff-based.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are from theoretical modeling and simulation.
