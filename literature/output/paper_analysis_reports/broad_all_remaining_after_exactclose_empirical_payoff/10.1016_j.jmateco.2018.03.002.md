## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in sender-receiver games with the possibility of credible punishment (a uniform punishment decision), there always exists an equilibrium (CTE) where both players cooperate and communicate, leading to expected payoffs for both that are at least as high as in the baseline game without communication, and often strictly higher. The existence of credible punishment is key: without it, such efficient equilibria may not exist. The paper provides examples where the introduction of a UPD enables partially revealing communication and joint decisions that strictly improve welfare for both players. The findings are theoretical and general, but the examples show that the efficiency gain from enabling credible punishment can be substantial.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support for the idea that enabling credible punishment (via a uniform punishment decision) in games with communication and cooperation can increase group efficiency/welfare relative to a no-punishment baseline. While the setting is not a standard public goods game, the logic is adjacent: credible punishment for non-cooperation enables more efficient equilibria. For prediction tasks, the paper suggests that, in environments where a credible punishment mechanism is available and can be used as a threat, enabling punishment should not decrease and may increase efficiency, sometimes strictly. The magnitude and details depend on the structure of the game and the nature of the punishment, but the direction is clear. However, the results are theoretical and do not provide quantitative effect sizes.

## Important Limitations
- The results are purely theoretical; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- The game is not a public goods game or a direct variant; it is a sender-receiver (principal-agent) game with communication and credible punishment.
- Punishment is modeled as a credible threat (uniform punishment decision), not as a costly, voluntary action as in standard PGG punishment treatments.
- All games are two-player and one-shot; results may not generalize to multi-player or repeated settings.
- No quantitative effect sizes or comparative statics are provided; only existence and direction of welfare effects are established.
- No analysis of parameter variation (e.g., punishment cost, action set size) beyond illustrative examples.
