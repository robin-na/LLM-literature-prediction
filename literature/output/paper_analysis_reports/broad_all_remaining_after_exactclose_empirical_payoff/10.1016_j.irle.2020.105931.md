# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Testing a fine is a price in the lab
Authors: Kornhauser, L., Lu, Y. J., & Tontrup, S.
Journal: International Review of Law and Economics
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- number of tables completed (performance)
- contract acceptance/rejection
- self-reported guilt
- social value orientation (SVO)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the number of tables completed by player 2 (performance), which is a measure of compliance/cooperation, not group payoff or efficiency. No explicit group payoff, welfare, or efficiency outcome is reported. Some discussion of payoffs is present in the design, but the main analysis is on behavioral outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that introducing a fine for non-compliance (either small or proportional) reduces compliance (performance) among pro-social subjects (crowding-out effect) and increases it among pro-self subjects (price effect). When the fine is framed as a penalty (collected by the experimenter, not the partner), the crowding-out effect disappears and pro-socials' compliance is restored. The overall mean performance across all subjects does not differ significantly between treatments with and without fines, due to the offsetting effects. The study provides strong evidence for the fine-is-a-price mechanism as the cause of crowding out, but does not measure or report group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment/fines on compliance in public-goods-like environments depends on the social value orientation of participants: fines crowd out compliance among pro-socials but increase it among pro-selfs. However, because the study does not report group efficiency or total payoff, it does not directly inform predictions about efficiency outcomes. The findings suggest that the net effect of punishment on efficiency may be neutral in mixed populations, but could be negative in highly pro-social groups and positive in highly pro-self groups. The design and manipulation of punishment (including framing as price vs. penalty) are critical moderators. For prediction tasks, the absence of explicit efficiency data limits direct applicability, but the behavioral patterns are informative for understanding potential efficiency effects in similar designs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game involves two players: player 1 and player 2.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each participant completes only one round/treatment; the game is a one-shot contract.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (performance) is continuous: player 2 can complete any number of tables from 0 to 20.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (fine) is imposed automatically for each table not completed: €0.05 (T2) or €0.20 (T3) per table. The cost to the punisher (player 1) is negative: player 1 receives the fine from player 2.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the amount deducted from player 2 and transferred to player 1 per table not completed: €0.05 (T2) or €0.20 (T3).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is present; only fines for non-compliance.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the structure: one contract, one task, one round.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (fine) is always linked to the contract partner (player 1) in T2 and T3, and to the experimenter in T4. The identity of the punisher is clear by design.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes; only individual compliance/performance is measured.
- The game is a contract-based real effort task with two players, not a standard linear public goods game.
- The findings may not generalize to larger groups or repeated interactions.
- The effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be directly inferred from the reported outcomes.
- The sample consists mainly of students in a lab setting, which may limit external validity.
