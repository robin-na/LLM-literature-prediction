## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper argues that human generosity and punitive behavior, even in one-shot or group contexts, can be explained by evolved psychological mechanisms that are sensitive to uncertainty and error costs, rather than requiring group selection. Agent-based simulations cited by the authors show that cooperation and punishment can evolve under individual selection, with quantitative variation in punitive tendencies supporting group cooperation. However, the paper does not report direct empirical or experimental results on efficiency or group payoff in public goods games with or without punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical context for why punishment and cooperation may be observed in public goods game-like environments, even when classic evolutionary theory would not predict them. However, it does not provide direct empirical evidence or quantitative estimates of efficiency or payoff effects of punishment in such games. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency outcomes, this paper is mainly useful for understanding possible psychological mechanisms and for interpreting why punishment may be present, but it does not inform the magnitude or direction of efficiency changes due to punishment under specific game design dimensions.
