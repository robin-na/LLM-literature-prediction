The main outcomes are behavioral: fractions of different strategies (cooperators, defectors, punishers), and exit probabilities. Payoff is used as a mechanism for updating strategies and exit probabilities, but group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff are not directly reported or analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that allowing individuals to adaptively exit the game based on their payoff relative to aspiration can significantly increase the prevalence of cooperation, even when punishment is weak. There is an optimal aspiration level at which adaptive exit best promotes cooperation. Adaptive exit protects cooperators from exploitation by defectors and reduces the burden of punishment on punishers by increasing defectors' exit probability. The model shows that adaptive exit can eliminate second-order free-riding and that the interplay between exit and punishment is crucial for the evolution of cooperation. However, the paper does not report on group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that adaptive exit mechanisms and the interaction between exit and punishment can strongly affect the prevalence of cooperation in spatial social dilemmas. However, because the outcomes are reported in terms of strategy frequencies and not group efficiency or total payoff, the findings are only adjacent to the prediction task. The results suggest that enabling exit (or similar mechanisms) may increase cooperation and reduce the cost of punishment, but do not provide quantitative guidance for predicting efficiency outcomes in public goods games with or without punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N players on an L x L lattice (L=100-500), so player count is known and varied in simulations.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for >5x10^5 Monte Carlo steps, with results averaged over the last 5000 steps. The number of rounds is large and fixed by simulation design.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between discrete strategies: cooperate, defect, or punish (which is a cooperator who punishes). Contributions are not continuous.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost per act is denoted by alpha (α), set to 0.1 in simulations. The cost is fixed and known.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the fine β imposed on defectors per cost α to the punisher. The ratio β/α is varied to study its effect.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- Outcomes are reported in terms of strategy frequencies (cooperation, defection, punishment) and exit probabilities, not group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff.
- The study is simulation-based and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The adaptive exit mechanism is specific to the model and may not correspond to real-world exit or opt-out options in public goods games.
- No analysis of the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff-based outcomes is provided.
