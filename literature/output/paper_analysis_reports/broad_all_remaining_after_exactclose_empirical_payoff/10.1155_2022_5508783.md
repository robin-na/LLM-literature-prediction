## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the probability of both firms adopting open (cooperative) green innovation strategies increases with higher excess benefits from cooperation, higher government subsidies, and higher government penalties for non-cooperation, and decreases with higher costs of cooperation and higher spillover benefits to non-cooperators. There are threshold values for each parameter that determine whether cooperation or non-cooperation is more likely. The model is theoretical and supported by numerical simulations, but does not report efficiency or total payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, mechanistic evidence about how punishment (government penalties) and reward (subsidies) can shift the likelihood of cooperative behavior in a two-player, repeated, public-goods-like innovation game. It does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or payoff outcomes, so it cannot be used to quantitatively predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. However, it supports the qualitative claim that increasing punishment or reward increases the probability of cooperative (open) strategies, which in analogous public goods games is often associated with higher efficiency. The model is adjacent to public goods games, but is not a direct PGG or experimental result.
