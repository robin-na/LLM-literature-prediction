# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The effect of voluntary participation on cooperation
Authors: Nosenzo, D., & Tufano, F.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2017

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average group earnings
- efficiency (earnings as % of maximum possible gains)
- realized earnings (including outside option)
- contributions as % of endowment
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate
- participation/withdrawal decisions
- classification as cooperator/free-rider
- beliefs about others' cooperation (optimism)
- selection into the game
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are contribution rates and efficiency (earnings as a percentage of maximum possible). Participation/withdrawal decisions and beliefs are also analyzed. Efficiency is explicitly calculated and compared across treatments.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment compares three main treatments: Baseline (forced participation), Unconditional (voluntary participation before knowing partner's contribution), and Conditional (voluntary participation after seeing partner's contribution). In Baseline and Unconditional, average contributions and efficiency are nearly identical (about 22% of endowment contributed; efficiency ~22%). In Conditional, average contributions and efficiency are much higher (47% of endowment; efficiency up to 81.9% among those who agree to play, and 46.6% overall). The positive effect of voluntary participation on efficiency is only observed when participation can be used as a threat against free-riding. There is little evidence that unconditional voluntary participation leads to assortative matching of cooperators, even when the outside option is increased. The main mechanism for increased efficiency is the threat of non-participation disciplining free-riders, not selection. However, frequent withdrawals can reduce realized efficiency gains.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a two-person one-shot PGG, enabling voluntary participation only increases efficiency if players can condition their participation on knowledge of the partner's intended contribution (i.e., if non-participation is a credible threat against free-riding). Unconditional voluntary participation (without information) does not improve efficiency, even with a higher outside option. For prediction, the presence of a conditional participation mechanism (a form of endogenous partner exclusion or exit threat) is a strong moderator of punishment-like effects on efficiency, even in the absence of explicit costly punishment. The results suggest that, in similar game designs, enabling conditional exit can substitute for explicit punishment in raising efficiency, but unconditional exit does not. Control efficiency (forced participation) is a good predictor of efficiency in unconditional exit games, but not in conditional exit games, where efficiency is much higher.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are two-person (dyadic) public goods games.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are one-shot (single round).
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: subjects allocate any number of tokens (0-20) between private and group account.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each token in group account gives 2 points to each player (total 4), so MPCR = 2/3 = 0.6667.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game is one-shot (single round).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. In Conditional treatment, subjects see the partner's contribution before deciding to confirm/withdraw participation. In other treatments, no information about partner's contribution is given before participation decision.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses only two-person, one-shot public goods games; results may not generalize to larger groups or repeated interactions.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanisms are present; only voluntary participation/exit is studied.
- The efficiency gains from conditional participation are reduced when accounting for the costs of withdrawals (i.e., realized earnings including outside option), and may be sensitive to the value of the outside option.
- The findings may not generalize to non-excludable public goods or real-world settings with richer communication or partner selection mechanisms.
- The study does not test explicit punishment or reward treatments, so direct comparison to those mechanisms is not possible.
