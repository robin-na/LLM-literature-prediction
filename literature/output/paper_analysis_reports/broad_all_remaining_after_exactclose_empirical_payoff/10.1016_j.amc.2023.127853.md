## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that social exclusion is a more effective strategy than punishment for promoting cooperation in spatial public goods games. Introducing antisocial punishment (defective punishers) helps eliminate second-order free-riding by cooperators who do not bear exclusion costs. The level of cooperation is sensitive to the exclusion probability, antisocial punishment fine, and exclusion/punishment costs. High exclusion probability and low antisocial punishment fine promote cooperation. The model demonstrates that cooperative excluders can protect cooperators from invasion by defectors and defective punishers, and that the combination of social exclusion and antisocial punishment can sustain high cooperation levels. However, the study does not report on group efficiency or total payoff, so its direct relevance to efficiency prediction is limited.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that mechanisms like social exclusion and antisocial punishment can increase cooperation rates in spatial public goods games, which may be associated with higher efficiency in practice. However, because the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings are most useful for understanding how the structure and parameters of punishment and exclusion mechanisms affect cooperation dynamics, which may inform priors about efficiency effects in similar game designs. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper's results should be treated as informative about behavioral mechanisms but not as direct evidence.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes; all results are in terms of cooperation rates and strategy frequencies.
- Findings are based on simulation of evolutionary dynamics, not empirical or experimental data.
- The model is specific to spatial public goods games with four strategies and may not generalize to other PGG variants or real-world settings.
- No direct evidence is provided for the effect of punishment or exclusion on efficiency or group payoff.
