Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are the probability that the system reaches a cooperative state and the frequency of different strategies at equilibrium. These are not direct measures of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, but are closely related to cooperation rates and strategy prevalence.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that asynchronous exclusion (where excluders act sequentially and avoid redundant costs) is more effective at promoting cooperation than synchronous exclusion (where all excluders act simultaneously and pay redundant costs). This effect is strongest when the probability of exclusion success is high and the public goods multiplier is low (well-mixed) or intermediate (structured). The main reported outcomes are the probability of the system reaching a cooperative state and the frequency of different strategies at equilibrium. The paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, so its findings are only indirectly relevant to predicting efficiency effects of punishment-like mechanisms in public goods games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about the effectiveness of exclusion (a sanctioning mechanism) in promoting cooperation in optional public goods games. While it does not report efficiency or group payoff, the increased probability of reaching cooperative states under asynchronous exclusion suggests that, under some parameter regimes, more effective or less costly sanctioning can increase cooperation rates. However, because efficiency is not measured, and because exclusion differs from standard punishment (it removes defectors from benefit sharing rather than imposing a direct cost), the findings should be used with caution for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in standard PGGs. The results are most relevant for settings where exclusion is the main sanction and where the cost structure of sanctioning can be varied.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size (M) is a parameter in the model, with main results for M=20, and supplementary results for M=50 and 100. Group size (N) is also specified (N=5).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 to 50,000 rounds to reach evolutionary stability; in structured populations, averages are taken over the last 1,000 rounds.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The investment amplification factor r (1 < r < N) is the equivalent of the PGG multiplier; MPCR = r/N. r is varied in the analysis.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Exclusion cost c_E is a function of the probability of exclusion success (beta), with c_E' > 0 and c_E'' > 0. The cost structure is central to the comparison of synchronous and asynchronous exclusion.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Probability of exclusion success (beta) is varied; effectiveness of exclusion per cost is central to the analysis.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus; all main outcomes are behavioral (probability of cooperation, strategy frequencies).
- Exclusion is not identical to standard punishment: it removes defectors from benefit sharing rather than imposing a direct cost.
- Findings are based on theoretical modeling and simulation, not empirical or experimental data.
- Parameter regimes where exclusion is effective may not map directly to standard PGGs with punishment.
- No direct evidence on how exclusion or punishment affects efficiency or group payoff in these models.
