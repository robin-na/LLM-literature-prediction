# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Regrouping to Reduce Overfishing: Evidence from a Series of Lab-in-the-Field Experiments in Mexico
Authors: Leibbrandt, A., Puerto, S., & Velez, M. A.
Journal: Marine Resource Economics
Year: 2021

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- individual extraction (resource use)
- group extraction (resource use)
- compliance with quota
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of sending sad faces (social disapproval intervention)
- message sending to authority (venting intervention)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is individual extraction (how much each participant extracts from the common pool resource), which is inversely related to group welfare/efficiency but is not directly reported as efficiency or total group payoff. Compliance with quota is also analyzed. No direct reporting of group efficiency as a ratio to the social optimum or maximum possible group payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a framed CPR lab-in-the-field experiment with real fishermen, regrouping participants by their past extraction levels (publicly ranking and then reassigning groups) leads to a significant reduction in individual and group extraction, both in the observation period (after announcement) and after regrouping. The effect is strongest for the most sustainable group but is present across all groups. Compliance with the quota increases, especially in the most sustainable group. Other interventions (social disapproval, venting, moral priming) do not reduce extraction. The control group shows no reduction in extraction. The results suggest that symbolic recognition and group reassignment can reduce overexploitation and improve compliance, indirectly improving efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that regrouping (a form of symbolic reward and group reassignment based on past behavior) can reduce overexploitation in CPR games, even in field-relevant settings. However, it does not test costly punishment or direct sanctioning mechanisms. The findings are most relevant for predicting the effect of non-costly, symbolic, or reputational interventions rather than monetary punishment. The game design is close to a public goods game but is a CPR extraction game. Efficiency is not directly measured, but reductions in extraction and increased compliance imply improved group welfare. For prediction tasks, this paper suggests that interventions leveraging social recognition and group identity can have positive effects on efficiency, especially when punishment is weak or absent.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 5 participants per session; all treatments use 5-person groups.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 10 rounds per session: 5 baseline, 5 post-intervention (with further division into observation and regrouping periods).
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No communication or chat between players is allowed; only information about group extraction is provided.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (extraction) is discrete but not all-or-nothing: participants choose 1-6 units to extract each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The payoff function is specified, with individual and group extraction affecting payoffs. The Nash equilibrium is to extract 6 units; the social optimum is 2 units. The marginal per-capita return is not given as a single number but is embedded in the payoff function.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Formal punishment: 1 peso per unit above quota if monitored (2% chance per round). Social disapproval intervention: sending a sad face costs 1 peso, but this is not the main focus of the paper.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Formal punishment is weak: 1 peso per unit above quota, with only 2% monitoring probability. Social disapproval (sad faces) has no monetary impact on the recipient.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Symbolic reward exists in the regrouping intervention: sustainable fishers are publicly recognized and grouped together. No monetary reward.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Symbolic reward (public recognition, group assignment) is costless to the experimenter and participants.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward is symbolic and public; effectiveness is demonstrated by reduced extraction and increased compliance.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game has 10 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants are told the total group extraction and can calculate their earnings.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the social disapproval intervention, sad faces are sent anonymously; in the regrouping intervention, public ranking is shown with subject identifiers (but not real names).

## Important Limitations
- The experiment does not test costly punishment or direct monetary sanctions as the main intervention; formal punishment is weak and infrequent.
- Efficiency is not directly measured or reported as a ratio to the social optimum; only extraction and compliance are reported.
- The game is a common pool resource extraction game, not a standard public goods game, though it is closely related.
- The main intervention is symbolic reward and group reassignment, not punishment.
- Findings may not generalize to settings where monetary punishment is available or where group reassignment is not feasible.
- The effect on actual field behavior is inferred but not directly measured.
