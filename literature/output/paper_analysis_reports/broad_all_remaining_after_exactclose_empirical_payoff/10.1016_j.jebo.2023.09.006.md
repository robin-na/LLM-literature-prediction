# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The behavioral mechanisms of voluntary cooperation across culturally diverse societies: Evidence from the US, the UK, Morocco, and Turkey
Authors: Weber, T. O., Schulz, J. F., Beranek, B., Lambarraa-Lehnhardt, F., & Gächter, S.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2023

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution level
- beliefs about others' contributions
- punishment assigned
- expected punishment
- cooperative attitudes (conditional cooperation)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are contribution rates, beliefs, and punishment behavior. The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings as a primary outcome, though average payoffs in local currency are reported in a sample characteristics table. All main analyses focus on behavioral outcomes, not on efficiency or welfare.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that differences in cooperation across culturally diverse societies are mainly explained by differences in beliefs about others' cooperativeness, not by differences in cooperative preferences or punishment behavior. In the presence of punishment, only participants in the US and UK significantly increase their contributions compared to the no-punishment condition. Actual punishment of free riding is similar across all four countries, and antisocial punishment is low everywhere. The paper does not report efficiency or group payoff as a main outcome, so the direct effect of punishment on efficiency is not analyzed.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence on how punishment affects contribution rates in one-shot public goods games across different societies, but does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. For the downstream prediction task, the findings suggest that the effect of punishment on efficiency is likely to be positive in settings similar to the US and UK (where contributions increase with punishment), but negligible in settings similar to Morocco and Turkey (where contributions do not increase). However, because efficiency is not directly measured, this inference is indirect and based on contribution behavior. The game design dimensions are well specified, allowing for mapping to prediction inputs, but the lack of direct efficiency data is a limitation.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 participants in all games.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. One-shot (single round) public goods games; each participant plays two games (N-Game and P-Game), each is one-shot.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous from 0 to 20 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per capita return (MPCR) is 0.4 (each token contributed increases each group member's payoff by 0.4).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point costs the punisher 1 MU.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the target's payoff by 2 MUs.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game is one-shot (single round).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the P-Game, after contributions, participants are informed of the contributions of their group members before assigning punishment.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is assigned to specific group members, but the paper does not specify whether the identity of the punisher is revealed to the punished. However, the design is anonymous and one-shot, so identification is not possible.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings as a main outcome; all main analyses focus on contribution rates and behavioral outcomes.
- The effect of punishment on efficiency or welfare is not directly analyzed, so any inference about efficiency must be made indirectly from contribution behavior.
- The study uses student samples from four countries, which may not be representative of the general population.
- The design is one-shot and does not address repeated interaction or long-term effects.
- No reward mechanism is included, so findings may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
