Outcome notes:
The model tracks whether the system reaches full cooperation or full defection, and analyzes the dynamics of cooperation under different parameter regimes. Utility is tracked for cooperators and defectors, but the main reported outcomes are behavioral (cooperation, norm compliance) rather than aggregate efficiency or group payoff. There is no explicit reporting of group efficiency, welfare, or total earnings as a primary outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model shows that strong punishment (high punishment strength) and high social capital (frequent encounters) support the emergence and resilience of cooperation in a common-pool resource dilemma. However, resource scarcity, high variability in resource inflow, and increased inequality (in land or skill) undermine cooperation, making collapse of social norms more likely. The system tends toward either full cooperation or full defection, with little evidence for stable mixed equilibria. The model does not directly report efficiency or group payoff, but the collapse of cooperation is interpreted as a breakdown of socially optimal resource use.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that punishment can sustain cooperation in public-goods-like resource dilemmas, but its effectiveness is strongly moderated by resource scarcity, variability, and inequality. The model is not a direct PGG but is structurally similar, with a focus on seasonal water allocation and peer punishment. The main outcome is the presence or absence of cooperation, not efficiency or group payoff, so the paper is most useful for predicting the conditions under which punishment will be effective at sustaining cooperation, rather than for quantitative prediction of efficiency gains. The findings suggest that in environments with high scarcity, variability, or inequality, the positive effect of punishment on efficiency (if any) is likely to be weaker or absent.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Community size N is a model parameter (default N=100), but the effect of varying N is not systematically analyzed.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Model is iterated over multiple time steps (years/seasons) until equilibrium is reached; number of rounds is not a primary focus but is present in the simulation structure.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate (restrain water use) or defect (over-extract). No continuous contribution levels.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost to punisher (mu) and to punished (omega) are key parameters. Higher punishment strength (omega/mu) increases cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is modeled as the ratio omega/mu; higher values make punishment more effective and support cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is entirely theoretical and not empirically validated.
- Outcomes are primarily behavioral (cooperation/defection), not efficiency or group payoff.
- Only two discrete strategies (cooperate or defect) are allowed; no continuous contribution levels.
- Assumes a well-mixed population with random encounters; no spatial or network structure.
- Punishment costs are fixed and do not adapt to prevalence of defection.
- No reward mechanism is modeled.
- Assumes symmetric division of remaining water in the dry season.
- Findings may not generalize to real-world settings without empirical validation.
