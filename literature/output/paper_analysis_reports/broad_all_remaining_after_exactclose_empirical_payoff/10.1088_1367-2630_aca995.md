## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that a switching strategy—punishing most defectors and expelling a few—can significantly increase the level of cooperation in a spatial public goods game. The effectiveness of this mechanism depends on the cost of punishment, the threshold for switching between punishment and expulsion, and the population density. Lower punishment costs, lower switching thresholds, and intermediate population densities maximize cooperation rates. The model demonstrates that combining expulsion and punishment is more effective at promoting cooperation than either strategy alone. However, all results are reported in terms of cooperation rates, not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that punishment (and expulsion) mechanisms can increase cooperation rates in spatial public goods games, and that the effect depends on design parameters such as punishment cost, threshold, and population density. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, it does not directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings suggest that, under certain conditions, punishment and expulsion can increase cooperation, which may correlate with higher efficiency, but this is not demonstrated or quantified in the paper.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report or analyze group efficiency, total payoff, welfare, or surplus; all results are in terms of cooperation rates.
- Findings are based on theoretical modeling and simulation, not empirical or experimental data.
- The model assumes spatial structure and specific update rules that may not generalize to all public goods game settings.
- No direct evidence is provided for the effect of punishment or expulsion on efficiency or group payoff.
