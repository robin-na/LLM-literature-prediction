## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- overall cooperation proportion (sum of exposure cooperation and ordinary cooperation)
- fractions of each strategy (exposure cooperation, exposure defection, ordinary cooperation, ordinary defection)
- evolutionary stability of strategy mixtures
Outcome notes:
The paper studies the evolution of cooperation and the fractions of different strategies in a theoretical model. It does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings. Outcomes are mainly behavioral (cooperation rates, strategy frequencies).

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in an infinite well-mixed population, exposure-based punishment (and especially the combination of reward and punishment) can promote the evolution of cooperation, but only if the cost of exposure is less than the punishment for defection. Rewarding exposed cooperators alone is insufficient to promote cooperation. There are threshold and nonlinear effects: simultaneous reward and punishment is most effective, and there are optimal values for exposure cost and reward/punishment strength. The model identifies parameter regions where cooperation can be sustained, but all results are in terms of the fraction of cooperators, not group efficiency or payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that punishment (and especially combined with reward) can increase cooperation rates in a prisoner's dilemma setting with exposure-based mechanisms. However, it does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, so it cannot be directly used to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. The model is adjacent to PGGs and focuses on cooperation rates, so its main value is as indirect support for the idea that punishment can promote cooperation under certain cost/benefit conditions. It does not provide quantitative predictions for efficiency or payoff outcomes.

- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment impact per exposure is β; higher β increases cooperation rates.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward for exposed cooperators (α) is a key parameter; reward alone is insufficient, but combined with punishment it increases cooperation.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward impact per exposure is α; higher α increases cooperation up to a threshold.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes.
- The setting is an infinite well-mixed population, not a finite group or repeated game.
- Outcomes are limited to cooperation rates and strategy frequencies, not payoffs.
- The model is a prisoner's dilemma variant, not a public goods game.
- No empirical or experimental data is provided.
- No explicit cost for rewarding is modeled; only exposure cost and punishment cost are considered.
