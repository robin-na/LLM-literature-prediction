# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Heterogeneous groups overcome the diffusion of responsibility problem in social norm enforcement
Authors: Przepiorka, W., & Diekmann, A.
Journal: Plos One
Year: 2018

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual sanctioning rate
- group-level sanctioning rate (at least one sanctions)
- efficient sanctioning rate (exactly one sanctions)
- stealing rate (norm violation)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: whether individuals or groups sanction norm violators, and whether norm violations occur. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, or total welfare as primary outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that diffusion of responsibility (lower individual sanctioning rates as group size increases) is present in symmetric groups but is largely overcome in heterogeneous groups where one member has a lower cost of sanctioning. Over repeated rounds, groups tacitly coordinate on the strong member to sanction, leading to higher and more efficient norm enforcement. However, the main outcomes are behavioral (sanctioning rates), not payoff-based efficiency or group welfare. The findings suggest that group heterogeneity can mitigate the bystander effect in norm enforcement, but do not provide direct evidence on how this affects group efficiency or total payoffs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides informative behavioral evidence about how group size and heterogeneity in punishment cost affect the likelihood and efficiency of norm enforcement in a volunteer's dilemma context. However, it does not report group efficiency or payoff outcomes, so it cannot be directly used to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. The findings suggest that introducing heterogeneity in punishment cost can increase the likelihood and efficiency of norm enforcement, especially in larger groups, but the translation to efficiency or welfare outcomes in standard PGGs is indirect.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Group size (n=2 vs n=5) is a main experimental manipulation. Larger groups show lower individual sanctioning rates in symmetric VOD (diffusion of responsibility), but group-level enforcement does not decrease significantly.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. 30 rounds per subject (15 without penalty, 15 with penalty). Learning effects observed: in asymmetric VOD, strong player's sanctioning rate increases over time, especially in larger groups.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (sanctioning) is a binary choice: each victim decides whether or not to sanction (all-or-nothing).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Sanctioning cost is manipulated: symmetric VOD (all K=25 MU), asymmetric VOD (strong K=25 MU, weak K=35 MU). Lower cost for strong member increases likelihood and efficiency of sanctioning.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sanctioning reclaims the stolen amount (U=50 MU) for all victims if at least one sanctions. Punishment is effective and binary (all stolen money reclaimed if any victim sanctions).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Subjects know the experiment consists of 30 rounds (15 per part).

## Important Limitations
- The main outcomes are behavioral (sanctioning rates), not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- The game is a volunteer's dilemma (step-level public good with binary sanctioning), not a standard linear public goods game.
- The experiment does not disentangle the effect of group size on first-order cooperation (stealing) from the effect of expected payoffs.
- No direct measurement or reporting of group efficiency or surplus; findings on efficiency are limited to 'efficient sanctioning' (exactly one person sanctions), not total group payoff.
- Translation of findings to standard PGGs or to efficiency prediction is indirect.
