Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is extraction (number of fish taken), which is inversely related to group conservation and thus to group payoff/efficiency. The paper does not directly report group efficiency as a ratio to the social optimum, but the extraction variable is a direct proxy. No explicit group payoff or total earnings are reported, but the mapping is clear from the game structure.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a lab CPR game with 5 players per group and 18 rounds, the introduction of mandates with fines (punishment) for over-extraction significantly reduced extraction (i.e., increased conservation and group welfare) compared to a normative information baseline. High fines had a persistent 'crowd-in' effect on conservation, especially among those who initially extracted the least. Low fines had weaker or no persistent effects. In contrast, a public revelation mechanism (where one player's extraction is revealed each round) did not reduce extraction and led to a 'crowd-out' effect among conditional cooperators, likely because the revealed extractions were higher than expected, shifting descriptive norms upward. The study does not report efficiency as a ratio, but the extraction results map directly to group welfare. Heterogeneity analyses show that the effects of punishment and public revelation depend on behavioral type and prosocial orientation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a CPR game with 5 players, continuous extraction choices, no chat, and a known social optimum, the introduction of monetary punishment (especially with high fines) increases group welfare by reducing extraction, both during and after the intervention. The effect is strongest for high fines and among those with low initial extraction. Public revelation mechanisms may backfire, especially among conditional cooperators, if the revealed behavior is less cooperative than expected. For prediction, the presence of monetary punishment (with sufficient magnitude and justification) should be expected to increase efficiency relative to a normative information baseline. The mapping from extraction to efficiency is direct, so these results are highly informative for predicting efficiency outcomes in similar CPR or PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups had 5 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 18 rounds per session, with interventions in rounds 7-12.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication was allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Extraction choice was continuous between 0 and 40 fish.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each round, the remaining fish double (up to 200), then are divided equally. The marginal per-capita return is not a fixed number but is determined by the group extraction; the social optimum is 20 per person.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a fine for over-extraction, with per-unit fine of 2 (low) or 12 (high). The cost is to the punished, not the punisher (external enforcement).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the per-unit fine and audit probability (10%). High fine (12 per unit) is sufficient to induce compliance for risk-neutral players; low fine (2 per unit) is not.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants did not know in advance when interventions would start or end, but the total number of rounds (18) is fixed.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each round, participants were shown the group's total extractions for that round.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. In the public revelation treatment, the audited player's ID and extraction were shown to the group; in fine treatments, punishment is via external audit, not peer punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is extraction (inverse of conservation/efficiency), not reported as explicit group efficiency or payoff ratio.
- The experiment uses a student sample from a Chinese university, which may limit generalizability to other populations.
- Punishment is externally imposed (not peer punishment), so results may not generalize to peer-sanction settings.
- No explicit group payoff or total earnings are reported, though the mapping from extraction to efficiency is direct.
- The public revelation mechanism may differ from real-world peer pressure or shaming interventions.
- The study does not include a reward or positive incentive treatment.
