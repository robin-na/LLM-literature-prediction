Non-payoff outcomes:

- compliance with interventions (e.g., vaccination, mask-wearing, social distancing)
- evolutionary stability of strategies
- heterogeneity in populations (e.g., coexistence of cooperators and defectors)
Outcome notes:
The paper is a theoretical and conceptual review. It discusses payoff structures and evolutionary stability but does not report empirical or simulated efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes. Outcomes are primarily about the conditions for cooperation, defection, and the dynamics of strategy frequencies.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper synthesizes theoretical and modeling work on PGGs in disease and cancer, highlighting how cooperation can be sustained or undermined depending on game parameters, network structure, and the presence of mechanisms like social norms or sanctions. It reviews how public health interventions (e.g., vaccination, mask mandates) can be conceptualized as public goods dilemmas, and how individual decision-making, social structure, and perceived costs/benefits shape compliance. In cancer, it discusses how cooperation among cancer cells can be modeled as a PGG, with implications for treatment strategies. However, the paper does not provide quantitative findings on efficiency or group payoff, nor does it systematically analyze the effects of punishment or sanctions on efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides conceptual background on how PGGs are used to model cooperation in health-related contexts and discusses mechanisms (including sanctions and incentives) that can promote cooperation. However, it does not provide direct evidence or quantitative results on the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff in PGGs. For the downstream prediction task, this paper may inform the qualitative understanding of how design dimensions (e.g., network structure, cost-benefit ratios, threshold effects) can influence cooperation, but it does not supply data or direct evidence to calibrate predictions of efficiency with or without punishment. Its discussion of sanctions is high-level and not tied to specific game parameters or outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Player count (N) is a central parameter in the theoretical description of PGGs, with discussion of its impact on dynamics, but not manipulated or analyzed for efficiency outcomes.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Both binary (cooperate/defect) and continuous contribution versions of the PGG are discussed.
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The marginal per-capita return (r/N) is discussed as a key parameter affecting incentives and dynamics.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is a theoretical and conceptual review; it does not present new empirical data or simulation results.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are not directly analyzed or reported.
- Punishment and sanctions are discussed only at a high level, with no quantitative modeling or parameterization.
- No direct evidence is provided for the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff in PGGs.
- Many referenced results are from other papers; this paper synthesizes but does not test or quantify effects.
