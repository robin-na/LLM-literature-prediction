The main dependent variables are resource abundance (a direct proxy for group welfare/efficiency), average payoffs, and the sustainability of the resource pool. The model also tracks the proportion of cooperators and defectors, but the focus is on resource sustainability and group-level outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a dynamic common-pool resource game with probabilistic punishment of defectors, increasing the monitoring probability and punishment severity generally increases the proportion of cooperators and can help maintain higher resource levels (i.e., higher efficiency/welfare). However, if the initial resource level is too low or the resource's maximum growth rate is insufficient, the system will experience resource depletion (tragedy of the commons) regardless of punishment strength. The model demonstrates bistability: depending on initial conditions, the system may converge to full cooperation with resource depletion, or to a mixed/cooperative state with sustainable resources. Thus, punishment can improve efficiency, but only when ecological conditions allow for resource regeneration.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-like resource environments is conditional: punishment (higher probability and severity) can increase efficiency and resource sustainability, but only if the resource's regenerative capacity and initial abundance are sufficient. In settings where the resource cannot recover (low growth rate or low initial stock), punishment does not prevent collapse. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the effect of punishment on efficiency is moderated by ecological parameters (resource growth, initial abundance) in addition to standard game design dimensions. The model is not a standard PGG but is closely related, and the efficiency outcome is directly modeled.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are used.
- The model assumes a fixed punishment penalty for defectors and does not model the cost to punishers.
- Ecological parameters (resource growth rate, initial resource level) are critical moderators, but their real-world values may be hard to estimate.
- The model assumes rational agents and does not account for bounded rationality or other behavioral factors.
- No reward mechanism is included in the main analysis, though it is mentioned as a possible extension.
- The model is not a standard public goods game but a closely related common-pool resource game with binary choices.
