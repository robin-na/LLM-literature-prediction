Journal: Chaos

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperators (cooperation rate)
- conditional fraction of cooperators given local umpire and neighbor configuration
Outcome notes:
The main reported outcome is the fraction of cooperators in the player layer at equilibrium. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. All results are in terms of cooperation rates, not payoffs.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that third-party punishment (umpire intervention) always increases the fraction of cooperators compared to no intervention, even if all umpires are corrupt and only accept bribes. The fraction of fair umpires, the spatial distribution of umpires, the punishment fine, and the bribe size all have strong effects on cooperation rates. In particular, higher bribe costs and higher punishment fines both increase cooperation, but the effect of bribe cost is more pronounced. The model is robust to network structure (square lattice vs. scale-free). However, all results are reported in terms of cooperation rates, not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the presence of a punishment mechanism (even if imperfect or corrupt) increases cooperation rates in PGG-like environments. However, because the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or any direct payoff-based outcome, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The results are most useful for predicting changes in cooperation rates as a function of punishment design, but not for predicting group welfare or surplus. The model's detailed exploration of punishment cost, effectiveness, and corruption is informative for mechanism design, but the lack of payoff reporting is a key limitation for efficiency prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use L x L square lattices (e.g., L=100, so 10,000 players), and also scale-free networks with 10,000 nodes. Player count is fixed per simulation but not varied as a treatment.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 20,000 steps, with outcomes averaged over the last 2,000 steps. The number of rounds is fixed and not varied as a treatment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: players are either cooperators (contribute) or defectors (do not contribute).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r is varied (e.g., r=1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, etc.). MPCR is r/5 (since each PGG has 5 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment fine (alpha) is varied. Higher alpha increases cooperation rates. Cost to punisher is not explicitly modeled (umpires are not strategic agents), but alpha is the cost imposed on defectors.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the fine alpha. Higher alpha increases cooperation rates.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or any direct payoff-based outcome; all results are in terms of cooperation rates.
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- Punishment cost is modeled as a fine imposed on defectors, not as a cost to the punisher (umpire), and umpires are not strategic agents.
- No behavioral or psychological realism (e.g., no communication, no learning beyond imitation, no explicit payoff feedback to umpires).
- No reward mechanism is modeled.
- Findings may not transfer directly to real-world or experimental PGGs with human subjects.
