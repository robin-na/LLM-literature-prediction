# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Short- & long-term effects of monetary and non-monetary incentives to cooperate in public good games: An experiment
Authors: Lefebvre, M., & Stenger, A.
Journal: Plos One
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- average individual contribution
- group contribution
- contribution dynamics over time
- effect of receiving punishment/reward points on later contributions
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is contribution to the public good (tokens contributed per round). No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total welfare is reported. All results are in terms of contribution rates, not payoffs or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Monetary and non-monetary punishments and rewards all significantly increase contributions to the public good compared to baseline. Monetary punishment and non-monetary reward are the most effective. After incentives are removed, contributions remain higher than baseline for several rounds, indicating some long-lasting effect, but eventually decline. The effect of punishment and reward on later contributions depends on whether individuals were high or low contributors and how many points they received. The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes, so the findings are limited to contribution behavior.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling punishment (especially monetary punishment) increases contribution rates in a standard linear public goods game with four players and 30 rounds. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The results are highly informative for predicting contribution behavior, but less so for efficiency unless one assumes a direct mapping from contribution to efficiency (which is not always valid, especially when punishment is costly). The design dimensions are well specified, so the paper is useful for understanding the behavioral response to punishment and reward mechanisms in similar lab PGGs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in fixed groups of 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 30 rounds in all main treatments; some robustness checks with different lengths for reward treatments.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication allowed between group members.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: subjects choose how many tokens (0-20) to contribute.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each token contributed yields 0.4 tokens to each of 4 group members).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In monetary punishment, each point assigned costs 0.25 tokens to punisher and reduces target's payoff by 1 token (1:4 cost-impact ratio). Non-monetary punishment is costless and has no payoff effect.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Monetary punishment: 1 token cost to target per 0.25 token cost to punisher (1:4 ratio). Non-monetary punishment: no payoff effect.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward mechanisms (monetary and non-monetary) are present in separate treatments.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Monetary reward: each point costs 0.25 tokens to rewarder, increases target's payoff by 1 token. Non-monetary reward: costless, no payoff effect.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Monetary reward: 1 token gain to target per 0.25 token cost to rewarder (1:4 ratio). Non-monetary reward: no payoff effect.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game is finitely repeated with 30 periods (except in one robustness check with uncertain end).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, subjects see the contribution levels of each other group member (not their payoffs).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment/reward is anonymous: subjects know how many points they received, but not who assigned them.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; all results are in terms of contribution rates.
- Punishment is costly, so higher contributions do not necessarily imply higher efficiency.
- No direct analysis of the effect of punishment on group earnings or surplus.
- The experiment is limited to short time frames (one-hour lab sessions); long-term effects beyond the session are not measured.
- The design does not allow for communication or reputation effects, which may limit generalizability to real-world settings.
