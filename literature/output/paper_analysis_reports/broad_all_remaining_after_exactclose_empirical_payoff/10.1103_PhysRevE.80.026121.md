# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Partner selections in public goods games with constant group size
Authors: Wu, T., Fu, F., & Wang, L.
Journal: Physical Review E
Year: 2009

## Task Relevance

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperators at equilibrium
- distribution of cooperation by degree
- effect of group size and partner selection regime on cooperation
- effect of reputation-based exclusion (soft punishment) on cooperation
Outcome notes:
The main reported outcomes are the fraction of cooperators in the population under different group sizes, partner selection regimes (degree-based, reputation-based), and network structures. The paper discusses payoffs in the model description and provides some analytical payoff difference calculations, but does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare as primary outcomes. Reputation-based exclusion is interpreted as a form of 'soft' punishment, but there is no explicit costly punishment mechanism.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that, in public goods games with constant group size on networks, increasing group size generally promotes higher cooperation rates, especially when individuals can select partners based on reputation. Reputation-based partner selection (interpreted as costless exclusion of defectors) is more effective at sustaining cooperation than degree-based selection. The model does not include explicit costly punishment or reward mechanisms. The main reported outcome is the stationary fraction of cooperators, not group efficiency or total payoff. Analytical calculations show that larger group size increases the payoff advantage of cooperators over defectors, but these are not translated into reported efficiency outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of enabling explicit punishment on efficiency in public goods games. It is relevant for understanding how group size and partner selection (especially reputation-based exclusion) affect cooperation rates, but not efficiency or payoff. The reputation-based exclusion mechanism is a form of costless, indirect punishment, which differs from the costly punishment typically studied in experimental PGGs. Therefore, the findings should not be directly transferred to predictions about the efficiency effects of enabling costly punishment. The paper may be useful for understanding the role of group size and partner selection in cooperation, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency changes due to punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size N is a parameter in the simulations (e.g., N=500), but the main focus is on group size (g+1), not total player count.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for thousands of rounds (e.g., 5000 rounds for transition, 1000 rounds for data collection), but the number of rounds is not a manipulated variable of interest.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The renormalized enhancement factor δ = r/(g+1) is the MPCR; varied in simulations.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not implement or analyze explicit costly punishment; reputation-based exclusion is a form of costless, indirect punishment.
- The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- Findings about the effect of group size and partner selection on cooperation rates may not generalize to efficiency or payoff outcomes, especially in the presence of costly punishment.
- Simulation-based results may not capture all dynamics present in experimental or real-world settings.
- No analysis of reward mechanisms or communication effects.
