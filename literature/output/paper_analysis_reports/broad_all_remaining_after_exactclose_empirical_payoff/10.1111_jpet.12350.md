# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Taxation, redistribution, and observability in social dilemmas
Authors: Brent, D. A., Gangadharan, L., Mihut, A., & Villeval, M. C.
Journal: Journal of Public Economic Theory
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- group efficiency (sum of payoffs relative to social optimum)
- mean group payoff
- individual payoffs
- inequality (payoff gap between high- and low-type subjects)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- extraction level (resource use)
- proportion extracting at maximum capacity
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are extraction (resource use), group efficiency (sum of payoffs relative to social optimum), and payoff inequality. Extraction is a behavioral outcome, but efficiency and payoffs are central and explicitly analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Both Pigovian tax and increasing block rate mechanisms, when combined with redistribution, lead to significantly higher group efficiency (mean payoff as a ratio to the social optimum increases from 0.86 in baseline to 0.97–0.98 with tax, p<0.01) and lower inequality, regardless of whether individual decisions are observable. Observability alone (without tax) increases extraction and reduces efficiency (mean payoff ratio drops to 0.82), contrary to the expectation that it would promote cooperation. When tax mechanisms are present, observability has no significant effect on efficiency or extraction. There is no significant difference in efficiency between the two tax mechanisms, but the Pigovian tax reduces inequality more. The results are robust across group types and over time.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a CPR environment with heterogeneous extraction capacities, introducing a monetary sanction mechanism (taxation with redistribution) substantially increases efficiency compared to a baseline with no sanctions, regardless of whether decisions are observable. The effect is robust to the presence or absence of observability (i.e., information about others' actions). The design is not a standard linear PGG, but the CPR game is closely related and the efficiency outcome is directly comparable. The findings suggest that, for similar game designs, enabling a monetary sanction mechanism (taxation) will predictably increase efficiency, and the effect is not moderated by observability. The results do not speak directly to peer-to-peer punishment, but do inform the effect of centralized monetary sanctions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players; fixed throughout all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 18 periods (rounds) per group in all treatments.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (extraction) is discrete and bounded (integer units between 0 and max capacity), not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Nonlinear payoff function; marginal per-capita return is not constant but is determined by parameters a=9, b=0.2, c=0.1, d=1. Social cost per unit is d=1 per group member. Social optimum and Nash equilibrium are analytically derived.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the number of periods (18) in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. In observability treatments, subjects see the type, extraction, and payoff of each group member after each period. In non-observability treatments, only own payoff is shown.

## Important Limitations
- The design uses centralized tax and redistribution mechanisms, not peer-to-peer punishment or decentralized sanctions.
- The CPR game is nonlinear and features heterogeneity in extraction capacity, which differs from standard linear PGGs.
- No direct manipulation of punishment cost or effectiveness; only tax rate and redistribution are varied.
- Observability is implemented as full information about others' actions and payoffs, not as chat or communication.
- Extraction capacity is assigned based on a real-effort task, which may create entitlement effects.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with random assignment of capacity, different forms of heterogeneity, or peer punishment.
