## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in repeated trust games with private information, efficiency (total group payoff) can be increased above the autarky (Nash) level through favor-exchange relationships (dynamic reciprocity) or through symmetric punishment (negative reciprocity). The optimal equilibrium structure depends on the parameters: when information asymmetries are low, symmetric punishment can support higher efficiency than favor-exchange. Hybrid equilibria (honeymoon period followed by favor-exchange or punishment) can further improve efficiency in some cases. The paper provides explicit formulas for the efficiency achieved under each regime. Immediate reciprocity (punishment for non-reciprocation) is not always necessary; dynamic reciprocity (future favor) is often sufficient. The model is not a PGG but is closely related in its focus on cooperation, trust, and efficiency under repeated interaction and private information.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical predictions for how efficiency in repeated cooperation games is affected by the availability and structure of punishment and favor-exchange mechanisms. While not a PGG, the model is adjacent and the efficiency formulas and comparative statics are informative for predicting efficiency in environments where punishment (symmetric, infrequent) is possible versus when only favor-exchange is available. The results suggest that, in some parameter regions, introducing symmetric punishment can increase efficiency above favor-exchange alone, especially when information asymmetries are low. However, the model is limited to two players and does not include standard PGG features like group size or marginal per-capita return. The findings are most useful for understanding the theoretical potential of punishment to enhance efficiency in repeated cooperation with private information, but direct quantitative prediction for PGGs would require caution.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is a two-player repeated trust game (players a and b).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is played over an infinite horizon (repeated game with discounting).
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Investment/contribution is continuous: the investor can send any x in [0,1].
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is q*k (expected return per unit invested). q<1 is probability of success, k>1 is multiplier. The investment is value-enhancing on average (qk>1).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is modeled as a symmetric reduction in continuation value (e.g., reversion to autarky or lower payoff). The effectiveness of punishment depends on the threat of future low payoffs. In some parameter regions, symmetric punishment supports higher efficiency.
- reward availability: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Reciprocity (rewarding trust) is possible: the trustee can reciprocate by sending some or all of the returns back to the investor. Immediate and dynamic reciprocity are both discussed.
- reward technology: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Reward is the ability to reciprocate by transferring returns. The effectiveness is determined by the model's incentive constraints.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is infinite horizon with discounting; players know the structure.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Transfers between players are publicly observed; investment levels are public, but investment outcomes are private to the trustee.

## Important Limitations
- The model is limited to two players; results may not generalize to larger groups or standard public goods games.
- Punishment is modeled as a symmetric reduction in continuation value (not as costly peer punishment); there is no explicit punishment cost or technology as in PGG experiments.
- The game is a repeated trust game with private information, not a standard PGG; mapping to PGG dimensions requires caution.
- All results are theoretical; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- The model assumes infinite horizon and common discount factor; finite games or heterogeneous patience are not analyzed.
- No communication or chat is allowed; the effects of communication are not addressed.
