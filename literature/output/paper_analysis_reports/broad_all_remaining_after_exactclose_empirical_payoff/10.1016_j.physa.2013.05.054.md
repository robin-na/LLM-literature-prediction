The main reported outcome is the equilibrium fraction of cooperators (\rho_C), a behavioral measure. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. Payoff is discussed in the model, but not as a reported outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in non-excludable public goods games, the ability to switch partners asymmetrically (i.e., only being able to sever outgoing ties) reduces the equilibrium level of cooperation compared to excludable (symmetric) PGGs. The addition of costly punishment, where cooperators pay a cost to reduce defectors' payoffs, substantially increases the equilibrium fraction of cooperators. The effect of punishment intensity (amplification factor) is also positive for cooperation. However, all outcomes are reported in terms of cooperation rates, not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that costly punishment increases cooperation rates in non-excludable PGGs with dynamic networks, but does not provide direct evidence about efficiency or group payoff. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, the results are only indirectly informative: higher cooperation rates typically imply higher efficiency, but the paper does not quantify this relationship or report actual payoffs. The model details (e.g., group size, network structure, punishment cost and effectiveness) are described, but the absence of payoff-based outcomes limits direct applicability to efficiency prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use M=1000 (main), with examples at M=100 and M=2500. Group size is determined by network degree (average degree 4).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 50,000 MC steps (plus 1,000 for averaging). Outcomes are equilibrium/steady-state values.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: agents are either cooperators (contribute c=1) or defectors (contribute 0).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is r/n(i), with r (amplification factor) varied in simulations (e.g., r=4.8, r=5). Group size n(i) is determined by network degree.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishers pay cost c=1 per punishment event (same as contribution cost).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is parameterized by amplification factor \phi (varied in simulations, e.g., \phi=3). Higher \phi increases cooperation.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Agents have access to reputation scores of neighbors for partner switching, but not explicit summaries of contributions or payoffs.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is the equilibrium fraction of cooperators, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- All results are from simulation of a theoretical model, not empirical or experimental data.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or total group payoff, limiting direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
- Model assumes binary strategies (C or D) and compulsory participation; no voluntary participation or continuous contribution.
- No reward mechanism is considered.
- Punishment is compulsory for cooperators, which may not reflect real-world voluntary punishment behavior.
