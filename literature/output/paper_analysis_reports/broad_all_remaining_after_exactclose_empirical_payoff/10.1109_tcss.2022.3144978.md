Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the emergence of social norms, cooperation rates, and the evolution of agent strategies (boldness and vengefulness) in metanorm games on hypergraphs. While payoffs are defined and used in agent learning, the main reported outcomes are behavioral (cooperation, norm compliance, punishment), not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in metanorm games on uniform random hypergraphs (URH), increasing group size (G) promotes the emergence of social norms, leading to higher cooperation rates and more agents willing to punish defectors. Network connectivity (number of groups) does not affect norm emergence. On heterogeneous hypergraphs (HRH), standard BV-learning fails to establish norms for 'leaver' agents, but a new dynamic relevance BV-learning algorithm enables both hub and leaver agents to establish norms and cooperate. Real-world hypergraph data (coauthorship networks) show similar patterns. The main findings are about the conditions for norm emergence and high cooperation, not about efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is informative for predicting the effect of punishment on cooperation and norm compliance in public-goods-like games with high-order (group) interactions, especially regarding the role of group size and network structure. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. The results suggest that larger group size and effective learning algorithms for punishment/metapunishment can promote cooperation and norm compliance, which may be associated with higher efficiency in related settings, but this is not directly measured.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=1000 agents; some figures use N=16 for illustration. Player count is fixed in most analyses.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 1,000,000 rounds per run, 10 runs per condition.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/defection is a binary (all-or-nothing) choice: agents choose to cooperate or defect in each opportunity.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost to punisher (E) is -2; cost to punished (P) is -9. These are fixed in the main simulations.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment impact per unit cost is determined by fixed parameters: P = -9 (to target), E = -2 (to punisher). No explicit manipulation of punishment effectiveness ratio.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents observe their own and neighbors' total scores (TS) and sub-scores (DS, PS, POS, HS) for learning; this is essential for the learning algorithms.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus; all main outcomes are behavioral (cooperation, norm compliance, strategy evolution).
- Findings are based on agent-based simulations and theoretical analysis, not empirical experiments with human subjects.
- The model is a metanorm game, which is a close but not exact variant of the standard public goods game.
- No variation or analysis of marginal per-capita return (mpcr) or public goods multiplier.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanism is included.
- The effect of punishment is interpreted through cooperation and norm compliance, not directly through efficiency or group payoff.
