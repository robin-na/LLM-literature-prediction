The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators at the end of evolutionary simulations, summarized as a cooperation index (Omega). No direct analysis of group payoff, efficiency, or welfare is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in structured (scale-free network) populations, the introduction of peer punishment does not always promote cooperation. When the cost-to-impact ratio (q/p) is high, punishment can actually reduce the prevalence of cooperation compared to the no-punishment baseline. Only when the cost of punishing is sufficiently low relative to its impact does punishment enhance cooperation. The effect is mapped across a range of game parameters (T, S) and punishment parameters (p, q), showing regions where punishment is beneficial or detrimental for cooperation. The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task. It does not report efficiency or group payoff, but shows that the effect of punishment on cooperation (and by implication, on efficiency in adjacent games) is highly sensitive to the cost-effectiveness of punishment (q/p) and to the network structure. In structured populations, punishment can backfire unless costs are low. For prediction, this suggests that the efficiency impact of punishment in public-goods-like games may be negative or mixed if punishment is costly relative to its impact, especially in networked or heterogeneous settings. However, the lack of direct efficiency or payoff outcomes limits the direct applicability of these findings to the prediction task.

- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost (q) is systematically varied; high cost relative to impact (q/p) leads to negative or mixed effects on cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment impact (p) is systematically varied; the ratio q/p is the key moderator of punishment's effect.

## Important Limitations
- The model studies two-player games (PD and SH), not public goods games; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- The main outcome is the final fraction of cooperators, not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are from agent-based simulations.
- No analysis of payoff-based efficiency or surplus; only cooperation prevalence is measured.
- No communication, reward, or more complex social mechanisms are modeled.
- Network structure is fixed (scale-free); other structures are not analyzed.
