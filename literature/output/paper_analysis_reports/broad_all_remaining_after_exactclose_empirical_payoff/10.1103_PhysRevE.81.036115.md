## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that allowing players to distinguish between opponents and update their strategies in the interaction that gave the worst payoff leads to the evolutionary stability of cooperation in well-mixed populations. Under synchronous update, cooperation eventually dominates, while under asynchronous update, the fraction of cooperation remains at the lower bound set by the initial mutual cooperation. The mechanism of 'implicit punishment'—where exploitative (C,D) interactions are replaced by mutual defection (D,D)—prevents defectors from exploiting cooperators indefinitely, but does not involve explicit costly punishment. The model does not include explicit payoff-based punishment or reward mechanisms, and does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is of limited direct use for predicting the effect of explicit punishment on efficiency in public goods games or their close variants, as it studies a repeated Prisoner's Dilemma with a novel update rule and no explicit costly punishment or reward. The 'implicit punishment' mechanism is a structural feature of the strategy update process, not a player choice to pay a cost to reduce another's payoff. The main outcome is the fraction of cooperation, not efficiency or group payoff. The findings may be relevant for understanding how mechanisms that neutralize exploitation without explicit costs can stabilize cooperation, but do not provide quantitative evidence on the efficiency effects of punishment in standard PGG-like environments.
