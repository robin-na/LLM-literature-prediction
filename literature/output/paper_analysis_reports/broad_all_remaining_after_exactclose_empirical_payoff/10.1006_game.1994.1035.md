# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: EQUILIBRIUM COOPERATION IN 3-PERSON, CHOICE-OF-PARTNER GAMES
Authors: DAVIS, D. D., & HOLT, C. A.
Journal: Games and Economic Behavior
Year: 1994

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate (proportion of times the selected player chooses the cooperative action)
- punishment behavior (switching away from a defector)
- reward behavior (reselecting a cooperator)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are cooperation rates and switching/rewarding behavior. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is reported. The paper discusses payoffs in the game structure and in the context of incentives, but does not report aggregate or average payoffs as an outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that the ability to punish by switching partners does not increase cooperation in two-stage games compared to single-stage controls, but does lead to much higher cooperation rates in ten-stage games. The increase in cooperation in longer games is attributed to both the opportunity for reputation building and the higher cost of being punished (switched away from) early in a long sequence. The punishment mechanism is salient and does not require coordination. However, the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be directly determined from the reported results.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that salient, unilateral punishment mechanisms (switching partners) can increase cooperation rates in repeated games, but only when the game is sufficiently long to allow for reputation effects and to make the cost of being punished significant. For prediction tasks, the findings suggest that the number of rounds (num_rounds) is a critical moderator of punishment effectiveness on cooperation. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, it does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency changes due to punishment. The results are most informative for predicting behavioral (cooperation) outcomes rather than efficiency, and the game is not a standard public goods game but an adjacent partner-selection game with similar strategic features.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played with three players (A, B, C) per group.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Number of rounds (stages) is the main treatment: 1, 2, or 10. Substantially higher cooperation rates are observed in the 10-stage games compared to 1- or 2-stage games.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/cooperation choice is binary: B and C choose either the cooperative (B1/C1) or defect (B2/C2) action.
- punishment cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Punishment (switching) is costless to the punisher (A), but imposes a cost on the punished (B or C) by denying them future earnings. The cost to the punished increases with the number of remaining rounds.
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is highly effective: switching unilaterally denies the punished player future earnings. Effectiveness increases with the number of rounds remaining.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is possible by reselecting a cooperator; this is measured and reported.
- reward cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Reward (reselection) is costless to the rewarder (A), but benefits the rewarded (B or C) by giving them future earnings.
- reward technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Reward is effective in that it provides future earning opportunities to the rewarded player.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed of the number of stages in each treatment (1, 2, or 10).

## Important Limitations
- The game is not a standard public goods game; it is a three-person choice-of-partner game with adjacent strategic features.
- No direct efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates and behavioral outcomes are analyzed.
- The punishment mechanism (switching) is costless to the punisher and may not generalize to costly punishment settings.
- The sample consists of undergraduate business students from a single university, which may limit generalizability.
- The experiment does not include communication or chat between players.
- Players only observe their own payoffs, not others', which may affect learning and strategy.
