# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Punishment and Cooperation in Stochastic Social Dilemmas
Authors: Xiao, E., & Kunreuther, H.
Journal: Journal of Conflict Resolution
Year: 2016

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average earnings
- efficiency (implied by group earnings relative to maximum possible)
- investment cost and punishment cost
Non-payoff outcomes:
- investment rate (cooperation rate)
- frequency and magnitude of punishment
- retaliatory punishment
- antisocial punishment
Outcome notes:
The paper reports both behavioral outcomes (investment/cooperation rates, punishment behavior) and payoff-based outcomes (average earnings per treatment). Efficiency is discussed in terms of average earnings relative to the maximum possible, but not always explicitly as a ratio.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In two-person repeated prisoner's dilemma games, both deterministic and stochastic (with outcome uncertainty), the restricted punishment mechanism (only cooperators can punish defectors) increases cooperation rates compared to no-punishment and unrestricted punishment. However, the efficiency gains (measured by average earnings) from restricted punishment are significant only in deterministic environments; in stochastic environments, restricted punishment does not significantly increase average earnings compared to no-punishment. Unrestricted punishment leads to frequent retaliation and antisocial punishment, resulting in lower average earnings than the baseline in both environments. The effectiveness of punishment in promoting cooperation and efficiency is reduced by outcome uncertainty, as noncooperators are less likely to be punished when outcomes are stochastic.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency depends on both the structure of the punishment mechanism (restricted vs unrestricted) and the presence of outcome uncertainty (stochastic vs deterministic). In games with two players, repeated for 10 rounds, with perfect information and no communication, restricted punishment can increase efficiency in deterministic settings but not in stochastic ones. Unrestricted punishment can reduce efficiency due to retaliation and antisocial punishment. For prediction, the presence of outcome uncertainty and the type of punishment mechanism are key moderators: restricted punishment is more likely to improve efficiency when outcomes are deterministic and less so when outcomes are stochastic. Control efficiency (no-punishment) is similar in both environments, but the efficiency boost from punishment is context-dependent.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are two-player (dyadic) prisoner's dilemma games.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject plays 10 rounds with one partner, then 10 rounds with a new partner. Main analysis focuses on the first 10 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players; all decisions are anonymous and simultaneous.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/investment decision is binary: invest or not invest.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not a standard PGG, but the marginal per-capita return can be inferred: in DPD, investing reduces both players' losses; in SPD, investing reduces probability of loss for both. Exact MPCR is not calculated but can be derived from payoff tables.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Every 3 talers deducted from the counterpart costs the punisher 1 taler.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: 1 taler spent by punisher reduces target's payoff by 3 talers. Maximum punishment per round is 24 talers.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are told the number of periods (10) in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, players are informed of their counterpart's decision, whether the negative event occurred, and both players' total losses.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. In two-player games, punishment is always attributable to the counterpart; players know who punished them.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses only two-player (dyadic) games, which may not generalize to larger groups or standard public goods games.
- The social dilemma is a prisoner's dilemma (binary invest/not invest), not a standard linear public goods game with continuous contributions.
- Efficiency is measured as average earnings, but the paper does not always report efficiency as a ratio to the maximum possible.
- The stochastic (SPD) and deterministic (DPD) games are matched on expected value, but risk preferences may affect behavior differently.
- No communication or reputation mechanisms are present; results may differ in richer social environments.
- The punishment mechanism is either fully restricted (only cooperators can punish defectors) or fully unrestricted; intermediate or more nuanced mechanisms are not tested.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanisms are included, so findings do not address the effects of rewards.
- The sample is from a single university population, which may limit external validity.
