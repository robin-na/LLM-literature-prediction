## Outcomes Measured

- probability of drivers yielding to pedestrians (Y)

Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are behavioral: the probabilities of yielding, monitoring, and supervising. The paper discusses system stability and the elimination of violations, but does not report group payoff, efficiency, or total welfare in the sense of public goods games. Payoff functions are modeled, but aggregate payoff or efficiency is not the main reported outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that: (1) Without external control, the system exhibits persistent oscillations in the probability of drivers yielding to pedestrians, and TMD monitoring disappears over time. (2) Introducing dynamic punishment (fines that increase with non-compliance) suppresses oscillations and increases compliance, but does not fully eliminate violations. (3) Combining dynamic punishment and reward (penalty-incentive control) leads to full compliance (all drivers yield) and eliminates the need for monitoring or pedestrian supervision. (4) Alternatively, a sufficiently high static monitoring rate by TMD (above a threshold) can also achieve full compliance. The results are framed in terms of behavioral probabilities and system stability, not aggregate efficiency or payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that dynamic punishment and/or reward mechanisms can stabilize and maximize compliance in a multi-agent system with monitoring and sanctioning, analogous to public goods games with punishment and reward. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot be used directly to predict efficiency outcomes. The findings are most useful for understanding the qualitative effect of punishment and reward on stabilizing cooperation and eliminating violations, especially in systems with endogenous monitoring and third-party enforcement. The model is adjacent to PGGs, but the outcome mapping to efficiency is indirect.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are reported.
- Outcomes are behavioral probabilities and system stability, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare as in standard public goods games.
- The setting is adjacent to, but not an exact variant of, the public goods game: it models compliance with traffic rules rather than voluntary contribution to a public good.
- The model uses continuous population-level strategies, not discrete small-N groups.
- Micro-level interactions and heterogeneity are not captured; the model is macro and aggregate.
- No direct mapping to efficiency or surplus outcomes, limiting direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
