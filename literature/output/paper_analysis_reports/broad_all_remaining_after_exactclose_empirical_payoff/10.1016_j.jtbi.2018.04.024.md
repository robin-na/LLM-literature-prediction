## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- speed to full cooperation (generations to reach full cooperation, G)
- stability of cooperation (standard deviation of G)
- probability of each model being fastest to full cooperation
Outcome notes:
The main outcome is the number of generations required to reach full cooperation (G), which is a measure of the speed of convergence to cooperation, not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. The models always reach full cooperation, so efficiency or payoff differences are not analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the presence and type of information dissemination strongly affect the speed at which full cooperation is achieved in a spatial public goods game with reputation and conformity. Negative disseminators (who spread information about defectors) most often lead to the fastest convergence to full cooperation, followed by neutral and positive disseminators. Silencers (no dissemination) are least effective. The effect of dissemination type is most pronounced when conformity synergy, link-neighbor degree, population size, or environmental noise are high, or when the synergy factor is low. However, all models eventually reach full cooperation, so there is no analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare differences between models.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it does not include a punishment or sanctioning mechanism, nor does it report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. However, it is informative about the role of information diffusion and reputation in accelerating the achievement of cooperation. The findings suggest that mechanisms which amplify reputational consequences for defectors (akin to negative gossip or public shaming) can speed up the transition to full cooperation, especially in certain parameter regimes. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency or payoff, this paper is only indirectly relevant, as it does not address whether the final payoffs or efficiency differ between conditions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size N is a key parameter, varied in simulations (N=100,200,300,400,500). Larger N reduces the advantage of negative dissemination.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for at least 5000 steps or until full cooperation is reached. The main outcome is the number of generations to full cooperation (G).
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: each agent chooses to cooperate (contribute 1) or defect (contribute 0).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r is varied (r=1.6,2,2.4,2.8,3.2); MPCR is r/(k+1), with k the number of neighbors (varied from 1 to 10).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players (agents) can observe the strategies of their link-neighbors and, via disseminators, possibly non-link-neighbors in their reference group.

## Important Limitations
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present; negative dissemination acts via reputation, not direct payoff reduction.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are not reported or analyzed; all models reach full cooperation, so only the speed to cooperation is compared.
- Findings are based on simulation/modeling, not empirical or experimental data.
- The models assume binary (all-or-nothing) contribution choices and do not consider continuous contribution levels.
- The results may not generalize to settings where full cooperation is not always achieved or where payoff differences persist between conditions.
