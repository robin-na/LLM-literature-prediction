The main reported outcome is the fraction of cooperators (\rho_c) in the population, not group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings. Some qualitative discussion of payoffs is present, but no explicit efficiency or group payoff metrics are reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing punishment into a spatial public goods game increases the fraction of cooperators, and that further reducing the cost of punishment through a synergistic effect (i.e., when multiple punishers share the cost) leads to even higher cooperation rates. The effect of synergy is most pronounced when the punishment fine is low; at high fines, the additional benefit of synergy is minimal. The main outcome is the steady-state fraction of cooperators, not group efficiency or total payoff. The paper qualitatively links higher cooperation to higher group payoffs, but does not report efficiency or payoff data directly.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that both punishment and reduced punishment cost (via synergy) increase cooperation rates in spatial public goods games. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, so it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings are most useful for predicting how design dimensions like punishment cost and punishment effectiveness affect cooperation rates, and by extension may suggest (but not demonstrate) positive effects on efficiency. For the downstream prediction task, this paper provides indirect support for the idea that lower punishment cost and higher punishment effectiveness (via synergy) should increase efficiency, but this is inferred from cooperation rates, not measured payoffs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a 100x100 lattice (10,000 players), each with 4 neighbors. Group size G=5 (focal + 4 neighbors).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 20,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with outcomes averaged over the last 5,000 MCS.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: cooperators contribute 1, defectors contribute 0.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Enhancement factor r is varied; MPCR is r/G. r/G is a key parameter in the results.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost per punisher is reduced by a synergy factor (gamma) when multiple punishers act. Lower cost (higher gamma) increases cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is set by fine beta; higher beta increases cooperation. Synergy (gamma) increases cost-effectiveness of punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency or group payoff; efficiency is not directly measured or reported.
- Results are based on agent-based simulations, not laboratory or field experiments.
- The model assumes spatial structure (lattice) and may not generalize to well-mixed or other network structures.
- No human subjects or real-world data are used; all findings are theoretical/simulation-based.
- No analysis of the effect of communication, information, or reward mechanisms.
