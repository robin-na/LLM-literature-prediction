The paper's main dependent variables are behavioral: mean cooperation rates, the dynamics of cooperation (peaks, troughs, transitions), and the role of norm internalizers in catalyzing, polarizing, and stabilizing cooperation. While payoffs are discussed in the model, the reported outcomes are not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare, but rather cooperation rates and their dynamics.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The presence of norm internalizers in the strategy space (even as a small minority, around 10% of the population) leads to higher mean cooperation rates, longer and more stable periods of high cooperation, and fewer long periods of low cooperation, compared to conditions with only group selection and punishment. This effect is most pronounced at intermediate benefit-to-cost ratios, where cooperation is possible but not guaranteed. The mechanism is indirect: norm internalizers polarize groups (increasing inter-group variance), catalyze spikes in cooperation when global cooperation is low, and stabilize high-cooperation states. The effect is not due to norm internalizers themselves cooperating more, but to their influence on the behavior of other agents. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes, so the direct effect on efficiency is not established.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in agent-based models of public goods games with group selection and imperfect punishment, adding a minority of norm internalizers can substantially increase mean cooperation rates, especially at intermediate benefit-to-cost ratios. However, because the reported outcomes are cooperation rates and not efficiency or group payoff, the findings should be used as indirect evidence for predicting efficiency: higher cooperation rates likely imply higher efficiency, but the mapping is not quantified here. The model assumes punishment is imperfect (not all defections are observed), and punishment is implemented as withholding benefits rather than costly punishment. The results are robust across two different model structures (abstract and spatial/naturalistic). For prediction tasks, this paper suggests that the presence of norm internalization mechanisms can amplify the effect of punishment and group selection on cooperation, but does not provide direct quantitative estimates of efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Group size is a fixed parameter in both models: 35 in the abstract model, 20 in the naturalistic model. Number of groups is also specified (60 and 10, respectively).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run for many rounds (not specified exactly, but figures refer to thousands of rounds and analysis of peaks/troughs over time).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is determined by the benefit-to-cost ratio (b/c), which is varied between 2.75 and 4.25. The public benefit b is shared among group members.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as withholding benefits from those caught defecting; it is not costly for the punisher.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is imperfect: only a fraction of defections are observed and punished (about half). Punishment is effective in that it withholds group benefits from defectors.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents observe the majority behavior in their group (for norm internalization), and the probability of being observed is sampled each round. Agents' strategies depend on observed group behavior.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes; all main results are in terms of cooperation rates and their dynamics.
- Punishment is modeled as withholding benefits (indirect reciprocity), not as costly punishment; this may limit generalizability to settings with costly punishment.
- The mapping from increased cooperation rates to efficiency or group payoff is not quantified.
- The models are agent-based simulations, not empirical experiments or field data.
- The effect of norm internalizers is most pronounced at intermediate benefit-to-cost ratios; at very high or low b/c, their impact is minimal.
- No reward mechanism is present; only punishment and group selection are modeled.
- No communication or chat between agents is modeled.
- The models assume agents can observe the majority behavior in their group, which may not always be realistic.
