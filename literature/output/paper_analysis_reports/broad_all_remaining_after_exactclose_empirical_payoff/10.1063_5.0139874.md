Outcome notes:
The paper's main dependent variable is the fraction of cooperators (\rho_c) in the population. There is no direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or total earnings. The study is simulation-based and focuses on the evolution of cooperation under different migration and tie retention regimes in the prisoner's dilemma game.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that tie retention (the ability to keep social ties with ex-neighbors after migration) can facilitate the emergence and maintenance of cooperation in the prisoner's dilemma game, even when migration is random. The maximum number of retained ex-neighbors (m) is a key parameter: higher m leads to higher cooperation rates. Social diversity in tie retention (variation in m across players) further enhances cooperation. Migration probability (p) has a non-monotonic effect: lower p generally favors cooperation, but diversity in p can create optimal conditions for cooperation. The results challenge the previous view that random migration is always detrimental to cooperation. However, all findings are in terms of cooperation rates, not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. It does not manipulate or model punishment, nor does it report efficiency, group payoff, or related outcomes. Its main relevance is in showing that network structure, migration, and tie retention can strongly affect cooperation rates in the prisoner's dilemma game. For the downstream prediction task, this paper suggests that social stickiness (tie retention) and migration patterns are important moderators of cooperation, but it does not inform the quantitative effect of punishment on efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Simulations are run on a 100x100 lattice (L=100), with population density \eta=0.5, so the number of players is approximately 5000. The player count is fixed in the model and not manipulated.
- number of rounds: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Simulations run for over 10^7 time steps, but the number of rounds is not a manipulated variable; results are reported after equilibrium is reached.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The prisoner's dilemma is a binary choice game: cooperate or defect (all-or-nothing).

## Important Limitations
- The study is simulation-based and does not involve human subjects or real-world data.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- Punishment and reward are not modeled; their effects cannot be inferred from this paper.
- The game is a two-player prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game or multiplayer social dilemma.
- Findings may not generalize to environments with explicit payoff-based sanctions or rewards.
