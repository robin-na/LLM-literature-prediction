# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Endogenous rewards promote cooperation
Authors: Yang, C. L., Zhang, B., Charness, G., Li, C., & Lien, J. W.
Journal: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America
Year: 2018

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- net profits (payoff) comparisons between reward and control conditions (SI Appendix, section 4.1 and Table S9)
- earnings of high contributors vs. group average (Fig. 2C)
- average income per subject
Non-payoff outcomes:
- average contribution levels
- trend in contributions over time
- reward assignment behavior
- conditional cooperation types and dynamics
Outcome notes:
The main reported outcomes are average contributions and their trends. Payoff-related outcomes are discussed in the SI Appendix, with some mention in the main text (e.g., net profits, earnings of high contributors), but efficiency (as group payoff relative to full cooperation) is not the primary dependent variable. The paper emphasizes behavioral outcomes (contribution, cooperation) and the mechanisms driving them.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The endogenous, budget-balanced reward mechanism in a repeated four-player PGG leads to a substantial and sustained increase in contributions compared to the standard PGG. The mechanism works by taxing each player's income and allowing players to allocate their share of the tax fund as rewards to others. Contributions remain high and even increase over time with rewards, while they decline in the control. The paper finds that most subjects are conditional cooperators, and that reward assignment is strongly correlated with contribution. Net profits (payoffs) are higher under the reward mechanism than in the control, and the mechanism is more efficient than punishment-based mechanisms reported in the literature, which often fail to increase net payoffs due to the cost of punishment. The study does not test punishment directly, but positions endogenous reward as a more efficient and socially desirable alternative.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that a budget-balanced, endogenous reward mechanism can substantially increase both contributions and net payoffs in a repeated PGG compared to a standard control. For prediction tasks, the findings suggest that enabling such a reward mechanism (with a 20% tax, peer allocation, and 1:1 reward effectiveness) will lead to higher efficiency than the control, and likely higher than typical punishment treatments (based on literature comparisons). However, the paper does not provide direct evidence on punishment, so its value for predicting the effect of punishment per se is indirect: it shows that reward can outperform punishment in terms of efficiency, especially when punishment is costly. The design dimensions (player count, rounds, feedback, etc.) are well specified and can inform parameterization for similar prediction tasks involving reward mechanisms, but not for punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All experiments use groups of 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each protocol consists of 10 rounds (20 rounds total per subject, with order of control/reward varied).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players; all interaction is via computer interface.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player chooses how many of 20 units to contribute.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 1.6/4 = 0.4 (each unit contributed increases group payoff by 1.6, split among 4 players).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Endogenous, budget-balanced reward mechanism is present in the treatment (not in control).
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is funded by a 20% tax on PGG income; each player allocates 30 points (no external cost, fully budget-balanced).
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is 1:1: each point allocated is redistributed from the tax fund, no multiplier.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of rounds in advance (10 rounds per protocol).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players receive feedback about the history of play, including others' contributions and rewards.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward assignment is anonymous but players know how much they received from each other group member (see SI Appendix, section 1).

## Important Limitations
- No punishment treatment is included; all comparisons to punishment are based on prior literature, not direct experimental evidence.
- Efficiency (group payoff relative to full cooperation) is not the primary reported outcome; most results focus on contribution rates and trends.
- The experiment uses a fixed group size (4 players) and a specific MPCR (0.4), limiting generalizability to other parameterizations.
- The reward mechanism is budget-balanced and endogenous, which may not be directly comparable to externally funded or multiplier-based reward or punishment mechanisms.
- All participants are university students from a single location, which may limit external validity.
