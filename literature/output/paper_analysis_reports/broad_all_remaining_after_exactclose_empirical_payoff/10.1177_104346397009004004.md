Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are the evolutionary prevalence of different strategies and the resulting 'level of solidarity', which is a proxy for cooperation or norm compliance, not direct group efficiency or total payoff. Payoff is discussed in terms of average pay-off per strategy and relative gains, but not as explicit group efficiency ratios.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in both dyadic and collective solidarity games (including public-goods-like settings), strategies that combine defection with active sanctioning of others (Hypocritical Cooperation) can robustly enforce high levels of solidarity (cooperation), provided the costs of sanctioning are not too high. However, this can also lead to 'overcompliance' or excessive norm enforcement unless checked by counterstrategies (Full Opposition). The evolutionary outcome depends on the mix of strategies, the costs and effectiveness of sanctions, and the distribution of 'poor' and 'rich' in the population. The model does not directly report efficiency or group payoff, but suggests that informal punishment can sustain cooperation under certain conditions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical support for the idea that informal punishment mechanisms can sustain cooperation in public-goods-like environments, especially when the cost of sanctioning is not prohibitive and when the population includes actors motivated to enforce norms even while defecting themselves. However, the findings are based on simulation and focus on the prevalence of strategies and 'level of solidarity' rather than explicit group efficiency or payoff. The results suggest that the effectiveness of punishment in raising efficiency will depend on the cost and effectiveness of sanctions, the strategic mix in the population, and the degree of inequality. The paper does not provide quantitative predictions of efficiency, so its value for direct prediction is limited to qualitative insights about when punishment is likely to be effective.

## Important Limitations
- The model is entirely simulation-based and does not provide empirical validation.
- Outcomes are reported as evolutionary prevalence of strategies and 'level of solidarity', not as explicit group efficiency or total payoff.
- Payoff structure is ordinal and not parameterized as in standard public goods games (e.g., no MPCR).
- Assumes random mixing and deterministic dynamics, which may not reflect real social networks or stochastic effects.
- Only two subgroups ('poor' and 'rich') are modeled; real populations are more heterogeneous.
- No modeling of communication, reward, or feedback mechanisms.
- The model does not address the sustainability of collective solidarity systems or the effectiveness of formal social control.
