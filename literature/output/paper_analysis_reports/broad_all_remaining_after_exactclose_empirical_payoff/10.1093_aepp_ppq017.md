Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is average group quality (success ratio), which is a behavioral outcome closely tied to efficiency but not a direct measure of group payoff or welfare. Individual and group earnings are not the primary focus, though they are determined by quality and are paid out to subjects.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that a blind, probabilistic punishment mechanism based on group performance can significantly increase average group quality (success ratio) in a cooperative production setting, but only when the cost of contributing high quality is not too high. In the LC treatment, average group quality rises from about 40% to about 70% of the maximum after punishment is introduced, while in the HC treatment, there is no significant improvement. The mechanism does not require individual monitoring, and its effectiveness depends on the underlying cost structure. The main outcome is group quality, which is closely related to efficiency, though not reported as explicit group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that introducing a group-level, blind punishment mechanism can substantially increase group performance (as measured by average quality/success ratio) in public-goods-like environments, but only when the cost of cooperation is not too high. For prediction tasks, the effect of punishment on efficiency is likely to be positive when the marginal cost of cooperation is low relative to the group benefit, and negligible when costs are high. The design features—no chat, fixed group size (n=4), 20 rounds per block, no individual monitoring, and punishment based on group outcome—are all relevant for mapping to prediction settings. The main limitation is that the outcome is group quality, not direct group payoff, but the two are tightly linked in this design.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players; fixed throughout all sessions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each block is 20 rounds; two blocks per session (NP then RP).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (quality) is continuous, not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Group benefit is average quality; individual cost is parameterized (c=200 or 300). The marginal per-capita return is not explicitly labeled as MPCR but is implied by the payoff structure.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is probabilistic exclusion from group benefit; cost is the loss of share for the punished, but there is no cost to other players (no active punisher).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is full exclusion from group benefit, with probability 1 - (group success ratio). Effectiveness is high (total loss of share if punished).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No explicit reward mechanism; only punishment (exclusion).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the number of rounds per block (20 rounds).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, subjects see their own delivered quality, own earnings, and the group success ratio.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is random and blind; subjects are told if they themselves were punished, but cannot identify who else was punished or who caused punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is group quality (success ratio), not direct group payoff or efficiency, though the two are closely linked in this design.
- The punishment mechanism is not peer-administered but is imposed probabilistically by the experimenter based on group performance.
- The experiment uses a specific cooperative production context (quality choice), which is not a standard linear public goods game, though it is closely related.
- The effect of punishment is highly sensitive to the cost of cooperation; results may not generalize to settings with different cost structures.
- No communication or reputation mechanisms are present; results may differ in more complex social environments.
- The study is conducted in a lab with student subjects, which may limit external validity.
