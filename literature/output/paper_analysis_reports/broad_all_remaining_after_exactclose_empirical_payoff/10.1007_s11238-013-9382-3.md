## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper shows that, while inequality aversion can theoretically motivate antisocial punishment under certain conditions, empirical data from several public goods game experiments indicate that most antisocial punishment acts do not fit this explanation. Instead, antisocial punishment is often carried out by the richest group members and is not typically accompanied by punishment of free riders, contrary to the predictions of the inequality aversion model. The findings suggest that other motives, such as revenge or a desire to harm others, are more likely explanations for antisocial punishment. The paper does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or group payoff, but notes that antisocial punishment is detrimental to the efficiency of informal punishment mechanisms.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is useful for understanding the behavioral mechanisms behind antisocial punishment in public goods games with punishment, but it does not provide direct quantitative evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff. It does, however, reinforce the point that the presence and frequency of antisocial punishment can undermine the positive effects of punishment on cooperation and efficiency, especially in environments where antisocial punishment is prevalent. For prediction tasks, the paper suggests that game design features or subject pool characteristics that foster antisocial punishment may reduce or even reverse the efficiency gains from enabling punishment. However, the paper does not report efficiency or payoff outcomes directly, so it cannot be used to calibrate quantitative predictions of efficiency changes.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not directly analyze or report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; its focus is on behavioral patterns of antisocial punishment.
- Empirical analysis is based on re-analysis of existing datasets, not new experiments.
- Findings on the determinants of antisocial punishment may not generalize to all public goods game variants or to environments with different punishment or reward technologies.
- The paper does not provide quantitative estimates of how antisocial punishment affects efficiency or group payoff, only qualitative discussion.
