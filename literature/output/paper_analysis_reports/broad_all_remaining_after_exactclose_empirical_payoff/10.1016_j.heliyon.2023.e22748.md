## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that a bonus-based mercenary punishment mechanism can substantially increase the proportion of cooperators in a public goods game, compared to the standard game without punishment. The effect is sensitive to the size of the bonus, the cost and effectiveness of punishment, and the allocation of fines collected from defectors. There is an optimal range for the bonus: too little fails to support punishers, too much burdens cooperators. Allocating more fines to punishers (rather than cooperators) is more effective in sustaining cooperation. The model is simulated on a network, and the main reported outcomes are the proportions of each strategy over time and under different parameter settings.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that introducing a punishment mechanism (with bonus support for punishers) increases cooperation rates in public goods games, especially when the parameters are tuned appropriately. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so its direct value for predicting efficiency outcomes is limited. The findings suggest that, in similar game designs, enabling punishment (with proper support) is likely to increase cooperation, which is often associated with higher efficiency, but this link is not directly quantified here. The paper is most useful for understanding the qualitative and parametric effects of punishment and bonus mechanisms on cooperation prevalence.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not report empirical or experimental data.
- The main outcome is the proportion of cooperators, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- The model assumes network structure and local interactions, which may not generalize to all public goods game settings.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or total group payoff, limiting direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
- Parameter values (e.g., player count, network structure) are not always fully specified.
