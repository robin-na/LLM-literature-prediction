## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperators
- frequency of full cooperation state

Outcome notes:
The paper's main dependent variable is the fraction of cooperators (i.e., cooperation rate) in the population at equilibrium. There is no direct analysis of group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. The study focuses on how adaptive dynamical linking affects the prevalence of cooperation, not on payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that adaptive dynamical linking—where agents lengthen or shorten social ties based on whether their partners cooperate or defect—can dramatically increase the prevalence of cooperation in both the prisoner's dilemma and public goods game. The effect is stronger when agents adjust their links more rapidly (higher adjustment rate, Δ), and under weak selection. The mechanism does not involve any explicit punishment, reward, or reputation system. The outcome measured is the frequency of full cooperation (all-cooperator absorbing state) in repeated simulations. There is no direct measurement or discussion of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for the prediction task of how punishment affects efficiency in public goods games. It is relevant as a demonstration that network adaptation (partner switching) can promote cooperation, but it does not include any punishment or sanctioning mechanism, nor does it report efficiency or payoff outcomes. Therefore, it should not be used as direct evidence for the effect of punishment on efficiency. It may be contextually useful for understanding alternative mechanisms (partner choice) that can promote cooperation, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency changes due to punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=1000 agents (see legend of Fig. 1).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 20,000–100,000 full time steps; each agent organizes a game per generation.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperator contributes c=1, defector contributes nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Enhancement factor r is varied; MPCR is r/(group size). Group size is variable (depends on degree), but average degree is 4.

## Important Limitations
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present; the study focuses on adaptive partner switching.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only cooperation rate is measured.
- Results are based on theoretical modeling and Monte Carlo simulations, not empirical or experimental data.
- Group size in the PGG is variable and depends on network degree, which may limit comparability to standard PGG designs.
- Findings are not directly transferable to the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games.
