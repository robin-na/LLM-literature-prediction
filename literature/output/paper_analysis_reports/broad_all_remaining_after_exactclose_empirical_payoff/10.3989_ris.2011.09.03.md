## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper reviews experimental evidence showing that in public goods games, the introduction of punishment mechanisms typically increases contributions and cooperation. However, it also highlights that punishment is costly and not always optimal from a collective (efficiency) perspective, especially when antisocial punishment or counter-punishment occurs. The effectiveness of punishment is robust to parameter changes (e.g., punishment cost/effectiveness), but can be undermined by incomplete information or network structure. Communication and group selection also enhance cooperation. The paper does not provide quantitative estimates of efficiency or group payoff, focusing instead on behavioral regularities and mechanisms.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong qualitative support that enabling punishment in public goods games generally increases cooperation and contributions, but warns that the net effect on efficiency depends on the costs of punishment and the prevalence of antisocial punishment. For prediction tasks, it suggests that punishment is more likely to improve efficiency when punishment is targeted at free riders, costs are not excessive, and antisocial punishment is rare. However, the paper does not provide direct quantitative evidence on efficiency outcomes, so its value is mainly in contextualizing and interpreting empirical findings from other sources.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is a theoretical review and does not report new empirical data or quantitative estimates of efficiency or group payoff.
- Findings are synthesized from multiple studies, so specific parameter effects (e.g., punishment cost, group size) are not systematically analyzed.
- Efficiency and payoff outcomes are discussed only contextually; the main focus is on behavioral outcomes such as contribution and cooperation.
- The review does not address reward mechanisms or their interaction with punishment.
- Some claims are based on cited literature rather than direct analysis.
