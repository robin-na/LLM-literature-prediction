## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in large-N social dilemmas (modeled after public goods games), targeted punishment strategies—where only a small, strategically chosen subset of defectors are punished at a time—can enable a small number of punishers to shift a population from full defection to full cooperation. This is in contrast to standard punishment strategies, which require much higher punishment capacity to achieve the same result. The main outcome is the stationary proportion of cooperators, not group payoff or efficiency. The model is robust to heterogeneity in player predispositions and to some randomness in the ordering of players. The findings are relevant for understanding how to design punishment institutions or strategies to escape the tragedy of the commons, but do not provide direct quantitative predictions about efficiency or payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical support for the idea that the structure and targeting of punishment can strongly affect the ability to achieve cooperation in public-goods-like environments, especially when initial cooperation is low. However, because the model's outcome is the proportion of cooperators rather than efficiency or group payoff, it does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency changes when punishment is enabled. The results suggest that, all else equal, targeted punishment strategies may be more effective at increasing cooperation (and thus potentially efficiency) than uniform punishment, but the lack of direct payoff analysis limits quantitative prediction. The model assumes binary (all-or-nothing) contribution choices, no explicit payoff or MPCR parameters, and does not model communication, reward, or information features. Thus, its main value is in highlighting the importance of punishment targeting and heterogeneity, rather than providing parameter-specific efficiency predictions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run with N=200 and N=1000 players; the model is designed for large N.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each player chooses between two states: cooperate or defect (binary choice).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is parameterized by π; higher π increases the ability to achieve cooperation.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Players are assumed to know each other's states (who is cooperating/defecting), but no summary statistics are modeled.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The main outcome is the proportion of cooperators, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- No explicit modeling of payoff structure, MPCR, or punishment/reward costs.
- No analysis of communication, information, or identification features beyond knowledge of others' states.
- Assumes binary (all-or-nothing) contribution choices.
- Findings may not translate quantitatively to real-world or laboratory PGGs with richer payoff structures.
