# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Evolution of repeated prisoner's dilemma play under logit dynamics
Authors: Ochea, M. I.
Journal: Journal of Economic Dynamics & Control
Year: 2013

## Study Type
theory; empirical subtype=N/A; experimental subtype=N/A

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average expected payoff
- group payoff (via population average payoffs)
- welfare (implied by average payoffs of strategies)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- strategy frequencies
- cooperation rates
- oscillatory/chaotic dynamics in population composition
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the average expected payoff of each strategy in the population, derived from the stationary distribution of the Markov process for each pairwise strategy interaction. The paper also reports on the long-run frequencies of strategies and the dynamic patterns (cycles, chaos) in the population, but these are secondary to the payoff-based analysis.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in repeated prisoner's dilemma environments with a finite set of memory-one strategies, the long-run average payoff (efficiency) and the prevalence of cooperation depend critically on the mix of strategies present and the game parameters. In some ecologies, strategies like Pavlov (WSLS), which can be interpreted as implementing endogenous punishment (by defecting after being defected upon), can dominate and sustain high payoffs, especially when unconditional cooperators are absent. However, the introduction of unconditional cooperators (AllC) can destabilize cooperation and lead to the dominance of defectors (AllD), reducing efficiency. The dynamics can be complex, with cycles and chaos emerging for intermediate values of the intensity of choice parameter. The paper provides explicit formulas for average payoffs and analyzes bifurcations in the system as parameters change. Overall, the effect of 'punishment-like' strategies on efficiency is mixed and highly sensitive to the strategic environment and parameter values.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that the effect of punishment-like strategies (e.g., Pavlov/WSLS) on efficiency in repeated social dilemma games is highly context-dependent. In environments without unconditional cooperators, such strategies can sustain cooperation and high payoffs, but their effectiveness is undermined when unconditional cooperators are present. The results suggest that the presence or absence of certain strategy types (especially unconditional cooperators) and the values of key parameters (benefit of cooperation, error rates, intensity of choice) are critical moderators of the efficiency impact of punishment-like mechanisms. For prediction tasks, this implies that the effect of enabling punishment (or punishment-like strategies) on efficiency cannot be determined solely by the presence of punishment, but must account for the broader strategic ecology and parameter settings. The findings are most directly relevant to repeated PD-like environments, but may inform predictions in PGG-like settings by analogy.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is based on a large population with random matching for pairwise repeated PD games. The effective 'player count' per game is always 2, but the population is large and well-mixed.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are modeled as infinitely repeated (no fixed number of rounds), with payoffs computed from the stationary distribution of the Markov process.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each stage game is a binary choice: cooperate (C) or defect (D).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is determined by the benefit and cost parameters (b, c) in the PD payoff matrix. MPCR = (b-c) for mutual cooperation, but the structure is PD, not PGG.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are infinitely repeated; there is no known or shown end round.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not include any empirical or experimental data.
- The setting is the repeated prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; while related, the results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- There is no explicit punishment or reward mechanism; 'punishment' is only endogenous via strategy choice (e.g., WSLS/Pavlov).
- All games are infinitely repeated with random matching; results may not generalize to finite repeated games or fixed groups.
- The analysis assumes a large, well-mixed population and does not consider network or group structure.
- Parameter values (benefit, cost, error rate, generosity, intensity of choice) are critical and results are highly sensitive to these settings.
- The model does not include communication, reputation, or other social mechanisms that may affect cooperation in real-world settings.
