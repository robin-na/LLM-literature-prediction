## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The chapter synthesizes theoretical models showing that cooperation in public goods games is difficult to sustain without mechanisms for targeted punishment or reward. When such mechanisms are available, cooperation can be stabilized at high levels. The chapter also reviews the evolutionary logic behind kin selection, reciprocity, and group selection, and discusses the cognitive and informational demands of indirect reciprocity and moral systems. However, it does not provide quantitative predictions or empirical data on efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical support for the idea that punishment and reward mechanisms can stabilize cooperation in public goods games, suggesting that their presence is likely to increase cooperation rates. However, because the chapter does not report efficiency or payoff outcomes, it cannot directly inform quantitative predictions of efficiency changes due to punishment. Its value lies in contextualizing why and when punishment might be effective, rather than in providing parameter-specific guidance for efficiency prediction.
