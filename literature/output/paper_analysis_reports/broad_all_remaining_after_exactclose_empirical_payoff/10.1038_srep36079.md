# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Think global, act local: Preserving the global commons
Authors: Hauser, O. P., Hendriks, A., Rand, D. G., & Nowak, M. A.
Journal: Scientific Reports
Year: 2016

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- PGG contribution level
- PD cooperation rate
- conditional cooperation in PD based on PGG contribution
- change in PGG contribution after receiving PD defection
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the level of contribution to the public good (PGG) and the pattern of cooperation in the pairwise PD stage. No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings is reported. The study focuses on behavioral outcomes (contribution/cooperation) rather than explicit payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that when participants can observe their neighbors' contributions to the public good and can condition their pairwise cooperation (in a PD) on this information, contributions to the public good are sustained at high levels, even in very large groups (up to 1,000 participants). In the control condition, where such information is not available, contributions decline over time. The mechanism is not direct punishment but rather the withholding of cooperation in a separate pairwise game, which acts as a social sanction. The effect is robust to group size and does not require participants to know the overall level of public good provision. The main outcome is sustained contribution, not measured efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that mechanisms allowing for targeted social sanctions (withholding cooperation in a pairwise PD) based on observed public good contributions can sustain high contribution rates in large groups. However, because the study does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The design is adjacent to punishment (withholding cooperation as a sanction) but does not involve costly punishment or explicit payoff reduction. The findings suggest that enabling targeted, observable social sanctions—even if not costly—can have a strong positive effect on contribution rates, which may translate to higher efficiency in similar designs, but this is not directly measured.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Experiment 1: average group size 39 (min 17, max 60); Experiment 2: group size 1,000. Group size is a key manipulated variable to test scalability.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Experiment 1: 20 rounds; Experiment 2: 10 rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. PGG contributions are continuous (0-20 MUs); PD choices are all-or-nothing (cooperate or defect).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. All PGG contributions are doubled and split equally among all group members. MPCR = 2 / group size (e.g., for group size 39, MPCR ≈ 0.051).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of rounds is fixed and known to participants (20 or 10 rounds).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are informed of their neighbors' PGG contributions in the treatment condition, but not the total or average group contribution.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know which neighbor cooperated or defected with them in the PD, and whose PGG contribution they observe.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings, so the effect on efficiency cannot be directly determined.
- The 'punishment' mechanism is not costly punishment but rather the withholding of cooperation in a separate game, which may not generalize to settings with explicit costly punishment.
- Outcomes are behavioral (contribution/cooperation) rather than payoff-based, limiting direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
- No analysis of heterogeneity by group size beyond the main treatment/control comparison.
- No communication or chat between participants, which may affect generalizability to real-world settings.
