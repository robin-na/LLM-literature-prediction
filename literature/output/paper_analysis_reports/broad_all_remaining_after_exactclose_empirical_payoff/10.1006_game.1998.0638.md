# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Reciprocity, trust, and payoff privacy in extensive form bargaining
Authors: McCabe, K. A., Rassenti, S. J., & Smith, V. L.
Journal: Games and Economic Behavior
Year: 1998

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- expected profit to each player under different strategies
- frequency of joint maximum (SJM) and subgame perfect (SP) outcomes
- average payoffs by treatment and branch
- conditional expected payoffs (e.g., E(pi_2|Left), E(pi_1|Defect))
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of left/right branch play
- frequency of defection
- frequency of punishment moves
- cooperation rates
Outcome notes:
The paper reports both behavioral outcomes (branch choices, cooperation, punishment) and payoff-based outcomes (expected payoffs, frequency of efficient outcomes). Efficiency is not directly calculated as a ratio, but the frequency of SJM (joint maximum) and average payoffs serve as close proxies.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in two-person extensive form bargaining games, the possibility of punishment (even if not rational in the subgame) increases the frequency of efficient, cooperative outcomes (SJM) compared to games where punishment is not possible. Repeated play with the same partner further increases cooperation and efficiency. When payoff information is private, cooperation and efficiency decline, and noncooperative (SP) outcomes predominate. The paper reports expected payoffs and frequencies of efficient outcomes by treatment, showing that punishment and repeated interaction both have positive effects on group efficiency. However, the effect of punishment is less pronounced than the effect of repeated interaction and complete information. The findings suggest that both punishment opportunities and the ability to build trust/reciprocity are important for achieving high efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect but informative evidence for the prediction task. While not a standard public goods game, the design is adjacent: it is a two-player extensive form game with opportunities for cooperation, defection, and punishment. The results show that enabling punishment increases the frequency of efficient outcomes and average payoffs, especially in single-play settings. Repeated interaction and complete information further enhance efficiency. The findings suggest that, in similar environments, enabling punishment is likely to increase efficiency relative to control, but the effect is moderated by information conditions and the possibility of repeated interaction. The paper supports the prediction that punishment will have a more positive effect on efficiency when players can observe each other's payoffs and when repeated interaction is possible.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are two-player (dyadic) bargaining games.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Single-play and repeated-play (20 rounds) treatments are compared. Repeated play increases cooperation and efficiency.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In Game 1, player 2 can punish at a cost (subgame dominated); in Game 2, punishment is not possible. The presence of a costly punishment option increases efficient outcomes.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is possible in Game 1 (direct, costly, but subgame dominated), not in Game 2. The effectiveness is limited but increases cooperation/efficiency.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `unclear` under this dimension. Subjects are told nothing about how many trials will be played, making the repeated game indefinite/unknown in length.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In complete information treatments, players know both their own and their counterpart's payoffs, enabling reciprocity and punishment. In private information treatments, only own payoffs are known, reducing cooperation.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Players know who made which move in the game tree; punishment is not anonymous.

## Important Limitations
- The game is not a standard public goods game; it is a two-player extensive form bargaining game, so results may not fully generalize to multi-player or standard PGG settings.
- Punishment is only possible in a specific subgame and is subgame dominated, so the strength and generality of punishment effects may be limited.
- Efficiency is not reported as a ratio of actual to maximum possible group payoff, but as frequency of efficient outcomes and average payoffs.
- No chat or communication is allowed, so effects of communication are not tested.
- No reward mechanism is present, so the interaction of punishment and reward is not addressed.
- The number of rounds in repeated games is not known to subjects, which may affect behavior compared to fixed-horizon games.
