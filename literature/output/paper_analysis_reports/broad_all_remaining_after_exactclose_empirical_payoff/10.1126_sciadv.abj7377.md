Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:

- probability of contribution

Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the probability or rate of contribution to the public good. The paper does not report group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare as a primary outcome. Payoff structure is described, but efficiency or group payoff is not directly analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study demonstrates that a central authority's costly action to improve the fairness of punishment (procedural fairness) increases citizens' willingness to contribute to a public good, even when material incentives are held constant. The effect is robust and persists over time. The mechanism is shown to be intrinsic (normative legitimation) rather than reciprocity or conditional cooperation. However, the main outcome is contribution rate, not efficiency or group payoff. The paper provides strong evidence that legitimacy-enhancing actions by authorities can increase cooperation, but does not directly report on efficiency or total group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
For the downstream prediction task, this paper provides strong evidence that features of the punishment regime that enhance perceived legitimacy (such as procedural fairness) can increase contribution rates in public goods games with centralized punishment. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be used directly to predict efficiency outcomes. The findings suggest that, in similar game designs, legitimacy-enhancing punishment regimes may increase efficiency indirectly via increased contributions, but the magnitude of the effect on efficiency is not quantified here. The paper is most useful for understanding mechanisms and moderators of cooperation under punishment, rather than for direct efficiency prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 5 players: 4 citizens and 1 authority.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 periods (rounds) per session.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players; interactions are anonymous and via computer.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: contribute all tokens or none (no intermediate contributions allowed).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per capita return (MPCR) is 0.4 for both citizens and authority.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is centralized: authority deducts 24 tokens from citizens; cost to authority is not specified as a per-punishment cost, but authority's investment in accuracy is costly.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is 24-token deduction per punished citizen; effectiveness depends on accuracy of signal (error rates manipulated).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of periods (20 rounds).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed of the authority's investment, enforcement rule, and realized accuracy level before making their contribution decision. No mention of summary statistics about others' contributions.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is centralized and administered by a known authority in the group; citizens know who the authority is (role B), but not the identities of other citizens.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is contribution rate, not efficiency or group payoff; efficiency is not directly analyzed.
- The experiment uses a stylized, laboratory environment with student samples, which may limit generalizability.
- Punishment is centralized and administered by an authority, not peer-to-peer; findings may not generalize to decentralized punishment settings.
- No reward mechanism is present, so results do not address the interaction of punishment and reward.
- Contribution choices are binary (all-or-nothing), which may differ from continuous contribution settings.
- The cost to the authority for punishing is not specified as a per-punishment cost, but as a cost for investing in accuracy.
- No direct measurement or reporting of group efficiency, welfare, or total group payoff.
