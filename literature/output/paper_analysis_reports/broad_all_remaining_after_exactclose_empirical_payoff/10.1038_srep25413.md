## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a probabilistic peer-punishment mechanism, where punishment is more likely and more severe when the payoff difference between punisher and target is larger, leads to a substantial increase in both cooperation and average payoff in spatial prisoner's dilemma simulations. This effect is robust across regular, random, and scale-free network topologies, and for different average degrees of connectivity. The model demonstrates that, contrary to some previous findings with standard peer-punishment, this form of punishment can increase group welfare (average payoff) rather than decrease it.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in spatial prisoner's dilemma environments, a punishment mechanism that is probabilistic and based on payoff differences can increase average payoff, in contrast to standard fixed-cost peer punishment which often reduces group payoff. While the model is not a public goods game, the findings are relevant for predicting the effect of punishment in adjacent game structures, especially where punishment is adaptive or conditional on payoff differences. The results suggest that the design of the punishment mechanism (e.g., making it proportional to payoff differences and probabilistic) is a key moderator of its effect on efficiency. However, direct quantitative predictions for PGGs should be made with caution due to the difference in game structure.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=1000 (and N=10000 in supplementary), but the effect of player count is not analyzed as a moderator.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 300 generations (rounds), with some supplementary runs up to 3000 generations.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate or defect (binary, all-or-nothing).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost is equal to the loss imposed on the target (symmetric), and is proportional to the punisher's own payoff and the coefficient r=0.15. The effect of varying r is discussed.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment impact per unit cost is 1 (cost to punisher equals cost to target). The mechanism is probabilistic and proportional to payoff difference, which is argued to be key to its positive effect.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game, so direct transfer to PGGs is limited.
- All results are from agent-based simulations, not laboratory or field experiments.
- Punishment is symmetric in cost and effect, and always proportional to the punisher's own payoff; real-world punishment may differ.
- No modeling of communication, reputation, or higher-level social processes.
- Parameter space is limited (e.g., r=0.15 is chosen for maximal cooperation; other values are not systematically explored).
- No analysis of stochasticity or robustness to noise in strategy updating or punishment application.
