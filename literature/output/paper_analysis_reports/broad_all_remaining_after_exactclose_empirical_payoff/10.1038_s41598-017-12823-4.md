## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that the optimal mobility (migration tendency) of punishers depends on the cost of punishment. For high punishment costs, a medium tendency to migrate (rather than always punishing or always running) maximizes the prevalence of punishers. For low punishment costs, always punishing is optimal. The presence of cooperators (second-order free-riders) changes the dynamics: in harsh environments (low enhancement factor), punishers benefit from moderate migration, but in more favorable environments, cooperators can dominate, and the advantage of punishment diminishes. The model shows that mobility can help punishers survive, but only under certain cost and environmental conditions. The paper does not directly report efficiency or group payoff, but higher prevalence of punishers and cooperators is interpreted as beneficial for cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for how punishment, its cost, and the mobility of punishers affect the prevalence of cooperation in spatial public goods games. While it does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, the results suggest that punishment can promote cooperation under certain conditions, especially when the cost is not too high and punishers have a moderate migration tendency. For prediction tasks, the findings imply that the effect of punishment on efficiency is likely to be positive when punishment is not too costly and when the game environment allows for spatial structure and mobility. However, the lack of direct efficiency or payoff data limits the precision of quantitative predictions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The model uses a spatial lattice with each player interacting with their Moore neighborhood (k=8), but the total population size is not fixed and is determined by the lattice size and density.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The model is evolutionary and runs for many Monte Carlo steps until a stationary state is reached; there is no fixed number of rounds as in standard repeated PGGs.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The enhancement factor r is varied and is equivalent to the group multiplier; MPCR = r / group size (group size = 9 including focal player).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost (gamma) is a key parameter, varied in the analysis. High cost reduces the prevalence of punishers; low cost favors punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment intensity (beta) is varied; higher intensity favors punishers if cost is not too high.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare directly; all results are based on the stationary fractions of strategy types.
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- The evolutionary setting with spatial structure and migration may not generalize to standard laboratory PGGs with fixed groups and rounds.
- No direct analysis of the effect of punishment on efficiency or total payoff is provided.
- No reward mechanisms or communication are considered.
