# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Male cleaner wrasses adjust punishment of female partners according to the stakes
Authors: Raihani, N. J., Pinto, A. I., Grutter, A. S., Wismer, S., & Bshary, R.
Journal: Proceedings of the Royal Society B-biological Sciences
Year: 2012

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment intensity (number of aggressive chases per second)
- frequency of cheating (female eating preferred prawn item)
- change in cooperative behavior (female foraging choices after punishment)
Outcome notes:
The study focuses on behavioral outcomes: punishment intensity, cheating frequency, and subsequent cooperative behavior. No direct measurement or analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that male cleaner wrasses adjust the severity of punishment towards female partners based on the stakes of the cheating event (client value) and the relative size of the female. More severe punishment is delivered when cheating is more costly to the male (high-value client) and when the female is similar in size to the male. In turn, females respond to more severe punishment by behaving more cooperatively (cheating less in subsequent opportunities). The results support the idea that punishment can be flexibly adjusted to circumstances and that such flexibility can influence cooperative behavior. However, the study does not report on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that punishment can be flexibly and contextually applied in cooperative dilemmas, and that increased punishment can reduce cheating and increase cooperative behavior. However, because the study does not measure or report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, it cannot directly inform quantitative predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-game-like environments. The findings are most useful for understanding the behavioral mechanisms and moderators (e.g., cost of cheating, partner asymmetry) that may influence the effectiveness of punishment in promoting cooperation, but not for direct efficiency prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All experiments are conducted with pairs (2 players: one male, one female).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Experiment 1: 56 trials per pair over 5 days; Experiment 2: 10 trials per pair, each with two plate presentations (P1 and P2).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is always directly observed and delivered by the male to the female partner; identity is always known.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not measure or report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; all outcomes are behavioral (punishment intensity, cheating frequency, cooperative behavior).
- The experimental design involves only two players (male-female pairs), not group interactions as in standard public goods games.
- Punishment cost to the punisher and punishment effectiveness per unit cost are not quantified or manipulated.
- Findings may not generalize to human or multi-player public goods games due to species and structural differences.
- No reward or positive reinforcement mechanisms are present or tested.
