Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the fraction of cooperators. The paper also analyzes the standard deviation of fitness coefficients as a mechanism, but does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. The outcomes are behavioral, not payoff-based.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a strategy changing penalty (a form of endogenous punishment for switching strategies) in a spatial prisoner's dilemma game increases the fraction of cooperators. Both higher penalty rates and longer memory (the period after which the penalty is lifted if the player does not change strategy again) monotonically promote cooperation, with the effect saturating at high memory. The mechanism works by inducing heterogeneity in fitness coefficients, which helps cooperator clusters survive and expand. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, or welfare, so the findings are about cooperation rates, not efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that endogenous penalty mechanisms can increase cooperation in spatial social dilemmas, but it does not report efficiency or group payoff. The game is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game, and the punishment is not peer-to-peer but is an automatic penalty for changing strategy. Thus, while the findings suggest that certain forms of punishment can promote cooperation, they do not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in public goods games. The main value is as adjacent, mechanistic support for the idea that punishment-like mechanisms can increase cooperation, especially when combined with memory or forgiveness.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use square lattices of size 100x100 to 400x400 (i.e., 10,000 to 160,000 players), but the main results are not about varying player count per se.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 200,000 Monte Carlo steps, with outcomes measured in the last 10,000 steps. The number of rounds is large and fixed for each simulation.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate or defect (binary, all-or-nothing).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The penalty rate (\alpha) determines the severity of the penalty for changing strategy. Higher \alpha increases the effect, promoting cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The game is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- The punishment mechanism is not peer-to-peer but an endogenous penalty for changing strategy, so it differs from standard punishment in PGGs.
- No efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates and related behavioral outcomes are analyzed.
- No explicit cost to a punisher is modeled, so the tradeoff between punishment cost and group benefit is not addressed.
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
