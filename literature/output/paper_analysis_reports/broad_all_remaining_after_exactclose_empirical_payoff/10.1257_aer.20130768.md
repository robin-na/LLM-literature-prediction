## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper proves that in repeated partnership games with private monitoring and evidentiary communication, permanent ostracism (permanent exclusion of deviators) cannot achieve higher efficiency or group payoff than bilateral enforcement, because victims have incentives to conceal deviations rather than report them. This limits the effectiveness of the harshest forms of community punishment. However, if ostracism is temporary and includes forgiveness (eventual readmission of deviators), then higher levels of cooperation and group payoff can be sustained, as the prospect of future cooperation with forgiven players incentivizes truthful reporting. The paper provides formal bounds and equilibrium constructions for these results.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that the design of punishment—specifically, whether it is permanent or temporary (with forgiveness)—is a critical moderator of the effect of punishment on efficiency in repeated partnership games with private monitoring. If punishment is permanent ostracism, efficiency does not improve over bilateral enforcement. If punishment is temporary (forgiveness is possible), efficiency can be strictly higher, especially as patience or group size increases. For prediction tasks, the presence of forgiveness/temporary ostracism should be coded as a key moderator, and the effect of punishment on efficiency should be expected to be positive only when forgiveness is possible. The results are derived in a variable-stakes partnership game, which is adjacent to but not exactly a standard public goods game.

## Important Limitations
- The results are theoretical and derived for a variable-stakes repeated partnership game, which is adjacent to but not exactly a standard public goods game.
- The model assumes private monitoring and evidentiary communication, which may not capture all real-world or experimental settings.
- No empirical or experimental data are provided; all results are theoretical.
- The analysis focuses on punishment via ostracism (exclusion), not on direct costly punishment or reward mechanisms.
- The construction of efficient temporary ostracism equilibria becomes challenging for groups larger than three players.
- The results depend on infinite or indefinite time horizons and may not generalize to finitely repeated games.
