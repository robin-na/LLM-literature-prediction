# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Trust, voluntary cooperation, and socio-economic background: survey and experimental evidence
Authors: Gächter, S., Herrmann, B., & Thöni, C.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2004

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution level in one-shot public goods game
- correlation of contribution with trust attitudes
- correlation of contribution with socio-economic background
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the amount contributed to the public good in a one-shot game. No group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported. The study focuses on behavioral contributions and their correlates, not on payoff-based efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that, in a one-shot public goods game (n=3, MPCR=0.5), both students and non-students contribute substantial amounts, with non-students contributing more on average. However, after controlling for socio-economic background, these differences disappear. Trust attitudes—specifically, beliefs that others are fair, helpful, and that strangers can be trusted—are significantly positively correlated with higher contributions. The widely used GSS trust question is not significantly correlated with contribution. Socio-economic background affects trust attitudes but not contribution behavior. No punishment or sanctioning is present in the analyzed experiment, and no efficiency or group payoff outcomes are reported.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff in public goods games, as the analyzed experiment does not include a punishment condition and does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. It does, however, provide informative context about baseline contribution behavior in a standard one-shot public goods game (n=3, MPCR=0.5) with a diverse subject pool. For prediction tasks involving the effect of punishment on efficiency, this paper can inform the expected baseline (control) contribution rates in the absence of punishment, but not the treatment effect of punishment. The paper also notes (in passing) that punishment can reduce the trust problem in repeated games, but this is only discussed, not tested.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 3 participants per public goods game.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. One-shot game; each participant makes a single contribution decision.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: participants can contribute any amount from 0 to 20 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per capita return (MPCR) is 0.5.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game is one-shot (single round).

## Important Limitations
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present in the analyzed experiment; thus, the paper provides no direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff.
- No efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes are reported; only individual contribution behavior is analyzed.
- The experiment is one-shot; findings may not generalize to repeated or multi-round public goods games.
- The paper only analyzes the baseline (control) condition; results from the punishment condition are reported in a companion paper (Gächter and Herrmann, 2003), not here.
- The explained variance in contribution behavior by trust attitudes is low (R^2 < 10%).
