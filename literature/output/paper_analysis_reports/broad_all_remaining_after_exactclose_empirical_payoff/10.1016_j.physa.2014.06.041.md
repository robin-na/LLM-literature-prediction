## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a standard public goods game, both strong altruistic punishment and reward are unable to sustain cooperation when implemented in the traditional (non-tax-based) way, due to their high cost. When a tax-based mechanism is introduced—where all players pay a tax that is redistributed to punishers or rewarders—these strategies become viable, leading to stable coexistence of cooperators and punishers/rewarders and the elimination of defectors. This results in higher group efficiency and welfare. Tax-based strong altruistic reward leads to even higher cooperation and efficiency than tax-based strong altruistic punishment under equivalent tax conditions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that the introduction of a tax-based institutional mechanism for funding punishment or reward can transform the efficiency impact of punishment in public goods games from negative or neutral to strongly positive. The results suggest that, for a given control efficiency (without punishment), enabling tax-based punishment or reward will substantially increase efficiency, with reward being even more effective than punishment. The findings are most directly applicable to settings with strong institutional support and compulsory tax collection, and less so to voluntary or peer-based punishment. The model is deterministic and assumes infinite, well-mixed populations, so predictions should be adjusted for finite or noisy environments.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and based on infinite, well-mixed populations; results may not generalize to finite or structured populations.
- No empirical or experimental data are provided; all results are from mathematical analysis and numerical simulation.
- The model assumes compulsory tax collection and institutional redistribution, which may not reflect real-world implementation constraints.
- No consideration of noise, errors, or bounded rationality in player behavior.
- No analysis of dynamic or repeated finite games; only evolutionary steady states are considered.
