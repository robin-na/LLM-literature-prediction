# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Third-party punishment and social norms
Authors: Fehr, E., & Fischbacher, U.
Journal: Evolution and Human Behavior
Year: 2004

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- dictator's expected payoff (earnings minus punishment costs)
- defector's expected payoff (earnings minus punishment costs)
- average earnings
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency and severity of punishment assigned
- proportion of punishers
- contribution/transfer levels
- expectations of punishment
- expectations of transfer
- emotional self-reports
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are punishment behavior (frequency, severity) and transfer/contribution levels. Payoff-related outcomes are reported as expected payoffs for dictators/defectors under different punishment regimes, but group efficiency (as a share of maximum possible) is not directly calculated. Efficiency can be inferred indirectly from payoff tables and punishment effects.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiments show that third-party punishment is common and increases with the severity of norm violations in both dictator games (distribution norm) and prisoner's dilemma games (cooperation norm). However, the magnitude of third-party punishment is lower than that of second-party punishment. In dictator games, third-party punishment reduces but does not eliminate the profitability of unfair transfers, while second-party punishment makes unfair transfers unprofitable. In prisoner's dilemma games, third-party punishment reduces the gains from defection but does not make cooperation strictly dominant, whereas second-party punishment does. Thus, punishment mechanisms—especially when strong or when multiple punishers are present—can increase efficiency by reducing the incentive to violate norms, but the effect is stronger for second-party punishment. The paper does not directly report group efficiency as a percentage of the maximum, but provides enough data to infer that punishment increases efficiency-related outcomes by reducing the profitability of norm violations.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the presence of punishment (especially second-party, but also third-party) increases the cost of norm violations and can shift incentives toward more efficient, norm-compliant outcomes. For prediction tasks, the findings suggest that enabling punishment (with sufficient strength or multiple punishers) will increase efficiency or group payoff relative to control conditions without punishment, but the effect size depends on the punishment's strength and whether it is second- or third-party. The design dimensions most relevant for prediction are the punishment cost/impact ratio, the number of punishers, and whether punishment is second- or third-party. The results are most directly applicable to dictator and prisoner's dilemma games, which are adjacent to but not exact public goods games. The paper does not provide direct evidence for standard PGGs, but the mechanisms and patterns are likely to generalize to PGG-like environments with similar punishment structures.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Dictator game: 3 players (A, B, C); Prisoner's dilemma: 3 players (A, B, C) in third-party punishment, 2 players in second-party punishment. The number of punishers (one vs. more) is discussed as a moderator.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are one-shot (single round) with no repetition.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed; subjects interacted anonymously.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Dictator game: transfer is continuous in increments of 10 (0,10,20,30,40,50); PD: all-or-nothing (keep all or transfer all 10 points).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. PD: transfer is tripled (MPCR = 1.5 for each of two players, i.e., 3/2); Dictator game: not a public goods structure, so MPCR not defined.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 1 point to reduce target's payoff by 3 points (cost: 1 per unit assigned).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: 1 point spent reduces target's payoff by 3 points.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game is one-shot; all information about the structure is provided in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishers observe the actions (transfers, cooperation/defection) of the other players before deciding on punishment.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; players are not informed of the identity of punishers.

## Important Limitations
- The games studied are dictator and prisoner's dilemma games, not standard public goods games; results are adjacent but not directly transferable to PGGs.
- All experiments are one-shot; repeated interaction and learning effects are not studied.
- Only one third-party punisher is present per group; the effect of multiple third-party punishers is discussed but not tested.
- Efficiency is not directly reported as a percentage of maximum possible group payoff; only individual expected payoffs are analyzed.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanisms are included.
- All subjects are university students from Zurich; generalizability to other populations is untested.
- No communication or reputation effects are present; real-world social contexts may differ.
