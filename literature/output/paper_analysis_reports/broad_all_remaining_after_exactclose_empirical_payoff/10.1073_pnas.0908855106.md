# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Neural responses to sanction threats in two-party economic exchange
Authors: Li, J., Xiao, E., Houser, D., & Montague, P. R.
Journal: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America
Year: 2009

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- trustee repayment amount
- trustee repayment ratio (back-transfer/tripled investment)
- investor sanction decision
- neural activation (VMPFC, LOFC, amygdala, PCC, DLPFC, parietal cortex)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the trustee's repayment (amount and ratio), which is a behavioral measure of cooperation/reciprocity. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare is reported. Neural correlates of decision making are also a primary outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a two-player repeated trust game, the threat of monetary sanctions (punishment) leads trustees to return significantly less to investors compared to when no sanction is threatened. This effect is robust across fair and unfair investor requests. Neural data show that the presence of a sanction threat reduces activation in brain regions associated with social reward (VMPFC, LOFC, amygdala) and increases activation in regions associated with rational, utility-based decision making (parietal cortex). The findings support the 'perception shift' hypothesis: sanction threats crowd out norm-based motivations and shift behavior toward self-interested, utility-maximizing choices. The study does not report group efficiency or total earnings, but the reduction in reciprocity implies a likely reduction in efficiency relative to the no-sanction condition.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a two-player repeated trust game, the introduction of a punishment (sanction) threat reduces cooperative behavior (reciprocity) by trustees. While the design is not a standard public goods game, the mechanism—punishment crowding out intrinsic motivation—may generalize to PGG-like settings. For prediction tasks, this suggests that enabling punishment can reduce efficiency or group payoff in environments where cooperation is norm-driven and where punishment is perceived as a signal of distrust or as crowding out intrinsic motivation. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or total earnings, the effect on efficiency must be inferred from the reduction in reciprocity. The findings are most relevant for two-player, repeated, sequential games with optional punishment, and less directly for standard multi-player PGGs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is played in pairs (2 players: investor and trustee).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each pair plays 10 rounds with fixed roles.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Players are mutually anonymous; no mention of chat or communication allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (investment and repayment) amounts are continuous choices, not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Investment from investor is tripled before reaching trustee, so effective MPCR for the investor's transfer is 3 (for the trustee).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Sanction is a fixed loss: a 4-MU deduction from trustee's earnings if the trustee does not meet the investor's request. The cost to the punisher (investor) is not specified, but the punishment is imposed at the investor's discretion.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is a fixed 4-MU deduction from trustee's earnings if the trustee does not meet the investor's request. No gradation or variable effectiveness.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know there are 10 rounds; roles and pairs are fixed for all rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each round, each player's earnings are revealed to both players.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (sanction) is imposed by the investor on the trustee, and both know the source of the sanction within the pair.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a two-player trust game, not a standard multi-player public goods game, so generalizability to PGGs is limited.
- No direct analysis or reporting of group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare; only behavioral (reciprocity) and neural outcomes are reported.
- Punishment is a fixed, binary sanction (4-MU deduction), not a variable or continuous punishment mechanism.
- The cost to the punisher (investor) for imposing the sanction is not specified.
- The neural findings are specific to the trust game context and may not generalize to other economic games or group settings.
