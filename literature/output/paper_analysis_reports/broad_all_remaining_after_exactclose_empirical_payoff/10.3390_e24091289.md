Journal: Entropy
Year: 2022

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- collaboration rate (proportion of group successfully resisting risk)
- strategy composition (proportion of C, CP, PP, D types)
- resource wastage (difference between contributed resources and threshold needed to resist risk)
- cost-benefit ratio of punishment types
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the collaboration rate (proportion of the group that successfully resists risk), which is not a direct measure of efficiency or group payoff. Resource wastage and cost-benefit ratios are discussed, but not in terms of total group payoff relative to a fully cooperative benchmark.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model shows that both central and peer punishment can promote group collaboration in risk-resistant (threshold) scenarios, with peer punishment generally having a stronger effect, especially as risk increases. Central punishment can suppress the effect of peer punishment at all risk levels, while peer punishment only constrains central punishment at high risk. Resource wastage from punishment is generally small compared to the loss from group failure, but increases with peer punishment intensity and risk. The paper does not report efficiency or group payoff in the standard sense, focusing instead on collaboration rates and resource wastage.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task. It supports the idea that punishment (both central and peer) increases group collaboration in threshold public goods-like environments, and that the effectiveness and interaction of punishment types depend on risk level. However, because the main outcome is collaboration rate (not efficiency or group payoff), and because the model is theoretical/simulation-based rather than empirical, its direct predictive value for efficiency is limited. The resource wastage analysis suggests that high peer punishment can lead to diminishing returns or inefficiency at high risk. The model's all-or-nothing threshold structure and explicit manipulation of punishment cost and effectiveness are informative for mapping to game design dimensions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Total group size N=100, divided into 10 subgroups of 10 for each round.
- number of rounds: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Game is played in multiple rounds, but the exact number of rounds is not specified; focus is on evolutionary stable state.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Group success is threshold-based: if total contributions >= U, all succeed; otherwise, all lose everything.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Central punishment: cost to supporter w1=2; total fund required w=4. Peer punishment: cost per act w2=1. These are varied in simulations.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Central punishment: each D loses t1 (varied 2–9.5). Peer punishment: each D loses t2 (varied 1–6.5).

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- The main outcome is collaboration rate (successful risk resistance), not efficiency or group payoff as a fraction of the maximum possible.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency, welfare, or total group payoff.
- The model uses an all-or-nothing (threshold) payoff structure, which differs from standard linear public goods games.
- No consideration of communication, reward mechanisms, or information feedback.
- Findings may not generalize to empirical settings without further validation.
