## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing tolerance-based expulsion in a spatial public goods game can substantially increase the level of cooperation (measured as the fraction of cooperators and expellers). The effect is strongest at intermediate tolerance thresholds and when expulsion costs are high. The mechanism allows expellers to balance the cost of expulsion with the benefit of removing defectors, leading to higher and more stable cooperation levels. However, the paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes, so the direct effect on efficiency cannot be determined from the reported results.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that tolerance-based expulsion mechanisms can increase cooperation rates in spatial public goods games, especially when the cost of expulsion is high and the tolerance threshold is optimally set. However, because the reported outcomes are cooperation levels rather than efficiency or group payoff, the findings should be used with caution for predicting efficiency effects. The results suggest that enabling punishment (in the form of expulsion) is likely to increase cooperation, but the impact on efficiency is not directly quantified and may depend on the costliness of expulsion and the structure of the population. The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are presented.
- The main outcome is cooperation level (fraction of cooperators and expellers), not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- The model assumes spatial structure and population density effects that may not generalize to non-spatial or well-mixed settings.
- Expulsion is modeled as a specific form of punishment (removal from group), which may not map directly to standard punishment mechanisms in lab PGGs.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes, limiting direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
