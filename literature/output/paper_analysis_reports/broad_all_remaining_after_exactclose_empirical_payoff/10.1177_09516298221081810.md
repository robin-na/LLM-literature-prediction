## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper argues that the credibility of threats and offers (punishments and rewards) is central to understanding social power and governance in non-cooperative games. It shows that resources, reputation, and coordination problems determine the ability to credibly punish or reward, and thus to exercise power. The analysis highlights the importance of commitment mechanisms and tipping points in reputation for sustaining credible threats. However, the paper does not provide empirical findings or quantitative predictions about efficiency or payoff outcomes in public goods or similar games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides conceptual and theoretical insights into how punishment and reward mechanisms may affect cooperation and social power in collective action settings, emphasizing the importance of credibility, resources, and coordination. However, it does not provide empirical or quantitative evidence that can be directly used to predict efficiency outcomes in public goods games with or without punishment. Its value for prediction is limited to high-level mechanism discussion and may inform the interpretation of empirical results from other sources.
