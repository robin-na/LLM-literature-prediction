# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Culture and group-functional punishment behaviour
Authors: Espín, A. M., Brañas-Garza, P., Gamella, J. F., Herrmann, B., & Martín, J.
Journal: Evolutionary Human Sciences
Year: 2022

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment assigned (amount spent to reduce others' payoffs)
- contribution to public good
- antisocial punishment (punishing cooperators)
- altruistic punishment (punishing free-riders)
- punishment by ethnicity, gender, and group composition
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are punishment behavior (amount spent to punish others) and contribution to the public good. The paper does not report group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare. Mean earnings are reported as a descriptive statistic, but not analyzed as a dependent variable.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that Gitanos punish much less than non-Gitanos in homogeneous groups, but increase punishment (especially Gitano males) in mixed groups with non-Gitanos, targeting Gitano free-riders. Non-Gitanos punish more overall, and in mixed groups, non-Gitano males punish Gitano free-riders more harshly than non-Gitano free-riders. Antisocial punishment (punishing cooperators) is generally low, but somewhat higher when outgroup members are present. Gender moderates punishment: Gitano females almost never punish, while males increase punishment in mixed groups. Contribution rates are high and do not differ significantly by ethnicity or group composition. The paper does not analyze efficiency or group payoff as a main outcome.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides detailed evidence on how punishment behavior varies by culture, gender, and group composition in a one-shot PGG with punishment. However, it does not report or analyze efficiency, group payoff, or welfare as outcomes, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. The design and behavioral results may be useful for understanding moderators of punishment behavior, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency changes due to punishment. If using this paper for prediction, one should note that the effect of punishment on efficiency is not measured, and that the main findings concern who punishes whom, not whether punishment increases group payoff.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group has 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. One-shot (single round) public goods game.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players choose how much of €10 to contribute.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per capita return (MPCR) is 0.5 (each €1 contributed is doubled and split among 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. €1 spent on punishment reduces the target's earnings by €3 (cost-to-impact ratio 1:3).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3:1 (each €1 spent reduces target by €3).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game is one-shot (single round).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are shown the contributions and earnings of each of the other three group members (by color/ethnicity).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous at the individual level, but in mixed groups, players can match punishment to the target's ethnicity (via scarf color), not to individual identity.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report or analyze efficiency, group payoff, or welfare as dependent variables.
- Only one-shot (single round) games are studied; no repeated interaction.
- The main outcomes are punishment and contribution behavior, not payoff-based outcomes.
- Cultural and gender effects are specific to the Spanish Romani (Gitano) and local non-Gitano populations; generalizability may be limited.
- Recruitment methods differed between Gitano and non-Gitano participants, which may affect representativeness.
