The paper focuses on the evolution of cooperation (frequency of cooperators) and spatial patterns, not on group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. Payoff is discussed in the model, but not as a reported outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing a neutral reward mechanism, where the majority strategy in a group is rewarded by the minority, promotes the evolution of cooperation in a spatial public goods game. As the reward factor increases, cooperation becomes more prevalent, and for sufficiently high reward, the system can reach full cooperation. The main outcomes are the frequency of cooperators and the spatial distribution of strategies. The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that reward mechanisms (even when not perfectly targeted) can promote cooperation in spatial public goods games. However, because the outcomes are limited to cooperation rates and do not include efficiency or group payoff, the findings are only adjacent to the prediction task. The game design dimensions are described in the model, but the absence of payoff-based outcomes limits the paper's direct utility for predicting efficiency effects of punishment or reward. The results may suggest that reward mechanisms can increase efficiency if cooperation translates to higher group payoff, but this is not demonstrated in the paper.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 5 players (square lattice, each player participates in 5 groups).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 50,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with results averaged over 20 realizations.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperate (contribute 1) or defect (contribute 0).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Enhancement factor r is varied; MPCR = r/5 (group size 5).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. A neutral reward mechanism is introduced and varied (parameter alpha).
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Cost to rewarder is determined by alpha and group composition; formulas provided in model.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward impact per unit cost is determined by the model's formulas; alpha is the key parameter.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus as outcomes; only cooperation rates and spatial patterns are analyzed.
- The model is simulation-based and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The reward mechanism is 'neutral' and not targeted specifically at cooperators or defectors, which differs from standard reward or punishment treatments.
- No punishment mechanism is present, so the findings are only adjacent to the effects of punishment.
- The spatial structure (lattice) and update rules may limit generalizability to other PGG settings.
