Journal: Physica A-statistical Mechanics and Its Applications

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperators (cooperation rate)
- spatial distribution of strategies
- cluster size and formation
- dynamics of cooperation over time
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the fraction of cooperators (\rho_c), i.e., the cooperation rate. No group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings are reported. All results are based on simulation of strategy dynamics, not on payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that allowing the strength of social ties (link weights) to adapt based on relative payoffs, and allowing agents to use memory of past strategies, can significantly increase the fraction of cooperators in a spatial prisoner's dilemma simulation. There are optimal values for the link weight adjustment intensity and memory length that maximize cooperation. However, the study does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or any direct payoff-based outcomes, and the model is not a public goods game but a pairwise prisoner's dilemma.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. Its results are based on a spatial prisoner's dilemma with dynamic link weights and memory, and the main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency or group payoff. The link weight adjustment mechanism is adjacent to punishment/reward but does not map directly to standard punishment in PGGs. Thus, while the findings may suggest that adaptive network structure and memory can promote cooperation, they do not provide quantitative or directly transferable evidence for the efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run on L x L lattices, with L typically 100 or 200 (i.e., 10,000 or 40,000 players). The player count is fixed for each simulation but varied for robustness checks.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 100,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with stationary state outcomes measured after long runs.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose to cooperate or defect (binary choice) in each round.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of Monte Carlo steps is fixed and known in the simulation setup.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanism with associated costs and impacts; link weight adjustment is only adjacent to these concepts.
- No efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes are reported; only cooperation rate is analyzed.
- All results are from simulation, not empirical or experimental data.
- The model assumes binary cooperation/defection and does not allow for continuous contributions.
