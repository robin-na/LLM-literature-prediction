The main dependent variable is average group payoff, which is directly analogous to efficiency. The model also tracks the emergence of division of labour and the distribution of roles, but these are not the primary outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that decentralized social sanctioning, implemented as a matrix of role-based rules for reward and punishment, enables groups of self-interested agents to learn and maintain efficient divisions of labour. This leads to substantially higher average group payoff compared to groups without sanctioning, or with only altruistic or selfish learning. The effect is robust across different environmental conditions and is close to the theoretical upper bound. Complexity regularization can promote the evolution of simpler, convergent social norms without sacrificing group payoff. The model shows that sanctioning transforms the incentive structure, making it individually rational to take on critical but otherwise unrewarding roles, thus solving the social dilemma.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and computational evidence that enabling decentralized social sanctioning (punishment and/or reward) in public-goods-like environments can substantially increase group efficiency, especially when critical roles are otherwise unrewarding. The effect is robust across a range of environmental parameters and is close to the optimal achievable by global optimization. The model is not a direct PGG but is structurally adjacent, with payoff interdependence and social dilemmas. The findings support the prediction that, all else equal, enabling punishment (and/or reward) will increase efficiency relative to a control with sanctioning disabled, especially in settings where division of labour is required. The model also highlights the importance of norm complexity and the potential for simple sanctioning rules to be highly effective.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Group size (number of individuals) is a parameter in the model, but the main results do not systematically vary or analyze its effect on efficiency. The spatial grid is described, but the effect of player count is not a focus.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is iterated for T steps (interpreted as rounds), but the effect of number of rounds is not systematically analyzed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Role selection is all-or-nothing: each individual selects one discrete role per round from a finite set.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sanctioning is zero-sum: the punisher's loss is the punished's gain (or vice versa), i.e., resources are redistributed but not destroyed. The cost is the amount transferred, and positive sanctions (rewards) are also possible.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment/reward impact per unit cost is 1:1 (zero-sum transfer). The sanctioning matrix determines the amount per interaction.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Both positive (reward) and negative (punishment) sanctions are possible, as determined by the sanctioning matrix.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward cost is the amount transferred; same as punishment (zero-sum).
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward impact per unit cost is 1:1 (zero-sum transfer).

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and computational, not empirical or experimental; results may not generalize to real human groups without further validation.
- The games are not standard public goods games but are adjacent, with role-based payoff structures and spatial interactions.
- Sanctioning is zero-sum (redistributive) rather than destructive; this differs from many lab PGG punishment paradigms where punishment is costly and efficiency-reducing.
- Agents cannot opt out of sanctioning; sanctioning is involuntary and determined by the group norm.
- No communication, reputation, or identification of punishers/rewarders is modeled.
- The effect of group size, number of rounds, and other design parameters is not systematically analyzed.
- No explicit analysis of marginal per-capita return (MPCR) or continuous contribution choices.
