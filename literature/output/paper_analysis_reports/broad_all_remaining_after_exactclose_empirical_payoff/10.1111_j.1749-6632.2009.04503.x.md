Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the psychological mechanisms (anger, unfairness) underlying altruistic punishment, and reports on punishment behavior in response to defection or low contribution. It does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The main finding is that anger, rather than the perception of unfairness, is the primary driver of altruistic punishment in social dilemma games. Experimental evidence from a one-shot, three-person public goods game with punishment shows that lower contributions from others evoke more anger and lead to harsher punishment, and that the effect of others' contributions on punishment is fully mediated by experienced anger. Priming participants with anger increases their tendency to punish defectors. The paper does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, so it does not directly inform the effect of punishment on efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff in public goods games. However, it does suggest that the presence and intensity of punishment are strongly influenced by emotional responses (especially anger) to defection or low contribution. For prediction tasks, this implies that punishment is likely to be used when players experience anger at norm violations, but the paper does not address whether this increases or decreases group efficiency. The findings are most relevant for understanding when and why punishment is deployed, not its payoff consequences.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. One study used a one-shot, three-person public goods game; other studies used sequential trust games (player count not always specified).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The public goods game was played 10 times (one-shot, repeated for 10 rounds); the trust game was played 6 times in the anger priming study.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes, so it cannot inform the effect of punishment on efficiency.
- Game parameters such as punishment cost, punishment effectiveness, and marginal per-capita return are not specified.
- The focus is on psychological mechanisms (anger, unfairness) rather than economic or payoff consequences.
- Sample sizes, experimental details, and statistical results are not fully reported in the summary.
- Findings may not generalize to field settings or to games with different structures or incentives.
