# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The perils of peer punishment: Evidence from a common pool resource framed field experiment
Authors: de Melo, G., & Piaggio, M.
Journal: Ecological Economics
Year: 2015

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=field_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- extraction level (number of nets used)
- punishment assigned (punishment points, flags)
- reaction to punishment (change in extraction after being punished)
- antisocial punishment frequency
- conformity to group average extraction
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is extraction level (resource use), which is inversely related to cooperation. The paper does not directly report group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare, but extraction levels can be mapped to efficiency in principle. Payoff tables are provided, but efficiency is not explicitly analyzed as an outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Nonmonetary peer punishment (social disapproval) reduces resource extraction (increases cooperation) in mixed-community groups but has no significant effect in in-group (same community) groups. The effect is measured as a reduction in the average number of nets used per person. There is substantial antisocial punishment (punishing cooperators), which can undermine the effectiveness of punishment. The study does not directly report group efficiency or total payoff, but lower extraction implies higher efficiency. The presence of antisocial punishment and lack of a unique social norm limit the positive effect of punishment. The findings suggest that the context (group composition) moderates the effect of punishment on cooperation, and that coordination is needed for punishment to be effective.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that enabling peer punishment (social disapproval) can reduce resource extraction (increase cooperation) in common pool resource games, especially in mixed-group settings. However, the effect on efficiency is not directly measured; it must be inferred from extraction levels. The presence of antisocial punishment and lack of norm consensus can limit or even reverse the positive effect of punishment. For prediction tasks, the paper suggests that punishment is more likely to improve efficiency in mixed-group contexts than in homogeneous groups, but the risk of antisocial punishment and unclear norms can reduce or negate efficiency gains. The specific game design (field context, nonmonetary punishment, no communication, continuous extraction choice) should be considered when generalizing.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players per round; subgroups reshuffled each period.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 periods per session, divided into four blocks of 5 periods each.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication allowed during the game; participants sat back-to-back and were instructed not to interact.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/extraction choice is continuous: 1 to 8 nets per round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Payoff function: π_i = 18*a_i + 12*sum_j(8 - a_j). For 4 players, the marginal per-capita return can be calculated from the payoff table (see Table A.2).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point costs the punisher 1 point; no monetary cost to the punished.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is nonmonetary for the punished (social disapproval via flags), but costly for the punisher. Punishment points are translated into colored flags (yellow, orange, red) based on total points received.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants knew the total number of periods (20) and the structure of the game.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, the total number of nets used by the subgroup was announced; individual choices were private.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment was anonymous in terms of who assigned it, but the punished individual had to display a colored flag in the next period, visible to others.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is extraction level, not group efficiency or total payoff; efficiency must be inferred indirectly.
- No direct reporting of group efficiency, welfare, or total earnings.
- Substantial antisocial punishment observed, which can undermine the positive effect of punishment.
- The effect of punishment is context-dependent (stronger in mixed-group than in-group settings).
- Lack of a unique social norm and coordination limits the effectiveness of punishment.
- The punishment is nonmonetary for the punished (social disapproval), which may not generalize to monetary punishment settings.
- Small sample size and field context may limit generalizability.
