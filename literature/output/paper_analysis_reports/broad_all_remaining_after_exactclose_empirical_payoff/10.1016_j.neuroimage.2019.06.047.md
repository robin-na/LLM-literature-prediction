# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The role of ventromedial prefrontal cortex and temporo-parietal junction in third-party punishment behavior
Authors: Lo Gerfo, E., Gallucci, A., Morese, R., Vergallito, A., Ottone, S., Ponzano, F., Locatelli, G., Bosco, F., & Lauro, L. J. R.
Journal: Neuroimage
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- decision to punish (binary)
- amount of punishment assigned
- altruistic punishment (punishing unfairness)
- antisocial punishment (punishing fairness)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the decision to punish and the amount of punishment assigned. No group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported. The study focuses on behavioral responses to norm violations and fairness, not on aggregate payoffs or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that modulating neural activity in the VMPFC increases the tendency to punish unfair behavior (altruistic punishment), while modulating the TPJ increases the tendency to punish fair behavior (antisocial punishment) in a third-party punishment game. The behavioral outcomes are measured as the decision to punish and the amount of punishment, not as group efficiency or total payoff. The study does not report on the effect of punishment on group welfare, efficiency, or aggregate earnings.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff in public-goods-game-like environments. Its findings are limited to individual punishment behavior in a third-party context, with no analysis of how punishment affects group outcomes or efficiency. The game is a modified Dictator Game, not a standard PGG or repeated PGG, and the outcomes are not payoff-based. Thus, the paper offers little direct support for the downstream prediction task, except as context for the psychological and neural mechanisms underlying punishment behavior.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each trial involves three roles: Player A (dictator), Player B (receiver), Player C (third-party punisher, always the subject). The game is not a group public goods game but a three-player modified Dictator Game.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each participant played 160 trials of the TPP game.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment choices are discrete: Player C can subtract 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, or 10 tokens from Player A. Contribution by Player A is continuous (0-20 tokens), but punishment is not all-or-nothing.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher (Player C) pays 1 token for every 2 tokens subtracted from Player A (i.e., cost:impact ratio is 1:2).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 2 tokens removed from A per 1 token cost to C.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the number of trials (160) in the session.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Player C observes Player A's transfer to Player B in each trial. No mention of aggregate summaries, but per-trial information is provided.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes; all outcomes are individual-level punishment decisions.
- The game is a third-party punishment variant of the Dictator Game, not a public goods game or repeated PGG.
- No analysis of how punishment affects group cooperation, efficiency, or total earnings.
- The neural manipulation (tDCS) is not a game design dimension relevant to the downstream prediction task.
- Sample size is moderate (N=60), and all participants are Italian students, limiting generalizability.
- No communication or group interaction; only individual responses to observed allocations.
