## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that incorporating human-inspired mechanisms such as punishment and withholding action into agent-based models of social dilemmas (including the PGG) enables agents to converge to cooperative strategies, even when some agents are initially non-cooperative. The presence of mechanisms like rewiring (withholding action) and punishment increases the proportion of agents adopting cooperative strategies and accelerates convergence. The results are robust to population size and the proportion of fixed-strategy (always-cooperative) agents. However, the paper does not directly report on efficiency or group payoff, so the findings are most directly about behavioral convergence and cooperation rather than efficiency per se.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the idea that enabling punishment and related human-inspired mechanisms in PGG-like environments can increase cooperation and likely improve efficiency, especially in decentralized, large-scale, or heterogeneous agent populations. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, it cannot be used to make quantitative predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings are most useful for understanding the mechanisms by which punishment and network adaptation can promote cooperation, and for anticipating that such mechanisms are likely to have positive effects on efficiency in similar environments, but the evidence is indirect.

- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run for a fixed number of games (e.g., 6,000n games, where n is the number of agents). Number of rounds is a parameter in the simulations.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are continuous, not all-or-nothing. The models explicitly allow for a continuum of actions.

- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as agents giving up a small amount c to reduce another agent's reward by a larger amount e. Example values: c unspecified, e=3.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is specified as e per unit cost c, with e > c. Example: e=3, c unspecified.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Agents are assumed to know the rewards of others in order to compute utility and decide on punishment, but the exact information structure is not detailed.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not directly report efficiency, group payoff, or total welfare outcomes; all main results are about behavioral convergence and cooperation.
- All results are from agent-based simulations, not human experiments or field data.
- The models assume agents can observe or infer others' rewards, but the information structure is not fully specified.
- Punishment and withholding action are implemented in a stylized computational way, which may not capture all aspects of human behavior.
- No quantitative estimates of the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff are provided.
