Outcome notes:
The review focuses on how people perceive and predict cooperativeness using various cues (behavioral, personal, situational). It summarizes empirical findings on the accuracy of these perceptions and the predictive value of different cues. Payoff-related outcomes such as efficiency or group earnings are not a focus; the main outcomes are behavioral (cooperation, trust, partner choice, expectations).

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The review finds that people are generally accurate in using behavioral and situational cues (such as past cooperation, communication, and game structure) to predict others' cooperativeness, but often misperceive the predictive value of personal cues (such as gender or attractiveness). Punishment is discussed as a behavioral cue, but its role is primarily in signaling trustworthiness rather than as a mechanism for increasing efficiency or group payoff. The review highlights that communication and increased mutual benefit (e.g., higher returns to cooperation) are situational factors that increase both actual and expected cooperation. However, the review does not provide quantitative or systematic evidence on how these factors affect efficiency or payoff in public goods games with or without punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, contextual evidence about which cues (behavioral, personal, situational) are most informative for predicting cooperation in social dilemmas. It suggests that situational factors like communication and the structure of payoffs (e.g., mutual benefit) are important for cooperation, which may be relevant for predicting the effects of punishment in public goods games. However, the review does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or payoff outcomes, nor does it systematically analyze the impact of specific game design dimensions (such as punishment cost, MPCR, or player count) on efficiency. As such, its value for predicting treatment efficiency from game design dimensions is limited and mainly contextual.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- chat: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Communication (chat) is discussed as a situational cue that enhances cooperation, mainly by allowing recognition of cooperative types.
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Mutual benefit of cooperation (analogous to MPCR) is discussed as a situational cue; higher mutual benefit leads to higher cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- This is a narrative review and does not provide new empirical data or meta-analytic effect sizes.
- The review focuses on perceptions and predictors of cooperativeness, not on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes.
- Punishment is discussed only as a behavioral cue for signaling trustworthiness, not as a treatment or mechanism for increasing efficiency.
- Game design dimensions such as player count, number of rounds, punishment cost, and reward mechanisms are not systematically analyzed.
- Findings are synthesized from diverse studies with varying designs, making it difficult to extract quantitative predictions for specific game settings.
