Journal: Plos One
Year: 2011

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- expected payoff (theoretical analysis)
- time spent in norm establishment/collapse zones (proxy for group welfare)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- average boldness (defection rate)
- average vengefulness (punishment/metapunishment propensity)
- frequency of norm establishment/collapse
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are the long-run frequencies with which the population is in a 'norm establishment' (high cooperation, high punishment) or 'norm collapse' (high defection, low punishment) zone, as measured by average boldness and vengefulness. Expected payoff is analyzed mathematically, but simulation results focus on behavioral states rather than explicit group efficiency or total payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the ability of punishment and metapunishment to sustain cooperation in a public-goods-like environment is highly sensitive to the underlying network structure. Specifically, higher clustering and interconnectedness (more triplets per agent) make it more likely that the population will spend most of its time in a cooperative, norm-enforcing state, which is associated with higher expected payoffs. In contrast, higher average degree (more neighbors per agent) can make norm collapse (defection, low punishment) more likely, reducing group welfare. The results are derived from both mathematical analysis of expected payoffs and extensive agent-based simulations, but the main simulation outcomes are behavioral (frequency of norm establishment/collapse) rather than explicit efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect but informative evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-like games, especially when considering the role of network structure. It suggests that punishment (and metapunishment) can lead to higher efficiency (more time in cooperative states) when the network has high clustering and interconnectedness, but may be less effective or even counterproductive in networks with high average degree and low clustering. However, because the paper does not report explicit efficiency or group payoff values, its support for quantitative prediction is limited to qualitative or relative effects based on network features. The findings are most relevant when the game design includes networked interactions, costly punishment, and possibly metapunishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use 50 agents (players) per network.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each generation consists of 4 rounds; simulations run for 50,000 generations.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: agents choose to cooperate or defect.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not stated as MPCR, but the payoff structure is: defector gets T=3, each other agent gets H=-1 per defector. This is a negative externality model, not a standard linear PGG, but is functionally similar.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays E=-2 to impose P=-9 on defector. Metapunisher pays ME=-2 to impose MP=-9 on non-punisher.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 4.5 (P=-9 per E=-2). Metapunishment is the same.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. The number of rounds per generation (4) is fixed and known in the simulation setup.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents observe the actions (defection, punishment) of their neighbors with some probability, conditional on network links.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment and metapunishment are targeted and agents know who punished whom, as required for metanorms to function.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report explicit efficiency, group payoff, or welfare values for the control (no punishment) or treatment (punishment enabled) conditions.
- Simulation outcomes are reported as time spent in norm establishment/collapse zones, which are proxies for cooperation and group welfare, not direct measures of efficiency.
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental with human subjects.
- The game structure is a metanorms game with punishment and metapunishment, which is similar but not identical to standard public goods games.
- Findings are most applicable to networked, evolutionary settings and may not generalize to one-shot or small-group lab experiments.
