## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that introducing penalty mechanisms for deviation and time in bargaining games with myopic agents ensures convergence to efficient (Pareto optimal or Nash) outcomes, but at a cost: the process is slower and less efficient than with fully rational, forward-looking agents. The efficiency loss is quantifiable as the 'price of unsophistication,' and depends on the penalty parameters and the agents' behavior. The results are robust across a wide class of utility functions and game structures, including Markov processes. The paper does not study public goods games or peer punishment, but the penalty mechanism is analogous to centralized sanctions rather than decentralized peer punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is not directly informative for predicting the effect of peer punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it studies bargaining games with centralized penalty mechanisms and myopic agents. However, it provides indirect evidence that introducing penalty costs can improve convergence to efficient outcomes even with unsophisticated agents, though always with some efficiency loss relative to the ideal. The results suggest that the design and magnitude of penalties (punishment costs) are critical for achieving near-efficient outcomes, but the context is bargaining, not public goods provision. The findings may be useful for understanding the general role of sanctions in promoting efficiency, but should be transferred to PGG-like settings with caution.

## Important Limitations
- The paper studies bargaining games, not public goods games; results may not transfer directly to PGG-like environments.
- Punishment is implemented as a centralized penalty mechanism, not as peer punishment or decentralized sanctions.
- All results are theoretical or based on numerical simulation; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- Efficiency losses are quantified, but the model does not address contribution rates, cooperation, or norm compliance.
- The effect of punishment parameters is context-dependent and may not generalize to other game structures.
