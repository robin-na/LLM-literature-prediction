# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Crime in the lab-detecting social interaction
Authors: Falk, A., & Fischbacher, U.
Journal: European Economic Review
Year: 2002

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- amount stolen (conditional on others' stealing)
- frequency of selfish, reciprocal, altruistic, and irregular stealing types
- relation between own stealing and others' stealing
- unconditional stealing decisions
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: how much subjects steal, and how this depends on the stealing of others. There is no direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare. The experiment manipulates the efficiency consequences of stealing (via the parameter gamma), but does not report group-level efficiency or total payoffs as outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates strong social interaction effects: subjects' willingness to steal increases with the observed or expected stealing of others. About half of the subjects are 'reciprocal' (conditional) types, while the rest are mostly selfish (always steal the maximum). The fraction of selfish types is higher when stealing is a pure transfer (no efficiency loss) than when it is inefficient. The study does not include a punishment or sanctioning phase, nor does it report efficiency or group payoff as an outcome. The findings are relevant for understanding norm compliance and conditional cooperation, but do not provide direct evidence on the effects of punishment or on efficiency outcomes in public goods or similar games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. It does, however, provide informative context about the importance of social interaction and conditional norm violation: when individuals see more norm violation (stealing), they are more likely to violate norms themselves. The manipulation of the efficiency consequences of stealing (gamma) shows that more selfish behavior occurs when norm violation is less costly to the group, which may be relevant for understanding the impact of efficiency parameters in related games. However, because there is no punishment mechanism and no direct efficiency or payoff analysis, this paper should be considered only as indirect, contextual evidence for the prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 subjects were used in all sessions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The experiment was played only once (one-shot game).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players was allowed; decisions were made anonymously via computer.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Stealing decisions were continuous: subjects could choose to steal any integer amount from 0 to 20.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The efficiency of stealing is manipulated via the parameter gamma: in the H-treatment, stealing is a pure transfer (gamma=1), in the L-treatment, stealing is inefficient (gamma=0.5).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects knew the experiment was played only once (one-shot).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects made conditional stealing decisions based on the (rounded) average stealing of others; this information was provided in the conditional decision table.

## Important Limitations
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present; the study does not address the effects of punishment on efficiency or cooperation.
- No direct analysis or reporting of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; only behavioral outcomes (stealing) are analyzed.
- The game is not a public goods game or direct variant; it is a 'crime' (stealing) game with different incentive structure.
- Findings are limited to one-shot interactions; repeated or dynamic effects are not studied.
- Results may not generalize to environments with explicit punishment, reward, or sanctioning mechanisms.
