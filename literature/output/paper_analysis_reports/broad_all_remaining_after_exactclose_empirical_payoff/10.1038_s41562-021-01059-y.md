## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model demonstrates that punishment of dominance (hawkishness) by withholding cooperation (defection in the PD game) can be evolutionarily stable and lead to egalitarian outcomes when the future benefits of cooperation outweigh the short-term gains from dominance. The efficiency of the group increases as leveller strategies proliferate, reducing the frequency of costly contests and increasing resource sharing. Simulations show that an ecological shift increasing the gains to cooperation leads to a transition from dominance/defection equilibria to egalitarian/cooperative equilibria with higher payoffs. Empirical analysis of forager societies finds that greater reliance on cooperative hunting predicts a higher probability of egalitarianism, consistent with the model's predictions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and simulation-based evidence that enabling punishment (here, the ability to withhold cooperation from dominant individuals) increases group efficiency and resource equality, especially when the marginal benefit of cooperation is high and the probability of future interaction is high. The model is adjacent to public goods games but not an exact match; however, the logic and payoff structure are highly relevant. For prediction tasks, the results suggest that in repeated, high-mpcr, high-future-interaction environments, enabling punishment (even in the form of exclusion or shunning) will increase efficiency relative to control. The effect is stronger when the benefits of cooperation are high relative to the benefits of dominance or defection.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not an empirical experiment with direct measurement of efficiency in a public goods game.
- The game structure is adjacent to, but not identical with, standard public goods games; it combines hawk-dove and prisoner's dilemma logic.
- Punishment is implemented as withholding cooperation (exclusion/shunning), not as costly punishment in the standard sense.
- Model assumes dyadic repeated interaction; group size effects are not directly modeled.
- No explicit communication, partner choice, or coalition formation mechanisms are included.
- Empirical test uses cross-cultural data with proxies for cooperation and egalitarianism, not direct measurement of efficiency or payoffs.
