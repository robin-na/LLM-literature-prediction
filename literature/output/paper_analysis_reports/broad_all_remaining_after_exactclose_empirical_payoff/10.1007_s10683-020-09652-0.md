# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Information exchange in laboratory markets: competition, transfer costs, and the emergence of reputation
Authors: Hoffmann, R., Kittel, B., & Larsen, M.
Journal: Experimental Economics
Year: 2021

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- payoff per round (trustor and trustee)
- return on investment (ROI)
- welfare
Non-payoff outcomes:
- information sharing frequency
- amount sent (trust)
- reciprocity in information sharing
- beliefs about information sharing and trustworthiness
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are payoff per round, ROI, and welfare, with extensive analysis of information sharing, trust, and reciprocity as mechanisms.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that voluntary, costless information sharing among trustors in a repeated investment game leads to higher trustworthiness (trustees return more), higher trust (trustors send more), and higher payoffs for both trustors and trustees. When direct costs are imposed on information sharing, or when competition among trustors is introduced (making information sharing privately costly), information sharing drops sharply, and the efficiency gains from reputation mechanisms disappear. The effect of direct costs is highly non-linear: even small costs cause a large reduction in information sharing and efficiency. Competition among trustors increases their risk-taking (sending more), but reduces information sharing and trustworthiness, so the net effect on efficiency is ambiguous or negative. The study provides direct evidence that barriers to information sharing (costs or competition) undermine the emergence of reputation and reduce market efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling costless, voluntary information sharing (a reputation mechanism) in repeated trust/investment games increases efficiency (group payoffs) relative to a control with no information sharing. However, even small costs to information sharing, or the introduction of competition among information holders, sharply reduce information sharing and eliminate efficiency gains. For prediction tasks, the presence or absence of a costless, voluntary reputation/information sharing mechanism is a strong moderator of the efficiency effect. The findings are most directly relevant to trust/investment games, which are adjacent to PGGs, and to endogenous, voluntary, non-institutionalized punishment/sanctioning via information sharing. The results suggest that in environments where information sharing is costly or where competition discourages sharing, the efficiency benefits of reputation mechanisms will be minimal or absent.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 3 trustors and 6 trustees (9 players per group).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. At least 24 rounds; after round 24, each subsequent round has a 0.5 probability of ending.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No free-form chat; information sharing is structured and limited to sharing transaction histories. No other communication allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (investment) is continuous: trustors can send any amount from 0 to 10 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Investment is tripled (multiplier = 3), but only the trustee receives the tripled amount. Not a standard PGG MPCR, but the marginal return to the group is 3x for the trustee, 0 for the trustor unless the trustee returns tokens.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know there are at least 24 rounds; after that, each round has a 0.5 probability of ending. The end is uncertain after round 24.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are shown their own and cumulative payoffs each round. In information sharing treatments, trustors can see shared transaction histories from other trustors.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. When information is shared, the receiving trustor is told the ID of the sending trustor and the trustee involved. Trustees are not told whether information was shared about them.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a repeated investment (trust) game, not a standard public goods game; results may not fully generalize to PGGs.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanism; only indirect sanctioning via information sharing.
- Information sharing is structured and limited to transaction histories, not free-form communication.
- Competition is induced via a tournament mechanism, which may not capture all aspects of real-world market competition.
- Players cannot freely choose partners; matching is random among fixed groups.
- The design abstracts from pricing mechanisms and allows only for truthful information transfer.
- The end of the game is stochastic after round 24, which may affect endgame behavior.
