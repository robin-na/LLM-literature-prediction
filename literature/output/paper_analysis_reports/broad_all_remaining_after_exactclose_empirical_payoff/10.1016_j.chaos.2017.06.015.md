The main reported outcome is the fraction of cooperators (cooperation rate) in evolutionary simulations. No explicit analysis of group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or surplus is provided.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a third-party agent who probabilistically rewards cooperators and punishes defectors increases the fraction of cooperators in both the prisoner's dilemma and snowdrift games across different network structures. The effect is stronger as the reward/punishment magnitude increases and as the probability of encountering the third-party agent increases. The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not group payoff or efficiency. The results are robust across square lattice, Erdös-Rényi, and scale-free networks.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the idea that probabilistic punishment (and/or reward) can increase cooperation rates in social dilemma games, but it does not report on efficiency or group payoff. The model is not a public goods game, but a two-player prisoner's dilemma or snowdrift game, so direct prediction of efficiency in PGGs is not possible. The findings may be useful for understanding the general effect of third-party sanctions on cooperation, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency outcomes in PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each simulation uses N=10,000 agents on a network; each game is two-player (pairwise) but the population is large.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 600,000 Monte Carlo steps, with outcomes averaged over the last 10,000 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each agent chooses to cooperate or defect (binary choice) in each round.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is parameterized by gamma (0 to 0.5), which is the amount subtracted from defectors' payoffs. Higher gamma increases cooperation.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward is present; cooperators receive gamma with probability 0.5 when encountering the third-party agent.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward effectiveness is parameterized by gamma (0 to 0.5), which is the amount added to cooperators' payoffs. Higher gamma increases cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is not a public goods game but a two-player prisoner's dilemma or snowdrift game, so direct transfer to PGG efficiency is limited.
- The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare.
- Punishment and reward are administered by a third-party agent at no cost to players, which differs from standard PGG punishment mechanisms.
- No explicit analysis of efficiency or group payoff is provided.
- Simulation-based results may not generalize to experimental or real-world settings.
