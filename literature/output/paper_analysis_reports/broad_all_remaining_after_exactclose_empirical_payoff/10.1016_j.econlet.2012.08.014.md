# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: An experimental investigation of a third-person enforcement in a prisoner's dilemma game
Authors: Kusakawa, T., Ogawa, K., & Shichijo, T.
Journal: Economics Letters
Year: 2012

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate in first stage (between X1 and X2)
- cooperation rate in second stage (between M and X1/X2)
- logistic regression of cooperation likelihood
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are cooperation rates in various roles and stages. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is reported. Some descriptive statistics on earnings are provided, but not analyzed as outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that when a third party (M) can observe the actions of two players in a one-shot PD, the cooperation rate between those two players is significantly higher than when M cannot observe. This is attributed to the threat of future punishment by M in subsequent repeated PD games. The effect is measured in terms of cooperation rates, not efficiency or total payoff. The study provides evidence that third-party observation and the possibility of future sanctioning can facilitate cooperation, but does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or welfare outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that introducing a third-party observer with future sanctioning power can increase cooperation rates in a prisoner's dilemma setting. However, because the study does not use a public goods game or directly measure efficiency or group payoff, its value for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like environments is limited. The main implication is that third-party monitoring and the threat of future punishment can be a strong facilitator of cooperation, which may translate to higher efficiency in related settings, but this is not directly tested. The design is adjacent to PGGs and does not manipulate standard punishment mechanisms (e.g., costly punishment within the group), so caution is warranted in transferring the findings.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 3 players: X1, X2, and M.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Multiple rounds per session; each round consists of 1 or more stages, with continuation probability 3/4 per stage. Table 2 reports total rounds and average number of stages per session.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Prisoner's dilemma with binary choices: Cooperate or Defect.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the continuation probability (3/4) but not the exact number of stages per round; rounds are randomly terminated.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. In Observable, M observes X1 and X2's actions and payoffs; in Non-observable, M does not. No mention of summary information shown to X1 or X2 about others.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game or direct PGG variant.
- No direct measure or analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare is reported.
- Punishment is not implemented as a costly, explicit mechanism but as the threat of future defection by a third party.
- Findings on cooperation rates may not directly translate to efficiency or payoff outcomes in PGG-like environments.
- No manipulation or analysis of standard punishment cost or technology dimensions.
