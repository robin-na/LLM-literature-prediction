## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a population of agents playing a two-action game (cooperate or defect), increasing the penalty for defection increases the mean probability to cooperate. There is a critical penalty threshold, dependent on initial conditions, above which the population becomes fully cooperative. For populations with higher initial cooperation, less penalty is needed to achieve full cooperation, and in some cases, no penalty is required. The model also shows that after a population is trained to cooperate with penalty, the penalty can be relaxed without immediate loss of cooperation. The structure of the population (well-mixed vs. 2D lattice) affects the level of cooperation at intermediate penalties, with lattices supporting higher cooperation. However, the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare as outcomes, so the findings are not directly about payoff-based efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that increasing the severity of punishment can increase cooperation rates in repeated social dilemma games, with a critical threshold for full cooperation. However, because the main outcome is the mean probability to cooperate and not group payoff or efficiency, the results cannot be directly used to predict efficiency outcomes in public goods games. The model is adjacent to PGGs and may inform qualitative expectations about the effect of punishment on cooperation, but not on efficiency. The findings suggest that punishment can be effective in increasing cooperation, especially when initial cooperation is low, and that population structure can moderate this effect. For the prediction task, this paper is best used as indirect, qualitative support for the idea that punishment can increase cooperation, but not as quantitative evidence for efficiency effects.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=2500 agents; model is general for arbitrary N.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Model is iterated until steady state; number of rounds is not fixed but the system is evolved over many time steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each agent chooses between two discrete actions: cooperate (C) or defect (D).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is modeled as a penalty parameter d imposed on defectors; cost to the punisher is not explicit, as punishment is implemented by an external authority funded by taxes.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is directly manipulated via the penalty parameter d; higher d increases cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is not a standard public goods game; it is a two-action (C/D) game with pairwise interactions and external punishment.
- The main outcome is the mean probability to cooperate, not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare.
- No direct analysis or reporting of efficiency or total group payoff is provided.
- Punishment is implemented by an external authority funded by taxes, not by peer punishment.
- No experimental or empirical data; results are theoretical and simulation-based.
- No analysis of marginal per-capita return or other PGG-specific parameters.
