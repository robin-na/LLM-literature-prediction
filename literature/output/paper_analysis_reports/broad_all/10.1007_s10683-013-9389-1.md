The study focuses on how recognition (identification) affects contribution behavior, not on monetary payoffs or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that displaying the identities of all contributors or only the lowest contributors (bottom two) significantly increases individual contributions to the public good, compared to a baseline with no recognition. Recognizing only the highest contributors (top two) does not significantly increase contributions. The effect is attributed to shame aversion rather than prestige seeking. The study does not report efficiency or group payoff, but the increased contributions in the ALL and BOTTOM treatments suggest that such recognition mechanisms could improve group outcomes in public goods settings.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that non-monetary, social recognition mechanisms—especially those that expose low contributors—can substantially increase contribution rates in public goods games. However, because the study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The recognition mechanism here is a form of social sanction (shame), not costly punishment. The findings are most relevant for predicting the effect of non-monetary, reputational sanctions on cooperation, and less relevant for monetary punishment interventions. For the downstream prediction task, this paper suggests that social identification of low contributors (shame) is a powerful lever for increasing cooperation, but does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or on the effect of monetary punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 5 players, fixed across all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 periods (rounds) per session.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between players; identification is via photo and name only.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players choose any amount from 0 to 80 francs.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (mpcr) is 0.4 (each franc contributed is multiplied by 0.4 and distributed to each of 5 players).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game lasts 20 periods.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, all players see the total group contribution and the individual contributions of all group members, sorted by amount.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In the ALL, TOP, and BOTTOM treatments, the names and photos of contributors are displayed according to the treatment (all, top two, or bottom two). This is a form of social identification, not monetary punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; only contribution rates are analyzed.
- No monetary punishment or reward mechanisms are present; only social recognition (identification) is manipulated.
- The findings are specific to non-monetary, reputational sanctions and may not generalize to monetary punishment treatments.
- The experiment is conducted in a lab with fixed groups of 5 undergraduate students, which may limit external validity.
- The effect of recognition on long-term participation or on settings with free entry/exit is not studied.
