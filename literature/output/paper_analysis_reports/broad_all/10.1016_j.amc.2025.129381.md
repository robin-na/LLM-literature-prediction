## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that incorporating both direct and indirect experience in interaction and strategy update rules, as well as introducing tolerance in link weight adjustment, significantly promotes cooperation in spatial Prisoner's Dilemma simulations. High sensitivity to defection (low tolerance) and greater weight on indirect experience both increase the frequency of cooperation and the average payoff of cooperators relative to defectors. However, the model does not include explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanisms, and does not report group efficiency or total welfare outcomes. The findings are based on simulation results and are not directly transferable to public goods games with punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that mechanisms allowing individuals to avoid defectors (via adaptive link weights and social learning) can increase cooperation and the average payoff of cooperators in spatial Prisoner's Dilemma settings. However, it does not study public goods games, does not implement explicit punishment or sanctioning, and does not report group efficiency or total welfare. Thus, its relevance for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games is limited and should be considered only as weak, mechanistic context.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a 2D lattice of size L x L, with L=200 in most cases (i.e., 40,000 players). Some robustness checks vary L.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with outcomes averaged over the last 3,000 MCS.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose to cooperate or defect (binary choice) in each round.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game or variant.
- No explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism is implemented; link weight adjustment and tolerance are not equivalent to costly punishment.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or total welfare are not directly analyzed; only average payoffs for cooperators and defectors are reported.
- Findings are based on simulation, not empirical or experimental data.
- Network structure is limited to regular lattices; real social networks may differ.
