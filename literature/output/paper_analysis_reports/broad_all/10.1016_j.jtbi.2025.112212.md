Authors: Kroumi, D.

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- fixation probability of cooperation (\rho_C)
- fixation probability of defection (\rho_D)

- thresholds for cooperation to dominate (in terms of group-level extinction bias \alpha and game payoffs)

Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are fixation probabilities (i.e., the probability that cooperation or defection takes over the population), which are direct measures of evolutionary success and thus group-level efficiency or welfare. The model does not report contribution rates or punishment frequencies, as it is a theoretical analysis of evolutionary dynamics.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that structuring a population into demes and introducing selective extinction—whereby groups with more defectors are more likely to go extinct—can dramatically increase the evolutionary success (fixation probability) of cooperation in the Prisoner's Dilemma. Without selective extinction (\alpha=0), defection is always favored, and cooperation cannot persist. As the selective extinction parameter \alpha increases, the fixation probability of cooperation increases, and that of defection decreases. There are explicit threshold values of \alpha (functions of game payoffs and demographic parameters) above which cooperation is favored by selection, and above which it is more likely to fix than defection. The model also shows how the baseline extinction rate (v) and the structure of payoffs (T-R, P-S) modulate these thresholds. The results are robust to several alternative life-cycle assumptions. The main outcome is that group-level demographic feedback (selective extinction) can overcome the individual-level advantage of defection and promote group-level efficiency (cooperation).

## Prediction Guidance
For the downstream prediction task, this paper provides strong theoretical evidence that, in structured populations with group-level demographic feedback (selective extinction of defect-dominated groups), the efficiency of the system (measured by the fixation probability of cooperation and thus long-run group payoff) can be substantially increased by increasing the strength of selective extinction (\alpha). In the absence of such feedback, efficiency remains low (defection dominates). The model gives explicit formulas for the critical value of \alpha needed to shift the system from defection-dominated to cooperation-dominated, as a function of game payoffs and demographic parameters (deme size, number of demes, baseline extinction rate). While the model is not a direct public goods game and does not include explicit punishment or reward mechanisms, the selective extinction process is functionally analogous to a group-level sanction. Thus, the findings support the prediction that introducing or strengthening group-level sanctions (even if not individual punishment) can increase efficiency in public-goods-like environments, especially when the control (no-sanction) efficiency is low.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model explicitly includes d demes each with N players; both N and d are parameters. The main results are derived in the limit of large d and large N, but finite values are also discussed.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is generational and infinite-horizon (evolutionary process), not a fixed finite number of rounds as in lab experiments. Each generation is a 'round' in the evolutionary sense.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses the Prisoner's Dilemma payoff matrix (R, S, T, P), with T>R>P>S. The marginal per-capita return is not explicitly defined as in standard PGG, but the payoff structure is fully specified.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The game is a pairwise Prisoner's Dilemma, not a standard public goods game; results may not transfer exactly to PGG settings.
- Selective extinction is a group-level demographic process, not an individual-level punishment or sanction; thus, the analogy to punishment is indirect.
- No explicit modeling of individual-level punishment, reward, communication, or information sharing.
- The model assumes infinite or very large populations and demes for analytical tractability; finite-size effects are not fully explored.
- Outcomes are long-run evolutionary fixation probabilities, not short-run group efficiency or average payoffs per round as in lab experiments.
