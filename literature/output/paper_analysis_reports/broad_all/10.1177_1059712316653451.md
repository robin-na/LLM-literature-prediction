Payoff is used as fitness to determine evolutionary success, but explicit group efficiency or welfare is not directly reported. The analysis is at the level of strategy success and risk-dominance, not aggregate group payoff or efficiency ratios.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that a strategy combining prior commitment and costly punishment (CPP) can achieve higher frequencies of cooperation than either mechanism alone, across a wide range of parameter values. The improvement is most significant when the cost of arranging commitments is high and the punishment impact is above a threshold. The model shows that commitment reduces the effect-to-cost ratio required for punishment to be effective, and punishment helps deal with free-riders who exploit commitment. The results are robust across different cost and punishment parameters, but are derived for the two-player, one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma, not for public goods games or group interactions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that combining commitment and punishment mechanisms can outperform either alone in promoting cooperation, especially when commitment is costly and punishment is sufficiently effective. However, the model is based on the two-player Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game or group setting. The outcomes are reported as frequencies of strategies and risk-dominance, not as group efficiency or total payoff. Thus, while the findings suggest that punishment (especially when combined with commitment) can improve cooperation and, by implication, group outcomes, the results cannot be directly mapped to efficiency predictions in public goods games. The paper is most useful for understanding the interaction of punishment and commitment mechanisms and their parameter dependencies, but less directly informative for quantitative efficiency prediction in PGG-like environments.

- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. The Prisoner's Dilemma is a binary (cooperate/defect) game, i.e., all-or-nothing contribution.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. - punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (delta_2) is varied; higher effectiveness increases the frequency of punishment and combined strategies, promoting cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is for the two-player, one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma, not for public goods games or group interactions.
- Outcomes are reported as frequencies of strategies and risk-dominance, not as group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are theoretical and simulation-based.
- No analysis of repeated games, communication, or group size effects.
- No direct mapping to marginal per-capita return or efficiency as defined in public goods games.
