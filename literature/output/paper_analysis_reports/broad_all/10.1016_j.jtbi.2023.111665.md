## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in structured populations, prosocial peer exclusion can uphold cooperation even in the presence of antisocial peer exclusion, except when exclusion costs are low. Antisocial peer exclusion alone suppresses cooperation, but when both types coexist, prosocial exclusion remains effective at sustaining cooperation through complex coexistence and cyclic dominance patterns. The results are reported in terms of cooperation rates and strategy frequencies, not group efficiency or total payoff. The findings are robust across different network sizes and parameter sweeps, but are based on theoretical simulations rather than empirical data.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that prosocial peer exclusion can maintain high levels of cooperation in spatial public goods games, even when antisocial exclusion is possible, except at low exclusion costs. However, because the outcomes are reported as cooperation rates and not as efficiency or group payoff, the direct quantitative effect of exclusion on efficiency cannot be extracted. The results suggest that, in structured populations, enabling prosocial exclusion (a form of targeted sanctioning) is likely to increase cooperation rates, and that the negative impact of antisocial exclusion does not fully undermine this effect. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper provides indirect support: higher cooperation rates likely correspond to higher efficiency, but the mapping is not explicit. The findings are most relevant for settings with spatial structure, peer-based exclusion, and variable exclusion costs.

- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 50,000 Monte Carlo steps, with results averaged over the last 5,000 steps to reach stationary state.

Both monitoring cost (c_m) and exclusion cost (c_e) are considered, with c_m = c_e in main simulations.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment (exclusion) effectiveness is parameterized by exclusion probability (beta), which is varied systematically. Higher beta increases exclusion effectiveness.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run for a fixed, known number of Monte Carlo steps.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players observe the strategies of their neighbors in their group before deciding on exclusion; thus, local summary information is available.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Exclusion is peer-based and targeted; excluders know the strategies of others in their group and act accordingly.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are used.
- Outcomes are reported as cooperation rates and strategy frequencies, not as group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- The model assumes a regular lattice (spatial structure) and may not generalize to other network topologies or well-mixed populations.
- The exclusion mechanism is modeled as peer-based and parameterized by cost and probability, which may not capture all real-world complexities.
- The mapping from cooperation rate to efficiency or group payoff is not explicitly provided, limiting direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
- The model does not include communication, reward mechanisms, or repeated/long-term memory effects.
