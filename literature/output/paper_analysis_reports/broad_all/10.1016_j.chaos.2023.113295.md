Payoff equations are specified, but aggregate payoff or efficiency is not analyzed as an outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a limited punishment pool in a spatial public goods game strongly suppresses defectors and promotes the dominance of cooperators, more so than a limited reward pool. Increasing the capacity or cost of the punishment pool further reduces defection and can lead to full cooperation under certain parameter ranges. In contrast, the reward pool is less effective, and its impact on defectors depends on the cost and capacity settings. The model does not report group efficiency or total payoff, but the results imply that punishment is a more robust mechanism for sustaining cooperation in resource-limited institutional settings.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in spatial public goods games with limited institutional resources, punishment mechanisms are more effective than reward mechanisms at promoting cooperation and suppressing defection. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, its direct value for predicting efficiency outcomes is limited. The findings suggest that, for similar game designs, enabling punishment is likely to increase cooperation rates and reduce defection, which may translate to higher efficiency, but this is not directly quantified. The model details (e.g., spatial structure, limited pool, agent types) should be considered when transferring these insights to other settings.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are arranged on a L x L lattice (L=30 in main simulations), so player count is 900. The model is spatial, with each player interacting with 8 neighbors.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for many Monte Carlo steps (e.g., 1000 or 10000), with averages taken over the last 1000 steps. The game is iterated, but not with a fixed, known number of rounds as in lab experiments.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r=2.5, group size is 9 (focal player plus 8 neighbors), so MPCR = r / group size ≈ 0.278.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost per punisher is γ, varied in simulations (e.g., γ=0.7, γ=2.0). Higher punishment cost increases suppression of defectors and promotes cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment intensity parameter α controls the fine imposed per unit cost. α=2.5 in main simulations. Higher α increases punishment effectiveness.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward mechanism (positive incentive) is modeled as an alternative to punishment.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Reward cost per rewarder is β, varied in simulations (e.g., β=0.7, β=2.0). Higher reward cost can increase both cooperators and defectors, with complex effects.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward intensity parameter α controls the reward per unit cost. α=2.5 in main simulations. Higher α increases reward effectiveness.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare as an outcome; only strategy frequencies and extinction dynamics are analyzed.
- Findings are based on agent-based simulations in a spatial evolutionary model, which may not generalize to non-spatial or human experimental settings.
- The model includes 'exiters' (players who leave the game and receive a fixed payoff), which is not standard in most PGG lab experiments.
- Parameter ranges (e.g., punishment/reward cost, pool capacity) are specific to the model and may not map directly to real-world or experimental values.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are theoretical/simulation-based.
