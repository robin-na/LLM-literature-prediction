Journal: Games and Economic Behavior

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of cooperators
- frequency of punishers/rewarders
- evolutionary stability of cooperation
- existence and stability of equilibria (strategy composition)
Outcome notes:
The paper studies the evolutionary dynamics of cooperation, defection, and (tax-based) pure punishment/reward strategies in the public goods game using replicator equations. The main outcomes are the frequencies of strategies at equilibrium and whether cooperation is sustained. Average payoff is defined in the replicator equations, but the main results and figures focus on the frequency of cooperators and the stability of cooperation, not on group efficiency or total payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a standard public goods game, pure punishment or pure reward alone (without tax) cannot sustain cooperation in a well-mixed population. Introducing a tax-based mechanism (where all players pay a tax to fund punishment or reward) enables the stable coexistence of cooperators and tax-based pure punishers or rewarders, thus sustaining cooperation. Tax-based pure reward leads to a higher frequency of cooperators than tax-based pure punishment. The analysis is based on replicator dynamics and focuses on the evolutionary stability of strategy frequencies, not on direct efficiency or group payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that institutional (tax-based) punishment and reward mechanisms can stabilize cooperation in public goods games, whereas individual-level (pure) punishment or reward cannot. However, the outcomes are reported in terms of the frequency of cooperators, not efficiency or group payoff. For the downstream prediction task, this paper suggests that enabling institutional punishment (via tax) is likely to increase cooperation rates, and that institutional reward may be even more effective. However, it does not provide direct quantitative predictions about efficiency or group payoff, so its value for predicting treatment efficiency is indirect and limited to the expectation that efficiency should increase when tax-based punishment or reward is enabled.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Group size N is a parameter in the model, with main results shown for N=5. The model is general for N>1.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is r/N, where r is the synergy factor and N is group size. Main results use r=3, N=5, so MPCR=0.6.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishers pay G_P to the punishment pool; cost per punisher is a model parameter (e.g., G_P=0.7 in main results).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is parameterized by fine per punisher (beta), e.g., beta=0.7 in main results.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward mechanism is modeled as an alternative to punishment; both are analyzed.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Rewarders pay G_R to the reward pool; cost per rewarder is a model parameter (e.g., G_R=0.7 in main results).
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is parameterized by mu (reward per rewarder), e.g., mu=0.7 in main results.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not report empirical or experimental data.
- Outcomes are reported in terms of evolutionary stability and frequencies of strategies, not direct measures of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- The model assumes an infinitely large, well-mixed population and continuous-time replicator dynamics, which may not generalize to finite or structured populations.
- No direct quantitative predictions about efficiency or group payoff are provided; all efficiency-related inferences are indirect.
- No behavioral or psychological mechanisms are modeled beyond strategy updating via payoffs.
