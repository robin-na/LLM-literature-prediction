## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that a mechanism combining behavioral punishment (severing ties with defectors) and reputation-based partner selection leads to high levels of cooperation in a dynamic networked prisoner's dilemma. The frequency of partner switching (time scale ratio W) is a key moderator: higher W leads to higher cooperation, and there is a critical W above which cooperation dominates. The effect is robust to temptation to defect and network degree, though higher values of these parameters require higher W for cooperation to prevail. The model does not report efficiency or group payoff, but the results suggest that cooperation can be sustained or reach fixation under the right conditions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that punishment-like mechanisms (here, behavioral punishment via partner switching) can promote cooperation in dynamic networked social dilemmas. However, the model is a prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game, and does not report efficiency or group payoff. The findings suggest that in environments where individuals can sever ties with defectors and form new ties based on reputation, cooperation is likely to be higher, especially when partner switching is frequent. For the prediction task, this paper supports the idea that punishment and partner selection mechanisms can increase cooperation, but does not provide direct evidence about efficiency or payoff outcomes in PGG-like settings.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size N=1000 (main), N=400 (some figures). Fixed in each simulation.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 50,000 iterations; steady state measured over last 1,000 generations.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Binary choice: cooperate (C) or defect (D) in each round.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented as breaking links with persistent defectors; this is the main intervention. The effect is strongly positive for cooperation, but not measured for efficiency.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players have access to neighbors' past actions (to determine defection) and reputation scores (for partner selection).

## Important Limitations
- The model is a prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- No efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates and network properties are analyzed.
- Punishment is implemented as behavioral punishment (severing ties), not as a costly action with explicit cost and impact.
- Simulation-based results may not generalize to empirical or experimental settings.
- No analysis of payoff-based outcomes, so implications for efficiency are indirect.
