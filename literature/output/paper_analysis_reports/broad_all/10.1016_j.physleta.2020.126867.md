## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that introducing both punishment and reward mechanisms, based on subjective well-being thresholds, greatly increases the proportion of travelers choosing the cooperative (low-carbon) option. Punishment is found to be more effective than reward in stabilizing high levels of cooperation. The ease of triggering incentives (by narrowing the threshold interval) further boosts cooperation. However, the study does not report on group payoff, efficiency, or welfare; all results are in terms of cooperation rates.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the prediction task by showing that punishment (and to a lesser extent, reward) mechanisms can substantially increase cooperation rates in a PGG-like setting. However, because the model and results focus on cooperation rates rather than efficiency or group payoff, the findings should be used with caution for predicting efficiency outcomes. The model structure and parameterization (e.g., cost and effectiveness of punishment/reward) are informative for understanding how design dimensions might affect cooperation, but not directly efficiency.
