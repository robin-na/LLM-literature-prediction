## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a closed community harvesting a renewable resource, cooperation can be sustained through social norm contagion, but is vulnerable to collapse if the temptation to defect becomes too strong (e.g., due to increased population or technology). The system can flip from a high-efficiency (cooperative) equilibrium to a low-efficiency (defective) equilibrium, with substantial welfare loss. Hysteresis is present: reversing the parameter change that caused collapse is not sufficient to restore cooperation. The model provides explicit formulas for welfare under cooperation and Nash equilibrium, showing large efficiency losses after collapse. The presence of social norm diffusion (but not explicit punishment) can sustain cooperation up to a tipping point.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is highly informative for understanding the efficiency dynamics of common-pool resource games with social norm diffusion, but it does not model explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanisms. Its results are most relevant for predicting efficiency in environments where cooperation is maintained by social pressure, persuasion, or contagion, rather than by costly punishment. The model shows that efficiency can be high and robust up to a tipping point, after which collapse is sudden and efficiency is lost. For the downstream prediction task, this paper provides strong evidence about the role of population size, technology, and social norm strength in determining efficiency, but does not provide direct evidence about the effect of enabling or disabling punishment. It is best used as a theoretical baseline for environments without explicit punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Player count (N) is a key parameter; increasing N increases the risk of collapse and reduces efficiency by making cooperation harder to sustain.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is determined by the resource growth and harvesting technology parameters (P, q, r, v, N). Higher resource productivity (higher P, q, r) supports higher efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The model does not include explicit costly punishment or reward mechanisms; cooperation is maintained by social norm diffusion and persuasion.
- Theoretical results may not generalize to environments where explicit punishment is available or central.
- Continuous-time, infinite-horizon model may differ from discrete, finite-round public goods games.
- No empirical or experimental data are provided; all results are theoretical.
- No communication, chat, or explicit feedback mechanisms are modeled beyond random interpersonal encounters.
