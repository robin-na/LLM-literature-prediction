# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Emotion and intergroup cooperation: How verbal expressions of guilt, shame, and pride influence behavior in a social dilemma
Authors: Nunney, S. J., van der Schalk, J., & Manstead, A. S. R.
Journal: Journal of Behavioral Decision Making
Year: 2022

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- total tickets won
- average node exit
- composite prosocial behavior (z-score of payoff-related variables)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- number of steals
- percentage of cooperative moves
- altruistic punishment (costly punishment measure)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are payoff-based (tickets won, node exit, prosocial composite), but behavioral outcomes (cooperation, stealing, punishment) are also reported and analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Across two lab experiments using an intergroup centipede game, the authors find that when a group expresses shame (or guilt) after behaving uncooperatively, the opposing group is more likely to cooperate in subsequent rounds, resulting in higher group payoffs (tickets won) and more prosocial behavior. In contrast, when a group expresses pride after uncooperative behavior, the opposing group is less likely to cooperate, resulting in lower payoffs and more costly punishment. The effect of emotion expression is causal (Study 2) and operates above and beyond the effect of prior behavior. The study does not use a standard public goods game or implement a formal punishment stage as in PGGs, but does include a costly punishment measure in Study 2.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that communication—specifically, the expression of self-conscious emotions—can significantly moderate the effect of prior uncooperative behavior on subsequent group efficiency in repeated, sequential social dilemmas. While not a standard PGG, the centipede game shares key features (sequential cooperation, group payoffs, potential for exploitation). The findings suggest that, in environments where communication is possible, expressions of shame or guilt can substitute for or augment formal punishment mechanisms in restoring cooperation and efficiency, while pride expressions can undermine them. However, the study does not manipulate punishment cost, technology, or other standard PGG design dimensions, so its direct predictive value for punishment-enabled PGG efficiency is limited. It does, however, highlight the importance of communication and emotional signaling as moderators of efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consisted of 3 players; games were played between two 3-person groups (6 players per session).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Study 1: 6 rounds per phase (12 total); Study 2: 2 rounds per phase (4 total).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Groups communicated via structured emotion expression (ratings of guilt, shame, pride) between phases; no free-form chat.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. In Study 2, altruistic punishment is measured as the number of tickets a group is willing to give up to remove double that amount from the opponent. Cost is 1 ticket lost per 2 tickets removed from opponent.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 2:1 (each ticket sacrificed removes 2 from opponent).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were informed of the number of rounds in each phase.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups saw the other group's emotion ratings and, by implication, could infer their behavior from game play.

## Important Limitations
- The game is a centipede game, not a standard public goods game; results may not generalize to simultaneous-move PGGs.
- Punishment is not implemented as an in-game, round-by-round mechanism but as a post-game, one-shot costly punishment decision.
- No manipulation or systematic analysis of standard PGG design dimensions such as MPCR, player count, or punishment cost/tech beyond the fixed settings used.
- Communication is limited to structured emotion expression, not free-form chat.
- Sample consists of undergraduate students in a lab setting; external validity may be limited.
- The effect of emotion expression may interact with other unmeasured group or individual differences.
