Outcome notes:
The paper reports on the sum and standard deviation of contributions as the main outcomes, which are behavioral measures of cooperation, not direct measures of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare. Payoff is discussed in the model, but not as a primary reported outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Publishing the donation list incompletely (only for those who contribute above a threshold) leads to higher total contributions and more equal contributions among players in a public goods game on a network. There is an optimal threshold for publication that maximizes these effects. The per capita number of neighbors and the amount of historical data players consider also moderate the effect, but the main result is robust: partial transparency outperforms full transparency or secrecy. The model is agent-based and does not include explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanisms; instead, it relies on social reputation and face loss as motivators.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that social information mechanisms (such as partial publication of contribution lists) can increase cooperation in public goods games, but it does not study monetary punishment or its effect on efficiency. The findings are relevant for predicting the effect of information disclosure or reputation-based interventions, not for predicting the effect of adding costly punishment. The sum of contributions is a proxy for group welfare, but efficiency (as defined by payoff relative to full cooperation) is not directly reported. Use this paper as informative for the effect of social/reputational incentives, not for monetary punishment treatments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size is set to N=300 in all simulations.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,001 generations, with results averaged over the last 5,001 generations.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players can choose how much to contribute, not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r=2, group size varies by network degree; MPCR is r divided by group size, but exact value depends on local group.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players can observe others' contributions via the donation list, either fully, partially (above threshold), or not at all, depending on the treatment.

## Important Limitations
- The paper uses agent-based simulations, not empirical or experimental data.
- No explicit punishment or monetary sanctioning mechanism is modeled; only social reputation and face loss are considered.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare are not directly reported; only sum and standard deviation of contributions are analyzed.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with explicit punishment, monetary incentives, or different network structures.
- The model assumes specific cultural and behavioral parameters (e.g., face culture, gossip) that may not apply universally.
