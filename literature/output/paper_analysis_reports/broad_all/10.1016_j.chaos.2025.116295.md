## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that insurance mechanisms in spatial PGGs can both promote and inhibit cooperation and group efficiency, depending on the synergy coefficient, insurance premium, coverage, and the structure of the population. At low synergy coefficients, insurance (especially when purchased by defectors) can sustain cooperation through self-organized cyclic states, increasing group efficiency relative to baseline defection. However, at high synergy coefficients or when insured cooperators are prevalent, insurance can hinder cooperation due to indirect territorial competition. When insurers are allowed to adapt their strategies (two-layer network), the cooperation-promoting effect of insurance is often reduced, as insurers may refuse to transact with defectors, limiting the spread of insurance and altering the evolutionary dynamics. The model provides detailed phase diagrams mapping these effects, with efficiency interpreted as the prevalence of cooperative strategies and the resulting group payoffs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical and simulation-based evidence on how insurance-like risk management mechanisms (adjacent to punishment) affect efficiency in spatial PGGs. While not a direct study of punishment, the findings are relevant for predicting efficiency in games with sanctioning or risk-mitigation features. The results suggest that the effect of such mechanisms on efficiency is highly sensitive to game parameters (e.g., synergy coefficient, insurance cost/benefit, network structure) and the ability of third parties (insurers) to adapt. For prediction tasks, the paper highlights the importance of considering not just the presence of a sanctioning or insurance mechanism, but also who can access it, its cost-effectiveness, and the possibility of coevolution with external agents. The evidence is most directly applicable to spatial, structured populations with risk-mitigation options, and less so to standard punishment in classic PGGs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a square lattice with L=200, so each individual participates in 5-person PGGs (self + 4 neighbors).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for many generations until absorbing or cyclic states are reached; specific round numbers (e.g., 100, 1000, 10^6) are shown in figures.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy coefficient r is varied (e.g., r=3.5, 4) in a 5-person game, so MPCR = r/5.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- The insurance mechanism is adjacent to, but not the same as, punishment or peer-sanctioning; results may not transfer directly to classic punishment-based PGGs.
- Outcomes are reported as phase diagrams and average strategy frequencies, not as direct measures of monetary efficiency or welfare in real populations.
- The model assumes spatial structure (square lattice) and synchronous updating, which may not generalize to well-mixed or asynchronous environments.
- Parameter regimes (e.g., synergy coefficient, insurance premium/coverage) are explored systematically, but real-world calibration is not attempted.
- No communication, reputation, or higher-order social mechanisms are included.
