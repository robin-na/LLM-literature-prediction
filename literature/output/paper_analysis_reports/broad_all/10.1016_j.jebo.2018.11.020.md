# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Why trust out-groups? The role of punishment under uncertainty
Authors: Pan, X. F., & Houser, D.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- trust decision (investor chooses IN or OUT)
- punishment decision (investor chooses to punish or not)
- beliefs about trustee reciprocity
- trustee reciprocity (ROLL or DON'T ROLL)
- in-group favoritism in trust
- frequency of punishment
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are trust (IN/OUT), punishment (punish/not), beliefs about reciprocity, and trustee behavior. Payoff-related outcomes such as efficiency, group earnings, or welfare are not directly analyzed or reported. Theoretical discussion references efficiency, but no empirical efficiency or group payoff results are presented.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds an asymmetric effect of punishment on trust: punishment opportunities increase non-social group investors' trust towards out-group trustees, but have no effect for social group investors or for in-group trust. Social group members are less likely to punish, and their trust in out-group members is higher even without punishment. The effect of punishment on trust is mediated by beliefs about trustee reciprocity. No direct measures of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare are reported, though the discussion suggests that social group formation may reduce the need for costly punishment and thus could improve efficiency in principle.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence about how group identity formation and punishment opportunities interact to affect trust and punishment behavior in a trust game with hidden action. However, it does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-game-like environments. The findings are most useful for understanding behavioral mechanisms (trust, punishment, beliefs) rather than payoff-based outcomes. The design is adjacent to PGGs, but not a direct PGG or linear public goods game. Use this paper for qualitative insight into how punishment and group identity interact, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency effects.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each trust game is played between two players (investor and trustee). Group formation stage involves groups, but the trust game is dyadic.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each participant plays two periods of the trust game, with random rematching.
- chat: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. No chat or communication is allowed during the trust game. Group formation stage (puzzle) encourages sharing and talking, but not during the trust game.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Investor's choice is binary: IN (trust) or OUT (do not trust). Trustee's choice is binary: ROLL (reciprocate) or DON'T ROLL (defect).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is costless to the punisher: investor can reduce trustee's earnings by $6 at no cost to self.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment reduces trustee's earnings by $6 per punishment decision (binary: $0 or $6).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told there are two periods; one is randomly selected for payment.

## Important Limitations
- No direct efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; all main results are on trust, punishment, and beliefs.
- The game is a hidden-action trust game, not a public goods game or linear PGG; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- Punishment is costless to the punisher, which differs from most PGG and real-world settings.
- Group size in the trust game is always two; group formation stage involves groups, but the main game is dyadic.
- No analysis of how punishment affects total surplus or efficiency; efficiency is only discussed in theory.
- No communication is allowed during the trust game, but group formation may induce social effects not present in standard PGGs.
