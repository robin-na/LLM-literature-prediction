# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Communication in intergroup conflicts
Authors: Leibbrandt, A., & Sääksvuori, L.
Journal: European Economic Review
Year: 2012

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- group conflict expenditures (inverse of efficiency)
- group payoffs
- within-group variation in payoffs
Non-payoff outcomes:
- communication content
- punishment frequency
- conditional behavior (matching, turn-taking)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is group conflict expenditure, which is directly related to efficiency (lower expenditures = higher efficiency). Group payoffs and within-group payoff variation are also analyzed. Non-payoff outcomes include communication content, punishment frequency, and behavioral mechanisms.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated intergroup contest game (two groups of four, 20 rounds), the opportunity for within-group peer punishment (REST+PUN) does not reduce group conflict expenditures compared to restricted communication without punishment (REST). Both treatments result in much higher conflict expenditures (i.e., lower efficiency) than the no-communication baseline. Open communication between groups, in contrast, dramatically reduces conflict expenditures and increases efficiency. Punishment is rarely used when communication is available, and its presence does not significantly affect group payoffs or efficiency. The main mechanism for efficiency gains is open communication and turn-taking, not punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in intergroup contest environments with intra-group punishment and communication, enabling punishment does not improve efficiency relative to communication alone. If the control (no-punishment) game has restricted communication, adding punishment does not increase efficiency. If open communication is possible, efficiency is much higher regardless of punishment. Thus, for prediction, the presence of peer punishment in this design should not be expected to increase efficiency, and may even be associated with high waste. The findings are most relevant for contest-like games with group-level competition and intra-group punishment, rather than standard linear PGGs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group has 4 players; two groups per contest (8 players per game).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 rounds (periods) per game; number of periods is common knowledge.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Communication is a key treatment variable: no chat, restricted (within-group) chat, or open (between-group) chat. Restricted chat increases waste; open chat increases efficiency.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: any integer amount from 0 to 1000 MUs can be contributed.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The contest is probabilistic: each group's chance of winning is proportional to its share of total expenditures. The prize is 4000 MUs per group (1000 per member). The marginal return is not a fixed MPCR as in linear PGGs, but is determined by contest success function.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment costs 1 MU per deduction point; each point reduces target's earnings by 3 MUs. Only available in REST+PUN treatment (within-group only).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3:1 (each MU spent reduces target's earnings by 3 MUs).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of rounds (20) is common knowledge.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants are informed of individual expenditures and earnings of their own group members, and total expenditures by the competing group.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous: participants do not know who punished them or who was punished.

## Important Limitations
- The game is an intergroup contest, not a standard linear public goods game; results may not generalize to standard PGGs.
- Punishment is only possible within groups, not between groups.
- Punishment is rarely used in the presence of communication, limiting power to detect its effects.
- The contest success function and probabilistic prize allocation differ from typical PGG payoff structures.
- The minimum-effort rule for prize allocation may encourage turn-taking, which may not generalize to other environments.
- All participants are students in a lab setting; external validity may be limited.
