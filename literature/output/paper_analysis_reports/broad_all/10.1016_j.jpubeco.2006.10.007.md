# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Leading by example with and without exclusion power in voluntary contribution experiments
Authors: Güth, W., Levati, M. V., Sutter, M., & Van der Heijden, E.
Journal: Journal of Public Economics
Year: 2007

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average group profit
- average individual profit
- efficiency (implied by group profit relative to maximum possible)
- earnings
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution levels
- frequency of exclusion
- voting for leadership
- correlation between leader and follower contributions
Outcome notes:
The paper reports both contribution rates and profit/payoff outcomes. Efficiency is discussed in terms of group profit and the effect of leadership/exclusion power on group welfare. The main dependent variables are average contributions and profits, with explicit comparison to the control (no leader) condition.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a linear public goods game with four players and 24 rounds, the introduction of a leader who moves first increases contributions and group profits compared to the standard simultaneous game. When the leader is given exclusion power (the ability to exclude a follower from the next round), both contributions and group profits rise substantially more. The effect of exclusion power is robust to whether the leader is fixed or rotates among group members. In the endogenous phase, only about 40% of groups successfully appoint a leader, but those that do achieve much higher contributions and profits than those that do not. The presence of exclusion power (punishment) is a strong moderator of the positive effect on efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling punishment via exclusion power in a public goods game with leadership substantially increases group efficiency compared to both the standard game and leadership without punishment. The effect is robust across fixed and rotating leader appointment. The control (no leader) game yields the lowest efficiency, leadership without punishment yields a moderate increase, and leadership with punishment yields the highest efficiency. For prediction, the presence of punishment (exclusion), the marginal per-capita return, and the group size are all well-specified and informative. The absence of communication, the use of continuous contribution, and the lack of reward options are also clearly specified. The findings are directly relevant for predicting the efficiency impact of punishment in similar game designs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in groups of 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consists of 24 periods (rounds).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication between participants is strictly forbidden.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: 0 ≤ c ≤ 25.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (mpcr) is 0.4 (beta = 0.4, group size 4).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is via exclusion; the cost is indirect (group loses a contributor for one round, excluded player earns only endowment). No explicit cost to punisher, but group efficiency is affected by exclusion.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is highly effective: exclusion removes a player from the group for one round, directly reducing their payoff and group size. This threat strongly increases contributions and efficiency.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are informed that there are 24 periods in total.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, participants are shown all individual contributions in their group (by membership number) and their period payoffs.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know who is the leader (the only one who can exclude), and who is excluded, but not who voted for whom in the endogenous phase. Exclusion is attributed to the leader.

## Important Limitations
- Punishment is implemented only as exclusion for one round, not as a direct monetary fine; generalizability to other punishment forms may be limited.
- The experiment uses a fixed group size (4) and a specific mpcr (0.4); results may differ with other parameters.
- No reward mechanism is tested, so findings do not address the relative effectiveness of rewards versus punishment.
- Communication is not allowed, which may limit external validity for real-world settings where discussion is possible.
- The cost of punishment is indirect (via group size reduction), which may differ from direct-cost punishment mechanisms.
