## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that when third parties can both punish and reward, the demand for costly punishment is 42.5% lower than when only punishment is available, and the demand for costly reward is 23% lower than when only reward is available. This reduction is due to fewer individuals choosing to punish or reward, not to a reduction in the amount assigned by those who do. The presence of both options allows individuals to signal approval/disapproval by withholding reward/punishment, reducing the need for costly enforcement. Transfers by Player A (the dictator) do not differ significantly across treatments, but efficiency (measured by earnings) is highest in the reward-only treatment, lowest in the punishment-only treatment, and intermediate when both are available. Thus, the presence of punishment opportunities reduces efficiency, while the presence of reward opportunities increases it.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that, in a one-shot dictator game with third-party punishment and reward, the presence of punishment opportunities reduces efficiency (earnings), while the presence of reward opportunities increases efficiency. When both are available, efficiency is intermediate. Although the game is not a repeated public goods game, the efficiency results are directly measured and relevant for predicting the effect of punishment and reward mechanisms on efficiency in similar norm-enforcement settings. The findings suggest that adding punishment to a setting with only reward will decrease efficiency, and adding reward to a setting with only punishment will increase efficiency. The effect is driven by the costliness of enforcement actions and the ability to signal approval/disapproval without incurring costs.

## Important Limitations
- The game is a one-shot, three-player dictator game with third-party punishment/reward, not a repeated public goods game; results may not generalize to repeated or group-based public goods settings.
- The efficiency results are based on earnings in a single round, not on sustained cooperation or long-term welfare.
- The study does not manipulate or analyze group size, marginal per-capita return, or other key public goods game parameters.
- No communication or reputation effects are present; real-world settings may differ.
- The findings may not generalize to settings where punishment/reward is not third-party or where roles are endogenous.
