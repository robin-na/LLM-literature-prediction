Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group contributions, punishment behavior, and decision times. The paper does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare, nor does it provide direct analysis of payoff-based outcomes. All results are in terms of contributions and punishment patterns.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study implements a 2x2 design varying punishment (present/absent) and instruction format (short/long, less/more explicit). In the punishment treatments, longer and more explicit instructions lead to higher and more stable contributions, with punishment more effectively targeted at low contributors. In the absence of punishment, instruction format has little effect on average contributions over time. Decision times are lower with more explicit instructions, indicating better comprehension. The paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, so the direct effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined, but the results suggest that the effectiveness of punishment in raising contributions depends on subjects' comprehension of the social dilemma.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effectiveness of punishment in raising contributions in public goods games depends on participants' comprehension of the incentive structure, which can be improved by more explicit instructions. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the realized effect of punishment on efficiency may be moderated by factors affecting comprehension, such as instruction clarity. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or payoff outcomes, it cannot be used to directly predict efficiency changes due to punishment. Instead, it provides indirect support that punishment is more likely to increase efficiency when comprehension is high and punishment is well-targeted.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in groups of 4.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use 20 rounds (partner-matching).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player chooses how many of 20 tokens to contribute.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.5 (each token in the group project yields 0.5 tokens to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost is 1 token per punishment point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point assigned costs the sender 1 token and reduces the recipient's payoff by 3 tokens (1:3 ratio).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game lasts 20 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, players see all group members' contributions (in descending order), total group contribution, and their own earnings. No subject identifiers are used.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; players are not told who punished them.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; all results are in terms of contributions and punishment behavior.
- Instruction format varies in more than one way (length, explicitness, number of examples), so the specific mechanism driving the effect is not isolated.
- Findings on the effect of punishment on contributions may not directly translate to effects on efficiency, especially since punishment can reduce total payoffs.
- The study is conducted with undergraduate students in a lab setting, which may limit generalizability.
