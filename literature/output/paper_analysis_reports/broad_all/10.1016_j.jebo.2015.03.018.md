# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Collusion among many firms: The disciplinary power of targeted punishment
Authors: Roux, C., & Thöni, C.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2015

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- mean profit
- total group profit
- income reduction from punishment
- total quantity produced (inverse proxy for collusion and profit)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- quantity choice
- punishment points assigned
- frequency and amount of punishment
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group profit and total quantity produced, which are direct or inverse measures of efficiency/collusion. Punishment behavior and quantity choice are also analyzed, but the central focus is on profit and collusion outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The availability of targeted punishment in Cournot oligopoly experiments leads to significantly higher group profits and lower total output (closer to the collusive benchmark) for all group sizes tested (2, 4, 6, 8). The effect of punishment on efficiency is positive and increases with group size beyond duopoly: in 6- and 8-firm markets, mean profits nearly double with punishment. Punishment is used in a targeted and proportional way, with defectors facing harsher penalties as group size increases. The study provides direct evidence that targeted punishment can sustain high-efficiency collusion even in large groups, contrary to standard theory and previous experimental results without punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong, directly relevant evidence that enabling targeted punishment in repeated Cournot (oligopoly) games leads to large increases in group efficiency (profit), especially as group size increases. The design is not a standard public goods game but is closely related in structure and in the punishment mechanism. The results suggest that, for games with similar punishment technology (high impact, targeted, known cost), the predicted efficiency with punishment enabled should be much higher than in the control (no-punishment) condition, and the effect is larger for larger groups. The findings are highly informative for predicting treatment efficiency from design dimensions, especially for settings with targeted, high-impact punishment and no communication.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Player count (n) is systematically varied (2, 4, 6, 8). The effect of punishment on efficiency is stronger for larger n (n > 2).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of rounds is fixed at 20 for all conditions and is commonly known.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (quantity) choice is continuous between 0 and 74 (in 0.1 increments), not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is determined by the Cournot payoff function: each unit produced reduces market price for all, with linear demand and constant marginal cost. Not a standard PGG, but the equivalent is present and fully specified.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment costs 1 unit to the punisher per point assigned; each point reduces the target's payoff by 5 units. This high impact/cost ratio is central to the strong effect.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is highly effective: 1 unit cost to punisher reduces target's payoff by 5 units. Punishment is targeted and can be assigned to specific individuals.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The total number of rounds (20) is commonly known to all participants.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, all firms are informed about all individual quantities and profits realized in the market.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a Cournot oligopoly game, not a standard public goods game, so direct generalization to PGGs requires caution.
- Punishment is highly effective (5:1 impact/cost ratio) and targeted; results may not generalize to settings with less effective or non-targeted punishment.
- No communication is allowed; real-world collusion often involves communication, which may interact with punishment effects.
- The maximum group size tested is 8; effects for larger groups are unknown.
- The laboratory setting and use of student subjects may limit external validity to real-world firms or other populations.
