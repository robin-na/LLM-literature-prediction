Outcome notes:
The model's main outcomes are the equilibrium and dynamic levels of cooperation and the resulting water quality, which is a direct proxy for group welfare/efficiency. The model does not report individual payoffs, but the group-level pollution outcome is a direct function of collective behavior and is interpreted as a welfare/efficiency outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the interplay between social pressure (which increases with both the number of cooperators and the level of pollution) and individual cost-benefit decisions can lead to complex group-level outcomes. Depending on parameter values, the system can settle into a high-cooperation/clean-water equilibrium, a low-cooperation/polluted equilibrium, or exhibit oscillatory or chaotic dynamics. The model shows that increasing the effectiveness of pollution-reducing technology can paradoxically worsen water quality if it reduces the perceived need for cooperation. Conservatism (slower behavioral change) can stabilize cooperation and maintain higher group welfare. The model does not include explicit punishment, but social pressure is discussed as a possible proxy for reputational or sanctioning effects.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical insight into how social feedbacks and behavioral dynamics can affect group efficiency in public-goods-like settings. While it does not directly model punishment, it suggests that mechanisms increasing social pressure (which could include punishment, reputation, or social norms) can promote cooperation and improve group outcomes, but may also lead to multiple stable states or complex dynamics. The findings support the idea that the structure and strength of social incentives (including, potentially, punishment) are critical moderators of efficiency. However, the absence of explicit punishment or experimental manipulation limits the direct applicability of the results to predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in empirical PGGs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. The model assumes a large number of players (N), but does not specify an exact value. The effect of small vs large N is discussed in the limitations.
- number of rounds: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. The model is dynamic and iterated over years, with no fixed number of rounds; time is treated as continuous or annual steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete options: cooperate (costly, low pollution) or defect (cheap, high pollution).
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The effectiveness of cooperation is parameterized as the reduction in phosphorus per cooperator; analogous to MPCR.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Players are assumed to observe the overall pollution level and possibly the fraction of cooperators, as social pressure depends on these.

## Important Limitations
- No explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism is modeled; social pressure is a broad construct that may include reputational or punishment-like effects but is not parameterized as such.
- The model is theoretical and not empirically validated or parameterized for real-world cases.
- Player count is assumed large and homogeneous; effects of small groups or heterogeneity are only discussed as future work.
- No communication, reward, or explicit institutional mechanisms are included.
- Payoff outcomes are modeled as group-level pollution/welfare, not as explicit monetary payoffs.
- The model assumes binary (all-or-nothing) contribution choices.
