Outcome notes:
The paper explicitly analyzes group payoffs and welfare (see Appendix G), as well as group contributions and punishment behaviors. The main text focuses on contributions and punishment, but the welfare/efficiency analysis is present and central to the conclusions.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Punishment opportunities increase contributions in all treatments, but the efficiency gains depend on the decision structure. In the control (NoR, self-representation), punishment increases contributions to 75% of the endowment and does not reduce group payoffs, so efficiency is maintained or improved. In representation treatments (RnoM and RM), punishment increases contributions but also increases antisocial punishment, leading to lower group payoffs and reduced efficiency compared to the no-punishment baseline. Communication (RM) between team members and representatives slightly improves outcomes compared to RnoM but does not fully restore efficiency. Thus, the effect of punishment on efficiency is positive in self-representation but negative or neutral under representation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games is moderated by the decision structure: punishment is most effective (and efficient) when individuals decide for themselves, but can reduce efficiency when representatives decide for groups, especially if communication is limited. For prediction, if the control (no-punishment) efficiency is known, enabling punishment will likely increase efficiency in self-representation settings, but may decrease or only modestly increase efficiency in representation settings, particularly when antisocial punishment is prevalent. The marginal per-capita return, punishment cost, and punishment effectiveness are all specified, allowing for direct mapping to prediction models. Communication channels (chat/messages) can moderate but not fully reverse negative efficiency effects under representation.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. NoR: 4 individuals per group; RnoM and RM: 4 teams of 3 (12 players per session, but decisions made at team level).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 rounds per session: 10 without punishment (Part NoP), 10 with punishment (Part P).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. In RM, non-representatives can send non-binding messages (cheap talk) to the representative; no free-form chat. RnoM and NoR have no communication.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: 0 ≤ C_i ≤ 20.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-team return is 0.4; with 4 teams, this is equivalent to MPCR = 0.1 per player.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishing team pays 1 point per punishment point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point assigned reduces the target's payoff by 3 points (punishment effectiveness = 3).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects are told in advance that each part lasts exactly 10 periods.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each stage, teams are informed about all other teams' contributions and earnings; after punishment, all punishment allocations are revealed anonymously.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous but team IDs are reshuffled each round, so punishment is not traceable to a persistent identity.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses randomly selected representatives rather than elected ones, limiting external validity for real-world delegation.
- The sample size per treatment is moderate, which may limit statistical power for detecting small effects.
- The design does not allow for strategic interaction within teams; all team members receive identical payoffs and have no intra-team bargaining.
- The marginal per-capita return is fixed and may not generalize to other MPCR values.
- Punishment is only possible across teams, not within teams, which may differ from some real-world settings.
- No reward mechanism is studied, so results may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
