## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperators (F_c)
- cooperation rate
Outcome notes:
The main reported outcome is the fraction of cooperators (F_c), i.e., the proportion of agents choosing to cooperate. No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or total earnings is reported. All results are in terms of cooperation rates, not payoffs.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that targeted punishment via the HPS mechanism increases the fraction of cooperators compared to random supervision, especially at higher synergy factors and with well-chosen exemption list parameters. Increasing supervision ability, penalty severity, and exemption list capacity generally increases cooperation rates. Moderately long observation periods and short removal times also promote cooperation. The results are robust across different network structures. However, all findings are in terms of cooperation rates, not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that targeted punishment mechanisms can increase cooperation rates in public goods games, and that design parameters such as supervision ability, penalty severity, and exemption list management are important moderators. However, because the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The results may be useful for predicting the direction of change in cooperation rates under different punishment regimes, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=1000 agents as default; population size is a parameter but not systematically varied for effect.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 steps; outcomes are averaged over the last 100 steps.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r=4, group size d+1=5, so MPCR = r/(d+1) = 0.8 in main simulations; r is varied in analyses.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a penalty to defectors (penalty coefficient tau), but the cost to the punisher is not explicitly modeled; punishment is external (regulator-imposed), not peer punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is controlled by the penalty coefficient tau, which is varied in simulations.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- No direct analysis or reporting of efficiency or total group payoff; results cannot be directly used for efficiency prediction.
- Punishment is implemented as external (regulator-imposed) rather than peer punishment, which may limit generalizability to standard PGG lab experiments.
- Simulation-based theoretical model; no empirical or experimental data.
- Assumes fixed agent risk preferences; does not model co-evolution of preferences and strategies.
- No analysis of the cost of supervision or punishment to the regulator, only the effect on defectors' payoffs.
