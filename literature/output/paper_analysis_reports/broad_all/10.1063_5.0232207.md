Outcome notes:
The main reported outcome is the fraction of cooperators in the population (both in the intervention and dispute layers). No explicit group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings are reported. Outcomes are behavioral (cooperation rate), not payoff-based.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of third-party intervention (punishment and/or reward) in a networked PD/SD game increases the fraction of cooperators in both the intervention and dispute layers. The effect is stronger when the intervention cost is lower and the intervention intensity (punishment/reward coefficients) is higher. The results are robust across different network topologies (square lattice and ER random networks) and game types (PD and SD). However, the paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, or welfare; all results are in terms of cooperation rates.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that third-party punishment and reward mechanisms can increase cooperation rates in networked social dilemmas, and that the effect is moderated by the cost and intensity of intervention. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be directly used to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. The results are most useful for predicting changes in cooperation rates, not payoffs. The model is also based on the PD/SD rather than a standard PGG, and is simulation/theory-based rather than empirical.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=10,000 per layer; all results are for large populations. No variation in player count is analyzed.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 60,000 Monte Carlo steps, with outcomes averaged over the last 1,000 steps. No variation in number of rounds is analyzed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate or defect (all-or-nothing).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Intervention cost parameter c is directly manipulated. Higher c (cost) reduces cooperation rates; lower c increases cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment intensity (parameter gamma) is manipulated. Higher gamma increases cooperation up to a saturation point.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward mechanism (parameter beta) is present and manipulated. Higher beta increases cooperation.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward effectiveness (parameter beta) is manipulated. Higher beta increases cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical/simulation-based, not empirical; no real human or field data.
- The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- The game is a two-player PD/SD, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- No explicit analysis of group payoff, surplus, or efficiency; cannot directly inform efficiency prediction.
- No variation in player count, number of rounds, or other key design dimensions beyond intervention cost/intensity.
- No analysis of communication, information, or identification effects.
