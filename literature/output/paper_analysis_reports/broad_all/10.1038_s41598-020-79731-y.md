Outcome notes:
The paper's main dependent variables are group-level payoff (net earnings), critical points for cooperation (as a function of game parameters), and the evolution of cooperation and punishment probabilities. The efficiency and group payoff are central to the analysis, both mathematically and in the computational model.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that costly punishment in public goods games lowers the critical synergy threshold required for cooperation to emerge, thereby increasing the likelihood of cooperation and improving group-level payoff (efficiency) compared to the no-punishment baseline. However, the cost of punishment reduces the total group payoff, so the efficiency gain from increased cooperation is partially offset by punishment costs. The effect is stronger when punishment is more efficient (higher fine per unit cost). The paper also shows that inclusiveness (redistribution of payoffs) is an even more effective way to promote cooperation and efficiency, often making punishment unnecessary if inclusiveness is high enough.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and computational evidence that enabling punishment in a standard public goods game increases group efficiency relative to the no-punishment baseline, especially when punishment is efficient (high fine, low cost). The effect is moderated by the costliness of punishment: higher punishment costs reduce the efficiency benefit. The findings also highlight that the structure of payoff redistribution (inclusiveness) can be a more powerful moderator than punishment. For prediction tasks, the paper supports the expectation that, all else equal, adding punishment will increase efficiency, but the magnitude depends on punishment cost, punishment effectiveness, and the degree of payoff redistribution.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The main model uses k+1 players (e.g., k=4, so 5 players per group). The critical point for cooperation is derived as a function of k.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: each player can either contribute 1 unit or defect (contribute 0).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is r/(k+1), where r is the synergy factor. The critical point for cooperation is derived as a function of r and k.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost (gamma) is explicitly modeled and varied. Lower punishment cost increases the positive effect of punishment on efficiency.

Punishment effectiveness (fine per unit cost, beta/gamma) is explicitly modeled and varied. Higher punishment effectiveness increases the positive effect on efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The analysis is entirely theoretical and computational; no human or field data are used.
- The evolutionary model assumes infinite or very large populations and may not capture all dynamics of small groups or finite repeated games.
- The model does not include communication, reputation, or other social mechanisms beyond punishment and payoff redistribution.
- No reward mechanism is considered; only punishment and redistribution are analyzed.
- The model uses binary (all-or-nothing) contribution choices, not continuous contributions.
