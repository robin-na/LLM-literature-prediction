## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a reputation-based warning/punishment mechanism—where players are less likely to imitate neighbors who have persistently defected—substantially increases the fraction of cooperators in a spatial PDG simulation. Stronger punishment (higher discount factor) and more timely punishment (lower threshold for triggering the discount) both further increase cooperation rates. The effect is robust across a range of temptation-to-defect parameters. However, the paper does not report on group payoff, efficiency, or welfare, so the findings are limited to behavioral outcomes (cooperation rates) rather than payoff-based outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that punishment-like mechanisms targeting persistent defectors can increase cooperation rates in spatial PDG environments. However, because it does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-game-like environments. The findings may be useful for understanding mechanisms that increase cooperation, but should be used with caution for efficiency prediction, as higher cooperation does not always translate to higher group payoff depending on the cost structure of punishment and the payoff matrix.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a square lattice of 100x100 players (10,000 total), each with 4 neighbors.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 time steps, with statistics averaged over the last 2,000 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate or defect (binary choice).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is operationalized as a discount factor (alpha) on the probability of imitating persistent defectors. Higher alpha means stronger punishment (greater reduction in imitation probability). Stronger punishment increases cooperation rates.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- The outcome of interest is the fraction of cooperators, not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare.
- Punishment is implemented as a reduction in imitation probability, not as a costly action with explicit cost to the punisher.
- No analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or surplus is provided.
- No empirical or experimental data; results are from agent-based simulations.
