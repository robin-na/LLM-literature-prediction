## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that graded punishment (where the probability of being punished increases with repeated defections) is generally more effective at promoting cooperation in spatial public goods games than traditional fixed-probability punishment. This effect is strongest when the punishment cost is low relative to the fine and when the initial punishment probability is not too small. When the punishment cost approaches the fine or the initial punishment probability is very low, the advantage of graded punishment disappears or reverses. The main reported outcome is the steady-state cooperation density, not group efficiency or total payoff. Payoff is used for local updating and is discussed in mechanism explanations, but efficiency is not directly measured.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the structure of the punishment mechanism (graded vs. fixed probability) can significantly affect cooperation rates in spatial PGGs, especially as a function of punishment cost, fine, and initial punishment probability. However, because the main outcome is cooperation density rather than group efficiency or total payoff, the results should be used with caution for predicting efficiency outcomes. The findings suggest that, in similar spatial PGGs, graded punishment is likely to increase cooperation (and thus, by implication, efficiency) except when punishment is very costly or the initial probability is very low. The paper provides detailed parameter sweeps for punishment cost, fine, enhancement factor, and initial punishment probability, which can inform predictions about the conditions under which punishment will be most effective. However, direct predictions of efficiency require caution, as efficiency is not directly reported.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a 100x100 lattice (N=10,000), with each player in groups of size G=5 (self + 4 neighbors).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 to 100,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCSs), with steady-state outcomes averaged over the last 1,000 iterations.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: players either contribute one unit or nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Enhancement factor r is varied (e.g., r=3.0, 3.5, etc.), with group size G=5, so MPCR = r/G. Higher r increases cooperation.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost (alpha) is varied (e.g., 0.1 to 0.6). Higher punishment cost reduces cooperation and weakens the advantage of graded punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the fine (beta) imposed on defectors per punishment. Higher beta increases cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The main outcome is cooperation density, not group efficiency or total payoff; efficiency is not directly reported.
- The model assumes a spatial lattice structure, which may not generalize to well-mixed or other network structures.
- No reward or positive incentive mechanisms are considered.
- No communication or reputation effects are modeled.
- Parameter sweeps are extensive, but only for a limited set of game design dimensions (punishment cost, fine, enhancement factor, initial punishment probability).
