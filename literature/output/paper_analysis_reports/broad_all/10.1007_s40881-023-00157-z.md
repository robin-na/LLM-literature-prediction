## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in public goods games with punishment, individuals who contribute more are punished less frequently and less severely. Both cooperators (contributors) and defectors (non-contributors) engage in punishing defectors, though full contributors are more likely to do so. There is little evidence that contributors are more likely to punish than defectors overall, and this pattern is consistent across most cultures studied. The study does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff in public goods games. It is useful for understanding behavioral patterns of punishment and contribution, which may inform mechanism design or expectations about who will use punishment, but it does not inform the downstream prediction task of treatment efficiency from game design dimensions and control efficiency. The absence of efficiency or payoff analysis limits its direct utility for the prediction task.
