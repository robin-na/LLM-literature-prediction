# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Equality as a benchmark for third-party punishment and reward: The moderating role of uncertainty in social dilemmas
Authors: de Kwaadsteniet, E. W., Rijkhoff, S. A. M., & van Dijk, E.
Journal: Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes
Year: 2013

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment assigned by third-party
- reward assigned by third-party
- affective (emotional) reactions of third-party
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the amount of punishment or reward assigned by a third-party to group members in a common resource dilemma, under conditions of certainty or uncertainty. No group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported. The study focuses on sanctioning decisions and their emotional drivers, not on the resulting group efficiency or earnings.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that third-party punishers and rewarders use the equality rule as a benchmark for sanctioning decisions only when the size of the common resource is certain. Under certainty, those who take more than an equal share are punished much more, and those who take an equal or smaller share are rewarded more. Under uncertainty, the amount of punishment or reward is linearly related to the amount harvested, with no sharp benchmark. The emotional basis for punishment is negative affect (anger), while for reward it is positive affect. The study does not measure or report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff in public goods or common resource games, as it does not report any payoff-based outcomes. However, it provides informative context about how the clarity of the cooperation norm (equality rule) and environmental uncertainty affect the application of punishment and reward. In settings where the cooperation norm is clear and known, punishment is more sharply targeted at norm violators; under uncertainty, punishment is more diffuse. This may indirectly inform predictions about the effectiveness of punishment in promoting cooperation or efficiency, but the paper does not provide direct evidence for such effects.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each scenario involved a group of five people sharing a common resource.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Single-trial (one-shot) game; participants made sanctioning decisions after one round.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Harvest (contribution) choices are continuous, not all-or-nothing; group members could harvest any number of coins.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is costly to the punisher: each coin of punishment assigned is worth €0.03 and is subtracted from the group member's earnings. The cost to the punisher is not explicitly stated, but participants were paid a flat fee, so punishment is not personally costly in this design.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a direct deduction from the target's earnings: 1 coin of punishment reduces the target's earnings by 1 coin.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Study 2 uses a reward mechanism, where third-parties can assign positive coins to group members.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is not personally costly to the third-party; participants are paid a flat fee. Each coin of reward increases the group member's earnings by 1 coin.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is implemented as a direct addition to the target's earnings: 1 coin of reward increases the target's earnings by 1 coin.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told it is a single-trial (one-shot) game; the number of rounds is known.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Third-party is shown the individual harvests of three group members (50, 100, 150 coins).

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report any group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare outcomes; only third-party sanctioning decisions are measured.
- The design is a single-trial (one-shot) common resource dilemma, which may not generalize to repeated or dynamic settings.
- The third-party making sanctioning decisions is not a member of the group, which differs from many public goods and punishment experiments.
- The game is a common resource dilemma, not a standard public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- Punishment and reward are not personally costly to the third-party, as participants are paid a flat fee.
