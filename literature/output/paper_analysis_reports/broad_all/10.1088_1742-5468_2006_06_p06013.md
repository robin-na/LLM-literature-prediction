Outcome notes:
The main reported outcome is the steady-state fraction of cooperators (c) in the population, as well as spatial and cluster properties. There is no direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare. Payoff is used as a mechanism in the model, but not as a reported outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that requiring agents to achieve a minimum payoff threshold (environmental pressure) in spatial Prisoner's Dilemma games can lead to the emergence of cooperation, even when the payoff for mutual defection is not very harsh. The fraction of cooperators increases with the threshold up to a point, and the addition of a Pavlovian update rule (win-stay, lose-shift) further increases cooperation and leads to power-law cluster size distributions in some parameter regions. However, the paper does not report on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, and does not include explicit punishment or reward mechanisms beyond the standard PD payoffs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is of limited direct use for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it does not study public goods games or explicit punishment mechanisms. The model is a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma with environmental thresholds and focuses on the fraction of cooperators as the main outcome. There is no analysis of group efficiency or total payoff, and no manipulation of punishment or sanctioning technology. The findings may be of some indirect relevance for understanding how environmental constraints can promote cooperation, but do not provide evidence for the effect of punishment on efficiency in PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The model uses large square grids (e.g., 50x50 to 500x500), with each cell representing a player. The number of players is fixed by the grid size.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The model is iterated for many rounds until a steady state is reached; typical transients last 100-200 rounds, but the number of rounds is not a manipulated variable.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Players have binary choices: cooperate (C) or defect (D), i.e., all-or-nothing.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game or a direct variant.
- No explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present; 'punishment' refers only to the PD payoff for mutual defection.
- The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are from theoretical modeling and simulation.
- No analysis of the effect of punishment cost, technology, or identification on efficiency.
- No communication, reward, or information-sharing mechanisms are modeled.
