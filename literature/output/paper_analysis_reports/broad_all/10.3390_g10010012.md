Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are cooperation (contributions to the public good), punishment behavior (deduction points assigned), and gossip behavior. The paper does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or other payoff-based outcomes as primary dependent variables. Some information about average earnings is provided in the methods, but not as an analyzed outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In four-person public goods games, the availability of punishment and gossip options increases cooperation (i.e., higher contributions to the public good). In dyads, punishment does not increase cooperation. Power asymmetry (high vs. low power) does not significantly affect cooperation, punishment, or gossip behavior. The effect of punishment on cooperation is robust in groups but not in dyads. The paper does not report efficiency or group payoff as an outcome, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be directly determined from this paper.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in four-person public goods games with MPCR=0.75, enabling punishment increases cooperation rates, but does not report on efficiency or group payoff. Thus, for the prediction task, the paper supports the expectation that punishment increases cooperation in group settings, but does not allow direct inference about efficiency (which depends on the costliness of punishment relative to the gains from increased cooperation). The absence of efficiency or payoff analysis is a limitation for direct prediction. The null effect of punishment in dyads is also informative for settings with only two players.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Study 1 uses dyads (2 players); Study 2 uses four-person groups. Punishment increases cooperation in groups but not in dyads.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each study uses 8 rounds of the public goods game (split into two 4-round blocks for within-subject punishment/gossip manipulations).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Gossip is implemented as a text message option in Study 2. When gossip is available, cooperation increases.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: participants choose how many MUs (0-10) to contribute each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.75 in both dyads (multiplier 1.5) and four-person groups (multiplier 3).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction MU costs the punisher 1 MU.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction MU reduces the punished individual's payoff by 3 MUs.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told the number of rounds in advance (8 rounds).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants see their own and others' contributions, the sum of contributions, and each person's payoffs.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment decisions are made simultaneously and publicly; in groups, participants learn about the deduction MUs assigned to them by each group member (i.e., punishment is identifiable).

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings as an analyzed outcome, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be directly determined.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (contribution, punishment, gossip), not payoff-based.
- The power manipulation is exogenous to the public goods game (based on a subsequent dictator game), which may limit ecological validity.
- The study uses US-based MTurk samples, which may limit generalizability.
- The design does not allow participants to choose between punishment and gossip; both are available in some conditions.
