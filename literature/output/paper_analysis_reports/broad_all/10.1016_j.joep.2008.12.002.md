# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Avoiding the sharp tongue: Anticipated written messages promote fair economic exchange
Authors: Xiao, E., & Houser, D.
Journal: Journal of Economic Psychology
Year: 2009

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- Offer amount in dictator game (as % of endowment)
- Frequency of fair (>=40%) and unfair (<=20%) offers
- Frequency of minimum (10%) offers
- Distribution of offers
- Message content and emotional valence
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the amount offered by the dictator (behavioral, not payoff-based). No group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff is reported, as the dictator game is not a public goods or group production environment. The paper discusses efficiency in the context of costly punishment in ultimatum games, but does not report efficiency or group payoff as a primary outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The opportunity for receivers to send ex post written messages (expressing disapproval or emotion) in a one-shot dictator game leads to a statistically significant reduction in the frequency of maximally selfish (10%) offers by dictators, compared to the standard dictator game. However, the frequency of fair (50/50) offers is not significantly increased. The effect of non-monetary sanctions (messages) is smaller than that of monetary punishment (rejection) in ultimatum games, which more strongly reduce selfish offers but at the cost of efficiency due to destroyed surplus. The study does not report efficiency or group payoff as an outcome, as the dictator game does not involve group production or a public good.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about the effect of informal, non-monetary sanctions (written messages) on selfishness in a simple allocation game, but not in a public goods or group production context. It shows that non-monetary sanctions can reduce selfish behavior, but less so than monetary punishment. However, because the game is a one-shot dictator game (not a repeated or multi-player public goods game), and efficiency is not measured, the findings are only adjacent to the prediction task. The results suggest that in environments where only non-monetary sanctions are possible, some reduction in selfishness may occur, but the effect on efficiency in public goods games cannot be directly inferred from this study.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game is played by two players: one dictator and one receiver.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject plays the game exactly once (one-shot).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In the DEE treatment, receivers can send a one-way written message to dictators after the decision. No bilateral chat or negotiation is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (offer) choices are discrete but not all-or-nothing: dictators choose from a set of possible splits ($2, 4, 8, 10, 12, 16, 18$ out of $20).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the dictator game, there is no monetary punishment. In the ultimatum game (referenced for comparison), rejection is costly (destroys surplus).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the dictator game, punishment is non-monetary (messages). In the ultimatum game, punishment is rejection (destroys all surplus).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects are told the game is played only once (one-shot).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Messages are anonymous; participants never learn the identity of their counterpart.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a one-shot dictator game, not a public goods game or repeated interaction, so findings may not generalize to group cooperation or efficiency contexts.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only individual offer behavior is measured.
- The non-monetary sanction (message) is one-way, limited in content (no foul language), and may be less impactful than more severe or bilateral communication.
- The sample is undergraduate students from a single university, which may limit generalizability.
- The study compares to ultimatum games for monetary punishment, but these are not public goods or multi-player environments.
