## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that social exclusion mechanisms (peer and pool) can allow excluders to dominate over defectors in finite-population public goods games, depending on exclusion cost, group size, and exclusion effectiveness. Peer exclusion is more effective at low costs, while pool exclusion is more robust at high costs and larger group sizes. Both mechanisms can sustain cooperation and prevent the dominance of defectors, which would otherwise reduce group payoff. The analysis is based on evolutionary game theory, using payoff functions and fixation probabilities, rather than direct simulation of group efficiency. The results suggest that enabling exclusion mechanisms can improve group outcomes compared to no-punishment baselines, especially under the right parameter conditions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical support that enabling exclusion-based punishment in public goods games can increase the prevalence of cooperation and, by implication, group efficiency, especially when exclusion is effective and/or costs are not too high. The results are parameterized by group size, exclusion cost, and exclusion probability, and show that pool exclusion is more robust in large groups or at high costs. While the paper does not report efficiency as a direct outcome, the dominance of excluders and cooperators over defectors implies higher group payoff. For prediction tasks, the findings suggest that the effect of punishment on efficiency will be more positive when exclusion is effective, costs are moderate, and group size is not too large for peer exclusion (but pool exclusion can handle larger groups). However, the lack of direct efficiency reporting and the theoretical nature of the results mean that quantitative predictions should be made with caution.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Group size N is a key parameter, varied in the analysis (e.g., N=5 in figures, but general results for N). Larger group size makes exclusion harder, especially for peer exclusion.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Enhancement factor r is varied; higher r (and thus higher MPCR) makes it easier for excluders to prevail and for cooperation to be sustained.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Exclusion cost (c_E for peer, c_F for pool) is a central parameter. Low cost favors peer exclusion; high cost favors pool exclusion. High costs can undermine both.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Exclusion probability (beta_E, beta_F) is varied; higher effectiveness (higher beta) makes exclusion more successful and supports excluder dominance.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is entirely theoretical and does not report direct measures of group efficiency or total payoff as a ratio to the fully cooperative outcome.
- Outcomes are mainly about strategy prevalence (excluder, cooperator, defector) and fixation probabilities, not direct group efficiency.
- No experimental or empirical data are provided.
- The model assumes perfect detection of defectors and does not consider errors in identifying free riders.
- No consideration of communication, reward, or other institutional features beyond exclusion.
- The evolutionary model does not specify a finite number of rounds as in laboratory experiments.
