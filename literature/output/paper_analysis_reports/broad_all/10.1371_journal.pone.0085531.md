Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the final ratio of reformed to unreformable individuals (P/U), which is interpreted as a societal order parameter. The model also reports crime rates, punishment rates, and recidivism, but does not report group payoff, efficiency, or welfare in the sense of public goods games.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model demonstrates that neither punishment alone nor rehabilitation alone is optimal for reducing crime and recidivism. Instead, the best outcomes (highest ratio of reformed to unreformable individuals) are achieved by balancing punishment severity and rehabilitation resources, subject to a fixed total resource constraint. There is an optimal allocation of resources between punishment and rehabilitation, and both too little and too much of either can worsen outcomes. The model also shows that the duration and intensity of rehabilitation matter, with intermediate durations being most effective. These findings are robust across different initial conditions and parameter values. However, the model does not report efficiency or group payoff in the sense of public goods games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, adjacent evidence for the prediction task. It supports the idea that the effect of punishment on group-level outcomes (here, the ratio of reformed to unreformable individuals) depends on the balance between punishment and rehabilitation, and that both are needed for optimal outcomes. However, because the model is not a public goods game and does not report efficiency or group payoff, its direct predictive value for efficiency in PGG-like environments is limited. The main implication is that punishment's effect is non-monotonic and context-dependent, especially when resources are constrained and when rehabilitation is possible. The findings suggest that in analogous PGG settings, enabling punishment without considering complementary positive interventions or resource tradeoffs may not maximize efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The model is not a public goods game and does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare in the sense relevant to PGG environments.
- Outcomes are behavioral (reform vs. unreformable) and crime/recidivism rates, not payoff-based.
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- Assumes all-to-all interaction and homogeneous parameters; does not model heterogeneity in player characteristics or network structure.
- Resource constraint is exogenous and may not map directly to real-world or PGG-like settings.
- No communication, contribution framing, or payoff feedback as in standard PGGs.
