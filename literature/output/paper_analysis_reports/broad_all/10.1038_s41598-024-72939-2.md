# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Punishment is slower than cooperation or defection in online network games
Authors: Dewey, G., Ando, H., Ikesu, R., Brewer, T. F., Goto, R., & Nishi, A.
Journal: Scientific Reports
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- mean accumulated wealth at end of game
Non-payoff outcomes:
- decision time for cooperation, defection, and punishment
- frequency of cooperation, defection, and punishment
- mechanism-specific punishment frequencies
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are decision time and frequency of each action (cooperation, defection, punishment). Payoff-related outcomes (mean accumulated wealth) are reported as descriptive statistics, not as primary outcomes or in relation to punishment treatment effects.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Punishment decisions are consistently slower than cooperation or defection in repeated network public goods games. The frequency of punishment is low (~6-7%) and is not significantly affected by the imposition of time pressure. Time pressure reduces decision times but does not shift the frequency of punishment, cooperation, or defection. The study classifies punishment decisions by hypothesized mechanism (retaliation, negative reinforcement, inequality aversion, unclassified) and finds no effect of time pressure on the frequency of any mechanism. The paper does not analyze the effect of punishment on group efficiency or total payoff, and payoff outcomes are only reported as descriptive means.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides high-quality evidence on the behavioral frequency and timing of punishment in repeated network PGGs, but does not report or analyze efficiency or group payoff as a function of punishment. Therefore, it does not directly inform the prediction of treatment efficiency from game design dimensions plus control efficiency. It does, however, confirm that punishment is rare and not easily suppressed by time pressure in this design, which may be indirectly relevant for predicting the magnitude of any efficiency effect (i.e., if punishment is rare, its effect on efficiency may be limited).

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Mean group size was ~15 (range 8-25 per game), but group size was not experimentally varied for treatment effect estimation.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game session lasted 15 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players is described or allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution and punishment choices are all-or-nothing per round: each player chooses one of three options (cooperate, defect, punish) and applies it to all connected players.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Cooperation: pay 50 points per neighbor, each neighbor gains 100 points. MPCR = 100/50 = 2 per neighbor, but since each player can have multiple neighbors, the effective MPCR per group is not standard; the cost-benefit ratio is 0.5 as described.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment: pay 50 points per neighbor, each neighbor loses 100 points.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: 1 unit cost inflicts 2 units loss per neighbor (50:100).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game lasts 15 rounds (practice rounds are separate).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, players are shown the decisions made by connected players and updated in-game points for themselves and their neighbors.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not analyze or report the effect of punishment on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare; payoff outcomes are only reported as descriptive means.
- Punishment is indiscriminate (applies to all neighbors) and unconditional, which differs from many real-world and experimental PGGs where punishment can be targeted.
- No reward or positive sanctioning mechanism is present, so the findings may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
- The use of MTurk participants and online implementation may limit generalizability.
- The payoff structure (punishment cost and effectiveness) is fixed and may not reflect real-world costs or incentives.
- No communication or chat is allowed, which may affect cooperation and punishment dynamics.
- The study does not measure or control for cultural, emotional, or psychological factors that may influence punishment behavior.
