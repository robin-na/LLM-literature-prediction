# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Punish in public
Authors: Xiao, E., & Houser, D.
Journal: Journal of Public Economics
Year: 2011

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- mean group contribution
- proportion of full cooperation (100% contribution)
- proportion of perfect free-riding (0% contribution)
- conditional cooperation (responsiveness to others' contributions)
- frequency and cost of punishment
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group and individual contribution rates, not efficiency or total group payoff. The paper does not report group efficiency or total earnings as a primary outcome, but contribution rates are closely related in this context.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Privately-implemented weak punishment reduces cooperation compared to a baseline with no punishment, while publicly-implemented punishment (where all group members are informed of punishment events) increases and sustains cooperation at higher levels than both baseline and private punishment. The effect is not due to shame, signaling, or differences in information about contributions, as all treatments provide full information about group contributions and maintain anonymity. The public punishment mechanism makes the cooperation norm more salient, leading to higher voluntary and conditional cooperation. The study does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, but the higher contribution rates in the public punishment treatment suggest that public punishment can reverse the detrimental effects of weak punishment on group outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the public observability of punishment (even when the punished individual's identity is anonymous) is a key moderator of punishment's effect on cooperation in public goods games. When predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency, the design dimension of whether punishment is public or private is critical: private, weak punishment can reduce cooperation below baseline, while public punishment can increase it above baseline. Although the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, the close mapping between contribution rates and group welfare in this linear PGG means that higher contributions in the public punishment treatment likely translate to higher efficiency. The findings are most applicable to settings with small groups, fixed partners, linear PGG structure, and exogenous, weak punishment. The results are less informative for settings with endogenous punishment, strong sanctions, or different information structures.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in fixed groups of 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments have exactly 30 rounds, known in advance.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous from 0 to 10 E$ in integer steps.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (mpcr) is 0.5 (each E$ contributed yields 0.5 E$ per player in a group of 4).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is exogenous and costless to other group members; the punished player loses a percentage of their payoff, but no one else pays or receives anything.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is a deduction from the lowest contributor's payoff, proportional to the difference between their contribution and the group average, with a maximum of 10% of round earnings.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is present in any treatment.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the exact total number of rounds (30) in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, all subjects see the contributions of all group members (anonymized, no IDs), their own earnings, and the difference between their contribution and the group average.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the public punishment treatment, all group members are informed when punishment occurs, but the identity of the punished individual is not revealed (anonymity is maintained). In the private punishment treatment, only the punished individual is informed.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is contribution rate, not efficiency or group payoff; efficiency must be inferred from contribution rates and the linear payoff structure.
- Punishment is exogenous, weak, and costless to other group members; results may not generalize to endogenous or costly punishment settings.
- All groups are small (n=4), fixed, and play for 30 known rounds; findings may not generalize to larger groups, random rematching, or indefinite time horizons.
- No reward mechanism is present; results do not address interactions between punishment and reward.
- The public punishment treatment reveals only that punishment occurred, not the identity of the punished individual; effects of fully identified punishment are not tested.
- The study is conducted in a laboratory setting with student subjects, which may limit external validity.
