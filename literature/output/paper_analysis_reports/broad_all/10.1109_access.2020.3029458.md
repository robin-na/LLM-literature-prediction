Outcomes are behavioral (strategy adoption, legal compliance, monitoring) rather than explicit group payoffs, efficiency, or welfare. There is no direct calculation or reporting of group efficiency or total payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that without dynamic penalty-incentive (punishment/reward) mechanisms, the system does not reach a stable equilibrium and fluctuates. Introducing dynamic penalty-incentive measures leads to a stable equilibrium where platforms monitor and car owners comply with regulations, even with minimal government supervision. The speed of convergence to this stable state depends on the magnitude of penalties, rewards, and initial conditions. The model demonstrates that appropriate punishment and reward levels are necessary for stability and legal compliance, but does not quantify efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, theoretical support for the idea that dynamic punishment and reward mechanisms can stabilize cooperation and compliance in multi-agent regulatory environments, similar to public goods games. However, it does not provide quantitative predictions of efficiency or group payoff, nor does it report empirical data. Its main value for prediction is in supporting the qualitative claim that punishment (and reward) can suppress fluctuations and promote stable compliance, but the lack of efficiency or payoff outcomes limits its direct applicability to predicting efficiency changes in PGG-like experiments.
