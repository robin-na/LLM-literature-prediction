The main outcomes are the equilibrium frequencies of cooperation and charity. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. Payoff is used in the model and for individual-level updating, but not as a reported group-level outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that charity (modeled as payoff transfer from egalitarians to the lowest-payoff neighbor) does not enhance cooperation under random selection update rules, but does promote cooperation under threshold selection rules (where only the lowest-payoff individuals update their strategies). The effect of charity is measured by the frequency of cooperation, not by group payoff or efficiency. The model shows that the presence of charity can lower the threshold for the multiplication factor (r) needed to sustain cooperation under threshold selection. The frequency of charity is positively correlated with the variance of payoffs in the population. The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is not directly informative for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it does not include punishment or report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. However, it provides indirect evidence about the effect of a redistribution mechanism (charity) on cooperation rates under different update rules. The findings suggest that the effect of a second-stage redistribution mechanism (distinct from punishment) on cooperation depends on the selection dynamics, but the lack of efficiency or payoff reporting limits its direct utility for the prediction task. The game structure and update rules are described in detail, which may be useful for mapping to similar simulation studies.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each local PGG group consists of 5 players (focal player plus 4 neighbors on a square lattice). The total population is a lattice of size L=50 (2500 players), but the local group size is always 5.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for more than 10^5 generations, with equilibrium frequencies averaged over 10^3 generations. The number of rounds is very large, representing evolutionary steady state.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: players are either altruists (contribute c) or selfish (contribute 0).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is r/n, where r is the multiplication factor (varied in simulations) and n=5. r is varied, typically 1 < r <= 5.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not include punishment or sanction mechanisms; charity is modeled as a distinct, non-punitive redistribution.
- No efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes are reported; only behavioral frequencies (cooperation, charity) are analyzed.
- Findings are based on simulation of evolutionary dynamics, not empirical or experimental data.
- The model uses a spatial lattice structure with local interactions, which may not generalize to well-mixed or other network structures.
- The results are specific to the modeled update rules (random selection, threshold selection) and may not generalize to other dynamics.
