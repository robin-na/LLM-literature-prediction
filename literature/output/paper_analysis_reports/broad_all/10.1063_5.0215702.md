## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that punishment can promote cooperation in a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma with reinforcement learning agents, but only if the punishment is sufficiently strong and not too costly. If the cost of punishment is high or the punishment intensity is low, introducing punishment can actually reduce cooperation below the baseline with no punishment. The system can exhibit both continuous and discontinuous transitions in cooperation as punishment parameters are varied. The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators; group efficiency or total payoff is not reported. The findings are based on agent-based simulations, not empirical data.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, mechanistic evidence about how punishment parameters (cost, intensity, probability) affect cooperation rates in a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma with reinforcement learning. While the outcome is not efficiency or group payoff, the results suggest that punishment does not always improve group outcomes: if punishment is too costly or too weak, it can reduce cooperation. For prediction tasks, this implies that the effect of punishment on efficiency may be non-monotonic and sensitive to cost-effectiveness. However, because efficiency or group payoff is not reported, the findings should be used as qualitative, mechanistic guidance rather than quantitative prediction.

- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for at least 10^7 steps (rounds), then average over 500 steps. No explicit finite horizon; effectively infinite/very long repeated game.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate (C) or defect (D).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost per act (c) is a key parameter; higher cost reduces cooperation, and at high cost, punishment can reduce cooperation below baseline.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment intensity (p) is manipulated; higher intensity increases cooperation, but only if cost is not too high.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare; only cooperation rate (fraction of cooperators) is analyzed.
- The game is a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- No empirical or experimental data; all findings are theoretical and mechanistic.
- No analysis of payoff-based outcomes such as group efficiency, surplus, or total earnings.
- No communication, reward, or information-sharing mechanisms are modeled.
