Outcome notes:
The main reported outcome is the stationary density of cooperators (\rho_C), i.e., the fraction of cooperators in the population. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. Reputation distribution is also analyzed, but not payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that embedding a reputation effect into the utility function of agents in a spatial PGG model leads to a substantial increase in the stationary fraction of cooperators, especially as the reputation factor increases. The critical synergy factor required for cooperation to emerge is reduced, and the region of parameter space supporting full cooperation is expanded. The model does not include explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanisms, and does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. The findings are based on Monte Carlo simulations and are relevant for understanding how reputation mechanisms can promote cooperation, but do not directly inform predictions about efficiency or payoff effects of punishment in PGG-like environments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that reputation mechanisms can promote cooperation in spatial PGGs, but does not address punishment, sanctions, or efficiency outcomes. For the downstream prediction task—predicting efficiency effects of punishment—this paper is only weakly informative. It suggests that non-monetary social mechanisms (like reputation) can increase cooperation, but does not provide data or analysis on how punishment affects efficiency, nor does it report group payoff or welfare. The game design dimensions are described in detail for the reputation mechanism, but not for punishment or efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a spatial lattice of size L^2, typically 200x200 (40,000 players), with other sizes (300x300, 400x400) tested for robustness. The number of players is fixed per simulation.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 50,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with results averaged over the last 5,000 steps. The number of rounds is fixed and known.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: each player is either a cooperator (contributes 1) or a defector (contributes 0) in each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The synergy factor r is varied; MPCR is r/(k+1), where k is the number of neighbors (typically 4 for a square lattice). r is systematically manipulated in simulations.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of Monte Carlo steps (rounds) is fixed and known to the experimenter; as a simulation, all parameters are explicit.

## Important Limitations
- The model does not include any explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism; all effects are due to reputation-based utility modification.
- No efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or total earnings outcomes are reported; only cooperation rate and reputation distribution are analyzed.
- Findings are based on simulation, not empirical or experimental data.
- The relevance to the prediction of punishment effects on efficiency is only indirect, as the model studies reputation rather than punishment.
- The model assumes binary (all-or-nothing) contribution choices and spatial lattice structure, which may limit generalizability to other PGG variants.
