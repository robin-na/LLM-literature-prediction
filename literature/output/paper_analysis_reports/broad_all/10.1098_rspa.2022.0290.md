Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the evolution and stability of cooperation (i.e., the fraction of cooperators) in repeated public goods games with indirect exclusion. Payoff functions are derived and used to determine evolutionary dynamics, but group efficiency or total payoff is not directly reported or analyzed as an outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that indirect exclusion—where a game organizer probabilistically selects cooperators for future participation—can promote the stable coexistence of cooperators and defectors or even lead to the dominance of cooperation in repeated public goods games. The effectiveness of indirect exclusion increases with higher identification probability and more repeated rounds. The model avoids the second-order free-rider problem associated with costly direct exclusion. However, the main reported outcomes are the equilibrium fraction of cooperators and the stationary distribution of strategies, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that indirect exclusion mechanisms can increase the equilibrium level of cooperation in repeated public goods games, especially as the number of rounds and the probability of correctly identifying cooperators increase. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, it does not directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment (or exclusion) on efficiency. The results are most relevant for predicting changes in cooperation rates rather than efficiency, and the exclusion mechanism studied is costless and indirect, differing from standard costly punishment treatments.
