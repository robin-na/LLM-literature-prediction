# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: When sanctions fail to increase cooperation in social dilemmas: Considering the presence of an alternative option to defect
Authors: Mulder, L. B., van Dijk, E., De Cremer, D., & Wilke, H. A. M.
Journal: Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin
Year: 2006

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- collective outcomes (group payoff, total earnings, welfare, surplus)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- number of chips allocated to self (defection)
- number of chips allocated to yellow pool (cooperation)
- number of chips allocated to blue pool (alternative defection)
- expectations of cooperation
- eventual expectations of cooperation
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is 'collective outcomes', which is a direct measure of group payoff/welfare. Behavioral outcomes (contribution, defection, alternative defection) and expectations are also reported and analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Across two lab experiments, the authors find that introducing a sanctioning system (punishment for defection) increases collective outcomes (efficiency) in a standard two-option social dilemma (cooperate or defect). However, when a third option to defect (alternative defection, more harmful to the group) is available, sanctions do not increase efficiency and can even decrease it, particularly when participants have low expectations of others' cooperation. The negative effect is mediated by the failure of sanctions to increase trust or expectations of cooperation in the presence of the third option. Thus, the effectiveness of punishment in increasing efficiency is contingent on the decision structure and social expectations.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-like games is not uniform: it is positive in standard two-choice settings but can be null or negative when alternative, more harmful defection options are available and when group members have low expectations of cooperation. For prediction, this means that the presence of alternative defection options (i.e., more than two choices, with at least one being more harmful to the group) and low trust/expectations are critical moderators. If the control game (no punishment) has low efficiency and the game allows for alternative defection, enabling punishment may not improve and could even worsen efficiency, especially if group trust is low. In standard two-choice games, punishment is likely to increase efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 participants in all conditions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Single-shot (one-shot) allocation decision; not repeated rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication between participants was explicitly prohibited.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants could allocate any number of 10 chips among available options (continuous allocation, not all-or-nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR for yellow pool (public good) is 0.5 (Study 1: 0.5/4=0.125 per capita; Study 2: 0.2/4=0.05 per capita).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sanction is a fixed fine per chip allocated to self (Study 1: DFL 0.45; Study 2: EUR 0.25). No cost to other players; punishment is automatic, not peer-administered.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is a fixed deduction per chip allocated to self (Study 1: DFL 0.45; Study 2: EUR 0.25). Effectiveness is direct and certain.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No explicit reward mechanism; only punishment (sanction) is present.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Single-shot game; participants know the structure in advance.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is automatic and impersonal (system-administered); no identification of who punished whom.

## Important Limitations
- The experiments are single-shot (not repeated), so findings may not generalize to repeated public goods games.
- The alternative defection option (blue pool) is not sanctioned, whereas in real life such options may sometimes be sanctioned.
- The laboratory setting may not capture all complexities of real-world dilemmas (e.g., garbage reduction, tax evasion).
- The effect of the harmfulness of the alternative defection option is not systematically varied.
- All participants are university students, which may limit generalizability.
