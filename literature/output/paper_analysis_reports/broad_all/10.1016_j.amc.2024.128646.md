## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a spatial PGG with pre-play commitment, both institutional punishment (of commitment non-compliance) and reward (of commitment compliance) can promote cooperation. Punishment is most effective at high commitment thresholds and with strong punishment, while reward can sustain cooperation at any threshold if the reward is strong enough. Intermediate thresholds require less severe punishment to sustain cooperation. The model does not report efficiency or group payoff, so findings are limited to cooperation rates and strategy dynamics.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task. It shows that punishment can increase cooperation rates in spatial PGGs with commitment, especially at high commitment thresholds and with strong punishment. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, it does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency outcomes. The results suggest that, in similar designs, enabling punishment is likely to increase cooperation rates, which may correlate with higher efficiency, but this is not directly tested or quantified in terms of payoff.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are reported.
- Outcomes are limited to cooperation rates and strategy frequencies; efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or total earnings are not reported.
- Punishment and reward are modeled as institutional and costless to individuals; real-world implementation costs are not considered.
- The model assumes homogeneous commitment thresholds and population structure; individual heterogeneity is not explored.
- No direct mapping from cooperation rate to efficiency is provided, so implications for efficiency must be inferred indirectly.
