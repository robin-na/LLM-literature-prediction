# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Consensual punishment does not promote cooperation in the six-person prisoner's dilemma game with noisy public monitoring
Authors: van Miltenburg, N., Przepiorka, W., & Buskens, V.
Journal: Plos One
Year: 2017

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- earnings
- group payoff
- predicted period earnings (excluding punishment endowment)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- probability to cooperate
- probability to punish observed defector
- punishment behavior
Outcome notes:
The paper reports both payoff-related outcomes (earnings, group payoff) and non-payoff behavioral outcomes (cooperation rate, punishment behavior). Efficiency is operationalized as earnings relative to the maximum possible group payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment compares three punishment institutions (individual decision rule, CDR2, CDR3) in a six-person prisoner's dilemma, with and without 20% noise in public monitoring of contributions. In the absence of noise, all punishment institutions increase cooperation and earnings relative to the baseline, with collective decision rules (especially CDR3) performing best. With noise, however, all punishment institutions result in lower earnings than the baseline (no-punishment) condition. The individual decision rule (IDR) produces slightly higher cooperation than collective rules under noise, but still does not improve earnings. The main reason is that noise leads to both mistaken punishment of cooperators and missed punishment of defectors, so resources are wasted and cooperation is not effectively enforced. Thus, punishment does not promote efficiency in noisy environments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a six-person all-or-nothing prisoner's dilemma with 20% noise in public monitoring, enabling punishment (regardless of whether it is individual or requires consensus) reduces group efficiency compared to the control (no-punishment) condition. The effect is robust across different punishment decision rules. The findings suggest that, when noise is present, the predicted efficiency with punishment enabled should be lower than the control efficiency. The results are directly relevant for settings with similar group size, binary contribution, and noisy monitoring, and less so for linear PGGs or settings with perfect information.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in groups of 6 (six-person PD).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consists of three sequences of 15 rounds (45 rounds total), with the first 15 as baseline and the next 30 with punishment (two different rules).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: each player chooses to contribute all (20 points) or nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is 2.4/6 = 0.4 (endowment w=20, multiplier m=2.4, n=6).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 2 points per punishment assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment costs 2 points to punisher and reduces target's payoff by 6 points (1:3 cost-to-impact ratio).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the number of rounds in each sequence (15 rounds per sequence).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, subjects see others' (noisy) contribution decisions and implemented punishments received by each group member.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are not informed about who punished whom; punishment is anonymous.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a six-person binary (all-or-nothing) prisoner's dilemma, not a linear public goods game, so results may not generalize to continuous-contribution PGGs.
- Noise is implemented as 20% probability of misperceiving each other’s contribution, which may differ from other forms of noise or imperfect monitoring.
- Punishment is only possible, not reward; no positive incentives are tested.
- Groups are randomly rematched each round, so repeated-game effects are limited.
- The findings may not generalize to smaller or larger groups, different MPCRs, or different punishment cost/impact ratios.
- The study does not test the effect of communication or chat between players.
- The effect of punishment on efficiency is only measured in the short run (within-session), not over longer time horizons.
