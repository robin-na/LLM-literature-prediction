# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: To cooperate or not to cooperate? An analysis of cooperation and peer punishment among Syrian refugees, Germans, and Jordanians
Authors: El-Bialy, N., Aranda, E. F., Nicklisch, A., Saleh, L., & Voigt, S.
Journal: Journal of Economic Psychology
Year: 2022

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- punishment frequency
- in-group favoritism in cooperation
- punishment of defectors
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are cooperation rates (i.e., the proportion of participants choosing to cooperate in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma) and punishment choices (i.e., whether participants choose to punish their partner at a cost). No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings is reported, though the payoff structure is described.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that Syrian refugees in both Germany and Jordan are more likely to cooperate with other refugees than with host country nationals, indicating in-group favoritism in cooperation. However, punishment behavior (the likelihood of punishing a defector) does not differ significantly based on whether the target is an in-group or out-group member. Host country participants (Germans and Jordanians) do not show significant differences in cooperation or punishment based on the partner's group. The study does not analyze efficiency or group payoff, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined from this paper.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence on the behavioral effects of punishment opportunities in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma with a punishment stage, but does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. Therefore, it does not directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. However, it does show that the presence of punishment does not lead to group-differentiated sanctioning, and that cooperation rates are high overall, especially within in-groups. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper is only indirectly informative, as it does not provide the necessary payoff-based outcome data.

- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 40 points lost per 10 points spent (4:1 ratio).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game is one-shot (single round).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed of the other player's choice before the punishment stage; no mention of additional summary information.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a one-shot prisoner's dilemma with punishment, not a repeated public goods game, so findings may not generalize to multi-round or group settings.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings outcomes are reported; only behavioral outcomes (cooperation and punishment rates) are analyzed.
- The sample is limited to Syrian refugees, Germans, and Jordanians, which may limit generalizability.
- Punishment is only possible in a single post-decision stage, not over repeated interactions.
- The study does not manipulate the presence versus absence of punishment; all games include the punishment stage.
