Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the evolution of fairness in the spatial ultimatum game, measured by the average proposal and acceptance thresholds. No group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The coevolution of game strategy and link weight in a spatial ultimatum game model leads to higher average proposal and acceptance levels, interpreted as greater fairness. The key driver is the link weight boundary control parameter (\delta), which, when increased, allows for greater heterogeneity in social ties and promotes fairer strategies. The adjustment amplitude (\Delta) has a smaller or context-dependent effect. The model does not analyze or report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, so its direct relevance to efficiency prediction in public goods or similar games is limited.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-game-like environments. The model is an ultimatum game (not a PGG or direct variant), and the main outcomes are behavioral (fairness, proposal/acceptance levels) rather than payoff-based. The 'punishment' in this context is the weakening of social ties, not costly punishment of the type used in PGGs. Therefore, while the findings may be conceptually adjacent—suggesting that dynamic social relationships can promote fairer behavior—they do not inform quantitative predictions about efficiency or payoff outcomes in PGGs with or without punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a 100x100 lattice (10,000 players), each with 4 (von Neumann) or 8 (Moore) neighbors. Player count is fixed and large.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 100,000 generations (rounds), with results taken at steady state.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (offer) is continuous in [0,1], not all-or-nothing.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Link weight can be increased (reward) or decreased (punishment) based on payoff relative to average utility.

## Important Limitations
- The model is an ultimatum game, not a public goods game or direct variant, so findings may not transfer directly to PGG-like environments.
- No efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes are reported; only behavioral measures of fairness (average proposal and acceptance levels) are analyzed.
- The 'punishment' mechanism is not costly punishment as in PGGs, but rather the weakening of social ties, which may not have the same strategic or payoff implications.
- All results are from Monte Carlo simulations of a theoretical model; no empirical or experimental data are presented.
- The model assumes large, regular networks and synchronous updating, which may not generalize to smaller groups or different network structures.
