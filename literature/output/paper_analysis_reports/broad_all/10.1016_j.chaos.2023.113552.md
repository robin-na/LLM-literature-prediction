## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in voluntary public goods games with shared-punishment, the introduction and increase of punishment fines (beta) promote cooperation and increase average payoffs, especially when the punishment cost (alpha) is not too high. In well-mixed populations, mild punishment is insufficient to sustain cooperation, but as the fine increases, cooperation becomes stable and can dominate, leading to higher efficiency. In structured populations, cooperation can emerge and coexist with defectors and loners under milder punishment than in well-mixed populations, but the threshold for full cooperation is higher. High punishment costs discourage participation and can lead to non-participation equilibria. The model provides phase diagrams and equilibrium analyses that directly relate game design parameters to efficiency outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct theoretical evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games with voluntary participation and shared-punishment. It shows that higher punishment fines (beta) generally increase efficiency by promoting cooperation, but higher punishment costs (alpha) can reduce efficiency by discouraging participation. The model explicitly links player count (N), marginal per-capita return (r/N), punishment cost (alpha), and punishment effectiveness (beta) to equilibrium payoffs and efficiency. The results are relevant for both well-mixed and structured populations, with clear phase boundaries and thresholds. The findings can be used to inform predictions of treatment efficiency from game design dimensions and control efficiency, especially in settings with voluntary participation and institutional/shared punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Player count (N) is a key parameter in the model and is varied in phase diagrams. Larger N can change the phase boundaries for cooperation and efficiency.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is a fixed amount (c=1) or zero; thus, the contribution choice is all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. mpcr is r/N, where r is the multiplication factor. Higher r (and thus higher mpcr) increases the likelihood of cooperation and efficiency.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost (alpha) is varied. Higher alpha reduces participation and efficiency.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the fine (beta) per unit cost. Higher beta increases cooperation and efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The model assumes all-or-nothing contributions and does not consider continuous contribution choices.
- Punishment is institutional and shared; individual-level punishment or antisocial punishment is not modeled.
- Population structure is limited to regular networks; heterogeneous or real-world social networks are not analyzed.
- Second-order free-riding (cooperators who do not punish) is not included in the main model.
