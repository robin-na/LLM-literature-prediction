# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Asymmetric interaction and indeterminate fitness correlation between cooperative partners in the fig-fig wasp mutualism
Authors: Wang, R. W., Sun, B. F., Zheng, Q., Shi, L., & Zhu, L. X.
Journal: Journal of the Royal Society Interface
Year: 2011

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- fitness correlation between partners (viable seeds vs. wasp offspring)
- seed production
- wasp offspring (gall) production
- oviposition efficiency
- pollination efficiency
Non-payoff outcomes:
- interference competition intensity
- resource utilization (saturation/unsaturation)
- proportion of seeds/wasp offspring
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the fitness correlation between the two partners (fig and wasp), which is a proxy for mutual efficiency or payoff. The study does not use the language of 'efficiency' as in public goods games, but the outcomes are closely related to group-level payoffs and resource division.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that asymmetric interference competition among cooperative actors (wasps) can stabilize cooperation by keeping resource use unsaturated, leading to positive fitness correlations (mutual benefit) between partners. When interference competition is prevented (sequential entry), or when wasp oviposition efficiency is high (e.g., in cold season), the system can shift to negative fitness correlation (conflict), indicating overexploitation and reduced group efficiency. The relationship between cooperation and efficiency is not fixed but varies stochastically with environmental and experimental factors. The paper also discusses the role of discriminative sanctions (host reducing egg deposition efficiency of less-cooperative wasps) and rewards in maintaining cooperation, but these are not directly manipulated as in PGG punishment/reward treatments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect but informative evidence for the prediction task. It shows that mechanisms analogous to punishment (sanctions by the host) and interference competition among cooperators can maintain or undermine group efficiency, depending on ecological context and the structure of interaction. The findings suggest that the effect of punishment-like mechanisms on efficiency is highly sensitive to the details of resource competition, the number of actors, and environmental conditions. However, the system studied is a biological mutualism, not a PGG or direct human social dilemma, and the 'punishment' is not implemented as a discrete, costly action by peers but as host-imposed sanctions. Thus, while the results are adjacent and informative, they should be transferred to PGG predictions with caution, especially regarding the direct effect of enabling punishment on efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Number of foundresses (wasps) per fruit is directly manipulated (2, 5, 7, 9, 15, 20, up to 30+ in nature). Higher player count increases interference competition, which can stabilize cooperation, but also increases potential for conflict if resource use saturates.
- reward availability: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward to cooperative wasps by host is discussed as a mechanism, but not directly manipulated or measured.

## Important Limitations
- The system studied is a biological mutualism, not a human public goods game; the actors are not making conscious choices.
- Punishment and reward are not implemented as discrete, costly, peer-administered actions, but as host-imposed sanctions or rewards, limiting direct transferability to PGG settings.
- No explicit measurement of group efficiency as a ratio to maximum possible payoff; fitness correlation is used as a proxy.
- No repeated rounds or temporal structure analogous to PGG experiments.
- Environmental and ecological factors (e.g., temperature, humidity, resource size) strongly moderate outcomes, making generalization to controlled PGG settings difficult.
- No direct manipulation of punishment or reward parameters as in PGG experiments.
