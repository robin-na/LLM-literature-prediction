## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that, in structured populations modeled as a square lattice, indirect punishment (punishing defectors who harm one's neighbors) can outperform direct punishment (punishing one's own defectors) in promoting cooperation, but only when both the cost and fine of punishment are low. Outside this narrow parameter region, direct punishment may be more effective, both may be equally effective, or neither may improve cooperation. The mechanism is that indirect punishment can impose more total fines on defectors without greatly increasing the cost to punishers, but only when punishment is cheap. The main reported outcomes are cooperation rates and the distribution of strategies; group efficiency or total payoff is not directly measured.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about how the cost and scope of punishment affect cooperation rates in spatial social dilemmas. While it does not report efficiency or group payoff, it suggests that indirect punishment can be more effective at promoting cooperation than direct punishment under low-cost, low-fine conditions, but not otherwise. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, the results are only adjacent, as the main outcomes are behavioral. The findings may inform expectations about when punishment is likely to increase efficiency, but direct quantitative mapping is not possible from this paper alone.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use square lattices with N ranging from 100^2 to 400^2 (i.e., 10,000 to 160,000 players). The main results are not sensitive to player count within this range.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for over 100,000 time steps, with averages taken over the last 2,000 steps. There is no fixed number of rounds as in a finite repeated game; the system evolves until steady state.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate or defect in each interaction.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost (gamma) is a key parameter, varied systematically. Low cost enables indirect punishment to outperform direct punishment; high cost reverses this.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (fine per cost, beta/gamma) is varied. The relative effectiveness of punishment types depends on the cost-to-fine ratio.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- The game is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- Outcomes are reported in terms of cooperation rates and punishment frequencies, not group efficiency or total payoff.
- Indirect punishment is operationalized as punishing second-order neighbors, which differs from reputation-based or norm-based indirect punishment in human experiments.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency, welfare, or group payoff; only behavioral outcomes are analyzed.
- No communication, reward, or information-sharing mechanisms are present.
