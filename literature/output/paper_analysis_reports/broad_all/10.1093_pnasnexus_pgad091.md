# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The competitive advantage of sanctioning institutions revisited: A multilab replication
Authors: Lo Iacono, S., Przepiorka, W., Buskens, V., Corten, R., van Assen, M., & van de Rijt, A.
Journal: Pnas Nexus
Year: 2023

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average profit (earnings) in SI vs SFI
- group profit
- individual profit
- payoff before and after round 5/10
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution levels
- migration/institutional choice (SI vs SFI)
- punishment and reward behavior
- proportion of high contributors who punish
Outcome notes:
The primary preregistered outcomes are average profit (payoff) and average contribution in SI vs SFI. Non-payoff outcomes such as contribution and punishment are also reported, but efficiency/payoff is a main focus.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Across seven European labs, allowing participants to choose between a sanctioning institution (SI) and a sanctioning-free institution (SFI) in a repeated public goods game leads to the majority of participants migrating to SI. In five labs, SI groups achieve significantly higher average profits and contributions than SFI groups, replicating the original findings. In the other two labs, the effect is weaker but still in the same direction. The payoff advantage of SI emerges after several rounds, as peer punishment sustains cooperation. The results are robust to variations in lab, language, and data collection method, but all parameter settings (e.g., group size, MPCR, punishment cost) are held constant as in the original study.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a 12-player, 30-round linear public goods game with endogenous institution choice, MPCR of 0.133, and a peer punishment institution with a 1:3 cost-to-impact ratio, enabling punishment leads to higher group efficiency (average profit) compared to no-punishment control. The effect is robust across multiple European labs. The design dimensions are tightly controlled and fixed, so the findings are most directly applicable to similar parameterizations. The paper does not vary key design dimensions (e.g., punishment cost, group size), so it cannot inform how the effect would change under different settings, but it provides a strong baseline for the canonical design.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 12 players, fixed by design.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 30 rounds per session, fixed by design.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No chat or communication between participants; interaction is anonymous.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: participants choose how much (0-20 tokens) to contribute each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 1.6/12 = 0.133 (public good multiplier 1.6, 12 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 1 token per negative token assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each negative token assigned reduces the target's payoff by 3 tokens (1:3 cost-to-impact ratio).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Positive tokens (rewards) can be assigned in SI; each costs 1 token and increases the recipient's payoff by 1 token.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Rewarder pays 1 token per positive token assigned.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each positive token increases the recipient's payoff by 1 token (1:1 cost-to-impact ratio).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game has 30 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants see all group members' contributions, sanctions, rewards, and payoffs (anonymized, order randomized). They also see average results for SI and SFI before choosing institution in each round.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment/reward is anonymous; order of players is randomized each round, so identities cannot be tracked.

## Important Limitations
- All key game design parameters (group size, MPCR, punishment cost/impact, number of rounds) are held constant; the study does not test how varying these dimensions affects the efficiency impact of punishment.
- All labs are in Europe; generalizability to non-European or more culturally diverse samples is untested.
- The institution choice is limited to SI (peer sanctioning) vs SFI (no sanctioning); other institutional forms (e.g., centralized punishment, communication) are not tested.
- Punishment and reward are both available in SI, but the main effect is attributed to punishment; the separate effects of reward are not isolated.
- The study does not report efficiency as a percentage of the full-cooperation benchmark, but uses average profit as the main payoff outcome.
