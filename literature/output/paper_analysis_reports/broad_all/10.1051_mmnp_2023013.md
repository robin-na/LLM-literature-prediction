Payoff functions are defined and used to derive replicator dynamics, but the main reported outcomes are behavioral (strategy frequencies, disappearance of cheaters, etc.), not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model shows that high toxicity rates (especially against cheaters), high toxin levels, and smaller group sizes promote the disappearance of cheaters and the persistence of cooperation (cooperators and/or toxin producers). The system exhibits bistability: depending on initial conditions and parameters, the population may converge to all cooperators, coexistence of cooperators and toxin producers, or coexistence of cooperators and cheaters. The interior equilibrium (all three strategies coexisting) is always unstable. Bifurcation analysis reveals thresholds in absorption rates and toxin parameters that determine which strategies persist. The main reported outcomes are the frequencies of each strategy at equilibrium, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, mechanistic insight into how costly sanctioning (here, toxin production) can suppress free riding and promote cooperation in N-player games. However, the model is not a standard public goods game with explicit peer punishment, and does not report efficiency or group payoff as a main outcome. The findings suggest that increasing the effectiveness or prevalence of costly sanctions (analogous to punishment) can reduce free riding and increase the fraction of task-performers, especially in smaller groups and with higher sanction effectiveness. For prediction tasks, this paper supports the idea that punishment-like mechanisms can increase cooperation, but does not provide quantitative estimates of efficiency changes or direct mapping to standard PGG dimensions.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and not empirically validated.
- The game is not a standard public goods game with explicit, voluntary peer punishment; toxin production is a biological analog.
- Efficiency or group payoff is not directly reported as a main outcome; main outcomes are strategy frequencies.
- No explicit analysis of group welfare, surplus, or total earnings.
- No consideration of communication, reward, or information feedback mechanisms.
- Model assumes infinite population and continuous-time replicator dynamics, not finite repeated games.
