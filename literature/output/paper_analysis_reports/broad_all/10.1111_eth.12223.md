# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Variation in Cleaner Wrasse Cooperation and Cognition: Influence of the Developmental Environment?
Authors: Wismer, S., Pinto, A. I., Vail, A. L., Grutter, A. S., & Bshary, R.
Journal: Ethology
Year: 2014

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- proportion of cooperative (against-preference) choices
- success in image scoring (bystander) tasks
- learning to prefer ephemeral (visitor) over permanent (resident) options
- aggressiveness (mirror fights)
- boldness (latency to approach novel plate)
Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on behavioral measures of cooperation, learning, and cognition in cleaner wrasse, not on efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings. Outcomes are primarily the frequency of cooperative choices and task performance, not payoff-based.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Cleaner wrasse from continuous (socially complex) reefs performed better in laboratory cooperation and cognition tasks than those from patch (socially simple) reefs. Specifically, they were more likely to feed against their preference (cooperate), respond to image scoring (bystander) situations, and learn to prefer ephemeral (visitor) options in a biological market task. Juveniles from both environments performed similarly and better than adults from simple environments, suggesting ontogenetic (learning/experience) effects. No differences in aggressiveness or boldness were found between habitats. The study suggests that social environmental complexity shapes the development and expression of cooperative and cognitive behaviors, but does not directly address efficiency or payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. While it uses game-theoretic tasks and discusses mechanisms like punishment, partner switching, and image scoring, the outcomes are behavioral (cooperation, learning) rather than payoff-based. The findings may be indirectly informative for understanding how social environment complexity moderates the development of cooperative strategies, but do not quantify efficiency or group payoff. Thus, the paper offers only adjacent, indirect support for prediction tasks focused on efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Laboratory tasks are single-animal (cleaner wrasse) interacting with simulated clients (plates), not multi-player games. The social environment in the wild (number of clients, cleaners) is discussed as a contextual factor.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Number of trials per experiment is specified (e.g., 30 rounds for feeding against preference, up to 200 for biological market task), but these are not iterated group games.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Choice is discrete: select prawn (cheat) or flake (cooperate) per item; not a continuous contribution.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not use a public goods game or direct analog; tasks are inspired by game theory but are not multi-player or payoff-based.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings; outcomes are behavioral (cooperation, learning, cognition).
- Punishment and sanctions are discussed as mechanisms in the wild but are not implemented or manipulated in the laboratory experiments.
- Findings are correlative regarding the effect of social environment complexity; causality is not established.
- Results may not generalize to human or canonical PGG-like environments due to species, context, and task differences.
