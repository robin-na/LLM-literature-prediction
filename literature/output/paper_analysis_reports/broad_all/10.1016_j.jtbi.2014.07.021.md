The focus is on the alleviation of first- and second-order social dilemmas, interpreted through the persistence of cooperation and punishment strategies.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing selfish punishment with an avoiding mechanism into a spatial prisoner's dilemma game can alleviate both first-order and second-order social dilemmas, depending on the punishment cost, fine, and identification probability. The presence of selfish punishers allows for the stable coexistence of cooperators and punishers, and under certain parameter regimes, reduces the prevalence of defectors and second-order free riders. Theoretical predictions using extended pair approximation and interaction web analysis match well with simulation results. The main reported outcomes are the frequencies of each strategy type, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the idea that punishment (especially when including mechanisms for selfish punishment and avoidance) can promote cooperation and reduce free riding in spatially structured social dilemmas. However, because the model is a prisoner's dilemma (not a public goods game) and the main outcomes are strategy frequencies rather than group efficiency or total payoff, the findings are only adjacent to the prediction task. The results suggest that the effectiveness of punishment depends on the cost/fine ratio and the ability to identify defectors, but do not provide direct quantitative predictions for efficiency outcomes in public goods games. The paper is most useful for understanding mechanisms and qualitative moderators of punishment effects, rather than for direct efficiency prediction.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a square lattice of size L x L (e.g., L >= 200), with each player occupying a site. Each player interacts with its four nearest neighbors plus itself (self-interaction). The effective group size for each local game is 5.

- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are binary: cooperate or defect (or punish variants).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost (alpha) is a key parameter, varied in the analysis. Its effect on cooperation and punishment strategy frequencies is systematically explored.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is represented by the punishment fine (beta) and the ratio beta/alpha. The impact of varying this ratio is analyzed.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is targeted at non-cooperative neighbors, but the model includes an 'avoiding mechanism' where defectors may avoid being identified with probability 1-gamma. The identification probability (gamma) is a key parameter.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial evolutionary prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- Primary outcomes are strategy frequencies, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- No direct empirical or experimental data; all results are theoretical or simulation-based.
- No analysis of communication, reward, or other institutional features beyond punishment.
- The evolutionary process is indefinite and does not correspond to a fixed number of rounds as in most lab experiments.
