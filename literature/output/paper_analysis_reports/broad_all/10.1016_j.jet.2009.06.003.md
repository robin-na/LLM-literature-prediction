Non-payoff outcomes like cooperation rate are discussed as part of equilibrium construction, but the main focus is on payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that community enforcement—whereby third parties punish cheaters based on first-order information about partners' past play—can sustain cooperation and high efficiency in repeated prisoner's dilemma games with random matching. Under the independent and indifferent equilibrium concept, for sufficiently high discount factors, any symmetric group payoff between 0 (full defection) and 1 (full cooperation) can be achieved. The paper also shows that these results are robust to noise in actions or records, and that the equilibrium construction extends to finite populations. The threshold for sustaining cooperation does not depend on population size under this mechanism, in contrast to contagious equilibria. The paper provides explicit equilibrium constructions and analyzes the stability of cooperation under shocks and noise.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support that, in repeated prisoner's dilemma environments with random matching and first-order information, enabling community enforcement (punishment by third parties based on partners' past play) can increase group efficiency up to the full cooperation level, provided players are sufficiently patient. The results are robust to population size and some forms of noise. For the downstream prediction task, this suggests that, in analogous public-goods-game-like environments, enabling punishment mechanisms (even with limited information) can substantially increase efficiency relative to the control (no-punishment) condition, especially when the discount factor is high. The paper also clarifies that the effect of punishment on efficiency depends on the structure of the stage game (supermodular vs. submodular) and the information available to players.

- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is an infinitely repeated game (infinite rounds), but equilibrium constructions with bounded memory (records of length T) are also discussed.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Section 4.7 analyzes the effect of cheap talk (payoff-irrelevant messages) and shows that it does not enable new equilibria in the classes considered.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. The stage game is a binary-action prisoner's dilemma: each player chooses C (cooperate) or D (defect).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The prisoner's dilemma payoff matrix is parameterized by g and l, which determine the marginal returns to cooperation and defection. The structure is analogous to MPCR in PGGs.
- punishment cost: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Punishment is not modeled as a costly action; rather, it is implemented via equilibrium strategies (e.g., future partners defect against cheaters). There is no explicit cost parameter for punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Punishment is implemented as future partners' defection, which reduces the cheater's future payoffs. The effectiveness depends on the equilibrium and discount factor, not a direct parameter.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is infinitely repeated, so the horizon is known to be infinite.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players observe their current partner's entire record of past play (first-order information), but not aggregate summaries or payoffs of others.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental evidence.
- The model is based on the repeated prisoner's dilemma, which is adjacent but not identical to standard public goods games.
- Punishment is implemented via equilibrium strategies (future defection), not as an explicit costly action or institution.
- The main results assume infinite or very large populations and infinite repetition, which may not fully capture finite, short-horizon experimental settings.
- No explicit cost or technology parameter for punishment; the effect of punishment is endogenous to the equilibrium construction.
- No explicit reward mechanism is considered.
- The model assumes perfect monitoring of actions (via the history repository), except in the section on noise.
