## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper reviews decades of research on experimental games, including public goods games and social dilemmas. It highlights that cooperation is common even when game theory predicts defection, and that punishment and reward mechanisms can influence cooperation rates. Altruistic punishment is often found to encourage cooperation, but punishment can also have negative side effects such as reducing trust or inducing revenge. The review notes that the structure and framing of games, as well as social context, can significantly affect behavior. However, the paper does not provide direct evidence or quantitative findings on efficiency or group payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical and qualitative context for how punishment and reward mechanisms may affect cooperation in public goods and social dilemma games. It suggests that punishment can increase cooperation, but may also have negative side effects. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency given game design dimensions and control efficiency. Its value is mainly in contextualizing the importance of design features such as group size, framing, and the presence of punishment or reward, rather than providing quantitative prediction support.
