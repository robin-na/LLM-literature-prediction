# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Cooperation due to cultural norms, not individual reputation
Authors: Baum, W. M., Paciotti, B., Richerson, P., Lubell, M., & McElreath, R.
Journal: Behavioural Processes
Year: 2012

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- amount returned by trustee (trustworthiness)
- frequency of returning more/less than requested
- frequency of fine imposed
- amount sent by investor
- requested back-transfer
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the share of the tripled amount returned by the trustee (trustworthiness). The paper does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare as a primary outcome. Some discussion of surplus and shares is present, but not as a main outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that in a trust game with a conditional fine, trustees return more to investors from their own ethnic group when no fine is available. When a fine is available and imposed, trustworthiness increases in cross-ethnic pairs, eliminating the in-group advantage. In same-ethnic pairs, the fine has no effect or may crowd out trustworthiness. The effect is similar when the fine is available but not used. The study does not report group efficiency or total earnings as a main outcome, but the behavioral results suggest that punishment is more effective at increasing trustworthiness across ethnic lines than within them.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment on cooperation (measured as trustworthiness) depends on group identity: punishment increases trustworthiness in cross-ethnic pairs but not in same-ethnic pairs. However, because the outcome is not group efficiency or total payoff, the results should be used with caution for predicting efficiency. The design is a modified trust game, not a public goods game, so transferability to PGGs is limited but informative for settings where group identity and punishment interact. The punishment is costless to the punisher and has a fixed impact, which should be considered when mapping to other designs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game is played between two players: one investor and one trustee.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject makes several decisions, but each decision is a one-shot game; there is no repeated interaction with the same partner.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (amount sent and returned) is continuous in increments of 10 Afs, not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Amount sent by investor is tripled (multiplier = 3), but this is a trust game, not a PGG; so the marginal return is 3 for the trustee, 0 for the investor.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (fine) is costless to the punisher (investor).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Fine reduces trustee's payoff by 40 Afs if the amount returned is less than requested.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the structure of the experiment and that each decision is one-shot; no repeated game uncertainty.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the identity (ethnic group) of their partner, but not the specific individual. The punisher is always the investor, and this is known.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a modified trust game, not a public goods game, so results may not fully generalize to PGG settings.
- The main outcome is trustworthiness (amount returned), not group efficiency or total payoff.
- Punishment is costless to the punisher, which differs from most PGG punishment designs.
- All subjects are adult males from two ethnic groups in Afghanistan; results may not generalize to other populations or to women.
- No communication or repeated interaction; only one-shot decisions.
- No reward mechanism is present, so cannot compare punishment to reward.
- The study does not report group efficiency or welfare as a main outcome.
