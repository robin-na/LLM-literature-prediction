## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper clarifies that punishment is a key control mechanism in the evolution and maintenance of cooperation, especially in repeated game structures. It distinguishes punishment from other mechanisms (e.g., reward, sanctions, partner switching) and situates it within a broader taxonomy of strategies that can stabilize helping behavior. The authors emphasize that the effect of punishment on cooperation and efficiency depends on the game structure, ecological context, and cognitive abilities of the players. However, the paper does not provide quantitative findings or direct predictions about efficiency outcomes in public goods games or their variants.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides high-level conceptual guidance for predicting the effects of punishment in public-goods-game-like environments. It supports the idea that punishment can stabilize cooperation when certain conditions are met (e.g., repeated interactions, ability to inflict costs, cognitive capacity for contingent strategies), but it does not provide empirical effect sizes or direct evidence on efficiency outcomes. For prediction tasks, this paper is most useful for understanding which dimensions (e.g., repeated rounds, punishment cost, cognitive requirements) are theoretically important moderators of punishment's effect on cooperation and efficiency. It does not provide direct evidence for quantitative prediction of efficiency changes due to punishment.
