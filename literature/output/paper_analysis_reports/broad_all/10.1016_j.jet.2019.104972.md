## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper proves that in repeated random matching games where players are matched with multiple partners and can observe modal actions, any efficient (strictly individually rational) payoff can be supported as a strict, sequential equilibrium. This includes full cooperation in the prisoner's dilemma and similar games. The mechanism relies on community punishment: players who deviate (i.e., are non-modal) are sanctioned by others, and those who fail to sanction are also sanctioned. The equilibrium is robust to trembles and the presence of noisy players, and the efficiency loss due to noise is linear in the noise rate. However, the mechanism is not robust to observation errors unless these are rare relative to other noise. The informational burden is low: in the prisoner's dilemma, only one period of memory is needed to sustain cooperation. The results generalize to a wide class of games and show that community enforcement with punishment can achieve high efficiency under realistic informational constraints.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support that, in repeated random matching games with multiple partners per period and observable modal actions, enabling community punishment can raise efficiency to the full cooperative level, provided players are sufficiently patient and the population is large. The results are robust to some forms of noise, and the informational requirements are minimal. For prediction tasks, the presence of multiple matching, observable modal actions, and community punishment mechanisms should be expected to enable high efficiency, even in large groups. However, if observation errors are frequent or if players cannot observe modal actions, efficiency may not be sustained. The findings are most directly applicable to settings structurally similar to the model (random matching, multiple partners, observable actions, and community punishment), and less so to standard public goods games with fixed groups and no community enforcement.

## Important Limitations
- The results are theoretical and not empirically tested.
- The model assumes infinite repetition and large populations, which may not hold in all real-world settings.
- The mechanism is not robust to observation errors unless these are rare relative to other noise sources.
- The model requires players to be able to observe modal actions and partners' recent actions; if this information is unavailable, the results may not apply.
- The results are most directly applicable to random matching games with multiple partners per period, not to standard public goods games with fixed groups.
