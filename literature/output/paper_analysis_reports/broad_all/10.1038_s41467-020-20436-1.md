# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Spite is contagious in dynamic networks
Authors: Fulker, Z., Forber, P., Smead, R., & Riedl, C.
Journal: Nature Communications
Year: 2021

## Study Type
theory; empirical subtype=N/A; experimental subtype=N/A

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average payoff per agent
- average payoff per strategy type
- payoff differences between 'Spiteful' and 'Social' agents
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of 'Spiteful' vs 'Social' strategies
- spread/fixation of spiteful behavior
- correlated and anti-correlated interaction patterns
- network structure evolution
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the spread and fixation of spiteful behavior and the structure of interactions. Payoff is tracked and used as a mechanism for imitation, but group efficiency or total welfare is not the primary focus. Payoff outcomes are reported at the individual and strategy-type level, not as group efficiency relative to a cooperative optimum.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in dynamic networks where agents can choose interaction partners and update their strategies by imitating higher-payoff individuals, spiteful (costly harmful) behavior can reliably invade and fixate in the population. This occurs even when the overall network does not exhibit strong anti-correlation, due to local and transient anti-correlated interactions. The spread of spite is facilitated by reinforcement learning and imitation based on average payoff per interaction. When spiteful behavior dominates, average payoffs are lower than in populations of only 'Social' agents, indicating a reduction in group efficiency. The findings challenge classic conditions for the evolution of costly harm and suggest that dynamic partner choice is a powerful mechanism for the emergence of antisocial behavior.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is primarily theoretical and does not directly study public goods games or experimental punishment interventions. However, it provides indirect evidence that dynamic partner choice and imitation based on payoffs can enable the spread of costly harmful behaviors, which reduce group efficiency. In environments where agents can choose partners and update strategies based on payoffs, the introduction of punishment-like costly harm may decrease efficiency, especially if such behaviors can spread by imitation. The findings suggest caution in predicting positive efficiency effects from punishment in dynamic or endogenous network settings, as costly harm can become self-reinforcing and reduce welfare. The model is adjacent to PGGs and does not include a cooperative contribution stage, so direct quantitative prediction is limited, but the qualitative mechanism is relevant for understanding the risks of antisocial punishment or spite in flexible social environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Simulations are run with population sizes N = 10, 20, 30, 40, 50. The spread of spite is robust across these sizes (see Table 1, Fig. 2a).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 1 million time-steps (rounds). Number of rounds is not varied as a treatment, but long-run dynamics are studied.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents choose between two pure strategies: 'Social' (do nothing) or 'Spiteful' (pay cost to harm). No continuous contribution.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. The cost to the punisher ('Spiteful' agent) is parameter c, varied in simulations and analytic model. Higher cost makes spite less likely to spread.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. The harm inflicted per unit cost is parameterized by b/c ratio. Higher b/c makes spite more likely to spread and dominate, reducing efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- The game studied is the 'Prisoner's Delight', which is adjacent to but not a public goods game; there is no contribution stage or group benefit.
- Punishment is modeled as unconditional costly harm (spite), not as conditional punishment of norm violations or free-riding.
- Efficiency is not measured as group payoff relative to a cooperative optimum, but as average payoff per agent or per strategy type.
- No direct manipulation or analysis of classic public goods game dimensions such as MPCR, contribution levels, or group size effects on cooperation.
- No communication, reward, or identification mechanisms are modeled.
- Findings may not generalize quantitatively to standard PGGs with punishment, but provide qualitative insight into the risks of antisocial punishment in dynamic networks.
