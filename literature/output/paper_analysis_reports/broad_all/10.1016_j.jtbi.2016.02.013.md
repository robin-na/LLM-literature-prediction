## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that institutional tagging, even when imperfect, can substantially increase the efficiency (payoff) of cooperation in social dilemmas like the Prisoner's Dilemma. The key mechanism is that tagged cooperators are more likely to be chosen for interaction, leading to non-uniform interaction rates that favor cooperators. The model provides analytical conditions under which cooperation is favored by selection, is risk dominant, or is more abundant in the mutation-selection equilibrium. The positive effect on efficiency is robust to moderate costs of tag production and search. The effect is highly sensitive to the institution's error rates, especially the rate at which defectors are mistakenly tagged as cooperators. The model does not include explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanisms, but institutional tagging serves a similar function by enabling partner selection based on observed tags.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that institutional mechanisms which increase the observability of cooperation (even imperfectly) can increase group efficiency in repeated social dilemmas. While not a public goods game per se, the findings are adjacent and likely transferable to PGG-like settings where partner choice or reputation is possible. The model suggests that, in environments where institutions can credibly tag cooperators and individuals can use these tags to select partners, enabling such mechanisms will increase efficiency relative to a control with no tagging. The effect is stronger when the institution is more accurate (especially in avoiding false positives for defectors), when more of the population is screened, and when individuals can search more for partners. The results are less directly applicable to settings where punishment is the main intervention, but the underlying logic (institutional information enabling discrimination) is relevant for predicting efficiency effects of punishment-like or reputation-based interventions.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and not empirically tested.
- The setting is a repeated Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game, though the mechanism is adjacent.
- No explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism is modeled; institutional tagging is not the same as punishment.
- Partner choice is based on institutional tags, which may not be available or feasible in all real-world or experimental settings.
- The model assumes individuals trust the institution and use tags for partner selection.
- Costs of tag production and search are modeled simply; real-world costs and incentives may be more complex.
- The model assumes error rates in tagging are exogenous and constant over time.
