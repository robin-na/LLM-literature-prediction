# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Context Modularity of Human Altruism
Authors: Alexander, M., & Christia, F.
Journal: Science
Year: 2011

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=field_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- average contribution to public good
- amount of sanctions assigned
- effect of group diversity on contributions
- effect of institutional integration on contributions
- effect of sanctions on contributions
Outcome notes:
The primary dependent variable is the average contribution to the public good per round. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings, but does discuss how contributions relate to group welfare in theory.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that costly punishment (sanctions) increases contributions to the public good, but only in institutional contexts characterized by integration (i.e., integrated schools). In segregated institutional contexts, the availability of punishment does not increase contributions. Group diversity (ethnic heterogeneity) reduces contributions in segregated contexts but not in integrated ones. The study does not report efficiency or group payoff, but the results imply that the effect of punishment on efficiency would be positive in integrated contexts and negligible in segregated contexts, assuming higher contributions translate to higher group payoffs.

## Prediction Guidance
For the downstream prediction task, this paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency (proxied by contributions) is highly context-dependent: punishment increases contributions only when institutional integration is present. The game design dimensions most directly supported are player count (4), number of rounds (20), continuous contribution, no chat, and the presence/absence of punishment. However, the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, so predictions must infer from contribution rates. The findings suggest that, in similar field settings, enabling punishment will only improve efficiency if the institutional context supports integration among participants.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consisted of 4 players per round.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 rounds per session (after 5 practice rounds).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players was allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution was continuous from 0 to the endowment.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The sum of contributions is increased by 20% and divided equally among 4 players, so MPCR = 1.2/4 = 0.3.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is costly to the punisher, but the exact cost per unit is not specified in the main text.
- punishment technology: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Punishment reduces the payoff of the punished; the exact impact per unit cost is not specified.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players knew the number of rounds (20 after 5 practice rounds).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players received information on each other's contributions before the punishment stage.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Identities of group members were withheld except for ethnicity; punishment was anonymous.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings; all results are based on average contributions.
- The punishment cost and effectiveness parameters are not specified in detail.
- The findings are from a specific post-conflict field context (Mostar, Bosnia-Herzegovina) and may not generalize to other settings.
- The institutional context (integration vs. segregation) is a key moderator, but this dimension is not typically available in standard lab PGGs.
- No reward mechanism is studied.
