Outcome notes:
The paper reports on the densities (proportions) of different strategy types (cooperators, defectors, punishing cooperators, punishing defectors) as the main outcomes. These are behavioral outcomes, not direct measures of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare. There is some discussion of benefit and cost structures, but no explicit reporting of group efficiency or total payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model shows that increasing the maximal fine of punishment has a greater impact on defectors than on cooperators, but as the fine increases, the final density of cooperators decreases. The maximal cost of punishment mainly affects the prevalence of punishing strategies, with higher costs reducing the number of punishing cooperators. The initial density of cooperators has a counterintuitive effect: a higher initial density leads to a lower final density of cooperators, and vice versa. The results are reported in terms of the densities of strategy types, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff in public goods games. Its findings are limited to the prevalence of cooperation and punishment strategies under different parameter settings. While the model structure and parameterization (e.g., punishment cost, fine, network dynamics) are informative for understanding mechanisms, the lack of efficiency or payoff outcomes means this paper should not be used as a primary source for predicting efficiency effects of punishment. It may be useful for understanding how punishment parameters affect cooperation rates, but not for direct efficiency prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run with 1000 nodes (players) in the network.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run until the system reaches a stable state; the number of rounds is not fixed but is sufficient for convergence.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperators contribute 1, defectors contribute 0.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The multiplication factor r=4 is used; MPCR is r divided by group size, but group size varies with network degree.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Maximal cost of punishment (gamma) is a key parameter; higher cost reduces prevalence of punishing cooperators and increases punishing defectors.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Maximal fine of punishment (beta) is a key parameter; higher fine increases defectors and decreases cooperators.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or any direct payoff-based outcome; all results are in terms of behavioral strategy densities.
- The model is simulation-based and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The network is time-varying and activity-driven, which may limit generalizability to static or simpler network structures.
- No reward mechanism is considered; only punishment (prosocial and antisocial) is modeled.
- Findings about the effect of punishment parameters are limited to cooperation rates, not efficiency or welfare.
