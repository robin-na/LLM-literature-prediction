## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing a reputation-driven mechanism for adjusting local synergy factors in a spatial public goods game can significantly promote cooperation. The effect is strongest when the maximum possible synergy factor is high, the minimum is low, the adjustment step size is small, and the reputation threshold is moderate. Both excessively high and low reputation thresholds hinder cooperation. The model does not include explicit punishment or reward actions; instead, reputation indirectly affects payoffs by modulating the local multiplier. The main reported outcome is the proportion of cooperators, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is not directly informative for predicting the effect of explicit punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it does not include a punishment mechanism. However, it is relevant for understanding how endogenous, reputation-driven changes to the payoff structure (via the synergy factor) can affect cooperation rates. The findings suggest that indirect, reputation-based incentives can substitute for or complement explicit punishment/reward mechanisms in promoting cooperation, but the results are reported in terms of cooperation rates, not efficiency or group payoff. For the downstream prediction task, this paper provides only adjacent, indirect evidence.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a 100x100 grid (10,000 players), each player is part of 5 overlapping groups (self + 4 neighbors).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 2000x10^4 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with results shown at various time points.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperate (contribute c=1) or defect (contribute 0).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r is variable and locally adjusted; payoff is divided among group members. MPCR is r/N, with r dynamically changing based on reputation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is entirely simulation-based and does not use empirical or experimental data.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanism is present; reputation only indirectly affects payoffs via the synergy factor.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with explicit punishment, reward, or human subjects.
- No analysis of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes is reported.
