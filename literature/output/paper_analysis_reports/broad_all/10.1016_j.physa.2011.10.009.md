Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:

- total earnings
Non-payoff outcomes:

Outcome notes:
The paper reports both the fraction of cooperators (a behavioral outcome) and mean payoff/total earnings (a payoff-based outcome). The main efficiency-related analysis is the comparison of mean payoff under punishment and reward interventions.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a spatial PGG with influential individuals, both punishment and reward mechanisms (administered by influential individuals) can promote cooperation and increase mean payoff compared to the baseline with no such interventions. Rewarding cooperators is more effective than punishing defectors in terms of both promoting cooperation and increasing mean payoff, especially at low multiplication factors (low MPCR). The cost of punishment or reward is paid by society, not by the influential individuals. The presence of influential individuals as mere exemplars (always cooperating, no sanctioning power) actually reduces cooperation and does not improve efficiency. The model demonstrates that, for a given intervention cost, reward leads to higher group efficiency than punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct simulation evidence that, in a spatial PGG with influential individuals, enabling punishment increases group efficiency (mean payoff) compared to the baseline with no punishment, but enabling reward increases efficiency even more. The effect of punishment on efficiency is positive, but smaller than that of reward, for a given cost. The findings are most relevant for settings with a small fraction of influential individuals, low MPCR, and where the cost of punishment/reward is paid by society. The results support the prediction that introducing punishment will increase efficiency relative to control, but that reward may be an even more effective intervention. The model is theoretical/simulation-based, so quantitative predictions should be interpreted with caution.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a square lattice of size n x n, with n=100 (i.e., 10,000 individuals). Each game group consists of a focal player, its 4 von Neumann neighbors, and any influential individuals on those sites.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for thousands of time steps (e.g., 2500 or 35000 after steady state), but the model is evolutionary and does not have a fixed number of rounds as in standard repeated PGG experiments.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate (contribute 1) or defect (contribute 0).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Multiplication factor r is varied (e.g., r=1.2, r=1.5). MPCR is r divided by group size (g). Higher r (higher MPCR) increases cooperation and efficiency.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a fine Vp imposed on defectors; the cost is paid by society, not by the punisher. Vp is varied in simulations.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the fine Vp per punished defector; higher Vp increases cooperation and mean payoff up to a point.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward mechanism is present in Role III; influential individuals can reward cooperators.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward is implemented as a bonus Vr given to cooperators; the cost is paid by society. Vr is varied in simulations.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward effectiveness is the bonus Vr per rewarded cooperator; higher Vr increases cooperation and mean payoff.

## Important Limitations
- The study is based on simulation/theoretical modeling, not empirical or experimental data.
- The model assumes a spatial lattice structure and may not generalize to other network topologies or real-world groupings.
- Punishment and reward costs are paid by society, not by the punisher or rewarder, which differs from many experimental PGGs.
- The model uses evolutionary updating and does not have a fixed number of rounds as in standard repeated PGG experiments.
- The influential individuals are a small, exogenously controlled subset of the population, which may not reflect typical human group dynamics.
- No direct analysis of the effect of player count, communication, or information feedback on efficiency.
