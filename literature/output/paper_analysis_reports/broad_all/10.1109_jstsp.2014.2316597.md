## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that distributed rating-based sanctioning protocols can fully or nearly fully restore efficiency in repeated network sharing games with strategic agents, provided certain incentive compatibility conditions are met. When the network's connectivity is not too high and agents' discount factors are sufficient, the protocol achieves the social optimum (PoA=1). As network connectivity increases or agents become more impatient, efficiency declines but remains higher than with direct reciprocity mechanisms. The protocol is robust to imperfect monitoring and can be tuned for dynamic networks. Theoretical and simulation results show that the protocol outperforms Tit-for-Tat and similar direct punishment schemes, especially in heterogeneous or asymmetric networks.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and simulation-based evidence that distributed, rating-based punishment mechanisms can substantially increase efficiency in repeated networked cooperation environments, often achieving the social optimum. The effect is most pronounced when the network is not too highly connected and agents are sufficiently patient. The protocol's performance is robust to imperfect monitoring and network dynamics. For prediction tasks, the presence of a distributed, indirect punishment mechanism (ratings affecting future access) is a strong positive moderator of efficiency, especially compared to direct, bilateral punishment. The results are most directly applicable to networked repeated games with costly sharing, which are adjacent to but not identical to standard public goods games.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based; no human-subject experiments are reported.
- The setting is adjacent to, but not identical with, standard public goods games: agents interact over a network, and sharing is directed and pairwise rather than pooled.
- Punishment and reward are implemented via future access to resources (ratings), not direct costly punishment or reward actions.
- The protocol assumes agents are rational and forward-looking, and that the software can enforce the rating protocol.
- Some results depend on the network structure (e.g., high connectivity can reduce efficiency), and the protocol may not achieve optimality in all networks.
- The analysis assumes agents know their own utility functions and local connectivity, and that the protocol can be configured and updated as needed.
