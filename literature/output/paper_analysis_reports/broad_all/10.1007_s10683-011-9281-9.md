## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that, even in asymmetric settings where upstream players can exploit downstream players, groups often achieve significant but not optimal efficiency. Downstream players use indirect punishment (reducing investment) when they feel exploited, which can lower group earnings. Communication helps groups coordinate and can improve efficiency and equality. Larger groups (5 players) have more difficulty coordinating, leading to lower efficiency than smaller groups (2 players). The presence of asymmetry leads to persistent payoff inequality, but not necessarily to total collapse of cooperation. Efficiency improves over rounds as groups learn to coordinate, especially when earnings are distributed more equally.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence on how group size, communication, and asymmetry affect efficiency in public-good-like games with indirect punishment. While there is no explicit costly punishment mechanism (e.g., direct deduction of others' payoffs at a cost), the design allows for indirect sanctions via reduced investment. The findings suggest that efficiency is higher when coordination and equality are achieved, and that communication is important for this. Larger groups face more coordination challenges and achieve lower efficiency. The results are most relevant for predicting efficiency in asymmetric, sequential-access public goods games with communication and indirect punishment, but less so for standard PGGs with direct costly punishment. Control efficiency (without indirect punishment) is not directly reported, but the baseline is the Nash equilibrium of zero investment (zero efficiency), which is rarely observed. The results support the idea that indirect punishment can reduce efficiency if not balanced by fairness and communication.

## Important Limitations
- No direct costly punishment mechanism; only indirect punishment via reduced investment, which may not generalize to settings with explicit punishment options.
- The game is an asymmetric common-pool resource dilemma, not a standard public goods game; results may not transfer directly to symmetric PGGs.
- Nonlinear, thresholded production function complicates direct comparison to linear PGGs.
- All treatments allow communication, so effects of punishment in the absence of communication are not tested.
- Efficiency is measured relative to a cooperative optimum, but control (no-punishment) efficiency is not directly reported.
- Indirect punishment may have different psychological and strategic effects than explicit costly punishment.
