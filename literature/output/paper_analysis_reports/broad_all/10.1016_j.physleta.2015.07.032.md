Journal: Physics Letters A
Year: 2015

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- emergence and abundance of parochial altruism
- conditions for strategies to be favored by selection
- stationary distribution of strategies
- effect of group number and affiliation on parochial altruism
Outcome notes:
The paper is a theoretical evolutionary model analyzing when parochial altruism (cooperation with in-group, punishment of out-group) is favored by selection. Outcomes are about the abundance and evolutionary stability of strategies, not direct group efficiency or payoff. Payoff matrices are specified, but the main results are about strategy prevalence, not group welfare or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that parochial altruism is more likely to emerge and be favored by selection when intergroup punishment is more effective (higher fine-to-cost ratio), when there are more groups (higher assortment), and when mutation rates are low. Affiliation (belonging to more groups) makes parochial altruism less likely for low mutation, but more likely for high mutation. The model provides explicit conditions (in terms of benefit, cost, punishment fine, and punishment cost) for when parochial altruism is favored. However, the main outcomes are about the evolutionary abundance of strategies, not about group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the idea that punishment can promote the emergence of cooperation-like strategies in group-structured populations, especially when punishment is effective and group assortment is high. However, because the model is theoretical and focuses on evolutionary stability of strategies rather than direct efficiency or group payoff, it does not provide quantitative predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. The results suggest that punishment is more likely to be beneficial (in terms of promoting cooperation-like behavior) when punishment is effective and group structure is strong, but the translation to efficiency outcomes in experimental PGGs is indirect.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Population size N is a parameter in the model, but the main results are for large N (limit of large population size). The number of players per group is not fixed, as group size varies with number of groups and affiliation.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution and punishment choices are binary: cooperate/defect in PD, punish/not punish in punishment game.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost (alpha) and punishment fine (gamma) are explicit model parameters. Higher fine-to-cost ratio (gamma/alpha) makes parochial altruism more likely to emerge.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is gamma/alpha; higher effectiveness promotes parochial altruism.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental data.
- Outcomes are about evolutionary abundance of strategies, not direct group efficiency or total payoff.
- The game is a prisoner's dilemma for in-group and a punishment game for out-group, not a standard public goods game.
- No direct analysis of efficiency, welfare, or group payoff is provided.
- Mutation rate, group number, and affiliation are key moderators, but their real-world mapping to experimental PGGs is indirect.
- No consideration of communication, reward, or information feedback mechanisms.
