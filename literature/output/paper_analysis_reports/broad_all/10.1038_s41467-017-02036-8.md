## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The presence of skilled storytellers in Agta hunter-gatherer camps is positively associated with higher levels of cooperation, as measured by the proportion of resources given to others in a resource allocation game. Storytelling is proposed to function as a mechanism for broadcasting cooperative norms and coordinating group behavior. Skilled storytellers are preferred as social partners and have higher reproductive success. The study is observational and correlational, not experimental, and does not directly analyze efficiency or group payoff. The resource allocation game is not a standard public goods game, and there is no manipulation of punishment or sanctions, though stories often contain themes of norm enforcement and punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-game-like environments. The resource allocation game is structurally different from a PGG, and the main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency or group payoff. There is no experimental manipulation of punishment, nor are efficiency or welfare outcomes reported. The findings may be contextually informative for understanding the role of norm transmission and social mechanisms (like storytelling) in promoting cooperation, but they do not provide quantitative or causal evidence relevant to the prediction task as defined.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Camp sizes vary from 7 to 156 individuals, but the resource allocation game was played in camps with at least 8 adults. The number of players in each game is not fixed and varies by camp size.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The resource allocation game is a one-shot (single round) decision for each participant.
- chat: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Participants were briefed in private and asked not to discuss their decisions; the game was designed to minimize collusion and communication about choices.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Participants could allocate each token (representing rice) either to themselves or to a campmate, for each token separately. This is a discrete allocation per token, not a continuous slider or all-or-nothing for the whole endowment.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The game is a one-shot allocation; participants know the structure in advance.

## Important Limitations
- The study is observational and correlational, not experimental; causality cannot be established.
- The resource allocation game is not a public goods game and does not measure group efficiency or total group payoff.
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present or manipulated in the game.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate (proportion of resources given to others), not efficiency or welfare.
- Findings may not generalize to PGG-like environments with explicit punishment or efficiency outcomes.
- The association between storytelling and cooperation may be confounded by unmeasured variables (e.g., camp cohesion, familiarity).
