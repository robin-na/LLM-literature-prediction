# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: People punish defection, not failures to conform to the majority
Authors: Philippsen, A., Mieth, L., Buchner, A., & Bell, R.
Journal: Scientific Reports
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- final account balance
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- moral punishment rate
- antisocial punishment rate
- hypocritical punishment rate
- punishment bias
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: cooperation and types of punishment, estimated via a multinomial model. Final account balance is reported as a descriptive outcome, but not analyzed as a primary dependent variable. No explicit analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiments show that people are more likely to cooperate and to punish defectors (moral punishment) when the majority of partners are cooperative. Antisocial punishment (punishing cooperators) is much less common and does not increase when defection is the majority behavior, contradicting a pure conformity account. The presence of partner punishment (Experiment 1) increases cooperation rates above the base rate, while its absence (Experiment 2) leads to cooperation rates more closely matching the environment. The study does not directly analyze efficiency or group payoff, but the final account balances suggest that punishment and cooperation patterns affect individual earnings.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence about how punishment is used in dyadic Prisoner's Dilemma games, showing that punishment is primarily directed at defectors and is sensitive to the prevalence of cooperation. However, it does not directly report or analyze efficiency or group payoff, so its value for predicting efficiency in public goods games is limited. The design is adjacent to PGGs: it is a repeated, one-shot, dyadic PD with a costly punishment option, not a multi-player PGG. The punishment mechanism is well specified (costly, with a fixed cost-to-impact ratio). The findings suggest that enabling punishment increases cooperation, especially when the environment is cooperative, but the effect on efficiency is not directly measured. For prediction, this paper supports the idea that punishment can increase cooperation and likely efficiency in similar dyadic games, but caution is needed in extrapolating to multi-player PGGs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is dyadic (2 players per round), repeated with new partners each round.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 30 rounds per participant (5 training, 25 main trials).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players; all interactions are anonymous and one-shot.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate or defect.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. In PD, mutual cooperation yields 10 points each; mutual defection yields 0; unilateral defection yields +20 for defector, -10 for cooperator. Not a PGG, but payoff structure is fully specified.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 1, 2, or 3 points to deduct 10, 20, or 30 points from partner (cost:impact ratio 1:10).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 10 points deducted per 1 point spent (1:10 ratio), up to 3 points/30 points.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism; only punishment is available.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the number of rounds (30 trials, 5 training, 25 main).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told the majority behavior (cooperation or defection) at the start; after each round, they see their own and their partner's choices and payoffs, but not aggregate summaries.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is attributed to the partner in each round; participants know who punished them (the current partner), but all partners are anonymous and unique per round.

## Important Limitations
- The game is a dyadic repeated one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma, not a multi-player public goods game; findings may not generalize to PGGs.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (cooperation and punishment rates), not efficiency or group payoff.
- No direct analysis of efficiency, welfare, or group surplus; only final account balances are reported.
- All partners are simulated and interactions are one-shot; no repeated interaction with the same partner.
- No reward mechanism is present; only punishment is studied.
- The study is conducted online with panel participants, which may affect generalizability.
