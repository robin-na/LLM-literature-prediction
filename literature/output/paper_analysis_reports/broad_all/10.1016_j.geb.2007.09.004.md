# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Obligations and cooperative behaviour in public good games
Authors: Galbiati, R., & Vertova, P.
Journal: Games and Economic Behavior
Year: 2008

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- average contribution
- distribution of contributions
- dynamics of contribution decline
- effect of obligation changes on contribution
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is individual and average group contribution. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings as primary outcomes. All analyses focus on contribution behavior, not on payoff or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that, for a given structure of low, fixed marginal incentives (probabilistic monitoring, penalty, and reward), increasing the minimum required contribution (the 'obligation') leads to higher average contributions in a repeated public good game. This effect is significant only when the obligation is set high (80% of endowment); a low obligation (40%) does not significantly increase contributions relative to no obligation. Contributions decline over time in all conditions, but the rate of decline is slower with higher obligations. Unexpected increases in the minimum contribution obligation cause a temporary jump in contributions, while decreases do not reverse the downward trend. The study does not report group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes, so the effect on efficiency is not directly measured.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in repeated public good games with low-probability, low-magnitude punishment and reward, increasing the minimum required contribution (obligation) can increase average contributions, especially when the obligation is set high. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, it does not directly inform the prediction of efficiency outcomes. The results suggest that, under similar game parameters (6 players, 10 rounds, continuous contribution, no chat, fixed MPCR, probabilistic monitoring, anonymous play), introducing or raising a minimum contribution obligation (with fixed marginal incentives) will likely increase contributions, but may not sustain them over time. The effect on efficiency would depend on the cost of monitoring and the penalty/reward structure, which are not analyzed in terms of group payoff. The findings are most relevant for predicting contribution behavior, not efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 6 players; group size is fixed and common knowledge.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games last 10 rounds; in some sessions, a second 10-round segment is added with a change in obligation.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication between participants is explicitly prohibited.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: participants choose any integer between 0 and 25 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.3 (total group contribution multiplied by 1.8, divided among 6 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is probabilistic: if monitored, 1.2 tokens are subtracted per token below the minimum contribution. The cost is only incurred if monitored, with probability 1/12 per round.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 1.2 tokens per token below the minimum, conditional on being monitored (probability 1/12 per round).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is symmetric to punishment: if monitored and contribution exceeds minimum, 1.2 tokens per token above minimum are added.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is paid by the experimenter, not by other players; cost to the rewarder is not applicable.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is 1.2 tokens per token above minimum, conditional on being monitored (probability 1/12 per round).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told the game lasts exactly 10 rounds; in two-segment sessions, the second segment is presented as a new experiment after the first ends.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, participants see the total group contribution and the result of the monitoring process (but not individual contributions of others).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. If a group is monitored, only the monitored individual knows their own monitoring result; others know that someone was monitored but not who. No identification of punisher or punished.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or surplus outcomes; all results are based on contribution behavior.
- Punishment and reward are implemented by the experimenter, not by peers; this differs from standard peer punishment designs.
- Punishment and reward are probabilistic and low in magnitude, which may limit generalizability to settings with stronger or more certain sanctions.
- The effect of obligations is only measured in terms of contribution, not in terms of efficiency or group welfare.
- The study does not analyze the cost of monitoring or the net effect of punishment/reward on group payoffs.
