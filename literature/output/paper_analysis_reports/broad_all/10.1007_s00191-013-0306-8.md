## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that introducing a group-level moral system into an agent-based model (based on repeated Prisoner's Dilemma interactions within groups) leads to sustained higher levels of altruism and cooperation compared to a model without morality. The simulation shows that, with morality, average altruism remains high and stable, while without morality, altruism cycles through booms and busts. However, the outcomes are reported as average levels of altruism and morality, not as group payoffs, efficiency, or welfare. The model is illustrative and not intended to predict real-world outcomes. The findings are relevant for understanding the evolution of cooperation and the role of moral systems, but do not provide direct evidence about the effect of punishment or sanctions on efficiency in public goods games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical and simulation-based support for the idea that group-level moral systems can sustain higher cooperation and altruism, which may be indirectly relevant to predicting the effects of punishment or moral norms in public goods games. However, it does not report efficiency or payoff outcomes, nor does it manipulate or analyze the specific game design dimensions used in the prediction task (e.g., punishment cost, MPCR, player count). The simulation is adjacent to PGGs (using repeated Prisoner's Dilemma within groups) and includes group-level institutions, but the lack of payoff-based outcomes limits its direct utility for predicting efficiency changes due to punishment. The main value is in providing conceptual and mechanistic context for how moral systems (which may include punishment) can stabilize cooperation.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The simulation uses a population of 200 agents divided into 10 or 20 groups, but does not systematically vary or analyze player count effects.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The simulation runs for 5000 generations, with repeated within-group games each generation. Number of rounds is fixed and not manipulated.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is primarily theoretical and uses an agent-based simulation for illustration; it does not report empirical or experimental data.
- The simulation is adjacent to public goods games but uses repeated Prisoner's Dilemma games within groups, not a standard PGG.
- No explicit payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported; all main outcomes are behavioral (altruism, morality, etc.).
- Punishment and sanctions are discussed in evolutionary terms but are not explicitly modeled or parameterized in the simulation.
- The model is highly simplified and not intended to predict real-world outcomes; parameter sensitivity and realism are not systematically addressed.
- No direct evidence is provided for the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments.
