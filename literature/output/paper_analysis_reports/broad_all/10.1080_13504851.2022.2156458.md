# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Grid-group cultural theory and behaviour in a public good experiment
Authors: Kingsley, D. C., & Smith-Walter, A.
Journal: Applied Economics Letters
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual contribution to public good
- probability of monitoring
- probability of punishing
- amount of punishment assigned
Outcome notes:
The paper's dependent variables are individual contributions, monitoring and punishment behaviors, and their relationship to cultural orientation. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that high-group individuals contribute more to the public good, both with and without punishment. High-grid individuals are more likely to monitor and punish others when the punishment mechanism is available. The presence of punishment increases average contributions for all groups, but the paper does not report on group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare. The main focus is on how cultural orientation moderates contribution and punishment behaviors, not on the efficiency effects of punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that the opportunity for punishment increases contributions in a standard public goods game, and that individual differences in cultural orientation moderate this effect. However, it does not report group efficiency or payoff outcomes, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. The design details (group size, rounds, MPCR, punishment cost/tech) are clearly specified, which may help contextualize the results for other studies, but the lack of efficiency data limits its direct predictive value for the downstream task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 participants per session.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 33 periods in total: Part 1 (periods 1-9), Part 2 (periods 10-27), Part 3 not described in detail.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No mention of chat or communication between players; assumed absent as is standard in such experiments.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each subject allocates their endowment between private and group account.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR (alpha) is 0.5.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Cost to punisher is 1 ED per reduction point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each reduction point assigned costs the punisher 1 ED and reduces the target's payoff by 4 EDs.
- reward availability: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No reward mechanism is present; only punishment is available.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of periods is known and fixed; the last period is known to participants.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants can monitor individual group account allocations (at a cost), so information about others' contributions is available if monitoring is chosen.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined.
- Cultural orientation is measured ex post, which may introduce bias.
- The sample size is relatively small (13 groups, 52 subjects).
- The experiment is conducted in a lab setting with student participants, which may limit generalizability.
- No analysis of group-level or aggregate outcomes; all results are at the individual level.
