## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that network adaptation (agents breaking unsatisfactory links and seeking new partners) enables the emergence of highly cooperative, high-efficiency steady states in a networked Prisoner's Dilemma. In these states, most agents cooperate, and the group achieves high average payoffs, even though defectors individually do better than cooperators. The system is robust to a range of parameters, but can be destabilized by noise or by the loss of key cooperative leaders, leading to cascades of defection and efficiency collapse. The model does not include explicit costly punishment or reward mechanisms, but the ability to break links with defectors functions as a form of social sanction/ostracism. The results show that such adaptive partner selection can substitute for explicit punishment in sustaining cooperation and efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and simulation evidence that adaptive partner selection (ostracism, link breaking) in networked social dilemmas can dramatically increase group efficiency, even in the absence of explicit costly punishment or reward. For the downstream prediction task, the findings suggest that enabling mechanisms for social exclusion or network adaptation (even if not labeled as 'punishment') can have similar or greater positive effects on efficiency as explicit punishment in public goods games. However, the model is a Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game, and the 'punishment' is indirect (ostracism, not costly reduction of others' payoffs). The results are most relevant for predicting the effects of partner choice, ostracism, or network adaptation, and less directly for classic costly punishment. The paper is highly informative for adjacent mechanisms but should be used with caution for direct predictions about explicit punishment in PGGs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=300 to N=10,000; main results shown for N=10,000. Large N is used to ensure generality.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to T=1000 time steps (rounds), with steady states typically reached within this window.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each agent chooses between full cooperation (C) or full defection (D) in each round; binary choice.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents observe the aggregate payoff of their neighbors and imitate the strategy of the neighbor with the highest payoff.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a networked Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGGs.
- No explicit costly punishment or reward mechanisms are present; only ostracism/partner switching is modeled.
- All results are theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data.
- Efficiency is measured via average payoff and cooperation rates, but the mapping to classic PGG efficiency is indirect.
- The model assumes large populations and may not generalize to small groups.
- Partner switching is costless and unconstrained, which may not reflect real-world or experimental settings.
