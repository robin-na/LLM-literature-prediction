# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Can intermediaries assure contracts? Experimental evidence
Authors: Mitzkewitz, M., & Neugebauer, T.
Journal: Games and Economic Behavior
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average round payoff
- group welfare
- frequency of efficient outcomes
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of cooperation (honest play)
- frequency of defection (cheating)
- frequency of refusal
- frequency of accepting to play
- frequency of becoming a customer of intermediary
- conditional frequencies by reputation
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is average round payoff (tokens), which is a direct measure of efficiency/welfare. The paper also reports behavioral outcomes such as cooperation rates, refusal rates, and use of intermediary institutions.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment compares five treatments: a baseline (no intermediary), two ENFO treatments (punishment fines of 10 or 20 tokens), and two INFO treatments (reputation-based, with or without a small service cost). In the baseline, average payoffs are low (mean 0.64 tokens/round). INFO treatments increase payoffs modestly (mean 1.75 and 1.19 tokens/round for INFO(0) and INFO(.5)), while ENFO treatments yield much higher payoffs (mean 2.38 and 5.18 tokens/round for ENFO(10) and ENFO(20)). The ENFO institution, which enforces monetary fines for cheating, is much more effective at increasing efficiency than the INFO institution, which only provides reputation information. Increasing the fine in ENFO from 10 to 20 tokens further increases efficiency. The INFO institution's effect is limited, in part because reputation can be quickly repaired and there is no centralized punishment. The study also finds that the cost to use INFO reduces its adoption and thus its effectiveness. Over time, ENFO treatments move toward higher efficiency, while INFO treatments tend to regress toward less efficient outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in repeated two-player prisoner's dilemma games with optional participation, introducing a punishment mechanism (ENFO) that imposes a monetary fine for cheating substantially increases efficiency compared to a control with no punishment. The effect is stronger with higher fines. An information-based reputation system (INFO) yields only modest efficiency gains, especially if reputation can be easily repaired and there is no centralized enforcement. For prediction, the presence and strength of punishment (punishment_cost, punishment_tech), as well as the absence of reward, are key moderators. The control efficiency (baseline payoff) is low, and the treatment efficiency (with ENFO) is much higher, especially with higher fines. The design features of the punishment mechanism (cost, effectiveness, centralization) are critical for predicting efficiency gains.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in pairs (2 players per game).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consists of 50 rounds.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No chat or communication between players is mentioned; interactions are anonymous and computerized.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: 'cheat' (defect) or 'honest' (cooperate).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. ENFO treatments impose a fine of 10 or 20 tokens on cheaters. The cost is paid by the cheater; there is no cost to the punisher (the intermediary is not a player).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is perfectly effective: cheaters pay a fine of 10 or 20 tokens if they are customers of ENFO. Higher fines increase efficiency.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is present in any treatment.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game lasts 50 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed about the other player's status (customer or not, reputation score) before the PDG is played.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know whether their partner is a customer of ENFO or INFO and see their reputation score, but punishment is automatic and not assigned by a specific player.

## Important Limitations
- The game is a repeated two-player prisoner's dilemma with optional participation, not a standard public goods game; results may not generalize to multi-player PGGs.
- Punishment is implemented by an external intermediary, not by peers; there is no cost to punishers, and no endogenous punishment behavior.
- The INFO (reputation) institution is relatively weak due to the ease of reputation repair and lack of centralized punishment.
- Parameter choices (e.g., fine size, cost to use INFO) may affect generalizability.
- No reward mechanism is studied, so effects of positive incentives are not addressed.
- The study uses anonymous, computerized lab settings with no communication, which may differ from real-world environments.
