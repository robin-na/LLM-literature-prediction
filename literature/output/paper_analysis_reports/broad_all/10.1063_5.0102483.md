Some figures and discussion refer to individual payoffs, but the main dependent variable is cooperation frequency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that a combined reward-punishment mechanism (RPM) in a dynamic network prisoner's dilemma can significantly increase the average frequency of cooperation compared to either pure punishment or pure reward alone. Pure punishment only promotes cooperation under high temptation to defect, while pure reward can actually undermine cooperation. The combination of both, with appropriately tuned intensities, sustains high cooperation even under strong temptation to defect. The model also shows that the RPM leads to spontaneous emergence of network heterogeneity, with cooperators becoming influential hubs. However, the main outcome is cooperation frequency, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that the presence and calibration of both punishment and reward mechanisms can strongly affect cooperation rates in dynamic networked social dilemmas. However, because the main outcome is cooperation frequency rather than efficiency or group payoff, and the game is a dynamic network prisoner's dilemma rather than a standard public goods game, the findings should be transferred to efficiency prediction with caution. The results suggest that in environments where both punishment and reward are available and appropriately balanced, cooperation (and likely efficiency) can be sustained at higher levels than with either mechanism alone, especially under high temptation to defect. The paper does not provide direct quantitative predictions for efficiency or group payoff.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run with N=1000 (main), and N=100 for illustrative figures. The model is scalable to arbitrary N.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 10,000 time steps to reach steady state; results are averaged over the final steady state.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each player chooses between two discrete actions: cooperate or defect (binary choice).
- punishment cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. The cost is not explicitly monetary but is operationalized as loss of a social tie. The effect of punishment intensity is systematically varied.
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is controlled by k1 (penalty/tolerance factor), which determines how quickly ties are severed after betrayal. The effect of varying k1 is analyzed.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward mechanism is present in some conditions (parameter k2 > 0), absent in others (k2 = 0). The effect of reward is systematically analyzed.
- reward cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Reward is operationalized as the probability of forming a new tie based on teaching activity, parameterized by k2 (reward/influence factor). The cost is not monetary but is the expansion of social ties. The effect of reward intensity is systematically varied.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is cooperation frequency, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- The model is a dynamic network prisoner's dilemma, not a standard public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- Punishment and reward are operationalized as changes in network ties, not as explicit monetary costs or fines.
- No direct analysis of group payoff, efficiency, or surplus is provided.
- All results are from simulations/theory, not empirical experiments with human subjects.
- The model assumes binary actions (cooperate/defect) and does not consider continuous contributions.
