# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Second and third party punishment under costly monitoring
Authors: Goeschl, T., & Jarke, J.
Journal: Journal of Economic Psychology
Year: 2016

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment frequency
- punishment intensity
- punishment discrimination (targeted vs indiscriminate)
- type of punisher (non-punisher, blind punisher, targeted punisher)
- incentive to cooperate (difference in punishment for defection vs cooperation)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are punishment behaviors and the incentive to cooperate, defined as the difference in punishment for defectors vs cooperators. The study does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, nor does it report actual cooperation rates or group earnings.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that when monitoring is free, almost all punishers are 'targeted punishers' who punish defectors more than cooperators. As monitoring becomes costly, the share of targeted punishers drops sharply, and more subjects either do not punish at all or punish indiscriminately. The average punishment for defectors falls substantially as monitoring costs rise, and the difference in punishment between defectors and cooperators (the 'incentive to cooperate') is much lower under costly monitoring. These patterns are similar for both second and third party punishment, with some subtle differences in how subjects switch between punisher types across roles. The study does not report efficiency or group payoff, but the authors argue that lower monitoring costs should lead to more efficient outcomes by making punishment more effective at supporting cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the cost of monitoring (i.e., the ability to condition punishment on observed behavior) is a critical moderator of the effectiveness of punishment in social dilemma games. When monitoring is costly, punishment is less targeted and less effective at creating incentives for cooperation. While the study does not report efficiency or group payoff, the findings suggest that in games where monitoring is costly or imperfect, enabling punishment will have a weaker (or possibly negligible) effect on efficiency compared to settings with free or perfect monitoring. For prediction tasks, the presence and cost of monitoring should be considered a key dimension when estimating the efficiency impact of punishment. However, because the study does not report actual efficiency or payoff outcomes, its direct predictive value for efficiency is limited.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of two players in the prisoner's dilemma stage; third party punishment involves a third player from another group.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is one-shot; each subject plays both a second party and a third party punishment condition, but each is a one-shot game.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat was allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution decision is binary: cooperate (transfer all 10 tokens) or defect (keep all 10 tokens).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. If a player cooperates, their 10-token endowment is tripled (to 30) and given to the other player. This is a prisoner's dilemma, not a standard PGG, but the effective MPCR for the recipient is 3.0.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is linear: each token spent by the punisher reduces the punished player's payoff by 3 tokens. Maximum punishment expenditure is 20 tokens.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3:1 (each token spent by punisher reduces target's payoff by 3 tokens).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game is one-shot; the structure is fully explained in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects are informed about the payoffs in each game after decisions are made; no mention of summary statistics during the game.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; subjects do not receive any information on the personal identity of any other participant.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes; all main results are based on punishment behavior and the incentive to cooperate (difference in punishment for defectors vs cooperators).
- The game is a one-shot prisoner's dilemma with a punishment stage, not a repeated public goods game; findings may not generalize to repeated or multi-player PGGs.
- No data on actual cooperation rates or group earnings are reported.
- The monitoring cost manipulation is implemented via the strategy method, which may differ from real-time or direct-response settings.
- The sample size for the baseline (M0) condition is relatively small (22 subjects).
