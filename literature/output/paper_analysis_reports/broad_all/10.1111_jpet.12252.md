## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that coordinated punishment in public goods games can lead to a concentration effect, making it easier to sustain cooperation and achieve high group efficiency as group size increases. When punishers are even slightly better coordinated than defectors, the set of states in which it is advantageous to be a punisher expands with group size, and the all-punisher (fully cooperative) equilibrium becomes stochastically stable in the long run. This result is robust to several variations, including the presence of unconditional punishment costs and some decay in coordination as group size increases. The model provides a theoretical explanation for empirical findings that larger groups can sometimes achieve higher cooperation and efficiency, contrary to standard free-rider logic.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support for the prediction that, in public goods games with coordinated punishment, increasing group size can increase the efficiency of the punishment-enabled game, even when the control (no-punishment) game would predict lower efficiency in larger groups. The key moderator is the degree of coordination among punishers relative to defectors: as long as punishers are at least slightly better coordinated, the efficiency benefit of punishment increases with group size. The model also shows that this result is robust to moderate punishment costs and some decay in coordination. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the effect of punishment on efficiency is not necessarily diminished in larger groups, and may in fact be enhanced, provided coordinated punishment is feasible.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical; no new empirical or experimental data are presented.
- The model assumes binary (all-or-nothing) contribution; results for continuous contribution (VCM) are only conjectured.
- The key result depends on the assumption that punishers are at least slightly better coordinated than defectors; if this does not hold, the group size advantage may not appear.
- The model does not address the effects of communication, information feedback, or identification of punishers.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanisms are considered.
- The model does not specify the effects of finite or small group sizes in detail; results are asymptotic as group size increases.
