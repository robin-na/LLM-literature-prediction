# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Group bias under uncertain environment: A perspective of third-party punishment
Authors: Yang, H. B., Zhang, Y. K., Lyu, Y. R., & Tang, C. M.
Journal: Acta Psychologica
Year: 2023

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment size assigned by third-party
- expected harvest (predicted contribution/withdrawal) by group members
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the size of third-party punishment assigned to group members under different conditions. The study also measures participants' expectations about how much others will harvest, but does not report group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that third-party punishment is harsher under environmental uncertainty than under certainty. Out-group members are punished more than in-group members (out-group derogation), and in-group favoritism is observed when violations are clear. The amount of punishment increases with the severity of the harvest (i.e., the more a player takes, the more they are punished). Gender of the third-party also moderates punishment patterns. However, the study does not report on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, so the direct effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined from this paper.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides informative evidence about how punishment behavior is affected by environmental uncertainty, group affiliation, and gender in a CRD setting similar to a public goods game. However, because it does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. It is useful for understanding behavioral mechanisms and moderators of punishment, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 5 players, with a third-party supervisor (not a group member) assigning punishment.
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The CRD is not a standard linear PGG, but the marginal return is determined by the resource pool and the number of players. If the group over-harvests, all get zero; otherwise, each keeps what they take. No explicit MPCR, but the structure is similar to a threshold public goods game.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 1 token to reduce a player's harvest by 20%, up to 5 tokens. Punishment is costly to the punisher.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each token spent by the punisher reduces the target's harvest by 20%. Maximum punishment is 5 tokens (100% reduction).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Third-party is shown the harvest (withdrawal) of each player and their group affiliation before assigning punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined.
- The sample consists only of college students from one region in China, limiting generalizability.
- Moral cognition and reflective thinking, which may influence punishment decisions, were not measured or controlled.
- The number of rounds and dynamic effects are not specified; the design may not capture repeated interaction effects.
- Gender of the punished player was not manipulated or analyzed.
