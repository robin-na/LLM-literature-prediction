## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Redistributing fines from defectors to cooperators and punishers in a spatial public goods game generally increases the persistence of cooperation and raises the total system payoff, especially when punishment costs are high. When punishment costs are low, the standard pool punishment model (without redistribution) can achieve higher cooperation and higher total payoff, as punishers dominate and defectors are eliminated. The effect of fine redistribution is thus mixed: it is beneficial for efficiency and cooperation under high punishment cost, but can reduce efficiency and cooperation when punishment cost is low. The distribution parameter (alpha) also matters: allocating more fines to punishers (lower alpha) increases cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games depends on the cost of punishment and the mechanism for handling fines. If fines are redistributed to cooperators and punishers, efficiency losses from punishment can be mitigated, especially when punishment is costly. However, in settings where punishment is cheap, standard punishment (without redistribution) may yield higher efficiency by allowing punishers to eliminate defectors. For prediction, the presence or absence of fine redistribution, the punishment cost, and the fine distribution parameter (alpha) are important moderators. The results suggest that enabling fine redistribution is likely to increase efficiency when punishment is expensive, but may not always do so when punishment is cheap.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each player is on a square lattice (L=100), with each player participating in 5 groups (self and 4 neighbors). The effective group size is 5.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 10,000 iterations (rounds), with results averaged over 10 runs.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: players are either cooperators (contribute c), defectors (contribute 0), or punishers (contribute c and pay punishment cost).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r is varied (e.g., r=2.0, r=3.0), group size N=5, so MPCR = r/N (e.g., 0.4 for r=2.0).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost (beta) is a key manipulated parameter. The effect of punishment cost on cooperation and payoff is central to the analysis.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is set by the fine imposed on defectors (fine parameter, e.g., fine=0.95). The ratio of fine to cost is a key parameter.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulation length (number of rounds) is fixed and known in advance.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not include empirical or experimental data with human subjects.
- Efficiency is proxied by total system payoff, but the ratio to the fully cooperative outcome (true efficiency) is not directly reported.
- The model is spatial (lattice-based), which may limit generalizability to well-mixed or other network structures.
- Only pool punishment and fine redistribution are considered; no direct comparison to peer punishment or other institutional forms.
- No analysis of stochasticity or robustness to different initial conditions beyond averaging over 10 runs.
