## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that combining incentive-based contracts (cost-sharing) with contingent penalties (punishment for delivery failure) in reverse auctions increases the ex ante expected profit (efficiency) of the system and the principal, compared to standard reverse auctions without these mechanisms. The efficiency gains are moderated by the number of agents (more agents increase efficiency), the fraction of low-risk agents, and the degree of loss aversion (higher loss aversion reduces efficiency). The presence and magnitude of the penalty (punishment) is a key design variable, and its optimal value depends on delivery risk parameters. The model provides explicit formulas for optimal efficiency under different mechanism designs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct theoretical evidence that introducing punishment (contingent penalty) in a competitive procurement game with cost uncertainty and risk can increase system efficiency, but the effect is moderated by agent risk aversion and the number of agents. For prediction tasks, the results suggest that, all else equal, adding a punishment mechanism to a reverse auction or similar environment will increase efficiency, especially when there are more agents and when agents are less loss-averse. However, the magnitude of the effect depends on the specific design of the punishment and incentive parameters. The findings are most directly applicable to procurement/auction settings but are informative for adjacent public-goods-like environments.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and not empirically tested.
- The setting is a reverse auction for procurement, not a public goods game; results may not fully generalize to PGG-like environments.
- Punishment is implemented as a contractual penalty, not as peer-to-peer or decentralized punishment.
- The model assumes risk-neutral or loss-averse agents, but not other behavioral types.
- Only one-shot (single-round) interactions are analyzed; repeated or dynamic effects are not considered.
- Communication, social preferences, and other-regarding concerns are not modeled.
