Authors: Li, Q., Iqbal, A., Perc, M., Chen, M. Y., & Abbott, D.
Journal: Plos One
Year: 2013

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of agents using each strategy (cooperation, defection, quantum strategies)
- dominance of strategies as a function of game and network parameters
- network structural properties (clustering coefficient, average path length, indegree distribution)
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are the prevalence of different strategies (cooperation, defection, quantum) under varying parameters, and the resulting network structure. While payoffs are used in the evolutionary dynamics, the paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare as a primary outcome. The closest is the discussion of which strategies dominate, which is related to but not equivalent to efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that when agents can punish by reducing link weights, cooperation can dominate for a wider range of temptation values, especially when the network structure is updated frequently. Stronger punishment (lower minimum link weight) increases the range where cooperation dominates. As the temptation to defect increases, quantum strategies eventually dominate. The number of neighbors (network degree) has little effect if all agents have equal degree. The network evolves to have higher clustering and small-world properties. However, the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, so the findings are about strategy prevalence, not direct payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about the effect of punishment-like mechanisms (reducing link weights) on the prevalence of cooperation in an evolving network Prisoner's Dilemma. It suggests that stronger punishment and more frequent network updating support cooperation, but does not provide direct evidence about efficiency or group payoff. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper is only adjacent: it supports the idea that punishment can sustain cooperation under some conditions, but does not quantify efficiency gains or losses. The game is not a public goods game, but a networked PD with quantum strategies, so transfer to PGG-like settings should be cautious.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=2500 agents; this is fixed and reported.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 50,000 time steps, with steady state defined as no strategy change for 1000 steps.
- punishment cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented by reducing the weight of a link to w_min; the cost is not explicit, but the effect is to reduce the punished agent's future payoffs. The intensity of punishment is varied via w_min.
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is controlled by the minimum link weight w_min; lower w_min means stronger punishment. Stronger punishment increases the range where cooperation dominates.

## Important Limitations
- The game is a networked Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game or direct variant.
- Outcomes are reported in terms of strategy prevalence, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- Punishment is implemented as reducing link weights, not as explicit costly punishment with direct cost to the punisher.
- Quantum strategies are included, which are not present in standard PGG or PD experiments.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes.
- Findings may not transfer directly to standard PGG-like environments.
