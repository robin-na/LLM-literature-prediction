# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Do descriptive social norms drive peer punishment? Conditional punishment strategies and their impact on cooperation
Authors: Li, X. H., Molleman, L., & van Dolder, D.
Journal: Evolution and Human Behavior
Year: 2021

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment assigned to defectors
- conditionality of punishment on descriptive norms
- cooperation rate
- punishment strategy classification (norm enforcement, conformist, independent, decreasing)
- model-based cooperation dynamics
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are punishment behavior (deduction points assigned) and cooperation rates. The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare as an outcome. The dynamic model simulates cooperation rates but does not report efficiency or group payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiments show that individuals' punishment of defectors is conditional on descriptive social norms: people punish more when cooperation or punishment is more common in a reference group. There is substantial heterogeneity: some punish independently, some increase punishment with higher cooperation (norm enforcement), and some with higher punishment (conformist). The dynamic model demonstrates that these conditional punishment strategies can facilitate the emergence and maintenance of high cooperation rates in populations. However, the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, so the direct effect of punishment on efficiency is not measured.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that punishment behavior is conditional on social context, and that such conditional punishment can promote cooperation in repeated social dilemmas. However, because the experiment is a one-shot prisoner's dilemma with punishment and does not report efficiency or group payoff, it does not provide direct quantitative evidence for predicting efficiency changes when punishment is enabled in public goods games. The dynamic model suggests that conditional punishment can help sustain high cooperation rates, which may imply higher efficiency in analogous settings, but this is an indirect inference. The paper is most useful for understanding behavioral mechanisms and heterogeneity in punishment, and for modeling the potential for punishment to support cooperation, but less so for direct efficiency prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are played in pairs (2 players per game).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The experiment is a one-shot game (single round). The dynamic model simulates repeated interactions, but the experiment itself is one-shot.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (cooperation) is binary: cooperate or defect.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not a standard PGG; in the prisoner's dilemma, mutual cooperation yields 18 points each, mutual defection 16 each, unilateral defection 25 (defector) and 9 (cooperator). MPCR is not directly defined, but the payoff structure is fully specified.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction point costs the punisher 1 point.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction point reduces the punished player's payoff by 3 points (punishment effectiveness = 3).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game is one-shot (single round).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are shown summary information about a reference group's cooperation or punishment levels (payoff-irrelevant), which is central to the experimental manipulation.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a one-shot prisoner's dilemma with punishment, not a repeated public goods game.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only punishment behavior and cooperation rates are measured.
- The dynamic model simulates cooperation rates but does not report efficiency or group payoff.
- The reference group information is payoff-irrelevant and does not affect actual payoffs.
- Findings on conditional punishment are based on behavioral responses, not on observed changes in group efficiency.
