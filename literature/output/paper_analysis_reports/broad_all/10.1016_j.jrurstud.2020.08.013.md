## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of conditional neutral punishment—where agents punish neighbors with the opposite strategy if their own payoff is below the neighborhood average—substantially increases the fraction of cooperators in a spatial prisoner's dilemma game. The effect is robust to variations in the temptation to defect and the cost and effectiveness of punishment. Cooperative punishers form protective clusters that shield cooperators, while antisocial punishment (defectors punishing cooperators) is largely ineffective. The main outcome is the fraction of cooperation, not group payoff or efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that conditional neutral punishment can increase cooperation rates in spatial social dilemmas, but it does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, the results are only indirectly informative: higher cooperation rates typically—but not always—imply higher efficiency, especially in prisoner's dilemma-like settings. However, without explicit efficiency or payoff data, the magnitude and reliability of efficiency gains from punishment in this design cannot be directly inferred. The spatial structure, conditionality of punishment, and the absence of communication or reward mechanisms are important contextual features.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The model uses a 2D lattice of size L x L (L=200), so the player count is 40,000. All players interact with their four neighbors.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Simulations run for 50,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with averages taken over the last 5,000 steps. There is no fixed number of rounds as in a laboratory experiment, but the system is iterated for many time steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate or defect (binary choice).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost (alpha) is varied; higher cost to punish reduces the effectiveness of punishment in promoting cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (social fine, beta) is varied; higher beta increases the ability of punishment to promote cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- The main outcome is the fraction of cooperation, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are from agent-based simulations.
- No analysis of group payoff or efficiency, so the effect of punishment on efficiency is not directly established.
- No communication, reward, or reputation mechanisms are modeled.
- The punishment mechanism is conditional and local, which may differ from standard laboratory punishment designs.
