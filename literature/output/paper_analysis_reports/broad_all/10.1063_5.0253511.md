## Outcomes Measured

Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the evolutionary dynamics and stationary frequencies of strategies (cooperation, defection, loner, punishing loner) in a spatial optional public goods game. It does not report group efficiency, total payoff, welfare, or surplus. Payoff functions are defined, but aggregate payoff outcomes are not the main dependent variable.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of loners who punish cooperators (LP) into the optional public goods game leads to new evolutionary phases, including three-strategy cyclic states (C+D+LP), pure cooperator phases, and coexistence of cooperators with both types of loners. The presence and intensity of punishment (punishment cost and fine) modulate the transitions between these phases. Under certain conditions (e.g., high punishment fine and/or cost, high loner income), the system can escape the typical cyclic dominance and reach a pure cooperation phase or a coexistence phase where cooperators are protected by traditional loners from punishing loners. However, the paper does not quantify the effect of punishment on group efficiency or total payoff, focusing instead on the prevalence and stability of cooperation as a strategy.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides detailed theoretical and simulation-based evidence on how punishment by loners affects the prevalence of cooperation in optional public goods games, under varying game parameters (synergy factor, loner income, punishment cost, punishment fine). However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The results are most useful for understanding how game design dimensions (e.g., punishment cost, punishment effectiveness, loner income) affect the likelihood of cooperation dominating or persisting, which may be indirectly informative for efficiency predictions if one assumes that higher cooperation rates translate to higher efficiency. Caution is warranted, as the mapping from strategy frequencies to efficiency is not explicit in this work.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is simulated on a square lattice, with each individual as a node. Typical system sizes up to 320x320 are used. Each local game involves 5 players (focal plus 4 neighbors).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 10^9 rounds or until stationary state is reached. The number of rounds is not a treatment variable but is sufficient for equilibrium analysis.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is a binary choice: invest a fixed cost (cooperate) or not (defect).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r is varied (e.g., r=3, r=3.5, r>3.74 for certain transitions). MPCR is r divided by number of contributors in the group.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost (alpha) is a key parameter, varied in phase diagrams. Higher cost can reduce the prevalence of punishing loners and affect cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (beta, the fine imposed on cooperators) is varied. Higher beta can promote or destabilize cooperation depending on the regime.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is entirely theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are presented.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare are not directly reported or analyzed; only strategy frequencies and phase diagrams are provided.
- The mapping from strategy prevalence to group efficiency is not explicit, so the results cannot be directly used to predict efficiency outcomes.
- The model assumes a spatial lattice and evolutionary updating, which may not generalize to well-mixed or real-world human groups.
- No consideration of communication, reward mechanisms, or information feedback beyond local payoffs.
