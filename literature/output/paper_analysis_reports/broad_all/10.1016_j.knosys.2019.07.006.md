Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are stationary distributions and average payoffs of strategies, which are directly linked to group efficiency and welfare. Behavioral outcomes (like cooperation rate) are discussed only as they relate to these payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper shows that in finite well-mixed populations playing a public goods game, prosocial pool exclusion (where cooperators can pay to exclude defectors from benefits) is more effective at promoting cooperation and group efficiency than costly punishment. Even when antisocial exclusion (where defectors exclude cooperators) is introduced, prosocial pool excluders maintain their evolutionary advantage and can dominate the population, especially when second-order sanctioning is allowed. When both exclusion and punishment (prosocial and antisocial) are available, prosocial pool excluders prevail and maximize group payoff/efficiency. Antisocial exclusion alone leads to defection and low efficiency, but does not undermine the effectiveness of prosocial exclusion when both are present.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that, in public goods games with the option for prosocial exclusion, enabling such exclusion mechanisms leads to higher group efficiency and welfare than enabling costly punishment, even in the presence of antisocial sanctioning. The results are robust to the inclusion of second-order sanctioning and to moderate changes in the marginal per-capita return (mpcr). For prediction tasks, the presence of prosocial exclusion as a punishment-like mechanism should be expected to increase efficiency relative to control, and to outperform standard costly punishment. The findings are most directly applicable to games with continuous contributions, no communication, and fixed group size and rounds, as modeled here.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size M is a parameter in the model (e.g., M=100 in main figures). Group size N=5 is used in main results.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is evolutionary and considers repeated games with stationary distributions; the number of rounds is not fixed but the process is effectively infinite/repeated.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. mpcr = r/N; main results use r=3, N=5, so mpcr=0.6. The effect of changing r is discussed and found not to alter main conclusions.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment and exclusion costs (G_P, G_A, δ_P, δ_A) are parameters; main results use δ_P=δ_A=0.4 or 0.5.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment impact per unit cost is specified via fines (B_P, B_A) and exclusion mechanism; main results use B_P=B_A=0.4.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is entirely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The model assumes well-mixed populations and does not consider network or spatial structure.
- Punishment and exclusion are implemented as pool (institutional) mechanisms, not peer-to-peer.
- No communication, reputation, or information feedback is modeled.
- The evolutionary process assumes rare mutation and may not capture all real-world dynamics.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with all-or-nothing contributions, communication, or other institutional features not modeled here.
