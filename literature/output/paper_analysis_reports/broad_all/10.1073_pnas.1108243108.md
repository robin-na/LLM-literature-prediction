# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Dynamic social networks promote cooperation in experiments with humans
Authors: Rand, D. G., Arbesman, S., & Christakis, N. A.
Journal: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America
Year: 2011

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- link formation/breaking behavior
- network structure (degree heterogeneity, link stability)
- reciprocation patterns
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the fraction of players choosing to cooperate. The paper also analyzes network structure and link dynamics. Payoff is discussed in the design, but not as a primary outcome or in efficiency terms.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that in large groups playing a repeated networked cooperation game, cooperation decays over time in static, random, or slowly updating networks, but is stably maintained when players can frequently update their social ties. Players preferentially break links with defectors and form links with cooperators, which disciplines defectors and incentivizes cooperation. This dynamic network updating acts as a decentralized ostracism mechanism, supporting cooperation without explicit costly punishment. The study does not report efficiency or group payoff, so its findings are most directly about behavioral cooperation, not payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that dynamic, fluid social networks can sustain high cooperation rates in repeated social dilemma games, even in large groups. However, it does not directly address the effect of explicit punishment mechanisms (costly punishment) on efficiency or payoff. Instead, it studies a form of endogenous, non-costly ostracism (link breaking), which is adjacent to but not the same as standard punishment in public goods games. For prediction tasks about the effect of enabling costly punishment on efficiency, this paper is only indirectly informative: it suggests that non-costly, decentralized exclusion can promote cooperation, but does not provide direct evidence about efficiency or about the effect of costly punishment. The game design dimensions are partially informative (e.g., player count, network updating rules), but the absence of explicit payoff/efficiency outcomes and standard punishment limits direct applicability.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Average network size is 19.6 (SD 6.4), with sessions of varying size. Large group size is a key feature.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game length is stochastic: after each round, there is an 80% chance of another round. Number of rounds varies by session.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject chooses a single action per round: cooperate (pay cost for each neighbor) or defect (pay nothing). Binary choice per round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each cooperation costs 50 units per neighbor and gives 100 units to each neighbor. MPCR per neighbor is 2.0, but since each player chooses for all neighbors, the effective MPCR per group is not standard PGG but is specified.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know that after each round, there is an 80% chance of another round, i.e., indefinite horizon with known continuation probability.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed about their neighbors' previous decisions and their own payoff after each round.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each rewiring round, each subject is told the number of others who broke links with her and the number who formed new links with her. When deciding to break/form a link, the subject is told the other's previous action.

## Important Limitations
- No explicit costly punishment or reward mechanisms; only endogenous, non-costly link breaking/formation.
- Does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare as an outcome; only cooperation rates and network structure.
- Game is a networked repeated binary cooperation game, not a standard public goods game; mapping to PGG is close but not exact.
- No analysis of how design dimensions would affect efficiency or payoff if costly punishment were enabled.
- Game length is stochastic and varies by session, which may affect comparability to fixed-horizon PGGs.
