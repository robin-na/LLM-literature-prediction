# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Defectors, not norm violators, are punished by third-parties
Authors: Bone, J., Silva, A. S., & Raihani, N. J.
Journal: Biology Letters
Year: 2014

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- probability of being punished by a third-party
- probability of being socially rejected (not chosen as a future partner)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are whether a player is punished by a third-party and whether a player is socially rejected. No group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings are reported or analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that third-party punishment in a one-shot public goods game is overwhelmingly directed at defectors, regardless of whether defection is the norm in the group. The manipulation of norm violation (defection being typical or atypical) does not affect the likelihood of defectors being punished. Cooperators are rarely punished, even when they are in the minority. The cost structure of punishment is fixed, so punishing multiple players is not more expensive than punishing one. Social rejection patterns mirror punishment: defectors are less preferred as future partners, regardless of norm status. The study does not report on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a one-shot PGG with third-party punishment and a fixed punishment cost, punishment is targeted at defectors rather than norm violators. However, because the study does not report efficiency or group payoff, it does not directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. The design details (one-shot, third-party punishment, fixed cost, no communication, binary contribution) are informative for mapping to other studies, but the lack of payoff outcomes limits its direct utility for efficiency prediction. The findings suggest that, under these design features, punishment is likely to be used against defectors, but the impact on group efficiency remains unmeasured.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 4 PGG players (players 1-4) and 1 third-party punisher (player 5).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is one-shot (single round).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: invest in public (cooperate) or private (defect) investment opportunity.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each public investment yields $0.20 to self and $0.20 to each of 3 others; MPCR = 0.8 (total return $0.80 per $0.20 invested, divided among 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays a fixed cost of $0.05 to reduce the earnings of any or all PGG players by $0.15 each.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: $0.15 deduction per punished player for a fixed $0.05 cost, regardless of number punished.
- reward availability: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No reward mechanism is available in the experiment; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game is one-shot (single round).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Player 5 observes the decisions of all 4 PGG players; no evidence that PGG players see summaries of others' actions.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is assigned by an identified third-party (player 5), but it is not specified whether PGG players know who punished them.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report or analyze group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes.
- The experiment is one-shot and uses third-party punishment, which may not generalize to repeated or peer-punishment settings.
- No reward mechanism is present, so findings may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
- The fixed cost structure for punishment is atypical compared to standard PGG punishment designs.
- All participants are US-based MTurk workers, which may limit generalizability.
