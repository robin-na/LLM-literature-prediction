## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper argues that human societies have a deeply ingrained tendency to punish cheaters, even at personal cost, and that this tendency supports higher levels of cooperation in commons dilemmas. It references experimental findings (e.g., Fehr and Gächter 2002) showing that allowing punishment in public goods games increases cooperation, but does not provide quantitative data or analyze efficiency or group payoff. The paper also discusses the importance of social norms, education, and regulation in sustaining cooperation and resource conservation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides conceptual support for the idea that punishment mechanisms can increase cooperation in public goods game-like environments, but does not provide quantitative evidence or direct analysis of efficiency or payoff outcomes. It is useful for understanding the mechanisms and social context of punishment, but does not inform prediction of treatment efficiency from game design dimensions or control efficiency. For prediction tasks, this paper is best used as background or for mechanism discussion, not for quantitative modeling.
