# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Cooperation in collective action
Authors: Bandiera, O., Barankay, I., & Rasul, I.
Journal: Economics of Transition
Year: 2005

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=observational; experimental subtype=N/A

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual productivity (kg/hr) as a proxy for cooperation (lower productivity = more cooperation under relative incentives)
- contribution to public goods (in literature review)
- maintenance status of common resources (in literature review)
- extraction levels from common resources (in literature review)
Outcome notes:
The main empirical outcome is individual productivity, interpreted as a behavioral proxy for cooperation in a relative incentive scheme. Lower productivity is considered more cooperative, but the paper does not directly report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare. Some reviewed studies in the literature survey report maintenance status or contributions, but not efficiency per se.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a workplace setting with relative incentives (where individual effort imposes a negative externality on co-workers), workers cooperate more (i.e., reduce their productivity) when in smaller groups, with a higher share of co-nationals, with more homogeneous ability, and with more co-workers who have more to lose from shirking. These group characteristics do not affect productivity under piece rates, where there is no incentive to cooperate. The literature review highlights that institutions enabling monitoring, communication, and punishment can increase cooperation in public goods and common pool resource settings, but monetary punishment often reduces group welfare due to its cost. The paper does not directly report efficiency or group payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence relevant to predicting the effect of punishment and group characteristics on cooperation in collective action settings, but does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or payoff outcomes. The empirical results are most relevant for understanding how group size, heterogeneity, and incentive structure affect cooperation (proxied by productivity), rather than efficiency. The literature review suggests that punishment can increase cooperation but may reduce efficiency due to its cost, and that communication and monitoring are important moderators. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, the paper's findings should be used as indirect signals about the likely direction of cooperation and the possible tradeoff between increased cooperation and the cost of punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Group size (number of workers per field-day) is directly measured and varied exogenously. Larger groups are less able to cooperate (i.e., higher productivity, less cooperation) under relative incentives.

## Important Limitations
- The main empirical outcome is individual productivity, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanism is present in the empirical study; punishment is only discussed in the literature review.
- The setting is a workplace with relative incentives, not a standard public goods game; the mapping to PGG-like environments is indirect.
- No direct measurement of efficiency or surplus; findings on cooperation are based on behavioral proxies.
- The study is observational, not experimental, and group assignment is not fully random.
- Some relevant dimensions (e.g., punishment cost, MPCR, communication) are not present or measured in the empirical study.
