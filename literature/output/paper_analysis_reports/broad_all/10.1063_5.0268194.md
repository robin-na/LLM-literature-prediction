Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are payoff-based: average payoff, group payoff, and the stability/attractiveness of full-contribution equilibria. The paper also discusses the frequencies of different strategies and the mitigation of second-order free-riding, but these are secondary to the payoff/efficiency focus.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper shows that in public goods games with peer punishment, introducing state-based feedback (where the multiplication factor increases with the number of contributors) significantly increases the efficiency of the system by making full-cooperation equilibria more likely and more stable. Local state-feedback (where the multiplication factor depends on the fraction of contributors in the group) is especially effective, sometimes enabling the system to reach and maintain full cooperation even when the baseline multiplication factor is not high. The positive effect of punishment on efficiency is robust to changes in punishment cost and fine, and state-based feedback can partially mitigate the second-order free-rider problem. The main outcome is the proportion of the state space (attraction basin) leading to efficient, full-contribution equilibria, which increases with both punishment and state-based feedback.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support that, in public goods games with peer punishment, enabling punishment increases efficiency (group payoff) compared to the control (no punishment), especially when the marginal per-capita return (mpcr) is enhanced by state-based feedback. The effect is robust to variations in punishment cost and fine, and is stronger when the feedback is local rather than global. For prediction, the presence of punishment, lower punishment cost, higher punishment effectiveness, and the presence of local state-feedback all predict higher efficiency. The findings are directly relevant for predicting treatment efficiency from game design dimensions and control efficiency in PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Player count is denoted as n (n >= 2), with most analysis and figures using n=5. The model is general for arbitrary n.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: individuals choose whether to contribute (cooperator/punisher) or not (defector).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. mpcr is the multiplication factor divided by n; both baseline (r0) and feedback-dependent (r0 + k*...) forms are analyzed. Higher mpcr (via r0 or feedback) increases efficiency and the likelihood of full cooperation.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost (gamma) is explicitly modeled and varied. Lower punishment cost increases the basin of attraction for efficient states.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (fine per unit cost, beta/gamma) is varied. Higher effectiveness (higher beta, lower gamma) increases efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The analysis is entirely theoretical, using infinite well-mixed populations and replicator dynamics; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- No finite-population or structured-population effects are considered; real-world deviations may occur.
- No explicit time horizon or rounds; results may not map directly to finite repeated games.
- No communication, reward, or other mechanisms beyond peer punishment and state-based feedback are considered.
- The model assumes binary (all-or-nothing) contribution choices.
