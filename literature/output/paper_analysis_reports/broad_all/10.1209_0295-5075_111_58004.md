Outcomes are primarily the equilibrium fraction of cooperators, with payoffs used as the basis for replicator dynamics but not reported as group efficiency or total payoff. No empirical or simulated group efficiency or welfare is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Theoretical results show that introducing punishment for defectors in threshold N-person snowdrift games increases the equilibrium fraction of cooperators, especially when punishment is always applied regardless of group success. Stronger punishment and higher cooperation thresholds further increase cooperation. However, the model does not include costs for administering punishment, and does not report group efficiency or total payoff. The findings are relevant for understanding how punishment rules and thresholds affect cooperation, but do not directly inform the effect of punishment on group efficiency or welfare.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the idea that punishment increases cooperation in threshold public goods-like games, especially when punishment is always applied and is strong. However, because the model assumes costless punishment and does not report group efficiency or total payoff, it does not directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings are most useful for understanding how punishment regime, threshold, and punishment strength affect cooperation rates, which may correlate with efficiency in some settings, but the lack of punishment cost and direct efficiency outcomes limits predictive value for efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The model assumes punishment is costless to the punisher, which is unrealistic for most real-world or experimental settings.
- No group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only equilibrium cooperation rates are analyzed.
- The analysis is entirely theoretical; no empirical or simulation data are provided.
- The model does not include communication, reward, or other social mechanisms beyond punishment.
- The evolutionary dynamics do not map directly to repeated rounds or finite games as in laboratory experiments.
