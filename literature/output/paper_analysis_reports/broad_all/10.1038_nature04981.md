# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Parochial altruism in humans
Authors: Bernhard, H., Fischbacher, U., & Fehr, E.
Journal: Nature
Year: 2006

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment assigned by third party
- frequency of punishment
- dictator transfer (sharing) decisions
- expectations about punishment
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are punishment behavior (how much the third party spends to punish the dictator) and dictator sharing decisions. There is no direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare. The study focuses on norm enforcement and parochialism in punishment, not on efficiency or surplus.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that third-party punishment is strongly parochial: punishers spend more to punish norm violations when the victim is from their own group, regardless of the violator's group. Dictators (the potential norm violators) expect more punishment and transfer more to the recipient when the recipient and the third party are from the same group. When the third party is from the dictator's group, dictators expect less punishment and transfer less. The results suggest that the threat of punishment is not impartial but is shaped by group membership, leading to greater protection for ingroup victims. The paper does not report on efficiency or total group payoff, so its findings are not directly about the effect of punishment on efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effectiveness and application of punishment in norm enforcement are highly sensitive to group affiliations, with punishment being much more likely and severe when the victim and punisher are from the same group. However, because the experiment is a one-shot dictator game with third-party punishment (not a repeated public goods game), and because it does not report efficiency or group payoff, its direct value for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in public-goods-game-like environments is limited. It is informative about the social and psychological moderators of punishment, especially parochialism, but not about efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game has 3 players: dictator (A), recipient (B), and third party (C).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is one-shot (single round).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication between participants was allowed before or during the experiment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Dictator can transfer any amount between 0 and 10 Kina (continuous choice), not all-or-nothing.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each Kina spent by the third party on punishment reduces the dictator's income by 3 Kina (punishment cost to punisher: 1 Kina per unit).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: 1 Kina spent by punisher reduces dictator's payoff by 3 Kina.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is one-shot; participants know this.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Third party is informed about the dictator's transfer before making punishment decision.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed about the group affiliation of all players, but not about individual identities. No evidence that punished player knows the identity of the punisher beyond group membership.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is a one-shot third-party punishment dictator game, not a repeated public goods game.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported or analyzed.
- The findings are about parochialism in punishment and norm enforcement, not about the effect of punishment on efficiency.
- The sample is from two small-scale societies in Papua New Guinea, which may limit generalizability.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanism is present.
