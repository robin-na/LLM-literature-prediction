# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: On cooperation in open communities
Authors: Gürerk, Irlenbusch, B., & Rockenbach, B.
Journal: Journal of Public Economics
Year: 2014

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate
- switching between communities
- punishment assigned
- reward assigned
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is contribution to the public good. The paper does not directly report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings as primary outcomes, but does discuss them in theoretical terms and in the context of maximum possible payoffs. The focus is on cooperation (contribution) rates and the dynamics of community choice.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that when subjects can self-select into a punishment community (VF-PUN), average contributions are much higher than in a reward community (VF-REW) or in exogenously assigned punishment communities (EX-PUN, FIX-PUN). The effect is attributed to self-selection rather than slow growth of the community. The presence of a punishment option is critical for high cooperation, and adding reward does not further improve outcomes. However, the paper does not directly report efficiency or group payoff; all main results are in terms of contribution rates.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that self-selection into punishment-enabled public goods games leads to higher cooperation (contribution) rates than exogenous assignment or reward-based institutions. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, the results are only indirectly informative: higher contributions likely imply higher efficiency, but actual efficiency or group payoff is not reported. The design details (player count, rounds, punishment cost/tech, etc.) are well specified, so the paper is useful for mapping design to expected cooperation, but not for direct efficiency prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each experimental group consists of 12 players. Community size varies endogenously in some treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is repeated for 30 periods (rounds) in all treatments.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication is allowed during the experiment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player can contribute any integer amount from 0 to 20 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR (a) varies with group size to keep total productivity R = n*a = 1.6 constant. For n=12, MPCR=0.13; for n=6, MPCR=0.27; for n=2, MPCR=0.80, etc.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishing costs 1 token per point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point assigned reduces the target's payoff by 3 tokens (1:3 punishment technology).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is available in the VF-REW treatment (not in VF-PUN, EX-PUN, or FIX-PUN).
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Rewarding costs 1 token per point assigned (same as punishment cost).
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each reward point assigned increases the target's payoff by 3 tokens (1:3 reward technology).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed that the experiment consists of 30 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each round, all players are informed about individual contributions and payoffs in both communities. In the punishment/reward communities, players also see tokens assigned/received.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment/reward assignments are shown anonymously; player identities are not revealed, but the amount each player assigned/received is shown.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is contribution rate, not efficiency or group payoff; efficiency is only discussed theoretically.
- No direct measurement or reporting of group efficiency, welfare, or total earnings as a primary outcome.
- The design is limited to laboratory settings with student subjects and artificial communities.
- The results may not generalize to real-world open communities or to settings with different public goods or institutional options.
- The effect of punishment on actual efficiency (accounting for punishment costs) is not directly analyzed.
