Outcome notes:
The paper primarily uses theoretical and simulation-based analysis to study the effect of network structure and social sanction (ostracism) on cooperation and resource sustainability. Payoff-related outcomes are reported as equilibrium resource levels and payoffs, but the main focus is on the survival of cooperation and the effect of network structure on sanction effectiveness.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study extends a previous analytical model of social sanction (ostracism) in common pool resource extraction to populations with complex network structures. It finds that, contrary to some prior work, cooperation and resource sustainability are promoted by higher network connectivity when social sanction is present. In networks with fewer social ties, defectors can cluster and avoid sanction, leading to lower cooperation and lower equilibrium resource levels. The model shows that the equilibrium resource level (a proxy for group efficiency) is higher in well-mixed or highly connected networks, and lower in sparsely connected ones. The findings are robust across different network topologies (random, scale-free, community-structured) and network sizes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical and simulation-based evidence that, in public-goods-like common pool resource games with social sanction (ostracism), increasing the average number of social ties (network degree) increases the effectiveness of punishment in promoting cooperation and sustaining higher resource levels (efficiency). For the downstream prediction task, this suggests that, all else equal, games with higher player connectivity or more opportunities for sanctioning will see a greater positive effect of punishment on efficiency. The paper does not provide direct experimental data, but its results are highly relevant for predicting the effect of punishment in networked or structured populations.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run for N=50 and N=500; results are shown for both small and large populations.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a Cobb-Douglas production function with parameters (gamma, alpha, beta) that determine returns to effort and resource. Not a standard linear MPCR, but analogous.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sanctioning (ostracism) is modeled as a cost imposed on defectors, with parameters h, t, g controlling maximum sanction, threshold, and growth rate. No explicit cost to punishers; sanction is a social cost to defectors.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sanction effectiveness is parameterized (h, t, g) and is nonlinear, increasing rapidly with the fraction of cooperators in the neighborhood.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- The model assumes a specific nonlinear form of social sanction (ostracism) and may not generalize to all forms of punishment.
- Payoff-related outcomes are reported as equilibrium resource levels and average payoffs, but the main focus is on cooperation dynamics rather than direct measurement of group efficiency as in standard PGG experiments.
- The model does not include explicit cost to punishers for sanctioning, only cost to defectors.
- The replicator dynamics and network structure may not capture all relevant features of real-world public goods games or human behavior.
