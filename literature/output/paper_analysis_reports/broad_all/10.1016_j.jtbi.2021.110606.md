Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are the long-run frequencies and coexistence of strategies (cooperators, defectors, punishers) in a theoretical eco-evolutionary model. Payoff is discussed as average fitness, but the focus is on population dynamics and stability, not on group efficiency or total welfare as in standard PGG experiments.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model shows that the introduction of punishment (policing) as a third strategy in a repeated PD game can lead to stable coexistence or oscillatory dominance cycles among cooperators, defectors, and punishers, depending on ecological and game parameters. The strength of punishment, temptation to defect, and ecological benefits (from 'free space') all modulate which strategies persist. Moderate punishment can suppress defectors and promote cooperation, but high temptation to defect can still undermine cooperation. The model also demonstrates that initial conditions and parameter choices can lead to multistability, with different attractors (e.g., dominance by one strategy, coexistence, or extinction). The focus is on the eco-evolutionary dynamics of strategy frequencies, not on group efficiency or total welfare.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that punishment can promote the persistence of cooperation in social dilemma settings, but the results are framed in terms of population dynamics (strategy frequencies and coexistence) rather than efficiency or group payoff. The model is based on a repeated PD game, not a public goods game, and does not report efficiency as defined by total group payoff relative to full cooperation. Therefore, while the findings support the idea that punishment can help sustain cooperation under certain conditions, they do not provide direct quantitative guidance for predicting efficiency changes in PGG-like environments with or without punishment. The results may be useful for understanding qualitative moderators (e.g., the importance of punishment strength, temptation, ecological context), but are not directly transferable to the efficiency prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost is modeled as parameter delta (δ), which is varied in the analysis. Moderate punishment cost can suppress defectors and promote coexistence; very high cost can reduce the abundance of punishers.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is modeled as the fine imposed on defectors (also δ). The impact of varying δ is analyzed; higher δ increases the cost to defectors and can promote cooperation, but also increases cost to punishers.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not report empirical or experimental data.
- The game is a repeated prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- Outcomes are reported as population frequencies and coexistence, not as group efficiency or total payoff.
- No explicit player count, group size, or round structure is modeled.
- No direct analysis of efficiency or welfare as defined in public goods games.
- The model assumes infinite, well-mixed populations and does not address finite group effects or stochasticity.
