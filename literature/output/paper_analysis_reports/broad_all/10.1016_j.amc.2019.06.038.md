Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the frequency of cooperators (cooperation rate) in spatial Prisoner's Dilemma and Snowdrift games. No explicit analysis of group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings is reported. The model is simulation-based and does not report payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that allowing link weights between players to adaptively increase or decrease based on reputation (interpreted as a form of social reward/punishment) leads to a substantial increase in the frequency of cooperation in spatial Prisoner's Dilemma and Snowdrift games. The effect is stronger as the range of possible link weights (controlled by ε) increases, which increases heterogeneity and supports the formation of cooperative clusters. The findings are robust across both game types. However, the paper does not analyze or report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, so its direct relevance to predicting efficiency outcomes is limited.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that dynamic, reputation-based social reward and punishment mechanisms (via link weight adjustment) can promote cooperation in spatial social dilemmas. However, because the outcomes are reported in terms of cooperation frequency rather than efficiency or group payoff, and because the model is a simulation of spatial PDG/SDG rather than a public goods game, its value for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like environments is limited and should be considered only as suggestive, not directly predictive.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run on a regular lattice of size L=100 (i.e., 10,000 players), with larger sizes (L=200, L=400) also tested for robustness. All players have 8 neighbors (Moore neighborhood).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 1,000,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with outcomes measured after the system reaches a stationary state (last 50,000 steps).
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate (C) or defect (D).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a decrease in link weight by u units when a player's reputation is lower than the average of neighbors. The magnitude of u and the range ε are varied. Increasing the range (ε) increases the effectiveness of the mechanism in promoting cooperation (not efficiency).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward is implemented as an increase in link weight by u units when a player's reputation is higher than the average of neighbors. The effect is to promote cooperation.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward effectiveness is determined by the size of u and the range ε. Higher ε allows greater heterogeneity and more effective reward/punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The model is simulation-based and not empirical; results are theoretical and may not generalize to real-world or experimental settings.
- The main outcome is cooperation frequency, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare; thus, the findings do not directly inform the prediction of efficiency effects.
- The game is a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma or Snowdrift game, not a public goods game or direct variant; MPCR and group-level payoff structures are not modeled.
- Punishment and reward are implemented as changes in link weights (network structure), not as costly actions with explicit payoffs.
- No analysis of payoff-based outcomes such as efficiency, total earnings, or surplus is provided.
