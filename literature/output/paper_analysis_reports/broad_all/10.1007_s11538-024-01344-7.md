## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the expected total cost to the institution for achieving a target cooperation level in finite-population PGGs depends strongly on the threshold for incentive application (t), the strength of selection (β), mutation rate (μ), and the type of incentive (reward, punishment, hybrid). For small thresholds (t=1), the cost function is always non-decreasing in the per-capita incentive cost, and explicit formulas are derived. For larger thresholds, the cost function can exhibit non-monotonic behavior and phase transitions, especially at high selection strength. Numerical results show that both reward and punishment incentives can be optimized for cost, but the optimal cost and its behavior depend on the game parameters and incentive scheme. The paper provides a framework for predicting the institutional cost required to achieve a desired cooperation level, given the game design and population parameters.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical and numerical evidence on how the cost of institutional incentives (reward, punishment, hybrid) required to achieve a target cooperation level in PGG-like environments depends on game design dimensions such as player count, group size, marginal per-capita return, and the structure of the incentive scheme (threshold t, cost per unit, effectiveness). While the main outcome is the institutional cost rather than group efficiency, the results are directly relevant for predicting how changes in punishment or reward design (e.g., cost, threshold, effectiveness) will affect the efficiency of institutional intervention. The paper is most informative for settings where the goal is to optimize the cost of achieving cooperation, rather than to maximize group payoff per se. It provides explicit formulas and numerical results that can be used to inform predictions about the efficiency of punishment or reward interventions, conditional on game design and baseline cooperation levels.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is entirely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- Analytical results are primarily for reward incentives; punishment and hybrid incentives are treated only numerically.
- The main outcome is the expected total cost to the institution to achieve a target cooperation level, not group efficiency or total group payoff per se.
- The model assumes well-mixed, finite populations and does not address structured populations or network effects.
- The analysis is limited to binary (all-or-nothing) contribution choices and does not consider continuous contribution levels.
- The approach assumes the institution has full information about population composition, which may not be realistic in all settings.
- The model does not include communication, reputation, or other social mechanisms beyond institutional incentives.
- The results may not generalize to games where the payoff difference between cooperators and defectors depends on the population composition in more complex ways (e.g., general prisoner's dilemma, collective risk games).
