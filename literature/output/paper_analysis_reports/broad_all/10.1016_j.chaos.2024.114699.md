Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is total social welfare (W_t), which is the sum of all player payoffs minus the cost of intervention. Fraction of cooperation (F_c) and fraction of groups with policy (F_g) are also tracked, but the focus is on payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that implementing endogenous reward and punishment policies in a regional public goods game increases both cooperation and total social welfare (W_t), especially when the intervention is targeted (partial intervention) rather than universal. The efficiency gains from punishment are largest when the cost of policy implementation is low or the punishment is strong. There is often an optimal fraction of groups to intervene in, beyond which additional intervention yields diminishing or negative returns due to policy costs. Size-based selection of groups for intervention is generally superior to random or degree-based selection. Allowing non-intervened groups to spontaneously adopt the policy can further boost cooperation and welfare, but only if the additional costs are offset by increased benefits. Terminating intervention leads to rapid collapse of cooperation and welfare, indicating the need for sustained policy.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical/simulation evidence that enabling punishment (and reward) in a PGG-like environment increases efficiency (total social welfare), but the magnitude and even the sign of the effect depend on the cost of policy implementation, the strength of punishment, and the fraction of groups covered. For prediction, the model suggests that punishment will generally increase efficiency relative to control, especially when the marginal per-capita return (mpcr) is not too low and policy costs are not too high. However, if policy costs are high or the environment is resource-poor (low mpcr), the efficiency gain may be small or even negative. The method of targeting intervention (e.g., size-based) can further moderate the effect. The findings are directly relevant for predicting efficiency outcomes in PGG variants with spatial or regional structure and endogenous institutional punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The main simulation uses N=1500 player nodes, but the model is general for large N.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 Monte Carlo Steps (MCS), with results averaged over the last 1,000 steps.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The normalized multiplier ω is varied; higher ω (higher mpcr) increases both cooperation and total social welfare, and makes punishment more effective.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. The cost of policy implementation (μ) is varied; higher μ reduces the net benefit of punishment and can make intervention less efficient.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (penalty β) is varied; higher β increases cooperation and social welfare, up to a point.
- reward availability: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward is implemented together with punishment: penalties collected from defectors are distributed to cooperators.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Groups can observe the status of neighboring groups' policies (SP) and imitate them, which facilitates the spread of cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The model assumes a specific spatial network structure (birandom geometric graph) and endogenous institutional punishment/reward, which may not generalize to all PGG settings.
- Reward is not independently parameterized; it is always coupled to punishment as redistribution of fines.
- No individual-level communication or chat is modeled.
- The model does not consider heterogeneity in player preferences or learning rules beyond stochastic exploration and imitation.
