The main dependent variable is the average payoff (group welfare) at equilibrium, as determined by the replicator dynamics. The paper also analyzes the equilibrium frequencies of different types of cooperators and defectors, but these are interpreted in terms of their effect on average payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a three-player repeated public goods game with an opt-out option (no explicit punishment), both tolerant and intolerant cooperators can survive, but their coexistence is not an evolutionary outcome. The equilibrium frequency of cooperators (and thus average payoff) increases with the benefit-to-cost ratio and decreases with the probability of group dissolution. The model shows that (partially) cooperative stable equilibria can exist, but the all-defector equilibrium is always asymptotically stable, making the initial evolution of cooperation difficult without additional mechanisms. The paper does not study punishment or sanctioning, so it does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is relevant for understanding the effect of voluntary partner refusal (opt-out) on cooperation and efficiency in small-group public goods games, but it does not address punishment or sanctioning. It provides analytic results on how group size, benefit-to-cost ratio, and group dissolution probability affect equilibrium cooperation and payoffs. However, it does not inform the prediction of efficiency changes due to punishment, as no punishment mechanism is present. Its results may be useful as a baseline for comparison to punishment-enabled games, especially regarding the efficiency achievable through partner choice alone.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The main analysis is for n=3 (three-player game), with some discussion of general n. All results are for three-player groups.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model assumes an infinite repetition of the stage game (repeated game with infinite horizon).
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: each player either cooperates (contributes) or defects (does not contribute) in each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is b/n, where b is the total benefit and n=3. The model varies b and c, with b>c>b/n>0.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Infinite repetition is assumed; the structure is common knowledge.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players observe the actions (cooperate/defect) of their partners in each round, as their strategy depends on the number of defectors among partners.

## Important Limitations
- The model does not include any punishment or sanctioning mechanism; all effects are due to voluntary exit (partner refusal).
- Results are specific to three-player groups; generalization to larger groups is not analyzed.
- The model assumes infinite repetition and infinite population size, which may not match experimental or real-world settings.
- No cost is imposed for opting out (stopping the interaction), which may not be realistic.
- No communication, reputation, or information constraints are modeled.
- The model only allows binary (all-or-nothing) cooperation choices.
