## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper argues that cooperation in resource dilemmas (including public goods and commons dilemmas) is shaped by individual value orientation (pro-social vs egoistic), situational constraints (such as group identification, communication, and social norms), and the degree and type of uncertainty (social and environmental). It proposes that pro-social individuals are more likely to cooperate regardless of uncertainty, while egoistic individuals are more likely to defect, especially under uncertainty. Situational factors (including the possibility of sanctions or rewards) can constrain egoism and promote cooperation. The paper reviews prior findings that rewards increase cooperation and sanctions decrease defection, but notes that the costs of such interventions may outweigh collective gains and that effects may not persist when interventions are removed. The analysis is theoretical and hypothesis-generating, not empirical.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides conceptual background on the psychological mechanisms that may moderate the effect of punishment, reward, and uncertainty on cooperation in public-goods-like environments. It suggests that punishment and reward can increase cooperation, but may not always improve efficiency due to their costs, and that their effects may not persist. The paper does not provide quantitative or empirical evidence for predicting efficiency outcomes from game design dimensions. Its main value for prediction is in highlighting the importance of situational constraints (including punishment/reward), group identification, communication, and uncertainty, and in warning that interventions may have costs that offset their benefits. However, it does not provide direct evidence for the efficiency impact of punishment in public goods games.
