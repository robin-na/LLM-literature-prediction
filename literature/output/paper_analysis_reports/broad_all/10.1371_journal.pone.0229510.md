# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The source of punishment matters: Third-party punishment restrains observers from selfish behaviors better than does second-party punishment by shaping norm perceptions
Authors: Chen, H. Z., Zeng, Z. J., & Ma, J. H.
Journal: Plos One
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- sharing behavior (amount shared in dictator game)
- descriptive norm perception
- injunctive norm perception
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the amount shared in a one-shot dictator game after exposure to punishment information. No group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings are reported. Outcomes are behavioral (sharing) and norm perceptions.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that third-party punishment (TPP) of unfair behavior in a dictator game leads observers to perceive higher descriptive and injunctive norms for sharing, and to share more themselves, compared to second-party punishment (SPP). Mediation analyses show that the effect on sharing is primarily through changes in descriptive norm perception. The effect is robust across two experiments with different manipulations (verbal blame and monetary punishment). However, the outcome is individual sharing behavior, not group efficiency or payoff, and the context is a dictator game, not a public goods game.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that the source of punishment (third-party vs. second-party) can affect observers' prosocial behavior by shaping norm perceptions, especially descriptive norms. While the findings are robust for sharing behavior in dictator games, they do not directly inform predictions about efficiency or group payoff in public goods games. The results suggest that punishment mechanisms that are perceived as norm-expressive (e.g., third-party punishment) may have stronger effects on prosocial behavior, but the transferability to efficiency outcomes in PGG-like environments is indirect and should be treated with caution.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Dictator game with three roles: dictator (A), recipient (B), and third party (C) in TPP; two roles in SPP. Observers are exposed to scenarios with 2 or 3 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. One-shot dictator game; participants make a single sharing decision after exposure to punishment information.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. In Experiment 1, the punishment is verbal feedback (message) from the second or third party to the dictator.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sharing choices are discrete but not all-or-nothing: participants choose among several possible transfer amounts (0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 points or 0-5 lottery tickets).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. In Experiment 2, monetary punishment costs the punisher (recipient or third party) CNY 1 to reduce the dictator's payoff by CNY 3. In Experiment 1, punishment is verbal and costless.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. In Experiment 2, each unit of punishment costs 1 and reduces the target's payoff by 3 (punishment effectiveness = 3). In Experiment 1, punishment is verbal (no direct payoff effect).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told the game is one-shot; the number of rounds is known and fixed.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are shown messages or information about previous players' actions and punishment decisions.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told whether the punisher is the recipient (second party) or a third party; the identity is specified in the scenario.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a dictator game, not a public goods game or its direct variant, so findings may not generalize to PGG-like environments.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are measured; only individual sharing behavior and norm perceptions are reported.
- The effect of punishment is only on observers' subsequent sharing, not on the original target of punishment or on group-level outcomes.
- Sample sizes are relatively small (about 120 per experiment), limiting statistical power.
- Punishment is manipulated as either verbal (costless) or monetary (costly), but only the observer's behavior is measured, not the effect on the punished party.
