Outcome notes:
The paper's dependent variables are primarily contribution rates and punishment behavior. There is no direct analysis or reporting of group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare. Payoff structure is described, but efficiency or total earnings are not reported as outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that the effectiveness of punishment in promoting cooperation in public goods games depends critically on the perceived probability of enforcement. When punishment is enforced with high probability (p=1 or p=0.8), contributions are higher and sustained over time. When enforcement is weak (p=0.2), contributions decline and punishment does not deter free-riding. The study also finds that punishment behavior is backward-looking: individuals who escaped punishment in the past are more likely to be punished later. However, the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes directly.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the credibility of punishment enforcement is a key moderator of punishment's effect on cooperation in public goods games. While it does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, the contribution results imply that efficiency gains from punishment are only realized when enforcement is highly credible. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the effectiveness of punishment in increasing efficiency is contingent on the probability of enforcement. However, the lack of direct efficiency or payoff data limits the precision of quantitative predictions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 10 periods (rounds) in all treatments.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each subject decides how much of their 20-unit endowment to contribute (0-20).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is 0.4 (each unit contributed yields 0.4 to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction point costs the punisher 1 unit.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction point reduces the punished player's payoff by 3 units.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism; only punishment is available.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of rounds (10) is publicly announced to participants.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, subjects are informed of group members' contributions and, when punishment is enforced, the punishment points received and costs.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contributions are identified by a fixed ID number; punishment is assigned to specific group members by ID, but it is not explicitly stated whether punished players know who punished them.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes directly; only contribution and punishment behavior are analyzed.
- No direct quantitative estimates of the effect of punishment on efficiency or total earnings are provided.
- The study only manipulates the probability of punishment enforcement; other dimensions (e.g., punishment cost, group size) are held constant.
- The experiment is conducted with undergraduate students in a lab setting, which may limit generalizability.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanism is included.
