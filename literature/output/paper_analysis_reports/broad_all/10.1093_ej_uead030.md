# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Rice Farming and the Origins of Cooperative Behaviour
Authors: Zhou, X. Y., Alysandratos, T., & Naef, M.
Journal: Economic Journal
Year: 2023

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution level in PGG (with and without punishment)
- punishment points assigned to free riders
- punishment points assigned to cooperators
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are contribution rates and punishment behavior. The paper does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare outcomes. All analyses focus on behavioral measures (contribution, punishment) rather than payoff-based efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that (1) participants from wetland rice regions contribute more in both the no-punishment and punishment conditions of a repeated PGG, and (2) participants from rice regions assign more punishment points to free riders than those from non-rice regions. The difference in contribution is larger in the punishment condition. However, the paper does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be directly determined. The results are robust to a wide range of controls and IV strategies, and are interpreted as evidence of persistent cultural norms of cooperation and punishment rooted in historical rice farming.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the presence of a punishment option increases contribution rates in a repeated PGG, and that the magnitude of this effect is moderated by cultural background (rice vs. non-rice regions). However, because the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings, it cannot be used to directly predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings are useful for predicting contribution behavior under different cultural backgrounds, but not for direct efficiency prediction as required by the downstream task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 participants in all PGG sessions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 8 rounds of PGG without punishment, followed by 8 rounds with punishment (16 rounds total).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is continuous: participants choose how many points (0-20) to contribute each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (group multiplier 1.6, group size 4).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment token costs 1 point to the punisher.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment token reduces the punished participant's earnings by 3 points.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism; only punishment is available.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know there are 8 rounds in each condition, but do not know the content of the second condition until the first is complete.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants see the contributions of all group members (in random order), group account earnings, and total earnings.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punished participants are told only the total punishment tokens received, not the identity of the punishers.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, total earnings, or welfare outcomes; all main results are based on contribution and punishment behavior.
- The effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff cannot be determined from the reported results.
- The sample consists of Han Chinese first-year university students, which may limit generalizability.
- Cultural background (rice vs. non-rice) is the main treatment variable; results may not generalize to other contexts or populations.
- No reward mechanism is included, so results are limited to punishment-only settings.
