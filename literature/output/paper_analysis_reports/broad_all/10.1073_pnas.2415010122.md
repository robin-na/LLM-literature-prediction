# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Maintaining cooperation through vertical communication of trust when removing sanctions
Authors: Posten, A. C., Ugurlar, P., Kube, S., & Lammers, J.
Journal: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America
Year: 2025

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution amount (tokens contributed to public good)
- self-reported trust in coplayers
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the amount contributed to the public good (as a proxy for cooperation). No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings is reported. Trust is measured as a psychological mediator.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that when punishment institutions are removed from a public goods game, contributions (i.e., cooperation) typically drop sharply. However, if the authority communicates trust in participants' willingness to cooperate, this drop is substantially reduced, and contribution levels remain similar to those observed when punishment is still in place. This effect is observed for centralized, third-party, and peer punishment mechanisms. The effect of vertical trust communication persists beyond a single round and is mediated by increased trust in coplayers. The study does not report efficiency or group payoff, so the direct effect on efficiency cannot be determined, but the findings strongly suggest that vertical trust communication can substitute for punishment in maintaining cooperation rates.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the removal of punishment institutions in public goods games leads to a sharp decline in cooperation (contributions), but that this decline can be prevented if the authority communicates trust in participants' willingness to cooperate. While the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, the close link between contribution rates and efficiency in standard PGGs suggests that vertical trust communication could maintain efficiency at levels similar to those with punishment, at least in the short term. For prediction tasks, the paper suggests that the presence or absence of vertical trust communication is a critical moderator when punishment is removed, but does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or payoff outcomes. The game design dimensions (player count, rounds, MPCR, etc.) are well specified, but the main outcome is contribution, not efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All studies use groups of 4 players in the public goods game.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Most studies use 2 rounds (with and without punishment); Study 4 uses 3 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players; setup was completely anonymous.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players decide how many tokens (0-10) to contribute.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each token contributed to the public good is doubled and split among 4 players: MPCR = 0.5.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost varies by institution: e.g., in centralized punishment, 1.6 tokens are subtracted per token not contributed (cost to punished, not punisher); in third-party and peer punishment, up to 15 tokens can be subtracted (destroyed) by the punisher. The cost to the punisher is not always specified, but in third-party/peer punishment, tokens are destroyed (not transferred).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is specified: in centralized, 1.6 tokens lost per token not contributed; in third-party/peer, up to 15 tokens can be subtracted per punisher's decision.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is present in any study; only punishment is used.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were informed about the total number of rounds in advance.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is contribution (cooperation), not efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings. No direct analysis of efficiency or welfare is reported.
- The effect of vertical trust communication is only tested in the context of removing punishment, not in the presence of punishment or in other institutional settings.
- The studies are conducted online with US participants recruited via Prolific, which may limit generalizability.
- No feedback is given to participants about others' behavior or payoffs during the experiment, which may affect ecological validity.
- The cost to the punisher in third-party and peer punishment is not always fully specified.
