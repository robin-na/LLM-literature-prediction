Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:

Outcome notes:
The paper develops a theoretical model of group population dynamics (cooperators, defectors, regulators) in self-organized societies, focusing on the stable fractions of each group. Outcomes are group composition and stability, not efficiency, welfare, or group payoff. No explicit payoff or efficiency measures are reported; the model is not a direct PGG or public goods game, but is conceptually related.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The authors develop a system-theoretic, utility-based model for the evolution of group fractions (cooperators, defectors, regulators) in self-organized societies. They show analytically that, under resource constraints, neither defectors nor regulators can exceed about 10% of the population in stable equilibrium. The model demonstrates that cooperation can persist without explicit punishment mechanisms, provided resources are finite. When regulators (punishers) are present, their fraction is also limited by resource costs. The model is applied to real-world data from countries, but only to estimate group fractions and stability, not efficiency or payoff. No direct predictions or measurements of efficiency, group payoff, or surplus are made.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar experimental designs. It offers a theoretical perspective that, in resource-constrained societies, both defectors and regulators (punishers) are limited in equilibrium, and cooperation can be stable even without explicit punishment. However, the model does not map onto the game design dimensions or efficiency outcomes used in experimental PGG research. Thus, it should not be used as direct evidence for predicting efficiency changes due to punishment in PGG-like environments, but may provide indirect, high-level context about the limits of punishment and defection in self-organized systems.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- punishment cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. The cost of regulators (punishers) is a key parameter (b) in the model, representing the resource burden of punishment. High punishment cost leads to elimination of regulators; moderate cost allows coexistence. However, this is not mapped to experimental punishment cost per unit.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not report experimental or empirical data on efficiency, payoff, or welfare.
- The model is population-based and does not correspond to discrete, finite-player, round-based public goods games.
- No direct measures of efficiency, group payoff, or surplus are reported; only group fractions and stability are analyzed.
- Punishment is modeled as a regulator group with resource cost, not as a strategic choice or action by individuals.
- No mapping to game design dimensions such as player count, rounds, MPCR, or punishment effectiveness.
- Findings are not directly transferable to experimental PGG settings or to the prediction of efficiency outcomes.
