## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperators (cooperation rate)
- spatial distribution of cooperators and defectors
Outcome notes:
The paper's main outcome is the fraction of cooperators (\rho_c), i.e., the proportion of agents using the cooperative strategy in the spatial prisoner's dilemma. No group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings are reported. The study is simulation-based and does not analyze payoff-based outcomes directly.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing environment-based preference selection (parameter \alpha) and environmental heterogeneity (parameter u) into a spatial prisoner's dilemma game increases the fraction of cooperators. The combination of these mechanisms is more effective than either alone. The effect is robust across a range of parameters and network sizes. However, the paper does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or any payoff-based outcome, and does not include punishment or reward mechanisms. The findings are thus relevant for understanding cooperation dynamics but not directly for predicting efficiency changes due to punishment in public goods games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. It does not include punishment, reward, or efficiency outcomes. Its findings on cooperation rates in spatial prisoner's dilemma games with preference selection and heterogeneity may be contextually informative for understanding mechanisms that promote cooperation, but are not directly transferable to the prediction task focused on punishment and efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a square lattice of 100x100 players (10,000), with robustness checks at 200x200 and 400x400. All players interact with 4 neighbors.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 60,000 Monte Carlo steps, with the last 10,000 used for averaging. The game is iterated, not a fixed number of rounds as in typical PGG experiments.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate (C) or defect (D), i.e., all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Payoff structure: T=b (temptation), R=1 (mutual cooperation), P=0 (mutual defection), S=0 (sucker's payoff). Not a PGG, so no MPCR per se, but analogous parameters are reported.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses agent-based simulations, not empirical data.
- The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- No punishment or reward mechanisms are included; thus, the paper does not inform about the effect of punishment on efficiency.
- The game is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game or direct variant.
- Findings may not generalize to PGG-like environments with punishment or to efficiency outcomes.
