## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that multi-step punishment (punishment lasting multiple time steps for low-reputation individuals) can promote cooperation in a spatial prisoner's dilemma game, but only if the punishment duration is neither too short nor too long. There is an optimal punishment time window that maximizes the frequency of cooperation. Too short punishment fails to deter defection, while too long punishment can backfire and reduce cooperation. The intensity of punishment (how much payoffs are reduced for punished individuals) is the dominant factor in promoting cooperation. The model does not include a cost for punishment, and does not analyze group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. All results are based on the proportion of cooperators in the population.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about the effect of punishment on cooperation rates in a spatial prisoner's dilemma, but does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. The findings suggest that punishment can increase cooperation rates, especially with optimal duration and sufficient intensity, but the absence of punishment cost and lack of payoff analysis limit its direct applicability to predicting efficiency in public goods games. The model is adjacent to PGGs and may inform qualitative expectations about punishment's effect on cooperation, but not on efficiency or welfare. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper is only indirectly informative.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a 100x100 lattice (10,000 players), each occupying a node. All results are for large populations.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for at least 50,000 Monte Carlo steps; steady-state cooperation is measured as the average over the last 5,000 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete actions: cooperate or defect (binary choice).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is explicitly modeled as costless to the punisher; there is no cost for administering punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is parameterized by intensity w (reduction in payoff for punished individuals). Higher w increases cooperation rates.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGGs.
- No cost for punishment is included, so efficiency, welfare, or group payoff effects of punishment are not analyzed.
- The main outcome is the frequency of cooperation, not efficiency or total group payoff.
- All results are from agent-based simulations, not empirical experiments.
- The model assumes a very large population (10,000 agents) and may not generalize to small groups.
