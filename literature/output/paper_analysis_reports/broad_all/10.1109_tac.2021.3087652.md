Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are theoretical characterizations of when cooperation (information sharing) can be sustained at equilibrium, and the value functions associated with different strategies. The payoff is not monetary but is defined as information gain (reduction in uncertainty) minus cost of sharing. Simulations illustrate the size of cooperation regions but do not report empirical group payoffs.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in dynamic information sharing games with two agents, cooperation (information sharing) is not sustainable in finite-horizon settings, but can be sustained in infinite-horizon settings using punishment strategies (CGT). The equilibrium region where cooperation is optimal is characterized, and its size increases as the cost of sharing decreases or the discount factor increases. Theoretical results show that the maximal equilibrium region is unique and can be computed iteratively. Simulations confirm that lower costs and more informative observations lead to larger cooperation regions. Thus, enabling punishment strategies increases the efficiency of the system (total expected information gain minus cost) compared to the baseline where no sharing occurs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that enabling punishment strategies in repeated/dynamic information sharing games can increase efficiency (total payoff) compared to the baseline with no punishment, provided the game is sufficiently long (infinite horizon) and the cost of sharing is not too high. The effect is more positive when the cost of sharing is low and the discount factor is high. The model is adjacent to public goods games: the 'public good' is information, and the payoff is information gain minus cost. The results suggest that, in similar dynamic games, enabling punishment will increase efficiency, especially as the game becomes more repeated and the cost of cooperation decreases. However, the model is limited to two players, binary actions, and does not include monetary payoffs or group sizes greater than two.

## Important Limitations
- The model is limited to two agents; results may not generalize to larger groups.
- Payoff is defined as information gain minus cost, not as monetary or standard public goods payoff.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are theoretical or simulation-based.
- Punishment is implemented as a perfect grim trigger; real-world punishment may be less effective or more costly.
- No reward mechanisms are considered; only punishment (withdrawal of cooperation) is modeled.
- No communication or chat channel beyond the sharing of observations.
- The model assumes agents are fully rational and have perfect monitoring of actions.
- Simulations are illustrative and do not report standard efficiency metrics from public goods games.
