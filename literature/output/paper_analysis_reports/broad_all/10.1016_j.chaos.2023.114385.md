## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a spatial public goods game with tax-supported punishment, the prevalence of cooperation, defection, and punishment strategies depends sensitively on the synergy factor, tax level, punishment cost, and fine. When the tax exceeds the cost of punishment, punishing cooperators can dominate. At low tax and high punishment cost, defectors dominate. Intermediate regimes can produce coexistence of multiple strategies, including cyclic dominance. The spatial structure allows for richer dynamics than well-mixed models, including new three-strategy coexistence states. However, the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, so the direct effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence about how the prevalence of cooperation and punishment strategies depends on game design dimensions such as player group size, number of strategies, spatial structure, tax level, punishment cost, and fine. However, because it does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. It is useful for understanding which parameter regimes are likely to support cooperation or complex coexistence, which may indirectly inform efficiency predictions if one assumes that higher cooperation rates correspond to higher efficiency, but this link is not established or quantified in the paper.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 5 players (G=5), arranged on a square lattice. System size (L) varies, but local group size is fixed.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run until stationary state; not round-based in the experimental sense, but evolutionary updates proceed until equilibrium.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: players either contribute 1 or 0 to the public good.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r is varied between 3 and 5; MPCR is r/G (G=5).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Individual cost of punishment (G_P) is varied between 0 and 1. High cost reduces prevalence of punishing strategies.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (fine per unit cost, beta) is varied between 0 and 1. Higher fines can promote punishing strategies and cooperation, but effects depend on other parameters.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or total earnings; only strategy frequencies and qualitative system states are reported.
- Findings are based on theoretical simulations, not empirical or experimental data.
- The mapping from strategy prevalence to efficiency is not established or quantified.
- The model assumes a spatial lattice structure, which may not generalize to well-mixed or other network structures.
- No human or animal subjects; results are for abstract agents in simulation.
