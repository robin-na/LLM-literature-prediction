## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that punishment can increase group efficiency (fitness) in public goods game environments, but the pathway depends on the type of punisher. Selfish punishers can invade selfish populations and reduce cheating, thereby increasing group payoff and paving the way for altruistic punishers, who further increase group efficiency. The effectiveness of punishment in raising group payoff is higher when punishment costs are low, group size is small, and the number of rounds (iterations) is high. The model directly analyzes how these design dimensions affect the ability of punishment to increase group efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that enabling punishment in a public goods game can increase group efficiency, especially when punishment costs are low, group size is small, and the number of rounds is high. The model is directly based on a standard PGG and manipulates key design dimensions, making its findings highly relevant for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency. The results suggest that the efficiency gain from punishment is larger when punishment is less costly and when group structure allows for stronger group selection (smaller groups, longer association). The findings are most applicable to settings where punishment leads to exclusion or strong consequences for defectors.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and agent-based, not empirical; results may not fully generalize to real human behavior.
- Punishment is modeled as expulsion from the group, which may not match all experimental or real-world punishment mechanisms.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanisms are considered.
- No communication, reputation, or identity effects are modeled.
- Contribution is a binary trait (altruist vs selfish), not a continuous choice.
- Power asymmetries and individual differences in punishment effectiveness are not included.
- The model assumes asexual reproduction and random group formation, which may not match all real-world settings.
