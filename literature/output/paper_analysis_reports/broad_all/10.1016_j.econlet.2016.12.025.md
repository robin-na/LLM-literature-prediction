## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in repeated games with heterogeneous players (random productivity), efficient outcomes may require some players to defect in certain matches. In such settings, standard public monitoring is insufficient to sustain efficiency, but contagious punishment strategies—where players switch to permanent defection after observing a deviation—can support high payoffs if the group is sufficiently patient, punishment is severe enough, and productivity differences are not too large. The paper provides explicit conditions under which these strategies are equilibrium and efficiency is achieved, and derives closed-form expressions for continuation payoffs. Thus, punishment (in the form of contagious, community-based sanctions) can enable high efficiency even in complex, asymmetric environments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical support for the idea that punishment mechanisms (specifically, contagious punishment) can increase efficiency in repeated social dilemma games, even when the efficient outcome involves some defections due to heterogeneity. The results are most directly applicable to repeated Prisoner's Dilemma games with random productivity, but the mechanisms and equilibrium conditions are relevant for predicting when punishment will improve efficiency in public-goods-like environments with heterogeneity. The paper does not provide empirical estimates or direct mappings to standard PGG parameters, but it does clarify that punishment can sustain high efficiency if players are patient, punishment is strong, and heterogeneity is moderate. For prediction, this suggests that enabling punishment in heterogeneous repeated games is likely to increase efficiency, provided the punishment is sufficiently severe and the group is not too heterogeneous.

## Important Limitations
- The analysis is entirely theoretical; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- The model is based on infinitely repeated games, which may not map directly to finite laboratory or field settings.
- Punishment is modeled as a switch to permanent defection (grim trigger), which may be more severe than typical experimental punishment mechanisms.
- The setting is a repeated asymmetric Prisoner's Dilemma, not a standard public goods game, though the social dilemma structure is similar.
- The model assumes random matching and private information about productivity, which may differ from standard PGG designs.
