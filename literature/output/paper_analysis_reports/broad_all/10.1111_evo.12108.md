# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: HUMAN COOPERATION BASED ON PUNISHMENT REPUTATION
Authors: dos Santos, M., Rankin, D. J., & Wedekind, C.
Journal: Evolution
Year: 2013

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- net gains (sum of received donations minus punishment costs)
- total payoffs
- group payoffs
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of cooperation
- probability of punishing defection
- punishment reputation score
- Donor's probability of giving
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are both behavioral (cooperation, punishment) and payoff-based (net gains, group payoffs). Efficiency per se is not directly reported, but group payoffs and net gains are closely related.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that the ability to punish defectors, combined with the visibility of punishment reputation, leads to higher cooperation and higher group payoffs. Punishers are compensated for their costs through increased cooperation from others, and in some groups, punishers even achieve net benefits. When punishment or its reputation is removed, cooperation and payoffs decline. The effect is driven by the threat of punishment and the reputational benefits it brings, not by the mere display of past punishment behavior.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling punishment, especially when punishment reputation is visible, increases group payoffs and compensates punishers, thus improving efficiency-related outcomes. For prediction tasks, the presence of punishment and the visibility of punishment reputation are key moderators. The game is not a standard public goods game but a helping game with punishment and reputation, so direct quantitative transfer to PGGs should be cautious, but the qualitative effect of punishment on efficiency is robustly positive when reputation is present.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 7 to 9 players; group size is fixed within each group but varies across groups.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 16 rounds per session; number of rounds not communicated to players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (donation) is binary: Donor can either donate (pay 1 CHF for Receiver to get 2 CHF) or refuse.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return for donation is 2 (Receiver gets 2 CHF for Donor's cost of 1 CHF), but this is a dyadic helping game, not a standard PGG.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher (Receiver) pays 1 CHF to make Donor lose 2 CHF.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: 1 CHF cost to punisher causes 2 CHF loss to target.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Total number of rounds (16) was not communicated to players.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Donor's decision and Receiver's ID and punishment score were displayed to all players during the first 8 rounds.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Receiver's ID and punishment score were displayed, but Donor's ID was not shown. Thus, punishment actions were attributed to Receivers by ID.

## Important Limitations
- The game is a dyadic helping game with punishment and reputation, not a standard public goods game, so direct quantitative transfer to PGGs may be limited.
- Efficiency is not directly measured as a ratio to full cooperation, but group payoffs and net gains are reported.
- Partner choice is not allowed; all interactions are assigned, which may affect generalizability to settings with partner selection.
- The number of rounds is not known to participants, which may affect endgame behavior.
- The effect of punishment is confounded with the visibility of punishment reputation; the effect of punishment without reputation is weaker.
