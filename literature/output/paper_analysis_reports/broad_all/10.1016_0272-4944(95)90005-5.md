## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperators (cooperation rate)
- transition states between cooperation and defection
- spatial distribution of cooperators and defectors
Outcome notes:
The paper reports on the fraction of cooperators and the dynamics of cooperation/defection, but does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or any direct payoff-based outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The main finding is that a reputation mechanism that rewards/punishes based on the number of consecutive rounds a player has cooperated or defected can substantially increase the fraction of cooperators in a spatial PDG. Higher reputation factors and higher upper bounds on consistent strategy times both further promote cooperation. The mechanism is more effective and leads to faster stabilization of cooperation than traditional reputation mechanisms. However, the paper does not analyze or report on efficiency or group payoff, so its findings are limited to behavioral outcomes (cooperation rates) rather than payoff-based outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that reputation-based punishment/reward mechanisms can increase cooperation rates in spatial PDG environments, and that the strength and structure of the reputation mechanism matter. However, because it does not report efficiency or group payoff, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-game-like environments. Its relevance is further limited by the use of a spatial PDG rather than a PGG or direct payoff-based analysis. It may be useful for mechanism design intuition, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a spatial grid of 100x100 players (N=10000), each with 4 neighbors.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 time steps, with statistics averaged over the last 2,000 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate or defect (binary choice).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reputation-based rewards exist for consistent cooperation and for defectors who switch to cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a spatial prisoner's dilemma game (PDG), not a public goods game (PGG), so results may not transfer directly to PGG-like environments.
- No efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates and behavioral transitions are analyzed.
- Punishment and reward are implemented as changes to reputation, not as direct payoff costs or benefits, limiting comparability to standard punishment/reward mechanisms in PGGs.
- The model is entirely simulation-based and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- No analysis of how the reputation mechanism would perform in non-spatial or non-networked environments.
