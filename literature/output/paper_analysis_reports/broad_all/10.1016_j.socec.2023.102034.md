# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Intragroup punishment and intergroup conflict aversion weaken intragroup cooperation in finitely repeated games
Authors: Tan, J. H. W., & Bolle, F.
Journal: Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Economics
Year: 2023

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- conditional cooperation (response to partner/rival behavior)
- use of punishment strategies (behavioral, not costly punishment)
- dynamic strategies (e.g., trigger strategies, tit-for-tat, unyielding defection)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the binary cooperation choice (contribute or not). The paper analyzes how cooperation rates change across treatments and over time, and how they respond to partner and rival actions. There is no direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare; payoff structure is discussed, but outcomes are reported in terms of cooperation rates and behavioral strategies.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that intragroup cooperation is sensitive to the presence of intergroup rivalry and the payoff structure. Cooperation is lower when it inflicts losses on rivals, even when socially efficient, and higher when synergy within the group is increased. Participants use dynamic strategies, such as punishing partner defection with unyielding defection, but do not respond similarly to rival actions. Outgroup altruism (conflict aversion) further reduces cooperation that would harm rivals. Cooperation rates decline over time due to these mechanisms. The study does not include a costly punishment stage or analyze efficiency or group payoff as an outcome.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of enabling costly punishment on efficiency in public goods games. The 'punishment' discussed is behavioral (trigger strategies, unyielding defection) rather than a costly punishment mechanism. The findings are relevant for understanding how dynamic strategies and group-specific preferences affect cooperation, but not for predicting efficiency changes due to the introduction of a punishment option. The game structure is close to a PGG, but the absence of a costly punishment treatment and lack of efficiency or payoff outcomes limit its direct applicability to the prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game has 4 players, divided into two groups of 2.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game is repeated for 10 periods (rounds).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication between participants was not allowed; computer terminals were partitioned to prevent communication.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate by contributing full endowment to group project or defect by contributing zero.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (mpcr) within group is r_in: 2/3 in PD, Info, Rivalry; 5/6 in Synergy.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants knew the game would last 10 periods.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Feedback on partner and rival actions was provided in all games except PD (no rival feedback in PD).

## Important Limitations
- No costly punishment mechanism is present; 'punishment' refers to behavioral strategies (e.g., unyielding defection) rather than a separate punishment stage.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; all main results are in terms of cooperation rates and behavioral responses.
- The game is a close variant of a public goods game but includes intergroup externalities (helping ingroup can harm outgroup), which may limit direct transferability to standard PGG settings.
- Findings on punishment are not about the effect of enabling a punishment option, but about endogenous behavioral strategies.
- No analysis of how introducing a costly punishment option would affect efficiency or payoff.
