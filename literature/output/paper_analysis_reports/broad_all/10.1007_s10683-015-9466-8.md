## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that third-party punishment of selfish behavior is robust to manipulations of third-party endowment (i.e., whether the punisher is relatively poorer or richer than the dictator) and to the use of the strategy method versus 'hot' decisions. Punishment is strongly associated with self-reported anger, not envy. The presence of third-party punishment is anticipated by actors and recipients, and actors who expect more punishment are less likely to behave selfishly. However, the paper does not report on group efficiency, welfare, or total payoffs, and thus does not directly inform the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-like environments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that third-party punishment in dictator games is motivated by anger at norm violations rather than by envy or artifacts of the strategy method. However, because the design is a one-shot (or single-round) dictator game with a third-party punisher, and does not report group efficiency, welfare, or total payoffs, it does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in repeated public goods games or similar environments. The findings are most relevant for understanding the psychological drivers of punishment, and suggest that punishment is robust to certain design manipulations (endowment, strategy method), but do not inform the efficiency consequences of enabling punishment.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 3 players: actor, recipient, and third-party punisher.

- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3:1 (each cent spent by the punisher reduces the actor's payoff by 3 cents).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game is one-shot (single round).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Third parties are informed of the actor's decision before punishing. No mention of additional summary information.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. The experiment is fully anonymous; punished actors do not know the identity of the punisher.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a one-shot (single round) dictator game with third-party punishment, not a repeated public goods game.
- No group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff outcomes are reported; only punishment behavior and emotions are measured.
- The findings may not generalize to repeated or multi-player public goods games, or to environments where efficiency is the main outcome of interest.
- The study is conducted online (MTurk) with low stakes, which may limit generalizability to higher-stakes or face-to-face settings.
- The design does not allow for endogenous emergence of cooperation or group-level efficiency effects.
