# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Can the Threat of Economic Sanctions Ensure the Sustainability of International Fisheries? An Experiment of a Dynamic Non-cooperative CPR Game with Uncertain Tipping Point
Authors: Selles, J., Bonhommeau, S., Guillotreau, P., & Vallee, T.
Journal: Environmental & Resource Economics
Year: 2020

Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the ratio of harvest to myopic strategy (a behavioral measure of cooperation). Payoff-related outcomes (profit, earnings) are reported in Table 4, but the main statistical analysis and effect direction focus on behavioral outcomes. Efficiency, welfare, or group payoff are not the primary focus, and efficiency as a ratio to the fully cooperative optimum is not directly reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Introducing an endogenous economic sanction (a fixed cost triggered by resource depletion below a threshold) in a dynamic CPR game leads to more cooperative behavior, less overexploitation, and higher average resource stocks compared to a baseline with no sanction. The effect is even stronger when the location of the threshold is uncertain. However, while sanctions reduce the frequency of myopic (selfish) strategies, many groups still fail to avoid the threshold, and only a few achieve near-optimal resource management. Average group profit is lower and more variable in sanction treatments due to the high penalty for crossing the threshold, but overall, the threat of sanctions shifts behavior toward greater cooperation and less rent dissipation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that introducing a punishment mechanism (here, an economic sanction triggered by overexploitation) in a dynamic, non-cooperative CPR game increases cooperative behavior and reduces overexploitation, which should translate into higher efficiency relative to the baseline. However, the effect on group profit is nuanced: while sanctions reduce overexploitation, groups that cross the threshold incur large losses, and only a minority achieve near-optimal outcomes. For prediction, the presence of a punishment/sanction mechanism is likely to increase efficiency on average, but the effect may be heterogeneous depending on group behavior and the risk of triggering the sanction. The game design here is a dynamic CPR with 3 players, no chat, continuous contribution (harvest), and a punishment that is endogenous and collective (not peer-to-peer). The findings are most informative for similar dynamic CPR or threshold public goods settings.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups consisted of 3 players, representing 'countries' in the fishery.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of rounds was unknown to participants (to approximate an infinite horizon), but there was a maximum duration and analysis focused on the first 15 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No face-to-face communication allowed; only non-binding pledges communicated to the group.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Harvest (contribution) choices were discrete but not all-or-nothing: players chose harvest from 0 to 5 units per round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not a standard linear PGG; returns are determined by a logistic growth function and profit formula. Marginal returns depend on stock and harvest, not a fixed MPCR.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is a fixed cost (alpha = 30 units) imposed on all players if the resource falls below a threshold. Not peer-to-peer; cost is collective and endogenous.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is highly effective: crossing the threshold triggers a large fixed cost for all. The threat of this sanction increases cooperation.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players did not know the total number of rounds; only the maximum duration was known. This was to avoid end-game effects.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants were informed about everyone's decisions, their own cumulative profit, and track records of total catch, profit, and own decision. They also saw a projection of future resource status.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is behavioral (cooperation, myopic strategies), not direct efficiency or group payoff; efficiency as a ratio to the fully cooperative optimum is not reported.
- The punishment mechanism is not peer-to-peer but a collective, endogenous sanction triggered by group overexploitation; findings may not generalize to standard PGG peer punishment settings.
- The experiment uses student subjects in a laboratory setting; external validity to real-world international fisheries or large-scale CPRs is limited.
- Communication is limited to non-binding pledges; no face-to-face or richer communication, which is known to affect cooperation in CPR settings.
- Only three-player groups were used; effects of group size or heterogeneity are not tested.
- The dynamic, threshold-based CPR design differs from standard linear PGGs, so direct quantitative transfer to classic PGGs may be limited.
