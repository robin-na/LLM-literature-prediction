# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Cooperation, punishment and immigration
Authors: Pin, P., & Rogers, B. W.
Journal: Journal of Economic Theory
Year: 2015

## Study Type
theory; empirical subtype=N/A; experimental subtype=N/A

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- expected lifetime payoff (utility) for cooperators, defectors, and immigrant defectors
Non-payoff outcomes:
- proportion of cooperators in society (q)
- probability of cooperation for immigrants and citizens (p_I, p_D)
- comparative statics of cooperation rates under different punishment regimes
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the equilibrium cooperation rates among citizens and immigrants, and the steady-state proportion of cooperators. Payoff-based outcomes (expected utility) are derived but not the main focus; efficiency or group payoff is not directly analyzed. The model is theoretical and does not report empirical or simulated group efficiency or welfare.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that increasing the punishment (expulsion) rate for immigrant defectors increases the overall cooperation rate in society, but decreases the cooperation rate among citizens. This is due to an equilibrium substitution effect: as immigrants are more strongly incentivized to cooperate, the overall level of cooperation rises above its 'natural' level, making defection more attractive for citizens. The model shows that the net effect of punishment is smaller than would be predicted if equilibrium effects were ignored. The paper does not directly analyze efficiency or group payoff, but expected payoffs for different agent types are derived. The results are robust to heterogeneity in agent preferences.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that punishment targeted at a subset of players (immigrants) can increase overall cooperation rates, but may also induce more defection among the untargeted group (citizens), reducing the net effect on cooperation. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff may be less positive than expected if substitution effects are present. However, the model does not directly report efficiency or group payoff, so its quantitative value for predicting efficiency is limited. The model is adjacent to public goods games, as it is based on a networked prisoner's dilemma with binary (all-or-nothing) choices and dynamic linking, rather than a standard PGG. The findings are most relevant for understanding equilibrium behavioral responses to targeted punishment policies.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The model uses a continuum mass of agents; there is no fixed finite player count, but each agent maintains k out-links. The results are not sensitive to a specific player count.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The model is dynamic and infinite-horizon (discrete time, steady state), not a fixed number of rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each agent makes a binary (all-or-nothing) choice at birth: cooperate or defect for life.
- punishment cost: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Punishment is modeled as expulsion of immigrant defectors; the cost to the punisher (government) is not modeled or analyzed.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a probability of expulsion (parameter v) for immigrant defectors. Increasing v increases the incentive for immigrants to cooperate and overall cooperation, but reduces citizen cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental evidence.
- The model is based on a networked prisoner's dilemma, not a standard public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are not directly analyzed; only expected payoffs for agent types and cooperation rates are derived.
- Punishment is modeled as government expulsion of immigrant defectors, not as peer punishment; costs of punishment are not modeled.
- The model assumes agents make a one-time, irreversible choice to cooperate or defect at birth, which differs from repeated or round-based decision-making in most PGG experiments.
- No analysis of the cost-effectiveness or optimality of punishment policies, as enforcement costs are not modeled.
- No communication, reward, or information-sharing mechanisms are included.
