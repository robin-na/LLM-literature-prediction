# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The impact of group membership on punishment versus partner rejection
Authors: Loustau, T., Glassman, J., Martin, J. W., Young, L., & Mcauliffe, K.
Journal: Scientific Reports
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment assigned (amount or binary)
- partner rejection (binary)
- sensitivity to group membership, intent, and outcome
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the amount of punishment assigned (either continuous or binary) and the decision to reject a partner (binary). No group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings are reported or analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Across two preregistered online experiments, participants responded to resource allocation decisions by either punishing (subtracting from the allocator's bonus) or rejecting (choosing not to interact again). In both a minimal group paradigm and a real-world political group context, participants punished outgroup members more than ingroup members, especially when the allocator's intent was selfish and the outcome was unequal. However, group membership did not affect the likelihood of partner rejection. The main outcomes are punishment and rejection rates; no efficiency or group payoff measures are reported.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency or group payoff in public goods or similar games with and without punishment. The design is adjacent to PGGs but focuses on dyadic allocation games with punishment and partner rejection as responses. The main contribution is to show that group membership increases punishment of outgroup members, but this is not linked to efficiency or welfare outcomes. Thus, the paper is not directly informative for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like environments, but it does provide evidence that group membership can moderate punishment behavior.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are dyadic (2 players: Decider and Responder).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each participant played twice (Study 1) or three times (Study 2) as Responder, each with a different group member. Each 'round' is a one-shot interaction with a new partner.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/decision is not all-or-nothing; Decider chooses between two allocation options with probabilistic outcomes. Punishment is continuous in Study 1, binary in Study 2.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is costless to the punisher in all conditions.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment removes $0.10, $0.20, or $0.30 from the Decider's payoff per action (Study 1); in Study 2, only maximum punishment ($0.30) or none. No cost to punisher.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants could also add up to $0.30 to the Decider's payoff in Study 1 (i.e., reward), but the main analyses focus on punishment (subtracting).
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is costless to the rewarder.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward adds $0.10, $0.20, or $0.30 to the Decider's payoff per action (Study 1).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know in advance how many times they will play (twice in Study 1, three times in Study 2).

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report or analyze efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or total earnings; only punishment and rejection rates are measured.
- The game is a dyadic allocation game, not a public goods game or direct variant; results may not generalize to multi-player PGGs.
- Punishment and reward are costless to the punisher/rewarder, which differs from most PGG punishment designs.
- Partner rejection is also costless and always results in a new partner from the same group, which may limit generalizability.
- All interactions are one-shot (no repeated interaction with the same partner), which may affect the dynamics of punishment and rejection.
- Online sample may be less attentive than in-person lab samples.
- No communication or reputation effects are present.
- The study focuses on second-party punishment and rejection; results may differ for third-party punishment or rejection.
