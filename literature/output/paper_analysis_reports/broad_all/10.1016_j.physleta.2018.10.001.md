The main dependent variable is the average total payoff of the population, which is equivalent to efficiency. The paper also reports the fraction of cooperators and defectors, but these are secondary to the payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that institutional intervention, modeled as a central authority forcing some defectors to contribute, can increase the efficiency (average total payoff) of a spatial PGG, especially when the synergy factor is high and the intervention cost is controlled. Weak intervention can paradoxically reduce cooperation and efficiency, but moderate intervention can lead to higher group incomes than the standard PGG. Excessive intervention may reduce cooperation. The effect of intervention is distinct from peer punishment, as the cost is shared by all contributors and there is no direct punisher. The findings are based on simulation results and analysis of average payoffs and cooperation rates.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that, in spatial PGGs, the introduction of an institutional intervention mechanism (analogous to centralized punishment) can increase group efficiency, but only under certain parameter regimes: moderate intervention degree/intensity and high synergy factor (mpcr). The effect is non-monotonic: weak or excessive intervention can reduce efficiency. The findings suggest that, when predicting the effect of punishment-like interventions on efficiency, one should consider the strength and cost of the intervention and the underlying mpcr. The results are most applicable to settings with spatial structure, no peer punishment, and institutional (not individual) enforcement.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each player is located on a 100x100 square lattice; each local PGG involves 5 players (the focal player and 4 neighbors).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 60,000 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), with results averaged over the last 1,000 MCS after convergence.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: players either cooperate (contribute 1) or defect (contribute 0), except for caught defectors who are forced to contribute a fixed amount.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Synergy factor r' (mpcr = r'/5) is varied; higher r' (mpcr) increases the effectiveness of intervention and can lead to higher efficiency.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Intervention cost is modeled as a reduction in the synergy factor (r - r'), shared by all contributors. Lower intervention cost (higher r') allows for higher efficiency.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is modeled by intervention intensity (beta) and degree (eta); higher values can increase forced contributions but excessive values may reduce cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- The intervention mechanism is not peer punishment but institutional (centralized) intervention, which may not map directly to standard punishment treatments in lab PGGs.
- The spatial structure (lattice) may limit generalizability to well-mixed or non-spatial settings.
- No reward mechanism is considered, and only binary contribution choices are modeled.
- Parameter regimes (intervention degree, intensity, and synergy factor) are critical; results may not generalize outside the tested ranges.
