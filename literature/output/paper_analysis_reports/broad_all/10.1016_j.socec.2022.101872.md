# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: On the fragility of third-party punishment: The context effect of a dominated risky investment option
Authors: Im, C., & Lee, J.
Journal: Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Economics
Year: 2022

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of third-party punishment
- expenditure on punishment (deduction points)
- frequency of investment
- expenditure on investment
- frequency and expenditure on safe option
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the frequency and amount of punishment and investment by third parties. No direct group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff outcomes are reported. The study focuses on behavioral allocation of tokens to punishment, investment, and safe options.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that when a third party in a dictator game is offered a dominated risky investment option in addition to the punishment and safe options, both the frequency and amount of punishment decrease significantly compared to when only punishment and safe options are available. The demand for investment increases when all options are available, indicating a substitution effect. The results are robust to risk attitudes and to making the investment option strictly dominated (negative expected return). The findings support the hypothesis that context effects (specifically, the compromise effect) can undermine third-party punishment, even when the alternative is unattractive in both material and psychological terms. However, the study does not measure or report group efficiency, welfare, or total payoffs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the presence of additional, even irrelevant, choice options can reduce the use of third-party punishment in dictator-game-like settings. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the design of the choice set (e.g., presence of unrelated alternatives) can moderate the effect of punishment on cooperation or norm enforcement. However, because the study does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, it cannot be used to directly predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings are most relevant for understanding behavioral mechanisms and the fragility of punishment as an enforcement tool in the presence of context effects.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 3 players: dictator (A), recipient (B), and third party (C).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. One-shot game (Task 2 is a single decision per role per treatment).
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (punishment, investment, safe) choices are continuous: players allocate any amount of tokens to each option.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment costs 1 token per deduction point for the third party.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction point reduces the dictator's payoff by 3 tokens (punishment effectiveness = 3).
- reward availability: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Rewarding is discussed in literature review but not implemented in this experiment.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. One-shot game; players know the structure in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Third party (C) is informed of the dictator's (A's) allocation to recipient (B) before making decisions.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff outcomes, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be directly assessed.
- The experiment uses a modified dictator game with third-party punishment, not a public goods game or its direct variant.
- Potential contamination from the risk elicitation task (Task 1) may affect decisions in the main task (Task 2), especially when the investment option is available.
- Findings may not generalize to repeated or multi-round games, as the design is one-shot.
- The context effect studied is specific to the addition of a dominated, unrelated risky investment option; other context effects or alternative options may have different impacts.
