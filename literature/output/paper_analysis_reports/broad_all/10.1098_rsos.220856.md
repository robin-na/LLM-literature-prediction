## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a linear division-of-labor game, the introduction of a defector sanction system (punishing defectors with a fine, with some probability of detection) can stabilize the all-cooperation equilibrium and thus maximize group efficiency, provided the expected punishment is sufficiently high relative to the cost of cooperation. The first role sanction system (punishing only the first role regardless of who defects) is less effective and only promotes cooperation when the cost of cooperation decreases downstream. The benefit from cooperation does not affect the stability of cooperation or efficiency. The model provides explicit conditions under which each equilibrium is stable, showing that punishment can transform a system from one where defection is stable (and efficiency is low) to one where cooperation and high efficiency are stable.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct theoretical support for predicting that, in public-goods-like environments with sequential or asymmetric roles, introducing a punishment mechanism that targets defectors (with sufficient expected punishment) will increase group efficiency relative to a no-punishment baseline. The effect is robust to the number of roles (player count) and the cost structure, provided the expected punishment exceeds the maximum cooperation cost. The findings suggest that the design of the punishment system (who is punished, detection probability, and fine size) is critical for achieving efficiency gains. The results are most directly applicable to settings with sequential dependencies and asymmetric roles, but the logic may transfer to some PGG variants. The model does not address repeated finite rounds, communication, or stochastic group sizes, so predictions should be cautious if those features are present.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model allows any number of roles/groups n ≥ 2, with most analysis for n ≥ 3. The results are shown to hold for arbitrary n.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: players are either pure cooperators or pure defectors; no mixed or continuous strategies.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is modeled as a fine f imposed on defectors, with cost to the punisher not explicitly modeled (punishment is exogenous). The effectiveness depends on the product of fine and detection probability (ρf).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the product of fine size (f) and detection probability (ρ); higher values increase cooperation and efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The game structure is a linear division of labor with asymmetric roles, not a standard public goods game; results may not transfer directly to symmetric or simultaneous PGGs.
- No repeated finite rounds, communication, or stochastic group sizes are modeled.
- Punishment is modeled as an exogenous fine with no explicit cost to the punisher.
- No reward or positive incentive mechanisms are considered.
- The model assumes infinite group size and deterministic replicator dynamics.
- The effect of information feedback, reputation, or identification of punishers is not addressed.
