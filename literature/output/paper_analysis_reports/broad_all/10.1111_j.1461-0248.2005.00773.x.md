Outcome notes:
The paper is a theoretical review of models of cooperation based on the Prisoner's Dilemma and Snowdrift games, including extensions to public goods games. Outcomes are primarily about the frequency and stability of cooperation, not direct group efficiency or payoff. Some discussion of average population payoff appears in the context of social dilemmas, but efficiency is not a primary dependent variable.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper synthesizes theoretical results on how various mechanisms (iteration, spatial structure, indirect reciprocity, punishment, reward) affect the evolution and maintenance of cooperation in social dilemmas. It notes that spatial structure promotes cooperation in the Prisoner's Dilemma but can reduce cooperation in the Snowdrift game. Indirect reciprocity, punishment, and reputation can promote cooperation, especially when individuals can condition their behavior on others' reputations. The review also discusses the generalization to public goods games and the impact of group size, voluntary participation, and continuous investment. However, the paper does not provide quantitative predictions about efficiency or group payoff, focusing instead on the frequency and stability of cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical context for how design dimensions such as group size, spatial structure, iteration, and the presence of punishment or reward mechanisms can affect cooperation in public-goods-like environments. It suggests that punishment and reputation mechanisms can promote cooperation, and that spatial structure can have divergent effects depending on the game. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff as a primary outcome, it is not directly usable for predicting treatment efficiency from game design dimensions and control efficiency. Its value is mainly in informing mechanistic expectations and highlighting possible moderators, not in providing quantitative effect estimates.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. The paper discusses the effect of group size in public goods games, noting that larger groups make cooperation more difficult (Box 3).
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Iteration (multiple rounds) is discussed as a mechanism that can promote cooperation via strategies like Tit-for-Tat and Pavlov in the iterated Prisoner's Dilemma and Snowdrift games.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Both all-or-nothing (discrete) and continuous investment games are discussed. The effect of continuous investment is explored in both PD and SD contexts.
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is discussed in the context of the multiplication factor in public goods games. Lower MPCR (i.e., higher group size or lower multiplier) makes cooperation harder.
- punishment cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost is discussed as a moderator of the effectiveness of punishment in promoting cooperation (Box 4, Figure B). Lower punishment cost relative to fine increases the basin of attraction for cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (cost/fine ratio) is discussed as a determinant of whether punishment can enforce cooperation (Box 4, Figure B).
- reward availability: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Reward mechanisms are discussed but are said to be less robust than punishment for promoting cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is a theoretical review and does not report empirical or experimental data.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare are not primary outcomes; the focus is on cooperation frequency and evolutionary stability.
- Punishment and reward are discussed in terms of their effect on cooperation, not directly on efficiency or group payoff.
- Many mechanisms are discussed at a conceptual or qualitative level, not with quantitative predictions.
- Public goods games are discussed as extensions, but most models are based on two-person games.
