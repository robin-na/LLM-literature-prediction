## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper surveys the literature on the economics of libraries, emphasizing their value as settings for studying social dilemmas, rule compliance, and the effects of different types of sanctions (e.g., monetary fines vs. suspensions) on user behavior. It notes that libraries often use both monetary and non-monetary sanctions to enforce timely return of items, and that these sanctions can affect compliance rates. The review highlights that most existing studies focus on behavioral outcomes (such as compliance and peer effects) rather than direct measures of efficiency or group payoff. The article calls for more research using libraries as natural laboratories for economic theory testing, including field and quasi-experiments, but does not itself provide new empirical evidence on efficiency or payoff effects of punishment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-game-like environments. It is useful for conceptual framing and for identifying libraries as a relevant adjacent setting for studying social dilemmas and sanctions, but it does not report efficiency or payoff outcomes, nor does it provide parameterized evidence on game design dimensions. For prediction tasks, this paper may inform the plausibility of certain mechanisms (e.g., that monetary sanctions can increase compliance), but it does not provide quantitative or directly transferable evidence for efficiency outcomes.
