The model discusses the maximal set of cooperators that can be sustained, but does not explicitly calculate group efficiency or total payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the ability of peer punishment to sustain cooperation depends critically on the social network structure and the reach of gossip. Incomplete networks with peripheral members cannot sustain full cooperation: the most peripheral members are likely to be persistently cheated and ostracized, as their negative gossip does not reach enough punishers to deter defection. Shocks that increase the gains from defection or that create more peripheral positions (e.g., through rapid population growth or enclave settlement) lead to a ratcheting down of cooperation, with more members becoming persistent cheaters. The model generates hypotheses about when and where cooperation will break down, but does not provide quantitative predictions about group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical insight into how network structure and information transmission affect the limits of peer punishment in sustaining cooperation. It is relevant for understanding moderators of punishment effectiveness, especially the role of network incompleteness and peripheral positions. However, it does not provide direct evidence or quantitative predictions about efficiency or group payoff in public goods games or their variants. For the downstream prediction task, the paper suggests that punishment will be less effective at increasing efficiency in settings with incomplete networks, high population turnover, or enclave settlement, but does not provide parameterized estimates or control-treatment efficiency comparisons.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is a theoretical model and does not provide empirical or experimental data.
- Outcomes are about the extent and distribution of cooperation and defection, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- The model is based on repeated prisoner's dilemma with binary actions, not a public goods game or its direct variants.
- Punishment is modeled as a forced capitulation in the next round, not as a costly punishment action with explicit cost/impact parameters.
- The model assumes perfect, truthful gossip transmission and does not consider errors, noise, or strategic misinformation.
- No quantitative predictions or parameterized estimates of efficiency or payoff are provided.
- Historical case discussion is illustrative and not causally identified.
