## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that adding small switching costs to frequently repeated games can overturn standard results from finitely repeated game theory. In the Prisoners' Dilemma, switching costs can enable cooperation (and thus higher efficiency) if the incentive to punish is greater than the incentive to cheat, but can also make cooperation impossible if the reverse is true. In coordination games, switching costs can make commitment to efficient outcomes credible, sometimes leading to unique, efficient equilibria. In some games, switching costs allow for more complex credible threats, which can result in equilibrium payoffs above Stackelberg levels for some players. The overall effect of switching costs on efficiency is thus mixed and highly context-dependent.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that the effect of punishment-like mechanisms (here, switching costs that make threats and commitments credible) on efficiency in repeated games is highly sensitive to the underlying payoff structure and the relative size of incentives to cooperate, cheat, or punish. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the effect of enabling punishment (or switching costs) on efficiency cannot be determined solely from the presence of punishment, but must also account for the specific incentive structure of the game. In some cases, enabling punishment (via switching costs) increases efficiency by sustaining cooperation; in others, it has no effect or even reduces efficiency by making punishment non-credible. The results are not directly in public goods games, but the mechanisms are adjacent and informative for settings where punishment credibility and switching costs are relevant.

## Important Limitations
- The analysis is entirely theoretical; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- All games are two-player, normal-form games; results may not generalize to larger groups or public goods games.
- The model does not include explicit public goods or marginal per-capita return structures.
- Switching costs are modeled as exogenous and symmetric; real-world punishment mechanisms may differ.
- The results are highly sensitive to the specific payoff structure and may not generalize to all repeated games.
- No stochasticity, noise, or bounded rationality beyond switching costs is considered.
