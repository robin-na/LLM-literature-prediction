# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Firing the right bullets: Exploring the effectiveness of the hired-gun mechanism in the provision of public goods
Authors: Liu, J., Riyanto, Y. E., & Zhang, R. K.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate (percentage of endowment contributed)
- frequency of full-contribution ties
- trend in contributions over time
- incidence of ties at various contribution levels
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the percentage of endowment contributed (contribution rate). The paper does not directly report group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare, but contribution rate is closely related in this context. Some discussion of implementation cost and social welfare is present in the introduction and theory, but not as a measured outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The hired-gun mechanism, a centralized punishment system targeting the lowest contributor(s), is highly effective at increasing contributions in public goods games when both unilateral and tie punishments are positive. The effect is robust to increasing the choice set (larger endowment). However, if the unilateral punishment is removed (set to zero), contributions decay over time, and the mechanism fails to sustain high cooperation, even if the tie punishment remains. Theoretical predictions suggest a range of punishment parameters should work, but empirically, zero unilateral punishment is insufficient. The main outcome is contribution rate, not direct efficiency or group payoff, but high contributions imply high efficiency in this context. The study also finds that punishment can be softened (reduced in size) to a degree without loss of effectiveness, but not eliminated.

## Prediction Guidance
For prediction tasks relating to efficiency in public goods games with centralized punishment, this paper provides strong evidence that the hired-gun mechanism (centralized, automatic punishment of the lowest contributor) is highly effective at increasing contributions (and thus likely efficiency) when both unilateral and tie punishments are positive. The effect is robust to larger choice sets and group size of 4. However, if the unilateral punishment is zero, the mechanism is ineffective, and contributions decay. The paper does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, but the near-full-contribution rates in the treatment with punishment strongly suggest near-maximal efficiency. Thus, when mapping from game design to expected efficiency, the presence and size of both punishment components are critical moderators. The absence of unilateral punishment is a strong negative moderator. The findings are most directly applicable to settings with centralized, automatic punishment and group size 4, with full information about others' contributions and payoffs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All experimental sessions used groups of 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 periods per session: 10 rounds without punishment, 10 rounds with hired-gun mechanism.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous (integer-valued) from 0 to endowment; not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR (alpha) is 2/3 in all main treatments.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is centralized and automatic; cost is not directly paid by group members but is implemented as a deduction from the lowest contributor's payoff. Implementation cost is discussed as low.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is varied by manipulating the size of unilateral and tie punishments. Effectiveness is high when both are positive; zero unilateral punishment is ineffective.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the total number of periods (20), with instructions for the last 10 periods given after the first 10.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each period, subjects are informed of everyone's contribution and earnings in the group.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is centralized and automatic; all group members see who was punished (lowest contributor), as all contributions and payoffs are revealed.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is contribution rate, not direct efficiency or group payoff; efficiency is inferred but not measured.
- All experiments use group size 4; results may not generalize to other group sizes.
- Punishment is centralized and automatic; findings may not generalize to peer punishment or decentralized settings.
- No treatments with reward or combined reward and punishment; results do not inform about reward mechanisms.
- No direct measurement of implementation cost or social welfare; only discussed theoretically.
- No communication allowed; results may differ if chat or communication is permitted.
- All subjects are university students in Singapore; external validity may be limited.
