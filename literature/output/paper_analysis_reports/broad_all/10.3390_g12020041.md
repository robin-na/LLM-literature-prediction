Outcome notes:
The paper is a theoretical analysis of collective punishment in threshold public good (CPG) and public bad (CPB) games. The main outcomes are equilibrium probabilities of resistance (or compliance), not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. Efficiency is discussed in a normative sense but not formally analyzed as an outcome variable.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that collective punishment (CP) is ineffective or counterproductive in threshold public good games where resistance is individually costly but can produce a public good (e.g., protests, strikes). In these cases, increasing the threat or severity of CP does not increase compliance and may even increase resistance. In contrast, in threshold public bad games (e.g., pollution, crime), CP can sometimes increase compliance, but only when the individual benefit from misbehavior is small compared to the social cost. The paper provides formal equilibrium analysis and discusses equilibrium selection, but does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. The findings are relevant for predicting the behavioral effects of punishment in public-goods-like environments, especially regarding when punishment is likely to deter undesirable behavior.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical guidance on when collective punishment is likely to be effective in public-goods-like games. It suggests that in threshold public good games (where cooperation is costly and produces a group benefit), punishment is unlikely to increase efficiency or compliance, and may even be counterproductive. In threshold public bad games (where defection is individually profitable but socially costly), punishment can increase compliance and, by implication, efficiency, but only when the individual incentive to defect is not too large. The paper does not provide quantitative predictions for efficiency, but its qualitative results can inform predictions about the likely direction of punishment effects given the structure of the game (public good vs. public bad, cost/benefit ratios). However, because the paper does not analyze efficiency or group payoff directly, its support for efficiency prediction is indirect and should be supplemented with empirical or simulation evidence.

## Important Limitations
- The analysis is entirely theoretical; no empirical or experimental data are presented.
- Outcomes are probabilities of resistance/compliance, not efficiency or group payoff.
- The model is limited to threshold public good and public bad games with binary actions; results may not generalize to linear or continuous public goods.
- No explicit analysis of efficiency, welfare, or group payoff as outcome variables.
- No consideration of repeated interactions, communication, or more complex institutional features.
- The cost to the punisher (principal) is not modeled; only the cost to punished agents is considered.
- Equilibrium selection is based on theoretical criteria (QRE, Linear Tracing), which may not match actual human behavior.
