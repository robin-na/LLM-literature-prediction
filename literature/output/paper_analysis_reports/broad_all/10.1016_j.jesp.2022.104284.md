# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Reflexive or reflective? Group bias in third-party punishment in Chinese and Western cultures
Authors: Guo, Z. Y., Guo, R., Xu, C. Y., & Wu, Z.
Journal: Journal of Experimental Social Psychology
Year: 2022

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- amount of punishment assigned by third party
- ingroup vs outgroup bias in punishment
- effects of group identity, culture, and decision mode on punishment
- self-reported emotions (anger, guilt)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the amount of costly punishment assigned by a third party to a norm violator (Player 1) in a mini-dictator/TPP game. No group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported. The focus is on behavioral punishment decisions and their moderators.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that both Chinese and Western participants show ingroup favoritism in third-party punishment under reflexive (fast, high cognitive load) conditions, punishing ingroup norm violators less than outgroup violators. Under reflective (slow, low cognitive load) conditions, Western participants—especially those with high group identity—reduce ingroup favoritism and may even punish ingroup violators more, while Chinese participants continue to show strong ingroup favoritism regardless of decision mode. Group identity amplifies these effects. The main outcome is the amount of punishment assigned, not efficiency or group payoff. The game is a third-party punishment mini-dictator game, not a standard public goods game, and does not report on group efficiency or welfare.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence about how group identity, culture, and decision-making mode moderate the behavioral use of costly punishment in a third-party punishment context. However, it does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. The design is adjacent to PGGs (mini-dictator/TPP), and the outcome is punishment behavior, not payoff. The findings are useful for understanding when and how punishment is used, but not for predicting efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game involves 3 players: Player 1 (divider), Player 2 (receiver), Player 3 (third-party punisher).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants play multiple rounds (e.g., 4 rounds in Experiment 1, 16 in Experiment 3a), but each round is a one-shot interaction with new simulated players.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players; all interactions are anonymous and computer-mediated.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. The allocation by Player 1 is all-or-nothing (0 or 15 out of 30 jiao/cents to Player 2). The punishment by Player 3 is continuous (0-10 jiao/cents).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not a standard PGG, but the punishment multiplier is specified: each 1 jiao/cents spent by the punisher reduces the divider's payoff by 3 jiao/cents.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 1 jiao/cents per unit of punishment assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each 1 jiao/cents spent by the punisher reduces the divider's payoff by 3 jiao/cents (punishment effectiveness = 3).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told the number of rounds and that each round involves new players; no repeated interaction or reputation effects.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants observe the allocation made by Player 1 (divider) before making punishment decisions.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. All interactions are anonymous; players do not know the identity of the punisher.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is the amount of costly punishment assigned, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- The game is a third-party punishment mini-dictator game, not a standard public goods game.
- No data on how punishment affects group efficiency or cooperation rates.
- Cultural comparisons use country as a proxy for culture, which may mask within-country variation.
- No communication or repeated interaction; all decisions are one-shot and anonymous.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanism is present.
