## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in public goods games with optional participation, cooperation can only be sustained (and thus group efficiency improved) when nonparticipants have positive outside payoffs and do not harm the public good. The classic 'loner' model is a special case of this. If nonparticipants harm the public good or have negative outside payoffs, cooperation collapses and efficiency is low. The model does not include punishment or reward mechanisms, and thus does not provide direct evidence about the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings highlight the limitations of bottom-up, voluntary participation schemes for sustaining cooperation and efficiency in public goods settings.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence about the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it does not include punishment or sanctioning mechanisms. However, it is highly informative about the baseline efficiency and cooperation levels in optional participation (loner) variants of the public goods game, which are sometimes considered as alternatives to punishment. For prediction tasks, this paper can inform the expected efficiency in control conditions where only optional participation is available, but not the effect of enabling punishment. It also clarifies that optional participation alone only supports cooperation (and thus higher efficiency) under narrow conditions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Model uses a group size of M=5 for all main results and figures; M>2 is required. The effect of varying M is not systematically analyzed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: cooperators contribute a fixed cost, defectors contribute nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Enhancement factor r=3, group size M=5, so MPCR = r/M = 0.6 in main results. r is a parameter in the model.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanisms are modeled, so the paper does not provide direct evidence about the effect of punishment on efficiency.
- The model assumes infinite, well-mixed populations and does not consider network structure or finite group effects.
- Mutation is modeled as homogeneous and exogenous; real-world heterogeneity is not addressed.
- The model only considers all-or-nothing contributions and does not analyze continuous contribution choices.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with communication, reputation, or other social mechanisms.
