# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: An experiment investigating the spillover effects of communication opportunities
Authors: Koukoumelis, A., & Levati, M. V.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution in public goods game
- change in contribution between two PGGs
- beliefs about partner's contribution
- self-reported emotions
- message content classification
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the change in individual contribution between two public goods games, not group payoff or efficiency. No direct analysis of group efficiency, welfare, or total earnings is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that recipients of unfair offers in a mini dictator game reduce their contributions in a subsequent public goods game. However, if they are allowed to send a message to the dictator, this reduction is significantly smaller—only in treatments without belief elicitation. When beliefs about the partner's contribution are elicited, the messaging opportunity does not significantly affect contribution changes for the full sample, but does for the subsample of recipients who actually reduce their contributions. The mechanism appears to be emotional relief from having a 'voice,' not a change in beliefs about partner behavior. The study does not analyze group efficiency or total payoff, and there is no monetary punishment mechanism.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. It is relevant for understanding the effects of non-monetary, symbolic communication (emotional expression) on cooperation, but not for monetary punishment or efficiency outcomes. The design and findings may be informative for adjacent questions about communication, emotion, and cooperation, but not for the core prediction task involving punishment and efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All public goods games are played in pairs (2 players per group).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject plays two one-shot public goods games (PGG1 and PGG2), with a mini dictator game in between. No repeated rounds within each PGG.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Messaging opportunity (one-way, recipient to dictator) is the main treatment variable. Messaging reduces contribution cuts among unfairly treated recipients in some conditions.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution in the PGG is continuous: 0 ≤ c ≤ 14.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per capita return (MPCR) is 0.75 (each €1 contributed returns €0.75 to each of 2 players).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know from the outset that there are three stages, but only learn the content of each stage after the previous one. The number of games is known, but not the content.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Feedback about the other's contribution and own payoff in PGG1 is given only after PGG2 is completed, to minimize path dependence.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Messages are anonymous; senders cannot identify themselves, but the dictator knows the message is from the recipient.

## Important Limitations
- No monetary punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present; only symbolic, one-way messaging is studied.
- No direct analysis of group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff; only individual contribution changes are reported.
- The public goods game is two-person and one-shot (per stage), limiting generalizability to larger groups or repeated games.
- Messaging is one-way and anonymous, not interactive or face-to-face.
- Findings on communication and emotion may not generalize to monetary punishment or to efficiency outcomes.
