## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Pairs of rats can achieve some degree of cooperation in an iterated PD game, with group payoffs (pellets received) higher when rats are well-fed and lower under food restriction. The frequency of mutual cooperation (Reward) increases over repeated trials, but most pairs do not reach the maximum possible group payoff. Under food restriction, cooperation declines and group payoffs fall, as rats defect more often to obtain the higher short-term payoff. There is no effect of dominance status, and only limited sex differences (females are more reciprocal in the direct reciprocity task, but not more cooperative in the IPD). The study does not include a punishment or sanctioning mechanism beyond the PD payoff structure, and does not manipulate or analyze punishment cost or technology. Efficiency, in the sense of group payoff relative to the cooperative maximum, is not directly reported but can be inferred from pellets received.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about how game context (e.g., resource abundance, repeated dyadic interaction) affects cooperation and group payoff in a two-player, repeated social dilemma. It does not study public goods games, nor does it include a punishment stage or vary punishment cost/technology, so it is not directly informative for predicting the effect of enabling punishment in PGG-like environments. However, it does show that environmental context (e.g., food restriction) can strongly moderate cooperation and group payoff, suggesting that baseline efficiency is sensitive to motivational state. The absence of explicit punishment means the paper cannot inform the effect of punishment on efficiency, but it does provide a behavioral baseline for dyadic cooperation under different conditions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in pairs (2 players).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 25 trials per session, repeated over 10 days; analysis focuses on last 4 days (100 trials total per pair per condition).
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No explicit chat or communication; rats are separated by a mesh screen but can see and hear each other.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each trial is a binary choice: cooperate (withhold or press lever, depending on group) or defect (press or withhold).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Payoff matrix: Mutual cooperation = 3 pellets each; Temptation = 5 pellets to defector, 0 to cooperator; Mutual defection = 0 each. This is a weak PD, not a PGG; no marginal per-capita return in the PGG sense.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Food pellets are the reward for cooperation or defection, depending on outcome.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of trials per session is fixed and repeated; rats likely learn the session structure.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Rats can see and hear when their partner receives pellets (audible clicker, visible consumption), but no summary statistics are shown.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a two-player iterated Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game or multi-player social dilemma.
- There is no explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism; 'Punishment' refers to the mutual defection outcome in the PD matrix.
- Efficiency is not directly reported as a ratio to the cooperative maximum, though total group payoffs are analyzed.
- Findings may not generalize to human PGGs or to environments with explicit costly punishment.
- Motivational state (food restriction) is manipulated, but other key PGG dimensions (e.g., group size, punishment cost/tech) are not.
