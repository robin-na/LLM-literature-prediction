# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Discretionary mechanisms and cooperation in hierarchies: An experimental study
Authors: Billinger, S., & Rosenbaum, S. M.
Journal: Journal of Economic Psychology
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- accumulated earnings
- receiving share
- group earnings
- manager vs worker earnings
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution share
- cooperation level
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are accumulated earnings (total tokens at end), receiving share (proportion of public good received), and contribution share (proportion of endowment contributed). The paper directly analyzes payoff-based outcomes, including group and role-specific earnings.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment compares a baseline PGG (no hierarchy) to four HPGG treatments varying in whether managers can contribute (discretionary contribution: present/absent) and whether managers can allocate returns (discretionary return: static/dynamic). All HPGG treatments yield higher average contributions and accumulated earnings than the baseline, indicating that hierarchy and managerial discretion can sustain cooperation and improve efficiency. However, when managers have full discretion over returns but cannot contribute (AD treatment), both contributions and worker earnings are lowest among HPGG treatments, showing that unchecked managerial appropriation undermines efficiency. When managers can contribute, both group earnings and cooperation are higher and more stable over time. Managers consistently earn more than workers, especially in dynamic return treatments, due to 'strategic luring'—managers initially reward workers to elicit contributions, then appropriate more in later rounds. The design of discretionary mechanisms thus crucially shapes both efficiency and the distribution of surplus.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that introducing hierarchical mechanisms (managerial discretion over inputs and/or outputs) in a PGG-like environment increases group efficiency relative to a standard PGG baseline. However, the efficiency gains depend on the specific design: allowing managers to contribute and/or restricting their ability to appropriate returns leads to higher and more sustained efficiency. If managers can appropriate returns without contributing, efficiency and worker payoffs suffer. For prediction, the presence of hierarchy and the structure of managerial discretion are key moderators: enabling managerial contribution and limiting appropriation both support higher efficiency. The findings are directly relevant for predicting treatment efficiency from game design dimensions, especially in settings with hierarchical or leader-follower structures.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games use groups of 4 players (1 manager, 3 workers in HPGG; 4 symmetric in baseline).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use 10 rounds, known in advance.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication was strictly forbidden in all treatments.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are continuous (0 to full endowment), not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.5 in the baseline (contributions doubled and split among 4). In HPGG, the public good is doubled and then distributed by the manager.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. In HPGG, managers can allocate returns (dynamic return treatments), which functions as a reward mechanism.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Rewarding is costless to the manager in the sense that allocation is a zero-sum division of the public good; no explicit cost per unit of reward.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward technology is direct allocation of shares of the public good; manager can allocate any share (dynamic) or is fixed (static).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of rounds (10) was known in advance to all participants.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. All prior and current-round contributions and returns for all group members were displayed each round.

## Important Limitations
- No direct punishment or costly sanctioning mechanism is present; only managerial allocation of returns (reward-like mechanism) is studied.
- The study uses a specific hierarchical structure (1 manager, 3 workers) and may not generalize to other group sizes or more complex hierarchies.
- All treatments are conducted in a lab setting with student subjects, which may limit external validity.
- The design tests only extreme points of discretionary contribution and return (fully present/absent, fully static/dynamic), not intermediate or continuous variations.
- The reward mechanism is not costly to the manager (allocation is zero-sum), so findings may not generalize to settings with costly rewards or punishments.
