Outcome notes:
The main reported outcomes are behavioral: cooperation rate, punishment/metapunishment rates, and the distribution of agent strategies. The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, though it discusses payoffs at the individual level as part of the Q-learning process.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a metanorm game with punishment and metapunishment, agents using self-regarding Q-learning can achieve high and robust cooperation rates (over 90% in many cases), even when agents lack information about others' payoffs. Punishment and metapunishment are essential for maintaining cooperation and suppressing defection and non-punishment. The model is robust to changes in network topology (scale-free, random, small-world), temptation payoff, population size, and observability. Highly connected agents are more likely to adopt punitive strategies. However, the paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, so the direct effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined from this work.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that punishment and metapunishment mechanisms can sustain high cooperation rates in social dilemma environments with local interactions and limited information, especially when agents use reinforcement learning. However, because the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. The results are most useful for understanding the behavioral and dynamic effects of punishment and metapunishment, and for anticipating that high cooperation is possible under a wide range of design parameters, but not for quantitative efficiency prediction.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Experiments are run with 20, 100, and 200 agents; the effect of population size on cooperation rate is analyzed.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 20,000 Monte Carlo iterations; the number of rounds is fixed and known in the simulation.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (cooperation/defection) and punishment choices are discrete (all-or-nothing) rather than continuous.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment and metapunishment cost to punisher is E = -2 per act; cost to punished is P = -9.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is P = -9 per act, cost to punisher is E = -2; ratio is 4.5.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of rounds (iterations) is fixed and known in the simulation setup.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, welfare, or surplus, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined.
- Outcomes are limited to behavioral measures (cooperation rate, punishment frequency, strategy proportions) and Q-values, not payoff-based group outcomes.
- The model is simulation-based and theoretical; no empirical or experimental data are presented.
- The payoff structure is not a standard public goods game but a metanorm game with local harm and punishment, which may limit direct transferability to PGG efficiency prediction.
- Punishment and metapunishment are modeled as all-or-nothing discrete actions, not continuous or variable in intensity.
