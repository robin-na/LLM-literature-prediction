# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Inequality and Peer Punishment in a Common-Pool Resource Experiment
Authors: De Geest, L. R., & Kingsley, D. C.
Journal: Strategic Behavior and the Environment
Year: 2021

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average group earnings
- individual earnings (Low and High types)
- aggregate earnings
- earnings relative to social optimum
Non-payoff outcomes:
- extraction levels
- variation in extractions
- frequency and magnitude of punishment
- coordination around extraction norms
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is group and individual earnings (payoff). Extraction (contribution) and punishment behavior are also analyzed, but efficiency/payoff is the primary outcome for the prediction task.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Peer punishment in a nonlinear CPR game reduces extractions in both equal and unequal endowment settings, but its effect on efficiency (group earnings relative to the social optimum) depends on the distribution of endowments. In the equal endowment treatment, punishment leads to lower earnings due to high punishment costs and lack of coordination. In the unequal endowment treatment, punishment does not reduce average earnings and increases earnings for low-endowment participants, as groups coordinate around the Equal Earnings norm. Punishment is more targeted and less frequent in the unequal treatment, leading to higher efficiency. The effect of punishment is thus moderated by the presence of endowment inequality, which creates a focal point for coordination and more effective norm enforcement.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency in CPR/public goods-like games depends on the distribution of endowments (inequality). When endowments are equal, punishment can reduce efficiency due to high punishment costs and poor coordination. When endowments are unequal, punishment is more effective, leading to higher efficiency, especially for low-endowment participants, as groups coordinate around a salient norm. For prediction, the presence of endowment heterogeneity should be considered a key moderator: punishment is more likely to improve efficiency when inequality is present and salient. Other design features (e.g., punishment technology, group size, number of rounds) are held constant in this study, so the main actionable insight is about the interaction between punishment and endowment inequality.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 15 periods (rounds) per session.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Extraction/contribution is continuous: players choose how much to extract from their endowment.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The CPR production function is V(G) = aG - bG^2 with a=6, b=0.025, n=4. The marginal per-capita return is not constant but can be calculated from the nonlinear function. Social optimum and Nash equilibrium are interior.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost is 1 ED per point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Standard 1:3 technology: 1 ED cost to punisher, 3 ED deduction to target per point.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects played 15 periods and this was known in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, subjects saw their own extraction, the group's aggregate extraction, and their individual period earnings.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment was assigned to group members identified by random ID; identities were anonymous but consistent within the group.

## Important Limitations
- The study only examines one level of endowment inequality (two players with 40 EDs, two with 60 EDs); results may not generalize to more extreme or different forms of inequality.
- The CPR game is nonlinear, so results may not generalize to linear public goods games.
- No communication or other institutions (e.g., voting, rewards) are present; results may differ with additional institutional features.
- The experiment uses a student sample from one university, which may limit external validity.
- The punishment technology (1:3) and group size (4) are fixed; effects of varying these parameters are not tested.
