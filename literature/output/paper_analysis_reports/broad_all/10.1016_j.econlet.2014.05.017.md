# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Conditional punishment
Authors: Kamei, K.
Journal: Economics Letters
Year: 2014

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- conditional punishment assigned
- unconditional punishment assigned
- classification of punishment types (free-rider, conditional punisher, pro-social, anti-social)
- relation between others' punishment and own punishment
- cooperation/contribution decisions
Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on how individuals' punishment decisions depend on others' punishment decisions (conditional punishment), and classifies types of punishers. It does not report group efficiency, total payoff, welfare, or surplus. Payoff is discussed only as a mechanism or in the context of punishment cost/impact, not as an outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that individuals' punishment of non-cooperators increases with the amount of punishment assigned by other group members. Most subjects are 'free-riders' who do not punish, but about 23% are conditional punishers who increase punishment as others do. There is also a distinction between pro-social and anti-social conditional punishers. The study provides evidence that conditional punishment types are heterogeneous and that these types can predict unconditional punishment behavior. However, the paper does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides detailed behavioral evidence on the heterogeneity and conditionality of punishment in a standard PGG with punishment, but does not report efficiency or payoff outcomes. It is useful for understanding the mechanisms and types of punishment behavior that may underlie efficiency effects in similar games, but cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper is only indirectly informative, suggesting that the presence of conditional punishers may amplify punishment effects when others also punish, but the net effect on efficiency is not measured.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players per session.
- number of rounds: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. The experiment uses a strategy method for conditional punishment schedules rather than repeated rounds; the number of actual repeated rounds is not specified.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No mention of chat or communication; standard VCM lab protocol implies no chat.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is all-or-nothing: each subject allocates all 10 points to either private or public account.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.5 (each 10-point contribution yields 5 points to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 1 point per punishment point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point assigned reduces the target's payoff by 3 points.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is present; only punishment is possible.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects are informed of the allocation decisions of the other three group members before making unconditional punishment decisions.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency.
- The experiment uses a strategy method for conditional punishment schedules rather than repeated rounds, which may not capture dynamic effects present in repeated games.
- The sample size is modest (52 subjects), and all participants are undergraduates from a single university.
- No analysis of how punishment affects actual cooperation rates or group outcomes is provided.
- No reward mechanism is present, so findings may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
