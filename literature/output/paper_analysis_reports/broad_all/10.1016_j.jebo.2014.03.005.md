# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Starting small toward voluntary formation of efficient large groups in public goods provision
Authors: Charness, G., & Yang, C. L.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2014

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average group earnings
- social efficiency
- group return
- MPCR
- relationship between contributions and earnings
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate
- exit frequency
- exclusion frequency
- merger frequency
- redemption rate
- conditional cooperation
- group stability
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are contribution rates and group earnings/social efficiency. The paper also analyzes behavioral outcomes such as exit, exclusion, and merger decisions, but these are interpreted in relation to their effect on efficiency and group payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that endogenous group formation with exit, exclusion, and merger options produces very high contribution rates and group efficiency in public goods games. In the main treatment, where the marginal per-capita return (MPCR) increases with group size, groups tend to grow large and stable, and efficiency is maximized. When the efficiency gain from larger groups is capped, groups remain smaller and less stable, and efficiency is lower, but still substantially higher than in fixed-group baselines. Exclusion (a form of sanction) is used to remove low contributors, which protects cooperators and sustains high efficiency. Redemption (the ability for excluded individuals to rejoin) further boosts efficiency. The results suggest that voluntary group formation with exclusion and merger mechanisms can solve the free-rider problem and achieve high efficiency in public goods provision.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in public goods games, mechanisms allowing voluntary group formation, exit, exclusion (as a sanction), and merger can dramatically increase efficiency compared to fixed groups. The effect is especially strong when economies of scale are present (i.e., when MPCR increases with group size). The exclusion mechanism functions as a sanction, but is not a direct costly punishment; rather, it is a group-level sanction that removes low contributors. The findings suggest that, for prediction tasks, the presence of exclusion/exit/merger mechanisms and increasing MPCR with group size are strong positive moderators of the efficiency effect of sanction-like mechanisms. However, the effect is not directly about costly punishment, but about exclusion as a sanction. The results are less informative for standard costly punishment, but highly informative for exclusion-based sanctions and endogenous group formation. Control efficiency (fixed group) is much lower than in the endogenous group treatments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each 'society' consists of 9 individuals. Group size is endogenous, but total player count per society is fixed at 9.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each segment consists of 15 periods, with two segments per session (30 periods total).
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each individual allocates 25 tokens between private and public accounts.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. MPCR varies with group size. In the Main treatment, MPCR increases with group size (up to 0.286 for 9-person group). In the Capped treatment, MPCR is capped at group size 4.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of periods per segment (15), and that there are two segments.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are shown group totals and, during regrouping, average contributions of each group and each individual in their group.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. During regrouping, individual contributions are shown by ID number within the group, so exclusion votes can be targeted.

## Important Limitations
- The exclusion mechanism is not a direct costly punishment; it is a group-level sanction (removal from group), so results may not generalize to standard costly punishment settings.
- The design is a 'top-down' approach, changing multiple elements at once, so the individual effect of each mechanism (exit, exclusion, merger) cannot be isolated.
- Group size is endogenous, and the effect of exclusion may depend on the presence of economies of scale (increasing MPCR).
- No direct test of standard punishment cost or technology; only exclusion as a sanction is studied.
- No communication or chat between players, which may affect generalizability to settings with communication.
