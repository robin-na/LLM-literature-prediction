## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that risk-averse individuals in evolutionary social dilemma games can achieve stable, high levels of cooperation with only rare third-party punishment. Both exogenous (authoritative) and endogenous (peer) punishment models are analyzed. Theoretical results show that the required frequency of punishment to maintain cooperation is much lower for risk-averse individuals than for risk-neutral or risk-prone individuals. Among punishment variants, punishing all defectors or punishing one defector (as a warning) are both effective in promoting cooperation, while punishing only one-sided defectors is less effective. The models predict that, under risk aversion, group payoff and cooperation rates are higher with punishment than without, and that the cost of maintaining cooperation via punishment can be minimized.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support that, in two-player social dilemma games with third-party punishment, enabling punishment (even rarely) can substantially increase cooperation and group payoff, especially when individuals are risk averse. The findings suggest that the efficiency gain from punishment is robust to low punishment frequency and that the structure of punishment (punish all vs. punish one) can moderate the effect. However, the results are derived from theoretical models, not empirical data, and the games are not public goods games but two-player dilemmas. The paper is most informative for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in environments where risk aversion is salient and punishment is rare.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The main models are two-player games (weak prisoner's dilemma, donation game), with some three-player interactions in the endogenous punishment model (two play, one observes).
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are binary: cooperate (C) or defect (D).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment cost to the punisher (C) and fine to the punished (F) are explicit model parameters. The effect of varying these is discussed in terms of stability and cost-effectiveness.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is modeled as the fine (F) per cost (C) to the punisher. The impact of increasing F is analyzed.

## Important Limitations
- The models are theoretical and not empirically tested.
- The games analyzed are two-player social dilemmas (weak prisoner's dilemma, donation game), not public goods games; results may not transfer directly to multi-player PGGs.
- Efficiency is not directly calculated as a ratio to full cooperation; outcomes are mainly in terms of cooperation frequency and expected payoff.
- Risk attitude is assumed homogeneous and constant across individuals.
- No explicit modeling of repeated rounds, communication, or group structure.
