# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The logic of costly punishment reversed: Expropriation of free-riders and outsiders
Authors: Hugh-Jones, D., & Perroni, C.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2017

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- group mean contributions
- group mean expropriations
- correlation between expropriations and contributions
- deadweight loss from expropriation
- public goods provision (aggregate contributions)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of expropriation
- targeting of minorities
- reaction to expropriation (change in contribution)
- targeting by contribution status
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group mean contributions (as a proxy for public goods provision), frequency and targeting of expropriation, and behavioral responses to expropriation. Efficiency is discussed in terms of contributions and deadweight loss, but explicit efficiency ratios (payoff relative to full cooperation) are not directly reported. Group earnings/payoff are not always directly tabulated, but the design allows inference about efficiency via contributions and expropriation rates.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that the threat of group expropriation (a form of punishment) can increase public goods contributions, but only when the expropriation mechanism is sufficiently difficult to coordinate (i.e., requires a large group to act together). When expropriation is too easy (requires only one or two people), it is less targeted at free-riders, more arbitrary, and leads to lower contributions and more frequent expropriation, reducing efficiency. In the baseline (punishment enabled, M=3), contributions are significantly higher than in the control (no expropriation), and expropriation is mostly targeted at unique lowest contributors. When the minimum group size for expropriation is reduced (M=2 or M=1), expropriation increases and contributions fall. In heterogeneous groups, minorities are more likely to be targeted in some treatments, especially when ambiguity or a history of targeting exists, and may respond less to punishment. However, in the main treatments, overall group efficiency is not significantly different between homogeneous and heterogeneous groups. The findings suggest that the design of the punishment mechanism (especially the ease of coordination required for punishment) is a critical moderator of its effect on efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games depends on the punishment technology, specifically the minimum group size required for successful punishment (expropriation). When punishment is difficult to coordinate (higher M), it is more likely to be used as a credible threat against free-riders, increasing contributions and efficiency. When punishment is too easy (low M), it is used more arbitrarily, contributions fall, and efficiency is reduced. For prediction, the dimension 'punishment_tech' (minimum group size for punishment, or ease of expropriation) is a key moderator: higher coordination requirements for punishment (higher M) are associated with more positive effects of punishment on efficiency, while lower requirements (lower M) can make punishment detrimental. Control efficiency (without punishment) is much lower than with well-targeted, hard-to-coordinate punishment. Heterogeneity (minority status) can moderate targeting and behavioral response, but does not robustly reduce efficiency unless ambiguity or a history of targeting is present. The findings are directly relevant for predicting treatment efficiency from game design dimensions, especially punishment_tech, and provide indirect evidence for the importance of targeting and coordination in punishment mechanisms.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All main treatments use groups of 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 rounds per session in main treatments; some variants have 10 rounds of one stage followed by 10 rounds of another.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No mention of chat or communication between players; standard zTree setup implies no chat.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each subject can contribute any amount from 0 to 50 ECU.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 1.4/4 = 0.35 in all main treatments.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (targeting) is costless to the punisher (k=0) in main treatments.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment technology is varied: minimum group size for successful expropriation (M) is manipulated (M=1,2,3). Higher M (harder to coordinate) leads to more targeted, less frequent expropriation and higher contributions/efficiency; lower M (easier) leads to more arbitrary expropriation and lower contributions/efficiency.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of rounds is fixed and known to participants (20 rounds).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, feedback on individual contributions is given; in expropriation stage, subjects learn how many people targeted each group member.

## Important Limitations
- Efficiency is inferred from contributions and expropriation rates, but explicit group payoff or efficiency ratios are not always directly reported.
- The punishment mechanism is group expropriation, which is not identical to standard costly punishment; results may not generalize to all punishment forms.
- The heterogeneity manipulation is minimal (color labels), so real-world group differences may have stronger effects.
- The experiment uses fixed groups of 4 and a specific MPCR; results may differ with other group sizes or payoff structures.
- Punishment is costless to the punisher in the main design, which differs from standard costly punishment games.
