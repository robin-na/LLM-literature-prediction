Payoff-based outcomes are discussed in terms of cost to the institution, not group welfare.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the sampling reward mechanism can effectively increase the average cooperation level in both PGG and CRD models. Higher reward thresholds and higher reward intensities promote cooperation, but also increase the cost to the institution. The effect of reward intensity is non-linear: above a critical threshold, further increases yield little additional cooperation. Sample size has a non-monotonic effect on cost, with intermediate sizes minimizing cost. The findings are robust across both game types. However, the paper does not report on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, and does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment or sanctions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that reward mechanisms (specifically, sampling reward) can increase cooperation rates in PGG-like environments, with effects moderated by reward threshold, intensity, and sample size. However, it does not report group efficiency or total payoff, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. The paper is most useful for understanding how reward design parameters affect cooperation rates and institutional costs, but not for predicting efficiency outcomes when punishment is enabled. It does not provide empirical or theoretical results on punishment, nor does it analyze efficiency as defined by group payoff relative to full cooperation.
