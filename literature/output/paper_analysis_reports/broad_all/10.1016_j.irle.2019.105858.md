# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Laws and norms: Experimental evidence with liability rules
Authors: Deffains, B., Espinosa, R., & Fluet, C.
Journal: International Review of Law and Economics
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- group efficiency (total group payoff relative to maximum possible)
- proportion of welfare-decreasing actions
- trade-off between private benefit and group loss
- social concern parameter (weight on group payoff)
- net period income
Non-payoff outcomes:
- proportion of harmful actions
- assignment of non-monetary and monetary punishment points
- disapproval behavior
- individual choices (harmful vs harmless action)
- beliefs about others' behavior
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are efficiency (group welfare), the proportion of harmful actions (especially those that are welfare-decreasing), and the social concern parameter. Punishment/disapproval is measured but is not the primary outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
This paper experimentally studies a 'Liability Game' that is structurally similar to a public goods game but with a binary choice (harmful vs harmless action) and state-dependent payoffs, so that sometimes the harmful action is efficient and sometimes not. The main treatments compare no law (no liability), strict liability, and negligence rule, each with perfect (Severe) or imperfect (Mild, 50% probability) enforcement. The key findings are: (1) Both strict liability and negligence rules increase efficiency relative to no law, even when enforcement is imperfect and sanctions are not fully deterrent. (2) Strict liability is more effective than the negligence rule at deterring welfare-decreasing actions under imperfect enforcement, but also deters some welfare-increasing actions (over-deterrence). (3) The presence of liability rules increases the weight individuals place on group welfare in their decision-making, beyond the direct material incentives. (4) Social disapproval (punishment) is mainly targeted at those who cause harm, regardless of whether the action was efficient or inefficient. (5) Informal sanctions (disapproval points) further reduce harmful actions, but the main efficiency gains come from the liability rules themselves.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that introducing a punishment-like liability rule in a public-goods-like environment increases efficiency, even when enforcement is imperfect and the expected sanction is not fully deterrent. The effect is robust across two types of liability rules (strict and negligence), but strict liability is more effective at deterring welfare-decreasing actions (at the cost of some over-deterrence). The game design is a close variant of a PGG: 4 players, 10 rounds per treatment, binary choice, no chat, and state-dependent payoffs. The efficiency gains are not solely due to material deterrence but also to increased salience of group welfare (norm activation). For prediction, this suggests that enabling punishment (liability) will increase efficiency relative to control, even if punishment is not fully effective or is only probabilistically applied. The magnitude of the effect may depend on whether the punishment is strict (always for harm) or negligence-based (only for inefficient harm), and on the enforcement probability.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 participants per round; fixed across all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 10 rounds per treatment (first with non-monetary punishment, then 10 with monetary punishment).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between participants; matching is anonymous and random.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: action Harmful (X) or Harmless (Y).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not a standard linear PGG, but the marginal per-capita return of the harmful action is state-dependent: action Harmful gives private benefit (14, 16, 20, or 22 ECU) and imposes 4 ECU loss on each of 3 others (total 12 ECU loss).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. For informal (monetary) punishment, each sanction point costs 0.5 ECU to both punisher and punished. For legal liability, the cost is the compensation paid (12 ECU per harmful action detected).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Legal punishment: 1-to-1 compensation (12 ECU per harmful action detected). Informal punishment: 1-to-1 cost (0.5 ECU per point).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told the game will be repeated 10 times (per phase).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants learn the total number of other group members who chose the harmful action. In law treatments, they also know whether each other group member was held liable (anonymously identified).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players can see which (anonymously identified) group members were held liable (punished) in law treatments, but not the specific action or state. Disapproval points are assigned individually to each group member.

## Important Limitations
- The game is a close variant of a public goods game but not a standard linear PGG: choices are binary and the marginal per-capita return is state-dependent.
- Punishment is implemented as legal liability (compensation) rather than the classic PGG punishment (costly deduction), though informal punishment is also included.
- The efficiency measure is based on the proportion of welfare-decreasing actions, not on total group payoff in the standard PGG sense.
- No communication is allowed, so results may not generalize to settings with chat or discussion.
- The experiment does not disentangle the effects of legal liability from those of informal sanctions, as both are present in some treatments.
- The over-deterrence effect of strict liability may reduce efficiency in settings where harmful actions are sometimes efficient.
- The study is conducted in a lab setting with student participants, which may limit external validity.
