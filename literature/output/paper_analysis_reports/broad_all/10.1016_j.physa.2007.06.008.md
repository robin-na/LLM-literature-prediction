Non-payoff outcomes:

Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the evolution of cooperation frequency and the structure of cooperating clusters. It does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. Payoff is discussed in the context of individual update rules, but not as an aggregate outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that (1) lower average degree and higher clustering coefficient in scale-free networks promote higher cooperation frequency in the iterated prisoner's dilemma; (2) introducing a punishment mechanism, where unsatisfied cooperators can sever and rewire links to defectors with some probability, further increases the frequency and persistence of cooperation. However, the study does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, and all results are based on simulation of strategy frequencies and network structure.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that punishment mechanisms can increase cooperation frequency in networked iterated prisoner's dilemma environments, especially when combined with certain network structures. However, because the study does not report efficiency or group payoff, it does not directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-game-like environments. The findings may be useful for understanding the behavioral mechanisms by which punishment can promote cooperation, but should be used with caution for payoff-based prediction tasks.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Each node in the network is an individual player; the total number of players is not specified but is implied to be large (simulation scale).
- number of rounds: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Simulations run for 10,000+ generations, but the number of rounds per se is not a manipulated variable.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Strategy choice is binary: cooperate (1) or defect (0).
- punishment cost: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Punishment is implemented as link rewiring; the cost to the punisher is not explicitly modeled as a payoff deduction.
- punishment technology: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Punishment reduces the defector's payoff by b (the temptation payoff) when a link is severed, but the effectiveness per unit cost is not quantified.

## Important Limitations
- The study is based on simulation of strategy frequencies and network structure, not on empirical or experimental data.
- The main outcome is cooperation frequency, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- Punishment is implemented as probabilistic link rewiring, which differs from standard costly punishment in public goods games.
- No direct analysis of payoff-based outcomes relevant to efficiency prediction.
- Network structure (scale-free, clustering, degree) is central, which may limit generalizability to standard public goods game environments.
