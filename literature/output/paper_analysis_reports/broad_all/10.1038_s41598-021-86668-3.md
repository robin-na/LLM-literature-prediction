Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are cooperation rates and the prevalence of different strategy types. The model tracks expected payoffs to determine strategy updating, but does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare as explicit outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that localizing either the game interaction or the punishment scope in a spatial PGG model leads to robust cooperation, while globalizing both undermines cooperation. The adaptation (learning) process also interacts with these effects: global adaptation can help spread cooperation if the regime is already cooperative, but can also help defectors invade if cooperation is fragile. The model uses expected payoffs to update strategies, but does not report efficiency or group payoff as an outcome. The findings are most directly about cooperation rates and the stability of cooperative regimes under different locality conditions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that local punishment and local game interactions are important for sustaining cooperation in spatial PGGs, but it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The results are useful for understanding which game design features (local vs global interaction, punishment scope) are likely to support high cooperation, which may correlate with higher efficiency, but the lack of explicit payoff or efficiency outcomes limits its direct predictive value for the efficiency task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=100 players; population size is fixed in the main results. Authors state population size is not expected to affect cooperation.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 100 generations (adaptation steps), with each generation involving many game plays for expected payoff calculation.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: a cooperator always contributes, a non-cooperator never does.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is b/(n+1) = 2/5 = 0.4 in main simulations (b=2, n=4, group size n+1=5).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays cost s=3 per punishment; fine to target is f=6.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is f/s = 6/3 = 2 per act; fixed in main simulations.

## Important Limitations
- The model does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare as explicit outcomes; only cooperation rates and strategy distributions are analyzed.
- Results are based on agent-based simulations with expected payoffs, not empirical or experimental data.
- The network structure is limited to a one-dimensional regular graph; results may not generalize to other network types.
- No stochasticity in payoffs (expected payoffs used), which may limit realism.
- No consideration of anti-social punishment, errors, or reward mechanisms.
- Parameter values for punishment cost and effectiveness are fixed in main results; cost-benefit tradeoffs are not systematically explored.
