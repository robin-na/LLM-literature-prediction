# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The stickiness of norms
Authors: Farrow, K., & Romaniuc, R.
Journal: International Review of Law and Economics
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution amount
- number of disapproval points sent
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the amount contributed to the public good. No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings is reported, though the payoff structure is described and discussed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that both peer disapproval and face-saving mechanisms increase contributions relative to baseline. When a temporary monetary sanction is introduced and then removed, peer disapproval groups experience a significant drop in cooperation (contributions fall below baseline), indicating a negative post-intervention spillover. In contrast, face-saving groups maintain or even increase cooperation after the sanction is removed, showing no negative spillover. The results suggest that the type of informal norm enforcement moderates the legacy of formal sanctions, with face-saving mechanisms supporting more robust cooperation after sanctions end.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment on cooperation (and by implication, efficiency) depends on the type of informal norm enforcement present. While the study does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, the contribution data imply that punishment can have negative, neutral, or positive legacy effects depending on whether peer disapproval or face-saving is salient. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the presence and type of informal norm enforcement (especially observability/social image vs. anonymous peer disapproval) are important moderators of punishment's effect. However, because efficiency is not directly measured, the findings should be interpreted as applying primarily to contribution rates.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 30 periods per session, divided into three sequences of 10 periods each.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No chat or communication between players is described; only structured feedback (contributions, disapproval points, photos) is provided.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players choose how many of 20 tokens to allocate to the public account.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per capita return (mpcr) is 0.4 (each token contributed yields 0.4 Ecu per player).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Monetary sanction is imposed by the experimenter: 0.3 Ecus subtracted per token not contributed. This is not peer punishment; it is a centralized, costless-to-punisher punishment. Peer disapproval points are non-costly and do not affect payoffs.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: each token not contributed is punished by a 0.3 Ecu deduction. Peer disapproval points have no direct payoff effect.
- reward availability: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No reward mechanism is implemented; only punishment and informal mechanisms are present.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed that the session consists of 30 periods divided into three sequences of 10 periods; the total number of rounds is common knowledge.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, players see the individual contributions of all group members. In the face-saving condition, photos are shown next to contributions.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Peer disapproval points are sent anonymously; the sender is not identified. The monetary sanction is imposed by the experimenter/central computer, not by other players.

## Important Limitations
- The primary outcome is contribution rate, not efficiency or group payoff; efficiency is not directly measured or reported.
- Punishment is implemented as a centralized, experimenter-imposed sanction, not as peer punishment with real costs to punishers.
- Peer disapproval points are non-costly and do not affect payoffs, limiting comparability to real-world peer punishment.
- The study does not analyze or report group earnings, welfare, or surplus, so direct prediction of efficiency is not possible.
- The findings may not generalize to settings with different group sizes, MPCRs, or real peer punishment with costs.
