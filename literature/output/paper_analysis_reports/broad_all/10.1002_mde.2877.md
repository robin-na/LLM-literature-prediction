# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The hidden costs of control revisited: Should a sanctioning policy be announced in advance?
Authors: Klempt, C., & Pull, K.
Journal: Managerial and Decision Economics
Year: 2018

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- agent activity level (effort)
- beliefs about sanctioning
- principal sanctioning choice
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the agent's activity level (effort), which is costly to the agent and beneficial to the principal. No direct group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported; all results are in terms of individual effort choices.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that the way sanctions are implemented and communicated matters: when the principal preannounces a sanctioning policy, agents interpret this as a signal of distrust, which can reduce their voluntary effort (crowding out). When the sanctioning decision is not announced in advance or is made by a computer, agents do not interpret it as a negative signal, and effort is higher. The study provides evidence for both incentive and signaling effects of sanctions, but all results are in terms of individual effort, not group payoff or efficiency. There is no direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or total surplus.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence relevant to predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-like environments, especially regarding the importance of how punishment is announced and who imposes it. If the prediction task involves settings where punishment is preannounced and personalized, one should expect possible crowding out of intrinsic motivation, potentially reducing the positive effect of punishment on efficiency. If punishment is not preannounced or is impersonal (e.g., random or computer-imposed), the negative signaling effect is absent, and the incentive effect dominates, likely leading to higher effort (and by analogy, higher efficiency). However, since the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, these implications are indirect and should be interpreted with caution.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game is played in pairs: one principal and one agent (2 players per group).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each pair plays one round of each of the three treatments (NO, PI, II) in a within-subjects design.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed; subjects are randomly and anonymously paired.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/effort choice is continuous: agent chooses activity level y in {0,1,...,120}.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is 2 for the principal per unit of agent effort; agent cost is 1 per unit effort.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (fine) is 10 points, imposed on the agent if effort is at or below threshold. No cost to the principal for imposing the fine.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is a fixed fine of 10 points deducted from the agent if effort is at or below threshold; no cost to the punisher.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the number and order of treatments in advance; each session includes all three treatments.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of all treatments, subjects receive feedback on their partner's decisions and resulting payoffs.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the participant condition, agents know whether the principal chose to sanction; in the computer condition, they know the decision is random. Identities are not revealed, but the source of punishment is known.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; all results are in terms of individual effort.
- The game is a principal-agent game, not a standard public goods game; results may not fully generalize to multi-player public goods settings.
- Punishment is costless to the punisher (principal), which differs from many public goods game designs.
- The experiment uses a within-subjects design and a relatively small sample size.
- No reward or bonus mechanism is studied, so results do not address the effects of positive incentives.
