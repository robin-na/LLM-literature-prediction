## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a public goods game with only rewards and meta-rewards (no punishment), cooperation can be sustained if players can observe and respond to the individual meta-rewarding rates of others. When players base their reward-giving on the observed likelihood of receiving a meta-reward from a specific cooperator, stable cooperation emerges. If players cannot condition on this information, cooperation collapses. The benefit-to-cost ratio of rewards and meta-rewards must be sufficiently high for cooperation to be maintained. The model does not include punishment, and efficiency or group payoff is not directly analyzed.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. It is relevant for understanding the role of reward and meta-reward mechanisms, and highlights the importance of observability and benefit-to-cost ratios for sustaining cooperation. However, since punishment is not modeled, and efficiency or group payoff is not a reported outcome, this paper should not be used as direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency. It may be useful for understanding adjacent mechanisms (reward-based), but not for the core prediction task.

## Important Limitations
- The model does not include punishment or sanction mechanisms; only rewards and meta-rewards are modeled.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare are not directly analyzed or reported as outcomes; only behavioral rates (cooperation, reward, meta-reward) are tracked.
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are presented.
- The model assumes all players can observe all relevant information in some scenarios, which may not be realistic.
- Findings may not generalize to environments where punishment is available or where efficiency is the primary outcome of interest.
