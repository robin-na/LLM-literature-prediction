# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Rent extraction and prosocial behavior
Authors: Cagala, T., Glogowsky, U., Grimm, V., Rincke, J., & Tuset-Cueva, A.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate
- prosocial behavior (measured as donations to non-profit associations)
- generosity (as inferred from contribution behavior)
- reciprocity (as inferred from contribution behavior)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the contribution rate (percentage of endowment donated to a non-profit association). No direct group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported. The study focuses on motivational effects of rent extraction on prosocial behavior, not on efficiency or total group earnings.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that when an administrator actively chooses to extract a 10% rent from contributions, average contributions to non-profit associations drop significantly (by 41%) compared to a control where the same share is assigned passively. This effect is robust to framing and is interpreted as a motivational effect: rent extraction undermines contributors' general willingness to give, rather than triggering targeted negative reciprocity. The effect persists even when contributors interact with a new, uninvolved administrator in a subsequent round, indicating a spillover on generosity. The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes, and the design is a donation game with some similarities to public goods games but is not a standard PGG.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that visible anti-social actions by an authority figure (rent extraction) can sharply reduce prosocial contributions in a donation context, even when the price of giving is held constant. However, because the outcome is contribution rate rather than efficiency or group payoff, and the game is a donation game rather than a standard public goods game, the findings should be transferred to PGG efficiency predictions with caution. The result suggests that introducing a salient, self-serving authority or punishment-like mechanism that is perceived as unfair or anti-social may reduce cooperation and thus efficiency, but the direct quantitative effect on efficiency in a PGG is not measured here.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 3 contributors and 1 administrator (4 players per group).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The experiment consists of two parts (rounds): Part 1 (Basic Rent-Extraction Game) and Part 2 (Extension of Basic Game). Each part involves a single contribution decision.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is continuous: contributors can choose any amount between 0 and 100 ECU.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The experimenter doubles transfers to associations (multiplier = 2), but the administrator may extract 10%. The effective MPCR for the association is 2*(1 - r)/3, but since the benefit accrues to the association (not the group), and the design is not a standard PGG, the MPCR is not directly comparable.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects are informed that the experiment consists of two distinct parts, and instructions for each part are given before the start of that part.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is contribution rate, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare; thus, direct implications for efficiency are not measured.
- The experimental design is a donation game with an administrator, not a standard public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present; rent extraction is an authority's self-serving action, not peer punishment.
- No direct measurement of group-level efficiency or total surplus is reported.
- The effect of rent extraction is measured as a reduction in prosocial contributions, not as a change in group payoff or efficiency.
- Spillover effects are measured only in the immediate subsequent round; long-term or delayed effects are not studied.
