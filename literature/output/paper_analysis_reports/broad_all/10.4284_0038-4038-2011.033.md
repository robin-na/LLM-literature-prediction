# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Do People Accurately Anticipate Sanctions?
Authors: Pérez, R. L., & Kisst, H. J.
Journal: Southern Economic Journal
Year: 2012

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- accuracy of expectations about sanctions (punishment/reward)
- sign and strength of anticipated sanctions
- prediction error (absolute and relative) in sanction expectations
- systematic bias in sanction expectations
- covariation of expectations and actual sanctions
- relation between beliefs and allocation choices
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the accuracy and bias of subjects' beliefs about sanctions (punishment/reward), not actual payoffs, efficiency, or group welfare. The study does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare as outcomes. Some analysis of allocation choices is provided, but not in terms of group payoff or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that subjects are generally able to anticipate the sign and strength of sanctions (punishment/reward) in two-player allocation games, with moderate errors. Punishment is better anticipated than reward, and nonmonetary sanctions are anticipated with smaller errors than monetary ones. Systematic underestimation occurs mainly where rewards are prevalent. The study does not analyze or report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes, and the games are not public goods games or close variants.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. The games studied are two-player allocation games with a sanctioning stage, not multi-player public goods games. The main outcomes are about belief accuracy, not efficiency or payoff. While the findings suggest that punishment is more salient and better anticipated than reward, and that errors in expectation are moderate, these insights are only indirectly relevant to predicting efficiency effects of punishment in public goods games. The paper may inform mechanism design regarding the salience and predictability of sanctions, but does not provide quantitative or qualitative evidence on efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are two-player (decider and sanctioner).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject plays four different games, but each is a one-shot (single round) allocation game; no repeated interaction within a game.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Decider chooses between two discrete allocations; not a continuous contribution or all-or-nothing public goods format.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays a fixed cost of 5 points to assign a sanction (punishment or reward) of up to 100 points (in multiples of 10).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment/reward technology: 5 points cost allows the sanctioner to increase or decrease the decider's payoff by up to 100 points (in multiples of 10).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sanctioner can both punish (decrease payoff) and reward (increase payoff) in the monetary treatment; can approve/disapprove in the nonmonetary treatment.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Cost to rewarder is the same as for punisher: 5 points per sanction (reward or punishment).
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward impact per unit cost: 5 points cost allows up to +100 points to decider (in multiples of 10).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the structure: four one-shot games, all presented at once; no uncertainty about number of rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. No feedback about other players' choices or payoffs during the experiment; no repeated game effects.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are anonymous; no identification of who punished or rewarded.

## Important Limitations
- The games are not public goods games or close variants; they are two-player allocation games with a sanctioning stage.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; the main outcomes are about belief accuracy, not payoff.
- The study does not analyze the effect of punishment on efficiency or group welfare.
- Findings may not generalize to multi-player or repeated public goods games.
- The within-subjects design and lack of feedback may affect external validity for real-world or repeated interactions.
- No manipulation of group size, MPCR, or other key public goods game parameters.
