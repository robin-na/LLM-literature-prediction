# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Sanctions that signal: An experiment
Authors: Galbiati, R., Schlag, K. H., & van der Weele, J. J.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2013

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- group effort (minimum effort in group)
- individual effort

Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual effort
- beliefs about partner's effort
- width of belief interval (uncertainty)
- sanctioning decisions by third party
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are effort (behavioral) and beliefs about others' effort. Payoff formulas are provided, but the analysis and results focus on effort and beliefs, not on group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. Some tables report mean effort and belief, but not efficiency as defined by total group payoff relative to the cooperative optimum.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that exogenous sanctions increase effort and beliefs about others' effort, with a stronger effect on effort than beliefs. Endogenous sanctions, where a principal chooses to sanction after observing first-round behavior, are less effective, especially for subjects who initially chose high effort. For low initial effort players, both types of sanctions have similar effects. The signaling effect—where the imposition of a sanction is interpreted as a signal that others are not cooperating—can counteract the incentive effect of sanctions for cooperators. The main analysis is on effort and beliefs, not on efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task. While it does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, it shows that sanctions can increase effort in a minimum-effort game, but the effect depends on whether sanctions are exogenous or endogenous and on subjects' prior behavior. The signaling effect can reduce the effectiveness of sanctions for cooperators. For prediction, this suggests that the context and perceived informational content of punishment mechanisms may moderate their effect on efficiency, especially in coordination settings. However, because efficiency is not directly measured, the findings should be used as informative but indirect evidence.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 3 players: 2 choose effort, 1 is a principal who may impose sanctions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is played for 2 rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Effort choice is continuous between 110 and 170, not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Payoff to each player is the minimum of the two efforts minus 0.85 times own effort. For the principal, payoff is 0.25 times the minimum effort. The marginal per-capita return is not standard as in PGG, but the structure is clear.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sanction cost to principal is 4 tokens per sanctioning decision.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sanction reduces each player's payoff by 0.5*(170 - effort), i.e., proportional to deviation from maximum effort.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Principal's payoff is proportional to minimum effort, but there is no explicit reward mechanism for players 1 and 2.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players do not know before the first round that there will be a second round; they are informed only after the first round.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players 1 and 2 do not observe each other's effort or payoffs before both rounds are over. Principal observes both efforts after round 1.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players 1 and 2 are told whether the principal (player 3) has chosen to introduce a sanction before round 2.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcomes are effort and beliefs, not efficiency or group payoff. Efficiency as defined by total group payoff relative to the cooperative optimum is not reported.
- The game is a minimum-effort coordination game, not a standard public goods game, so findings may not fully transfer to PGG settings.
- The experiment uses only two rounds, which may limit generalizability to repeated or longer games.
- The signaling effect studied is specific to settings where a principal has superior information and can choose to sanction, which may differ from peer punishment or democratic sanctioning mechanisms.
- No explicit analysis of payoff-based efficiency or welfare outcomes.
