## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a punishment mechanism—where players with below-average payoffs are punished by a reduction in their fitness coefficient—substantially increases the stationary frequency of cooperators in a spatial prisoner's dilemma model. Increasing the punishment rate and memory length both further promote cooperation, leading to full dominance of cooperation under many parameter settings. The mechanism's effect is attributed to increased heterogeneity in individual fitness, which supports network reciprocity. However, the paper does not report on group payoff, efficiency, or welfare, so its findings are limited to behavioral outcomes (cooperation rates) rather than payoff-based outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that punishment mechanisms can increase cooperation rates in spatial prisoner's dilemma environments, especially when punishment is more intense and lasts longer. However, because the model does not report efficiency or group payoff, and because the game is a prisoner's dilemma rather than a public goods game, the findings should be transferred to PGG-like efficiency prediction with caution. The results suggest that, in similar spatial or networked environments, enabling punishment may increase cooperation, which could plausibly increase efficiency, but this is not directly demonstrated. The specific punishment mechanism (punishing those with below-average payoffs) is also nonstandard compared to typical PGG punishment designs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a large fixed population on a 400x400 lattice (160,000 players), with each player interacting with 4 neighbors. Player count is not varied.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 50,000 Monte Carlo steps, with outcomes averaged over the last 5,000 steps. The number of rounds is fixed and not varied.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate or defect (binary choice).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is modeled as a reduction in the punished player's fitness coefficient by a factor (1-α) if their payoff is below the neighborhood average. There is no explicit cost to the punisher; punishment is automatic and not chosen by players.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the punishment rate α, which directly reduces the punished player's fitness coefficient. Higher α leads to greater impact and more cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial evolutionary prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates and heterogeneity are analyzed.
- Punishment is not voluntary or costly to the punisher; it is an automatic reduction in fitness for low-payoff players, which differs from standard PGG punishment mechanisms.
- The model uses a very large, fixed population and does not explore variation in player count or group size.
- No communication, reward, or information-sharing mechanisms are present.
