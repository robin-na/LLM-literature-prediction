Outcome notes:
The model's main outcome is the long-term average payoff per round for both actors and recipients, which is used as the measure of fitness and efficiency. Behavioral strategies (helping, harming, avoidance) are analyzed as mechanisms, but the main dependent variable is payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in asymmetric games where only one party (the actor) can help or exploit, and the other (the recipient) can only choose whether to interact, a form of indirect reciprocity can evolve. Actors help when the gains from exploitation are low to maintain a positive image, which encourages recipients to approach them in the future, allowing profitable exploitation when the gains are high. This strategy is stable and increases the long-term average payoff for both actors and recipients, provided certain conditions on observability, state-switching, and payoff parameters are met. The model shows that efficiency (average group payoff) is higher under these conditions than if actors always exploit or if recipients always avoid interaction.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that, in asymmetric games with image scoring and conditional approach, mechanisms analogous to punishment (here, avoidance of exploiters) can increase efficiency by supporting conditional cooperation. The findings suggest that when harmful behavior is observable and actors' states vary, efficiency is maximized for intermediate values of key parameters (cost of harm, switching probability, observability). While not a PGG or direct punishment setting, the model is adjacent and supports the idea that social mechanisms that deter exploitation (even without direct punishment) can improve group efficiency. For prediction, the paper suggests that enabling mechanisms for reputation-based avoidance or indirect sanctions can increase efficiency, especially when exploitation is not always profitable and can be detected.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Model is based on random pairwise interactions in a large population; the number of players is not fixed but assumed large enough that repeated encounters are negligible.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Model assumes a large number of rounds; long-term average payoff per round is the main outcome.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Actors choose between discrete actions: help or harm (all-or-nothing choice).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Recipients observe whether actors harmed others in the previous round with probability e; this observability is crucial for the stability of conditional helping and higher efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and not empirically tested in this paper.
- It is not a public goods game or direct punishment setting, but an asymmetric dyadic game with image scoring.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanism; only avoidance based on observed behavior is modeled.
- Assumes large population and random matching, so repeated encounters between the same individuals are negligible.
- Parameter values (e.g., observability, state-switching) are abstract and may not map directly to experimental PGG settings.
