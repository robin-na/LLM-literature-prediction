## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The reputation-based payoff transfer mechanism, which redistributes payoffs from low- to high-reputation players, can significantly increase the fraction of cooperators in a spatial public goods game. The effect depends on the parameters controlling the proportion of players who lose or gain payoffs (transfer ratios) and the strength of the transfer. When both the proportion of punished (transferred) and rewarded (accepted) players are moderate and balanced, cooperation can quickly dominate the population. The mechanism is most effective when all players are either punished or rewarded (transfer ratio = 1). However, the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, so the effect on efficiency is not directly assessed.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that mechanisms combining endogenous punishment and reward based on reputation can increase cooperation rates in public goods games, especially when the redistribution is balanced and covers the whole population. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, it does not directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings may be useful for predicting cooperation rates, but not for predicting efficiency outcomes as required by the downstream task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a lattice of size L=100, so 10,000 players; each player has 4 neighbors (von Neumann neighborhood).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for at least 10,000 steps, or until cooperation stabilizes.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: each player chooses to cooperate (contribute 1) or defect (contribute 0).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r=3, group size n+1=5, so MPCR = r/(n+1) = 0.6.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a proportion (transfer strength s) of the payoff of low-reputation players being transferred; s is varied in simulations.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is endogenous: high-reputation players receive transferred payoffs as a bonus.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is determined by the size of the bonus fund and the number of accepted players; both are varied via parameters a and b.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players can see their own and others' reputation scores and rankings; full information is available.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings; all outcomes are in terms of cooperation rate.
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- The punishment and reward mechanisms are endogenous and based on reputation rankings, not standard peer or institutional punishment with explicit costs.
- Findings may not generalize to standard PGG punishment treatments with explicit costs and direct punishment choices.
- No analysis of the effect of the mechanism on group welfare or efficiency, so relevance to efficiency prediction is limited.
