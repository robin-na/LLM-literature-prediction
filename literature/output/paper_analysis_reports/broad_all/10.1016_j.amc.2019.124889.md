## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a costly cooperative loner (CL) as a third strategy in a prisoner's dilemma game does not prevent the collapse to all-defection, nor does it improve social average payoff (efficiency) compared to the standard two-strategy game. The CL strategy is always outcompeted and goes extinct, after which defectors dominate. This is in contrast to models with costly punishment, where punishment can sometimes sustain cooperation and higher efficiency. The paper provides a detailed analysis of the payoff matrix and evolutionary trajectories, showing that the addition of CL does not alter the efficiency outcome: the system always ends in the least efficient (all-defector) state.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides a negative case: simply adding a third strategy that is not an active punisher (but pays a cost to avoid defectors) does not improve efficiency in a prisoner's dilemma environment. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the presence of a 'costly loner' or similar non-punishing third strategy is not sufficient to increase efficiency. The results reinforce that only certain types of third strategies (e.g., active costly punishment or rewarding) can shift efficiency outcomes. The findings are most relevant for infinite, well-mixed populations and may not generalize to finite or structured populations. The paper does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment per se, but its comparison to punishment models supports the view that punishment (with sufficient impact and cost structure) is necessary for efficiency gains in these settings.

- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses replicator dynamics, which is equivalent to infinite repeated rounds (continuous time).
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Strategies are discrete: cooperate, defect, or costly cooperative loner (all-or-nothing choices).
- punishment cost: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Punishment cost is discussed in comparison models (Table 1c), but not implemented in the main model. The CL strategy pays a cost to avoid defectors, but this is not a punishment cost.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and assumes an infinite, well-mixed population; results may not generalize to finite or structured populations.
- No direct implementation of punishment or reward mechanisms in the main model; only discussed in comparison.
- The model is a prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game, so direct transfer to PGG settings may be limited.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are from mathematical analysis and simulation.
- No consideration of communication, reputation, or other social mechanisms.
