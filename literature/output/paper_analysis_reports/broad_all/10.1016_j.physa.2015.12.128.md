Some discussion of expected payoffs is present in the theoretical analysis, but efficiency or group welfare is not a primary reported outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Conditional dissociation, modeled as a punishment mechanism where players can break up with defectors and must wait before re-entering the game, promotes the evolution of cooperation in an iterated Prisoner's Dilemma. The effect is stronger when the punishment is more severe (longer waiting time) and when the population's lifespan is longer. Allowing mixed strategies (as opposed to only pure strategies) reduces the probability of ending up with a fully cooperative population. The model is clean, excluding other cooperation-promoting mechanisms, and shows that both intolerant cooperators and tolerant defectors can be stable outcomes. The main outcomes are the frequency of cooperative vs. defective strategies, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the effect of punishment-like mechanisms (conditional dissociation) on cooperation in repeated pairwise social dilemmas. While not a public goods game, the findings suggest that punishment mechanisms that impose a cost on defectors (and sometimes on punishers) can promote cooperation, especially when the punishment is more severe and when the game is longer. The absence of direct efficiency or group payoff outcomes limits its direct applicability to efficiency prediction, but the results support the general idea that increasing punishment severity (e.g., higher punishment cost or effectiveness) can increase cooperation, though possibly at the expense of overall efficiency if punishers also incur costs. The negative effect of mixed strategies on cooperation may also be relevant for predicting outcomes in more flexible or noisy environments.

## Important Limitations
- The model is for pairwise Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to multi-player settings.
- Outcomes are primarily behavioral (cooperation level, strategy frequencies), not group efficiency or total payoff.
- Punishment (waiting time) imposes costs on both punisher and punished, which may reduce overall efficiency even as cooperation increases.
- No empirical or experimental data; results are from theory and simulation.
- No communication, reputation, or other social mechanisms are included.
- Population is well-mixed and large; effects of population structure or size are not analyzed.
- No analysis of the effect of showing others' contributions, payoffs, or punishment identity.
