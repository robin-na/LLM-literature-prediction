## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
This field-lab experiment in rural South Africa and Namibia used a repeated common-pool resource game with 5-person groups over 20 rounds. After 10 rounds, groups voted to implement one of three rules: penalty (punishment), reward, or communication. The introduction of any rule led to a significant reduction in resource extraction (i.e., more cooperation), but there was no significant difference between the effects of penalty, reward, or communication rules on extraction levels after accounting for group dynamics. Group composition (e.g., gender, wealth heterogeneity) and dynamic social interactions had larger effects on cooperation than the specific institutional rule. Payoff-related outcomes (individual and group payoffs) were analyzed as predictors of behavior, but efficiency (group payoff relative to optimum) was not directly reported. The main behavioral outcome is the number of sheep chosen, which is inversely related to cooperation and efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that introducing a punishment (penalty) rule in a repeated common-pool resource game with 5 players and 20 rounds improves cooperation (reduces extraction), thus likely increasing efficiency relative to the control (no-rule) condition. However, the effect of punishment is not significantly different from reward or communication rules after accounting for group dynamics. The efficiency gain from punishment is thus positive, but the magnitude is similar to that of other rule-based interventions. Group composition and social context are strong moderators. For prediction, the presence of a punishment rule should be expected to increase efficiency compared to no rule, but the effect size may be modest and context-dependent.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consisted of 5 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 rounds per session: 10 rounds baseline, 10 rounds after rule change.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. One of the three possible rules implemented after round 10 was face-to-face communication.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/extraction choice was discrete: each player chose an integer number of sheep (0 to 8).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The payoff function is specified; Nash equilibrium and social optimum are calculated. For N=5, parameters are given (p, c, d).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward rule (randomized reward) was one of the three possible rules implemented after round 10.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of rounds (20) was fixed and known to participants.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants knew the aggregate cooperation level of other players in the group.

## Important Limitations
- Efficiency (group payoff relative to optimum) is not directly reported; only extraction levels and payoffs are analyzed.
- Punishment and reward are externally imposed rules, not peer-to-peer; costs and effectiveness per unit are not specified.
- The main outcome is behavioral (number of sheep chosen), not direct group efficiency.
- Group composition and social context are strong moderators, making generalization to other settings difficult.
- The experiment is a framed field-lab study with a specific rural population, which may limit external validity.
