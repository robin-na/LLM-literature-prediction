Outcome notes:
The paper analyzes the stability and nature of equilibria in evolutionary dynamics of pairwise social dilemma games with destructive and constructive agents. It does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. Outcomes are about which strategies persist or dominate, not about aggregate payoffs.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in pairwise social dilemma games, introducing destructive agents (who opt out and harm others) does not promote cooperation, in contrast to their effect in public goods games. Instead, destructive agents destabilize or replace defection (in prisoner's dilemma and stag-hunt) or undermine cooperation (in harmony and chicken games). Constructive agents (who opt out and benefit others) can have similar destabilizing effects if their own payoff is high, but if their payoff is lower than what they give, they mainly destabilize defection without harming cooperation. When both agent types are present, the system exhibits complex multistable equilibria, but cooperation is not promoted. The analysis is entirely theoretical, using replicator dynamics and stability analysis. No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or their close variants. It does not model punishment, sanctions, or payoff-based outcomes, and its focus is on pairwise (dyadic) games rather than multiplayer public goods games. The closest relevance is in its discussion of how certain opt-out strategies (destructive/constructive agents) affect the stability of cooperation, but not efficiency or group payoff. Therefore, this paper should not be weighted strongly in predicting efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like environments. It may be useful for understanding the limits of voluntary participation and opt-out mechanisms in pairwise games, but not for the core prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is for pairwise (dyadic) games: all interactions are between two players at a time.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Theoretical model assumes infinite repeated interactions in a well-mixed population (replicator dynamics), not discrete rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are discrete: cooperate, defect, or opt out (as destructive/constructive agent). No continuous contribution.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is entirely theoretical and does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or any aggregate payoff-based outcomes.
- The model is for pairwise (dyadic) social dilemma games, not multiplayer public goods games.
- No punishment or reward mechanism is modeled in the sense relevant to PGGs; destructive and constructive agents are opt-out strategies, not punishers or rewarders.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are from replicator dynamics and stability analysis.
- Findings about destabilization or replacement of equilibria do not translate directly to predictions about efficiency or group payoff.
