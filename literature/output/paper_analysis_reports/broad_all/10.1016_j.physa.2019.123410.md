## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation level (normalized average claim, f_C)
- strategy distribution
- effect of learning mechanism (PSO vs Fermi dynamics)
- impact of self-cognition vs social cognition
Outcome notes:
The main outcome is the normalized cooperation level (f_C), which is the normalized average claim in the traveler's dilemma game. While higher f_C implies higher group payoffs, the paper does not directly report efficiency, total payoff, or welfare as a primary outcome. The focus is on behavioral dynamics and the effect of learning mechanisms.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that using a PSO-based learning mechanism in the traveler's dilemma game leads to higher levels of cooperation (higher average claims) compared to traditional Fermi dynamics, across different network types. The effect of the reward/punishment parameter (R) and the balance between self-cognition and social cognition (c1) are explored: social learning promotes cooperation when the reward/punishment is small (large strategy interval), while self-cognition becomes more important as the reward/punishment increases (small strategy interval). The main outcome is the normalized cooperation level, not efficiency or total group payoff. The results are robust across network types, but the study is simulation-based and does not report direct efficiency or payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence about how learning mechanisms and the structure of reward/punishment in a traveler's dilemma game (an adjacent but not exact variant of the public goods game) affect the prevalence of cooperative behavior (high claims). It does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency or group payoff in public goods games with or without punishment. However, it suggests that the effectiveness of punishment/reward mechanisms in promoting cooperation depends on the size of the incentive and the learning dynamics. For the downstream prediction task, this paper is only weakly informative, as it does not report efficiency or payoff outcomes and studies a different game structure.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use Moore lattices of size 200x200 (40,000 players) and ER/BA networks of size 1000. The effect of player count is not analyzed as a moderator.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 time steps, with averaging over the last 1,000 steps. The number of rounds is fixed and not varied as a moderator.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (claim) is continuous in [R, M], not all-or-nothing.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment/reward parameter R is varied; R is the amount deducted from the higher claimant and added to the lower claimant. The cost to the punisher is not separated from the impact, as in TDG the mechanism is symmetric and automatic.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment/reward effectiveness is determined by R; higher R increases the strength of the incentive. The impact per unit cost is not separately analyzed, but R is the key parameter.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is present in the TDG mechanism: the lower claimant receives R as a reward.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is implemented as part of the TDG mechanism; the cost is not separated from the impact, as it is a zero-sum transfer between players.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is determined by R, as with punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The study is simulation-based and does not report direct efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; the main outcome is normalized cooperation level (average claim).
- The traveler's dilemma game is only adjacent to public goods games; results may not transfer directly to PGG-like environments.
- The punishment/reward mechanism is automatic and symmetric, not voluntary as in most PGG punishment studies.
- No experimental or empirical data; all results are from agent-based simulations.
- No analysis of how control efficiency (without punishment) compares to efficiency with punishment, as required for the downstream prediction task.
