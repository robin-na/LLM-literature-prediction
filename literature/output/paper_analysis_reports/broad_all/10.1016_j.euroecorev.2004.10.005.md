# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Social status and group norms: Indirect reciprocity in a repeated helping experiment
Authors: Seinen, I., & Schram, A.
Journal: European Economic Review
Year: 2006

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- expected return for various levels of social status (e.g., highest return for keeping a score of 4 out of 6 in HCI is 85 cents per round, lowest for score 0 is 58 cents per round)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of helpful (cooperative) choices per treatment and group
- conditional cooperation based on recipient's social status
- individual strategy classification (e.g., unconditional cooperation, defection, cut-point strategies)
- development and variation of group norms
- impact of information about recipient's past behavior on donor's decision
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the fraction of helpful (cooperative) choices. Some expected payoff calculations are reported for different strategies, but efficiency or group payoff is not the primary focus. No explicit group efficiency or welfare analysis is conducted.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds strong evidence for indirect reciprocity: donors are significantly more likely to help recipients who have helped others in the past, as measured by the recipient's social status. When information about social status is available, cooperation rates are much higher (e.g., 70% in high-cost/information vs. 22% in high-cost/no-information). Individual strategies often use cut-points based on the recipient's or donor's own social status, and group-level norms about the minimum acceptable status to be helped emerge and are stable within groups but differ across groups. The paper does not directly measure efficiency or group payoff, but does provide some expected return calculations for different strategies. There is no explicit punishment mechanism; 'punishment' is indirect, in the sense that donors withhold help from low-status recipients.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides informative evidence about the effects of information and social norms on cooperation in repeated helping games, which are adjacent to public goods games. The design does not include explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanisms, but the withholding of help from low-status individuals functions as an indirect sanction. The main outcome is the rate of helping (cooperation), not efficiency or group payoff, though some expected returns are calculated. For prediction tasks about efficiency in public goods games with punishment, this paper is only indirectly informative: it suggests that information about others' past behavior and the emergence of group norms can sustain high cooperation rates, but does not provide direct evidence about the efficiency effects of explicit punishment mechanisms. The design dimensions most relevant here are information availability, cost/benefit structure, and group size, but the absence of explicit punishment and the focus on individual rather than group payoffs limit direct applicability.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session had 28 participants, split into two groups of 14 for random matching. Large group size is a key design feature.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. At least 90 rounds per session, with additional rounds possible (each with 90% continuation probability).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/helping decision is binary: help (pay cost, recipient gets benefit) or pass (no cost, no benefit).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is b/c ratio: recipient gets 250, donor pays 50 (LCI) or 150 (HCI/HCN). MPCR = 250/50 = 5 (LCI), 250/150 ≈ 1.67 (HCI/HCN).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants knew there would be at least 90 rounds, then continuation with 90% probability per round. The end was not exactly known.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Donors saw a summary of the recipient's last six choices as donor (number of help/pass). Participants also saw their own last six choices and their own outcomes. No summary statistics about others beyond the recipient.

## Important Limitations
- No explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanism; only indirect sanctions via withholding help.
- Primary outcome is cooperation/helping rate, not efficiency or group payoff.
- Design is not a public goods game but a repeated helping game with random matching and binary choices.
- No analysis of group-level efficiency or welfare; only some expected return calculations for individual strategies.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with explicit punishment or to standard public goods games.
