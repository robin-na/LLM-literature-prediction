# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The Detrimental Effects of Sanctions on Intragroup Trust: Comparing Punishments and Rewards
Authors: Irwin, K., Mulder, L., & Simpson, B.
Journal: Social Psychology Quarterly
Year: 2014

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- trust in group members
- contribution amount (cooperation)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are trust and cooperation (measured as contribution amount in a public goods game). No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings is reported, though contribution is a closely related behavioral outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Both punishment and reward systems, when present, can increase short-term cooperation (especially when sanctions are moderate rather than extreme), but their removal leads to a significant decline in trust, and in some cases, a decline in cooperation. Strong sanctions do not increase cooperation above control, and their removal reduces cooperation. Weaker sanctions increase cooperation while present, but their removal does not immediately reduce cooperation, though trust is still undermined. The negative effects on trust are similar for both punishment and reward. The paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, so findings must be interpreted through the lens of contribution/cooperation rates.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that the presence and removal of punishment (and reward) systems can have complex effects on cooperation, which is a behavioral proxy for efficiency but not a direct measure. Strong punishment does not increase cooperation above control, and its removal reduces cooperation. Weaker punishment increases cooperation while present, but its removal does not immediately reduce cooperation. The undermining of trust by sanctions is robust, regardless of sanction strength. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the effect of punishment on efficiency may depend on the strength of the sanction and the baseline level of cooperation, but direct efficiency outcomes are not reported. Use caution when extrapolating to efficiency, as only contribution rates are measured.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants believed they were in groups of 4 (themselves plus 3 others).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each experiment consisted of two one-shot public goods games (phases), with sanctions present in phase 1 and removed in phase 2.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication with others was prohibited; all instructions and interactions were via computer.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution was a continuous choice: participants decided how much of their endowment to contribute (0-100 points).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Total group contributions were doubled and divided equally among 4 players. Thus, MPCR = 0.5 (multiplier 2 / 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment was externally imposed and automatic; in Study 1, the two lowest contributors lost 100% of their endowment (5 Euros); in Study 2, they lost 60%. No cost to any punisher, as punishment was not assigned by players.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment was highly effective: in Study 1, 100% of endowment; in Study 2, 60%.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward condition present: two highest contributors received a reward (5 Euros in Study 1, 60% of endowment in Study 2).
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward was externally imposed and automatic; no cost to any player, as rewards were not assigned by players.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward was highly effective: in Study 1, 100% of endowment; in Study 2, 60%.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were not aware of the second phase during phase 1, but each phase was a one-shot game. The total number of phases was not known in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. No information about others' decisions was provided; participants did not receive feedback about others' contributions or outcomes.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were not informed as to who was punished or rewarded; punishment/reward was anonymous and automatic.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcomes are trust and cooperation (contribution), not efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings.
- Punishment and reward were externally imposed and automatic, not peer-assigned; thus, findings may not generalize to peer punishment settings.
- The design used only two one-shot games (phases), so long-term or repeated effects are not measured.
- No direct measure of attributional mechanism (external vs. internal motivation) was included.
- The effect of sanction removal on cooperation may require more repeated interactions to manifest, especially for weaker sanctions.
- All participants received the same payment regardless of performance, which may affect external validity for payoff-based outcomes.
