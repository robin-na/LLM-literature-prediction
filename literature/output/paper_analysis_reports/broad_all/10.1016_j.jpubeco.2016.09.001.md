# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Static and dynamic underinvestment: An experimental investigation
Authors: Agranov, M., Fréchette, G., Palfrey, T., & Vespa, E.
Journal: Journal of Public Economics
Year: 2016

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- total welfare (group surplus)
- public good investment (relative to efficient and equilibrium benchmarks)
- static and dynamic inefficiency (relative to planner's solution)
- payoffs to individuals and coalitions
Non-payoff outcomes:
- strategy type (MM, EM, EE)
- frequency of minimum winning coalitions
- punishment/reward behavior in coalition formation
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group welfare (total surplus) and public good investment, both compared to efficient and equilibrium benchmarks. Behavioral outcomes such as coalition type and strategic punishment/reward are also analyzed, but the focus is on payoff-based inefficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that underprovision of the public good is substantial in both static and dynamic settings, with dynamic inefficiency (the additional inefficiency due to intertemporal linkage) increasing as the survival rate of the public good rises. When the public good is highly durable, dynamic inefficiency accounts for a large share of total inefficiency. Subjects use both minimum winning coalition (MWC) strategies and strategic cooperation, including informal punishment (exclusion from coalitions) to reward or punish past behavior. However, the game does not include a formal punishment technology (e.g., costly punishment as in standard PGGs). Welfare losses are decomposed into static and dynamic components, and the results show that dynamic inefficiency is empirically significant and increases with the strength of the dynamic link. The findings are robust across different strategy types and are consistent with theoretical predictions, though the magnitude of inefficiency is somewhat less than predicted by theory.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is highly informative for understanding how dynamic linkages and coalition bargaining affect efficiency in public good provision, especially in legislative or committee settings. However, it does not manipulate or include a formal punishment mechanism as in standard PGGs. Instead, it studies endogenous, informal punishment via coalition exclusion. Therefore, while the results are directly relevant for predicting efficiency in dynamic, coalition-based public goods environments, they are only indirectly relevant for predicting the effect of adding formal punishment to a standard PGG. The main lesson is that efficiency is sensitive to dynamic linkages and coalition rules, and that informal punishment can partially substitute for formal mechanisms, but the quantitative effect of formal punishment cannot be inferred from this study.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games use committees of 3 players (n=3).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each match consists of 2 periods (rounds). Each subject plays 10 matches.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (public investment) is continuous: players propose any split of the budget between public good and private allocations.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is determined by the utility function u(g) = 5*sqrt(g), with diminishing returns. Not a linear PGG; MPCR is not constant.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game is two periods per match; the structure is fully known.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects can follow the full history of the experiment in a box at the bottom of the screen.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players can identify who made which proposal and who is included/excluded from coalitions; IDs remain the same within a match.

## Important Limitations
- The game is not a standard public goods game (PGG) but a two-period legislative bargaining game with endogenous coalition formation.
- There is no formal punishment or reward technology; punishment is only via coalition exclusion, which is costless to the punisher.
- The results do not directly estimate the effect of adding formal punishment to a PGG; transferability to standard PGGs is limited.
- The public good has diminishing returns (nonlinear utility), so results may not generalize to linear PGGs.
- Only three-player groups and two periods are studied; results may differ with larger groups or longer horizons.
- The experiment does not manipulate or test communication (chat) or default contribution framing.
