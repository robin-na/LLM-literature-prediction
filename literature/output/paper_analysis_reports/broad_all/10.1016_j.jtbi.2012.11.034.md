## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that both peer-punishment (overpunishing) and shared-punishment (fixed sanction per defector, cost shared among punishers) in voluntary PGGs can lead to the fixation of cooperation and high group efficiency, provided antisocial punishment is absent. The main difference is quantitative: overpunishing increases the fraction of initial conditions leading to full cooperation, but shared-punishment is sufficient to achieve efficient outcomes in most cases, especially when stochasticity (finite populations) is considered. The likelihood of efficient cooperation increases with group size, sanction severity, and the public goods multiplier. The paper provides explicit payoff formulas and analyzes the basins of attraction for efficient (cooperative) equilibria under a wide range of parameter settings.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support that enabling punishment in voluntary PGGs increases group efficiency, even when punishment is mild (shared-punishment). The effect is robust across a wide range of group sizes, round numbers, and parameter values. The paper gives explicit formulas for payoffs and the conditions under which cooperation (and thus efficiency) is achieved. For prediction tasks, the presence of voluntary participation, absence of antisocial punishment, and the ability to punish defectors (even with fixed, shared-cost sanctions) are sufficient to expect a large increase in efficiency relative to the no-punishment baseline. The effect is stronger with larger groups, higher punishment severity, and higher public goods multipliers. The findings are directly relevant for predicting efficiency in PGG-like games with similar design features.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not report experimental or field data.
- The model assumes infinite or large finite populations and evolutionary dynamics, which may not capture all features of real-world or laboratory PGGs.
- Antisocial punishment is assumed absent; the positive effects of punishment on efficiency do not hold if antisocial punishment is allowed.
- No communication, reputation, or memory is allowed; results may not generalize to settings with these features.
- The model does not specify a finite number of rounds, so direct mapping to finite repeated games may be limited.
