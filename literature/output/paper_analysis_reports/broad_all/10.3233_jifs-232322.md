Outcome notes:
The paper reports on the evolution of cooperation (i.e., the proportion of cooperators) as the main outcome. While payoffs are calculated in the model, the reported results and figures focus on cooperation rates, not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that (1) heterogeneous social preferences (especially altruism) promote higher cooperation rates in spatial PGGs; (2) dynamic punishment mechanisms are more effective than static punishment in increasing cooperation and addressing second-order free-riding; (3) the enhancement factor (r_e, analogous to MPCR) is a key moderator—when it is low, cooperation is hard to sustain even with punishment, but when it is high, cooperation is high even without punishment; (4) inequality aversion slightly inhibits cooperation, and reciprocity has a mixed effect depending on r_e. All findings are based on simulation of agent-based models, with the main outcome being the proportion of cooperators.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the prediction task: it suggests that enabling punishment (especially dynamic punishment) increases cooperation rates in spatial PGGs, particularly when the enhancement factor is moderate. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, it does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency changes. The findings are most useful for understanding how punishment and social preference heterogeneity affect cooperation rates, which may correlate with efficiency in some settings. The model's focus on spatial structure and dynamic punishment is relevant for games with similar features.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Simulations are run with N=100, 200, 500, 1000; increasing player count slightly increases cooperation rate, but effect is small compared to other factors.
- number of rounds: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Simulations run for up to 10^5 rounds; number of rounds is not varied as a treatment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: players choose to cooperate (contribute c) or defect (contribute 0).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Enhancement factor r_e is varied from 1 to 5.5; higher r_e (higher MPCR) increases cooperation and makes punishment more effective.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost to punisher (M1) is set to 1; dynamic punishment makes cost proportional to number of defectors. Lower cost per punisher when few defectors.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness (fine to defector, M2) is set to r_e; dynamic punishment increases fine as number of defectors increases.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Players update strategies based on their own and neighbors' payoffs/utilities, implying some local summary information is available.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare as an outcome; only cooperation rates are analyzed.
- All results are from agent-based simulations, not empirical or experimental data.
- The model assumes all-or-nothing contribution and does not include communication, reward, or identification of punishers.
- Social preferences are assigned as fixed and mutually exclusive for each agent, which may not reflect real human heterogeneity.
- Network structure is static; dynamic networks are not considered.
- Findings may not generalize to well-mixed or non-spatial PGGs.
