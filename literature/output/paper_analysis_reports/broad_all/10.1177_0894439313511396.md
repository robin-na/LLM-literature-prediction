Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the contribution rate (i.e., the proportion of rounds in which the human subject contributes to the public good). There is no direct reporting of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare. The payoff structure is described, but outcomes are not reported in payoff or efficiency terms.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that when punishment is inflicted by the entire group, human subjects are more likely to cooperate (i.e., contribute to the public good) than when punishment is uncoordinated, even though the material cost to defectors is held constant. The authors attribute this to the norm-signaling function of group punishment, which is hypothesized to activate both instrumental and normative motivations for compliance. Agent-based simulations support the idea that norm salience, as modeled in the EMIL-I-A architecture, can explain the observed human behavior. However, the study does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare, so the findings are limited to behavioral outcomes (contribution rates).

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the structure of punishment (specifically, whether it is coordinated by the group or uncoordinated) can significantly affect cooperation rates in public goods games, even when the material incentives are held constant. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, the paper suggests that group punishment may lead to higher efficiency than uncoordinated punishment, but this is inferred from contribution rates rather than directly measured. The absence of direct efficiency or payoff data limits the ability to calibrate quantitative predictions of efficiency gains from punishment. The findings are most useful for understanding the qualitative impact of group punishment structure and norm-signaling on cooperation, which may be relevant for predicting efficiency when combined with other sources.

- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game lasts 40 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants are informed about individual earnings and the decisions of each participant in their group.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; all results are in terms of contribution rates.
- The experiment uses one human subject and three confederate virtual players per group, which may limit ecological validity.
- The exact cost to the punisher for administering punishment is not specified.
- The findings on norm-signaling and group punishment are inferred from behavioral outcomes, not directly from payoff or efficiency data.
- The generalizability to settings with all-human groups or different group sizes is not tested.
