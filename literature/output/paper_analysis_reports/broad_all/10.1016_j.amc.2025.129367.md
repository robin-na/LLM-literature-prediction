Outcome notes:
The main reported outcome is the fraction of cooperators in the steady state. The paper also analyzes the density of different group types and the spatial evolution of strategies. While a theoretical expression for the payoff difference between cooperators and defectors is provided, the paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare as a primary outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that punishing the minority strategy in each group (regardless of whether it is cooperation or defection) can strongly promote the emergence of cooperation in a spatial public goods game. As the punishment fine increases, the fraction of cooperators rises, and for high punishment, the system can abruptly transition from full defection to full cooperation as the multiplication factor increases. Theoretical analysis shows that the payoff difference between cooperators and defectors increases with punishment severity, favoring the expansion of cooperator clusters. However, the paper does not report group efficiency or total payoff, so the effect of this punishment rule on overall welfare or efficiency remains unclear.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that punishing the minority strategy in a spatial PGG can increase the fraction of cooperators, especially with higher punishment fines. However, because the main outcome is cooperation rate and not efficiency or group payoff, the findings should be used with caution for predicting efficiency outcomes. Theoretical payoff differences are provided, but without explicit efficiency or welfare analysis, it is unclear whether the increased cooperation translates to higher group efficiency, especially given the costs of punishment. The paper is most informative for predicting cooperation rates under minority punishment rules, but less so for efficiency predictions required by the downstream task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each PGG group consists of 5 players (a focal individual and its 4 neighbors on a square lattice).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 1,000,000 Monte Carlo steps, with outcomes averaged over the last 10,000 steps. The game is iterated for many rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: cooperators contribute 1 unit, defectors contribute 0.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. MPCR is r/5, where r is the multiplication factor varied in the simulations. Higher r increases cooperation rate.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost is shared among majority punishers; cost per punisher is a function of the fine α and group composition. Higher α increases cooperation rate.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment fine α is the effectiveness per punished minority; higher α increases the impact of punishment and promotes cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not group efficiency or total payoff; efficiency effects of minority punishment are not directly analyzed.
- The model is theoretical/simulation-based and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- Punishment is applied to the minority strategy, which can include both cooperators and defectors, making the mechanism different from standard antisocial or prosocial punishment.
- The spatial lattice structure may limit generalizability to other settings.
- No analysis of the cost of punishment relative to group welfare or efficiency.
