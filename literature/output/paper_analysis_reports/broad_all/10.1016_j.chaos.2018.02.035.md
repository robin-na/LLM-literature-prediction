Outcome notes:
The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators (cooperation rate) in the population. No explicit analysis of group payoff, efficiency, or welfare is reported. The study is based on simulation of a spatial prisoner's dilemma model, not a public goods game.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that reputation-based coevolution of link weights in a spatial prisoner's dilemma game promotes cooperation. When link weights are allowed to vary (parameterized by delta), higher heterogeneity in link weights leads to higher levels of cooperation. Punishment is implemented as a reduction in link weight to the lowest-reputation neighbor, and reward as an increase to the highest-reputation neighbor. The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators, not efficiency or group payoff. The effect of punishment and reward is to facilitate the formation of cooperative clusters, which resist invasion by defectors. The effect is stronger for larger delta (greater possible link weight variation).

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that reputation-based punishment and reward mechanisms can increase cooperation rates in spatial social dilemma settings. However, because the model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma (not a public goods game), and the outcome is cooperation rate rather than efficiency or group payoff, its direct applicability to predicting efficiency effects of punishment in public goods games is limited. The findings may be useful for understanding the role of network structure, reputation, and coevolving relationships in promoting cooperation, but do not provide quantitative guidance for efficiency prediction in PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run on a square lattice of size L=200, so each simulation has 40,000 players. The player count is fixed and not varied.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 100,000 Monte Carlo steps, with outcomes averaged over the last 1,000 steps. The number of rounds is fixed and not varied.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate or defect (binary choice).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a reduction in link weight (by beta) to the lowest-reputation neighbor. The cost is not explicitly modeled as a payoff loss to the punisher, but as a change in network structure.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the parameter beta (amount by which link weight is reduced). Beta is varied in the simulations.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is implemented as an increase in link weight (by beta) to the highest-reputation neighbor.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is determined by beta (amount by which link weight is increased).

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- Punishment and reward are implemented as changes in link weights (network structure), not as explicit payoff transfers or costs.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are from agent-based simulations.
- No analysis of how these mechanisms affect group efficiency or total payoff.
