Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:

Outcome notes:
The paper reports the stationary fractions of each strategy (D, C, M) as the main outcome, which are behavioral rather than direct payoff or efficiency measures. There is no explicit calculation or reporting of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that adding a 'smart cooperator' strategy (who both punishes defectors and rewards cooperators, at a cost) to a spatial prisoner's dilemma model leads to cyclic dominance among defectors, cooperators, and smart cooperators. This cyclic dominance produces counter-intuitive effects: for example, increasing the temptation to defect can reduce the prevalence of defectors by indirectly supporting smart cooperators. Similarly, increasing punishment does not always reduce defection, and lowering the cost of smart cooperation can benefit unconditional cooperators more than smart cooperators. The main reported outcomes are the stationary fractions of each strategy, not group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, mechanistic insight into how punishment and reward mechanisms interact in a spatial social dilemma with multiple strategies, but does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency or group payoff outcomes in public goods games with or without punishment. The findings suggest that the introduction of complex sanctioning strategies can lead to non-linear and sometimes counter-intuitive effects on cooperation and defection rates, but the lack of efficiency or payoff reporting limits its direct utility for predicting efficiency changes due to punishment in PGG-like environments. The main value is in highlighting the potential for cyclic dominance and unexpected behavioral dynamics when multiple sanctioning strategies are present.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Model is on a large L x L lattice (L=1000 or 1200), so effective player count is 1,000,000 or 1,440,000. All players interact with their neighbors.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 Monte Carlo steps to reach stationary state; effectively infinite/steady-state analysis.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Strategies are discrete: unconditional defector, unconditional cooperator, or smart cooperator. No continuous contribution.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment is parameter U (punishment to defectors) and G (cost to smart cooperator for monitoring and sanctioning). Both are varied. Increasing punishment (U) does not always reduce defection; can harm smart cooperators.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is parameter U (impact on defectors per encounter with smart cooperator). Varying U shows non-monotonic effects on strategy frequencies.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Smart cooperators reward cooperators with parameter W. Reward is present and varied.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Cost to smart cooperator for rewarding/punishing is parameter G, which is varied.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Reward effectiveness is parameter W (reward to cooperators per encounter with smart cooperator). Varying W affects strategy frequencies.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and computational, not empirical or experimental.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (strategy frequencies), not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- The game is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- No explicit reporting of efficiency or total group payoff, limiting direct relevance for efficiency prediction tasks.
- Complex cyclic dominance dynamics may not generalize to simpler two-strategy or non-spatial games.
