# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The cost of a divided America: an experimental study into destructive behavior
Authors: Mill, W., & Morgan, J.
Journal: Experimental Economics
Year: 2022

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- spite score (proportion of possible points destroyed)
- probability of behaving spitefully
- attitudes (social distance, feeling thermometer)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the 'spite score', which measures the proportion of points destroyed (i.e., payoff taken away from the other) in a one-shot allocation task. There is no group production or public good; the task is about direct destruction of the other’s payoff with no benefit to self. No group efficiency, welfare, or total earnings are reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that both Clinton and Trump voters are willing to destroy the payoff of another participant, but the probability of doing so is significantly higher when the other is an outgroup member (opposing voter). This effect is mainly driven by Clinton voters, who increase their probability of spiteful behavior by 34% when facing an outgroup member. Trump voters are generally more spiteful overall, but do not differentiate between ingroup and outgroup. Attitudinal measures (social distance, feeling thermometer) are strongly correlated with spiteful behavior. The study does not use a public goods game or measure group efficiency, but rather focuses on direct, antisocial destruction of another’s payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. The design is a one-shot, two-player allocation task with the option to destroy the other’s payoff at no benefit to self (pure spite), not a repeated public goods game with punishment. There is no group production, no opportunity for cooperation, and no efficiency or welfare outcome reported. The findings are relevant for understanding the prevalence and moderators of destructive behavior (spite) in polarized contexts, but do not inform predictions about the efficiency effects of punishment in public goods games. The closest relevance is to the literature on antisocial punishment or joy-of-destruction, but the mapping to PGG-like efficiency outcomes is weak.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All tasks are two-player (dyadic) interactions: one participant and one matched opponent.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The main task is a one-shot decision (three allocation choices, but only one is payoff-relevant, randomly selected). No repeated or multi-round structure.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between participants is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/destruction choices are continuous: participants choose among several allocations, allowing for partial destruction of the other’s payoff.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Destruction of the other’s payoff is costless to the punisher (participant can reduce the other’s payoff with no cost to self).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (destruction) is perfectly effective: each point not allocated to the other is a point destroyed, with no cost to the punisher.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the structure: one-shot allocation task, with three decisions, one randomly selected for payment.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is not a public goods game or a repeated social dilemma; it is a one-shot, two-player allocation task focused on destructive (spiteful) behavior.
- No group efficiency, welfare, or total earnings are measured or reported; the main outcome is the likelihood and intensity of destroying the other’s payoff.
- The destruction (punishment) is costless to the punisher, which is unlike most punishment mechanisms in PGGs.
- Political identity of the opponent is made highly salient, which may not generalize to less salient real-world contexts.
- Sample is from MTurk and not fully representative of the US population, though more diverse than student samples.
- No communication, cooperation, or group production is possible; findings may not transfer to environments with these features.
- No analysis of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes relevant to the downstream prediction task.
