## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- evolutionarily stable strategy (positive or negative cooperation)
- proportion of positive cooperation
- impact of punishment, sharing, income distribution, and willingness on cooperation strategy
Outcome notes:
The paper analyzes the conditions under which construction enterprises will adopt positive or negative cooperation strategies in a repeated game theoretical model. The main outcomes are the equilibrium strategies (behavioral), not group payoffs or efficiency. While the model includes payoff parameters, the reported results focus on strategy selection, not explicit efficiency or total payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that stronger punishment mechanisms (higher penalty and indirect punishment) increase the likelihood that both parties will adopt positive cooperation strategies, thus reducing opportunistic behavior. Similarly, higher willingness to cooperate, greater positive spillover (sharing), and more favorable income distribution also promote positive cooperation. However, the outcomes are reported in terms of the probability or stability of cooperation strategies, not in terms of group efficiency or total payoff. The model is adjacent to public goods games, as it involves cooperation with spillovers and punishment, but is tailored to the context of construction enterprise innovation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the prediction task: it shows that increasing punishment intensity (both direct and indirect) makes positive cooperation more likely in a repeated game with spillovers, which is structurally similar to public goods games. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, so its value is in supporting the mechanism that punishment can deter defection and promote cooperation under certain parameter regimes. The findings are most useful for understanding how punishment, sharing, and income distribution interact to affect cooperation, but less directly useful for quantitative prediction of efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is a two-player (two-enterprise) game, as explicitly described in the model setup and equations.
- number of rounds: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. The model is an evolutionary game with repeated interactions, but the number of rounds is not fixed or specified; it is a continuous-time replicator dynamic.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between positive cooperation and negative cooperation (binary choice).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is modeled as a combination of direct penalty (E, liquidated damages) and indirect penalty (a, loss of reputation/government support). Increasing 2a+E increases the likelihood of positive cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is captured by the magnitude of 2a+E; higher values make punishment more effective and promote cooperation.
- reward availability: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Indirect rewards (a) are included for positive cooperation (e.g., government support, reputation), but not as a separate, voluntary reward action.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and not empirically tested.
- Outcomes are reported as strategy selection (cooperation vs. defection), not as group efficiency or total payoff.
- The context is specific to construction enterprise innovation in China, which may limit generalizability.
- The model is a two-player game, not a multi-player public goods game.
- No explicit marginal per-capita return (mpcr) or standard public goods game structure.
