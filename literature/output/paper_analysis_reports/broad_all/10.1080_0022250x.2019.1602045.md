## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in iterated two-person games with group-level mixed strategies and no individual recognition, evolutionary dynamics lead to the extinction of one group as mistrust parameters evolve. This occurs even in games (like the Prisoner's Dilemma) where initial conditions could support stable coexistence. The absence of individual recognition prevents the emergence of reputation, reciprocation, and retribution (punishment), which are necessary for stable cooperation and high group efficiency. As a result, group-level efficiency is undermined, and one group is always driven to extinction. The model is theoretical and does not directly manipulate or analyze punishment mechanisms, but frames punishment as a key missing ingredient for stable cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect but strong theoretical evidence that the absence of individual recognition (and thus the inability to implement punishment or reputation-based strategies) leads to the collapse of group-level efficiency and the extinction of one group in iterated two-person games. For the downstream prediction task, the findings suggest that in environments where punishment is not possible (due to lack of individual identification), efficiency will be low and unstable. Conversely, the presence of punishment (enabled by individual recognition) is implied to be necessary for stable, high-efficiency outcomes. However, the model does not directly analyze public goods games or explicit punishment treatments, so its relevance is more mechanistic and cautionary than directly predictive for PGG-like lab experiments.
