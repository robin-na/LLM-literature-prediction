Journal: Journal of Statistical Mechanics-theory and Experiment
Year: 2010

theory; empirical subtype=N/A; experimental subtype=N/A

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperators (cooperation rate)
- number of cooperators in newcomers vs deleted nodes
- degree distribution of network
Outcome notes:
The main reported outcomes are the stationary fraction of cooperators and related measures of cooperation, not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. There is no direct analysis of efficiency or total group payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a group penalty mechanism—removing groups whose collective payoff falls below a threshold—substantially increases the stationary fraction of cooperators in spatial public goods games across a variety of network topologies. The effect is robust to initial conditions and is sensitive to the deletion threshold and the number of connections of newcomers. However, the paper does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, so the findings are limited to cooperation rates rather than payoff-based outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that group-level punishment (group penalty) can promote cooperation in spatial public goods games, but it does not provide direct evidence about the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, the results are only indirectly informative: higher cooperation rates may suggest higher efficiency, but this is not demonstrated or quantified. The model is theoretical/simulation-based and does not manipulate or report most of the game design dimensions in a way that allows for direct prediction of efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N=3000 players in all main results.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10^5 time steps (rounds), with results averaged over the last 10^4 steps.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperate (contribute c=1) or defect (contribute nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is defined as η = r/(k+1), varied in simulations (e.g., η=0.2 to 0.8).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented as group deletion when collective payoff falls below a threshold T; the 'effectiveness' is controlled by T. Higher T increases punishment severity.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or any direct payoff-based outcome; all results are in terms of cooperation rates.
- The punishment mechanism is a group-level deletion rule, not individual punishment as in most experimental PGGs.
- The model is entirely simulation-based and does not include human subjects or empirical data.
- No analysis of the cost of punishment or its impact on group welfare is provided.
- Many game design dimensions relevant to experimental PGGs (e.g., chat, reward, information conditions) are not present or discussed.
