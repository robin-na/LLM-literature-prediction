# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Ethnic cooperation and conflict in Kenya
Authors: Barriga, A., Ferguson, N. T. N., Fiala, N., & Leroch, M. A.
Journal: Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Economics
Year: 2023

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- proportion of rounds cooperated
- proportion of rounds punished
- proportion of rounds withdrew
- proportion of rounds defected in response to defection
- effect of being punished on subsequent cooperation
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: cooperation rate, punishment rate, withdrawal rate, and responses to defection. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare is reported, though the payoff structure is described and average earnings are mentioned in the methods.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that allowing participants to switch partners (partner choice) increases cooperation rates compared to when switching is not possible. When switching is not possible, participants are more likely to punish defectors than to withdraw, but when switching is possible, punishment and withdrawal are used equally. Unexpectedly, punishment rates are higher when switching is possible. Importantly, punishment does not increase the likelihood that defectors will cooperate in the next round. The study does not report group efficiency or total payoff, but the behavioral results suggest that partner choice is more effective than punishment at sustaining cooperation in this dyadic context.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task: in two-player Prisoner's Dilemma games, enabling punishment does not increase cooperation or efficiency as much as enabling partner choice (switching). Punishment does not increase subsequent cooperation, and its use is not associated with higher group welfare. The results suggest that, in dyadic games, punishment may not improve efficiency, and its effect may be limited or even negative if not paired with partner choice. However, because the study does not directly report efficiency or group payoff, its value for predicting efficiency outcomes in public goods games is limited and should be interpreted with caution, especially for multi-player settings.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are two-player (dyadic) Prisoner's Dilemmas.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 40 rounds per session; number of rounds was unknown to participants.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication was not permitted.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate or defect.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not a standard PGG, but payoff structure is: mutual cooperation = L$3 each, mutual defection = L$1 each, defect against cooperator = L$5 (defector), L$0 (cooperator).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost: L$1 to punisher per use.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: L$3 loss to target per L$1 spent by punisher.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of rounds was unknown to participants.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants saw their partner's decision and both players' earnings.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. In a two-player game, the identity of the punisher is always known.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a two-player Prisoner's Dilemma, not a multi-player public goods game, so results may not generalize to group settings.
- No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare is reported; only behavioral outcomes (cooperation, punishment, withdrawal) are analyzed.
- The effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff cannot be determined from the reported results.
- Partner choice is implemented as switching partners, not as exclusion or ostracism in a group context.
- The findings may not generalize to settings where the identity of the punisher is not known, or where retaliation is less likely.
