## Source
Title: Cooperation and the scale of competition in humans

Journal: Current Biology
Year: 2006

empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- proportion of cooperative decisions
- frequency of cooperation
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the proportion of cooperative choices in repeated Prisoner's Dilemma games. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is reported. Payoff structure is described, but outcomes are reported as cooperation rates.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that human participants cooperate more frequently in repeated Prisoner's Dilemma games when rewards are based on global competition (top scorers in the whole class) than when rewards are based on local competition (top scorer in each small group). This effect is robust across individuals, groups, and classes. The experiment does not include explicit punishment or reward mechanisms, and the main outcome is the proportion of cooperative choices, not efficiency or total group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that the scale of competition (local vs global) can strongly affect cooperation rates in repeated Prisoner's Dilemma games, which are adjacent to public goods games. However, it does not manipulate or include punishment, nor does it report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. Therefore, it is not directly informative for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, but it does highlight the importance of competitive structure as a moderator of cooperation. The findings suggest that contextual factors like competition scale may interact with other mechanisms (such as punishment) in affecting cooperation, but no direct evidence is provided for punishment or efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games were played in groups of three; each student played each of the other two students.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game consisted of an average of seven repeated interactions; the number of rounds was unknown to participants and randomly determined.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or signaling was allowed between participants during the game.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is a standard Prisoner's Dilemma with binary (cooperate/defect) choices.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Payoff structure is given: T=5, R=3, P=1, S=0. Not a standard PGG, but the marginal return for cooperation is defined by the PD payoffs.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants did not know the number of rounds in advance; the number of rounds was unknown and randomly determined.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each interaction, students were told the strategy chosen by both players and the points payoff before the next interaction.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a repeated Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game or direct variant.
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present or manipulated.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only cooperation rates are analyzed.
- The study does not test or report the effect of punishment on efficiency.
- Sample size is limited (four classes, 57 individuals), and participants are university students.
- The findings may not generalize to public goods games or to settings with explicit punishment or reward mechanisms.
