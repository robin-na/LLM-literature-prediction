## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that informal punishment and reward schemes, when based on social preferences and ex post moral judgment, can partially or fully internalize externalities and improve efficiency, but the effect depends on the type of justice (intent-based vs. outcome-based), the quality of information, and the nature of the externality (positive or negative). Intent-based justice can achieve high efficiency with good monitoring but is less robust and can result in multiple equilibria, including inefficient ones. Outcome-based justice is more robust but less efficient, as it does not exploit side information and always incurs transfer costs. The model provides explicit formulas for the efficiency loss under each regime and characterizes when punishment will or will not be used.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical predictions about how the structure and effectiveness of punishment (and reward) mechanisms, the cost and technology of punishment, and the quality of information affect efficiency in environments with externalities. While not a direct PGG, the model is highly informative for predicting efficiency changes when punishment is enabled, especially in settings where a principal or arbitrator determines sanctions based on observed outcomes and beliefs. The distinction between intent-based and outcome-based justice, and the explicit efficiency bounds derived, can inform predictions about the likely efficiency gains or limits from punishment in related experimental or field PGGs, especially when considering the role of monitoring precision, transfer costs, and the possibility of multiple equilibria.

## Important Limitations
- The model is not a symmetric multi-player public goods game; it is a principal-agent model with one active agent and a passive group, so direct quantitative predictions for standard PGGs may be limited.
- The model is theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental validation.
- The analysis is single-shot; repeated interaction, learning, or dynamic effects are not modeled.
- The principal's social preferences are assumed and parameterized; real-world preferences may differ.
- The model assumes the principal can observe payoffs and an informative signal, but does not address noisy or strategic reporting by the agent.
- Equilibrium multiplicity under intent-based justice may make predictions less robust in some environments.
