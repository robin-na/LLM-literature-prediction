## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that reputation-based exclusion mechanisms, which filter out low-reputation (likely free-riding) individuals before the PGG round, can significantly increase cooperation and total system payoff in spatial public goods games. The homogeneous exclusion model requires higher exclusion probability to match the performance of traditional (post-game) exclusion, but both converge to high cooperation and payoff at high exclusion probability. The heterogeneous exclusion model, which tailors exclusion probability to the reputation gap, outperforms both the homogeneous and traditional models in promoting cooperation and system payoff at high exclusion intensity. Both reputation-based models are especially effective at suppressing second-order free-riding, a limitation of traditional exclusion. The models are simulated on a 100x100 lattice with three strategies (C, D, EC), and results are robust across a range of synergy factors and exclusion costs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that pre-game, reputation-based exclusion mechanisms (a form of access control) can increase group payoff and cooperation in spatial PGGs, especially when exclusion is sufficiently strict. The findings suggest that, for games with spatial structure and reputation tracking, enabling such exclusion mechanisms is likely to increase efficiency relative to control (no exclusion or punishment). The effect is stronger for the heterogeneous (adaptive) exclusion model. The results are most directly applicable to spatial PGGs with continuous contribution, reputation tracking, and exclusion as the main sanctioning mechanism. The paper does not study classic costly punishment (where players pay to reduce others' payoffs post-game), so direct transfer to standard punishment-enabled PGGs should be cautious. However, the results are highly informative for predicting the effect of exclusion-based access control on efficiency, especially in spatial/reputation contexts.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not include laboratory or field experiments with human subjects.
- The exclusion mechanism studied is reputation-based access control (pre-game exclusion), not classic costly punishment (post-game, targeted payoff reduction).
- Efficiency is proxied by total system payoff, not explicitly defined as a ratio to the fully cooperative optimum.
- The results are specific to spatial (lattice) PGGs with reputation tracking and may not generalize to well-mixed or non-reputation settings.
- No analysis of communication, reward, or other institutional features beyond exclusion.
- Parameter choices (e.g., group size, update rules) are fixed by simulation design and may not map directly to all empirical PGGs.
