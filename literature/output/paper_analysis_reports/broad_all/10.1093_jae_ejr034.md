# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Ostracism and Common Pool Resource Management in a Developing Country: Young Fishers in the Laboratory
Authors: Akpalu, W., & Martinsson, P.
Journal: Journal of African Economies
Year: 2012

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- average number of months spent fishing (resource extraction effort)
- frequency of ostracism
- voting behavior for ostracism
- variance in extraction effort
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the number of months spent fishing (i.e., resource extraction effort), which is a behavioral outcome. The paper does not directly report group payoff, efficiency, or welfare, but the extraction effort is tightly linked to efficiency in the context of the CPR game.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Allowing group members to ostracize (permanently exclude) over-extractors via majority vote, even at a cost to the remaining group, sharply reduced overfishing compared to a baseline with no ostracism. In the baseline, extraction effort increased over time toward the Nash equilibrium (over-exploitation), while with ostracism, effort dropped toward the social optimum. The effect was immediate and strong after ostracism was introduced. Voting to ostracize was targeted at over-extractors, and the number of votes received was the main determinant of reduced extraction. The costliness of ostracism did not prevent its use. The study does not report group payoffs or efficiency ratios, but the reduction in extraction effort implies improved efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling a credible, group-administered punishment mechanism (ostracism) in a CPR environment can substantially reduce over-extraction and move behavior toward the social optimum, even when punishment is costly to the group. While efficiency is not directly measured, the reduction in extraction effort is a strong proxy for increased efficiency in this context. For prediction tasks, the presence of a strong, enforceable punishment mechanism (ostracism) is likely to increase efficiency relative to a no-punishment baseline, especially in settings with no communication, fixed group membership, and a known time horizon. The specific design (majority vote, permanent exclusion, cost to punishers) is important for generalization.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consisted of 8 members.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 30 periods (rounds) per session; ostracism introduced after round 15.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication allowed between subjects during the experiment, including during the break.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (fishing effort) is continuous: subjects choose any number of months (0-8) to fish each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The payoff function is non-linear (hump-shaped), not a linear MPCR. For x ≤ 24, group return is 0.26x per unit; for x > 24, return decreases. Social optimum is 3 months per person (24 total).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Ostracism is costly to the remaining group members; cost is set so that exclusion always reduces their net payoff (e.g., 27 units per exclusion).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is permanent exclusion from the game (no further earnings); requires ≥50% of votes. High impact per use.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects knew in advance that the experiment would last 30 rounds, with a break after 15.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, each subject was given a record sheet with the number of months all other group members fished.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Voting was anonymous; subjects remained anonymous to each other. Ostracised members were informed privately.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is behavioral (extraction effort), not direct measurement of efficiency or group payoff.
- The punishment mechanism is ostracism (permanent exclusion), which is more severe and less granular than typical monetary punishment in PGGs.
- The experiment uses a non-linear, hump-shaped payoff function rather than a linear public goods or CPR game.
- Subjects are young fishers from a single Ghanaian community, which may limit generalizability.
- No communication was allowed, which may differ from real-world CPR management.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanism was tested.
