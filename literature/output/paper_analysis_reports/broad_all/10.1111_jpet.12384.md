## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated public goods game with four players and 20 rounds, adding a mandatory, costless peer rating stage (where players rate each other's contributions on a 0-10 scale, with no monetary consequences) increases group contributions and efficiency relative to a baseline with no rating. This effect is robust to whether rating information is private (only the recipient sees their ratings) or public (all see everyone's ratings). When a small cost (1 token per peer rated) is introduced for assigning ratings, most players stop rating, and the effect on contributions and efficiency vanishes. The study provides direct evidence that non-monetary, payoff-irrelevant peer approval can foster cooperation and efficiency in public goods games, but only if it is costless to provide.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that introducing a costless, non-monetary peer approval mechanism (rating) in a standard repeated public goods game increases efficiency, even when ratings have no direct payoff consequences and are anonymous. The effect is robust to whether rating information is private or public. However, even a small cost to rating eliminates the effect. For prediction, the presence of a costless, mandatory, non-monetary peer approval mechanism should be expected to increase efficiency relative to control. If rating is costly, no efficiency gain should be expected. The findings are directly relevant for settings where the only sanction/reward is social approval, not monetary punishment or reward.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups consisted of 4 players in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All sessions had 20 periods (rounds) of interaction.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players was allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice was continuous: players could contribute any amount from 0 to 20 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (MPCR) was 0.4 (40% of total group contribution divided among 4 players).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Non-monetary reward (approval via rating points) was present in the rating treatments; no monetary reward.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. In 'Private with Cost' treatment, assigning a rating (approval/disapproval) cost 1 token per peer rated; in other treatments, rating was costless.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward (approval) was non-monetary and had no direct payoff effect; only social/psychological impact.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players knew the game had 20 rounds (finitely repeated).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. In all treatments, players saw the vector of individual contributions in their group each period. In Public treatment, players also saw all group members' received ratings.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Anonymity was preserved: players did not know the identity of raters; group labels were reassigned each round to prevent reputation formation.

## Important Limitations
- The peer rating mechanism is non-monetary and payoff-irrelevant; results may not generalize to monetary punishment or reward settings.
- The effect of costless rating is confounded with mandatory rating; there is no treatment with costless but optional (non-mandatory) rating.
- The study uses a fixed group size (4 players) and a specific MPCR (0.4); results may differ with other parameters.
- The experiment is conducted in a lab with university students, which may limit external validity.
- No direct comparison to standard monetary punishment or reward mechanisms is provided.
