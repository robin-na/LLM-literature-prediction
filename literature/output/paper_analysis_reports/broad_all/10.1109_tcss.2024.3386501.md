Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the evolutionary dynamics of cooperation, defection, and guidance strategies (peer and pool guidance) in public goods games. Outcomes are reported as frequencies of strategies in the population, not as group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. Payoff functions are specified, but the main results are about strategy prevalence, not aggregate payoff or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper introduces and analyzes two forms of preemptive guidance strategies (peer and pool guidance) in a one-shot public goods game with a pregame negotiation phase. Both forms of guidance can reduce the prevalence of defectors and second-order free riders (cooperators who do not guide). Pool guidance is more effective than peer guidance, especially at higher guidance costs. The main reported outcomes are the steady-state frequencies of each strategy, not group efficiency or payoff. The model does not include standard punishment or reward mechanisms, and does not report on group payoff or efficiency as an outcome.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical and simulation evidence that preemptive guidance strategies can reduce free riding and increase the prevalence of guidance strategies in PGGs, especially when guidance costs are low. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment or sanctions on efficiency in PGG-like environments. The guidance strategies studied are adjacent to, but not the same as, standard punishment or sanctioning mechanisms. The findings may be useful for understanding the dynamics of pre-commitment or preemptive intervention, but are not directly transferable to the prediction of efficiency outcomes from punishment in standard PGGs.

- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is a one-shot public goods game (single round), as explicitly stated in the model and conclusion sections.

- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: each agent chooses to cooperate (contribute c) or defect (contribute nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is r/N, where r is the growth factor and N is group size. Example: r=3, N=5, so MPCR=0.6 in figures.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is explicitly one-shot; all players know the game is a single round.

## Important Limitations
- The model studies guidance strategies, not standard punishment or sanctioning mechanisms; results may not generalize to punishment effects.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only frequencies of strategies are analyzed.
- The model is one-shot and does not address repeated interactions.
- Guidance is assumed to be perfectly effective (never fails), which may not reflect real-world conditions.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are theoretical or simulation-based.
- No analysis of the effect of guidance on total group payoff or efficiency, limiting direct relevance to efficiency prediction tasks.
