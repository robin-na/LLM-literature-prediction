# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Abuse of power An experimental investigation of the effects of power and transparency on centralized punishment
Authors: Hoeft, L., & Mill, W.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual contribution to public good
- difference in contributions between punishers and non-punishers
- punisher's contribution relative to norm imposed
- change in contribution gap over time
Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on contribution behavior (tokens contributed to the public good) and the difference between punishers and non-punishers. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare is reported. The payoff function is described, but outcomes are reported in terms of contributions, not payoffs.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a repeated public goods game with a single designated punisher, punishers contribute significantly less than non-punishers, and the gap widens over time. Punishers enforce contribution norms on others that they do not themselves follow. These results are robust to information conditions (private vs. public knowledge of contributions). The paper does not report on group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare, so it does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence on the behavioral effects of punishment in a public goods game, specifically that designated punishers may enforce cooperation while themselves contributing less. However, because the paper does not report group efficiency or total payoff, it does not directly inform the prediction of efficiency outcomes when punishment is enabled. The findings suggest that the presence of a designated punisher can stabilize higher contributions among non-punishers, but the punisher's own low contributions may offset some of the efficiency gains. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper signals that the structure of punishment (e.g., designated vs. peer) and the behavior of punishers are important, but quantitative efficiency effects cannot be extracted from this study.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players (1 punisher, 3 non-punishers) in all sessions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 30 rounds per session.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player allocates 0-20 tokens per round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.5 (each token contributed returns 0.5 to each group member).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is costless to the punisher: each punishment token deducts 1 token from the target, but unused tokens are not added to the punisher's payoff.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment token deducts 1 token from the target's payoff (1:1 impact per token).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward mechanism does not exist; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know there are 30 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Only the punisher sees all individual contributions; non-punishers do not see others' contributions or punishment assigned to others. In the robustness check, all contributions are public knowledge.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is assigned by a designated punisher (role D), and the punished know they are punished, but do not know the contributions or punishment of others. The identity of the punisher is fixed and known by role assignment.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare outcomes, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be directly determined.
- Outcomes are reported in terms of individual contributions, not payoffs.
- The punishment mechanism is costless to the punisher, which differs from many standard public goods experiments.
- Only a single designated punisher per group is allowed, which may not generalize to peer punishment settings.
- No reward mechanism is present, so results may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
