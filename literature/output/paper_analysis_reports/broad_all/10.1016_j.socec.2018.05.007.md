# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The joint benefits of observed and unobserved social sanctions
Authors: Glöckner, A., Kube, S., & Nicklisch, A.
Journal: Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Economics
Year: 2018

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average group efficiency (average monetary payoff)
- average tokens earned per player
- sanctioning effectiveness (change in contribution per punishment point)
- total group payoff
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate
- punishment frequency and severity
- pro-social and anti-social punishment
- punishment channel usage
Outcome notes:
The primary dependent variable for the prediction task is group efficiency (average monetary payoff relative to maximum possible). The paper also reports on contributions and punishment behavior, but these are secondary to the efficiency analysis.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated public goods game with four players over ten rounds, the authors compare four conditions: no punishment, observed punishment only, unobserved (delayed) punishment only, and both punishment channels available. Both observed and unobserved punishment alone increase contributions but do not improve efficiency (average payoff) compared to the no-punishment baseline, due to the costliness of punishment. However, when both punishment channels are available, group efficiency is significantly higher than in all other conditions. This is because the threat of unobserved punishment increases the effectiveness of observed punishment, leading to higher cooperation with less punishment expenditure. The combination of punishment channels thus overcomes the typical efficiency losses seen in standard lab PGG punishment treatments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the efficiency impact of punishment in public goods games depends critically on the punishment institution. Standard punishment (observed or unobserved alone) does not improve efficiency over the control, but the combination of both channels leads to a large positive effect. For prediction, this means that the presence of multiple punishment channels (observed and unobserved) is a key moderator: if only one is present, efficiency is not improved; if both are present, efficiency is substantially increased. The game design dimensions most relevant here are the punishment technology (timing/observability), cost, and whether multiple channels are available. Control efficiency (no punishment) is not a reliable predictor of treatment efficiency unless the institutional details of punishment are considered.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played with 4 players per group.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played for 10 periods (rounds).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player chooses any integer from 0 to 20.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per capita return (MPCR) is 0.4 (each ECU contributed yields 1.6 to the group, split among 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Punisher pays 1 ECU per punishment point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the punished player's income by 3 ECU per point (punishment effectiveness 3:1). Both observed and unobserved punishment have the same cost and impact.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game lasts exactly 10 periods.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, players are informed about all group members' contributions and their own payoff. They do not learn the identity of other players.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous: punished players do not know who punished them.

## Important Limitations
- Sample size is moderate (23 groups, 92 subjects), which may limit statistical power for some comparisons.
- The experiment is conducted in a lab setting with student participants, which may limit generalizability to field or real-world settings.
- Only one set of punishment cost and technology parameters is tested; effects may differ with other cost/impact ratios.
- No reward mechanisms are tested, so results may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
- The study does not explore variation in group size, number of rounds, or MPCR beyond the chosen parameters.
- The study does not test for the effects of communication or reputation.
- The findings are specific to the combination of observed and unobserved punishment channels; other institutional variations are not explored.
