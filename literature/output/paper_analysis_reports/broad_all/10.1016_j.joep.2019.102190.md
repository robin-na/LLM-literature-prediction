# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The effects of voice with(out) punishment: Public goods provision and rule compliance
Authors: Morgan, S. N., Mason, N. M., & Shupp, R. S.
Journal: Journal of Economic Psychology
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual contribution to public good
- compliance with minimum contribution rule (MCR)
- level of MCR set by rule-maker
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are contribution to the public good, compliance with the MCR, and the MCR set by the rule-maker. Group or individual payoffs, efficiency, or welfare are not directly reported or analyzed as primary outcomes. The paper does report average earnings, but does not analyze efficiency or group payoff as a main outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that: (1) Comments alone do not significantly increase contributions or compliance with the MCR compared to control. (2) Enforcement (punishment) alone increases contributions and compliance, but the effect is significantly larger when comments are combined with enforcement. (3) The comment with enforcement treatment leads to the highest and most sustained contributions and compliance. (4) Rule-makers exposed to comments set higher MCRs, which in turn increases contributions. (5) The effect of comments on contributions is partly mediated by the higher MCRs set by rule-makers. (6) The paper does not directly report efficiency or group payoff, but the increased contributions and compliance in the comment with enforcement treatment imply higher efficiency in that condition.

## Prediction Guidance
For the prediction task, this paper provides strong evidence that in a linear public goods game with a probabilistic punishment mechanism, adding a comment-based participation mechanism (private, one-way comments to a rule-maker who sets a minimum contribution rule) increases contributions and compliance above punishment alone. While efficiency is not directly measured, the structure of the game (MPCR = 0.4, group size 5, continuous contributions, no chat, no rewards) means that higher contributions and compliance will translate into higher efficiency. The effect of punishment alone is positive but smaller than the effect of punishment plus comments. The paper does not provide direct evidence on efficiency, so predictions should be based on the observed contribution and compliance patterns. The findings are most relevant for games with third-party rule-making, probabilistic exogenous punishment, and no peer-to-peer punishment or reward.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 5: 4 regular players and 1 rule-maker per game. Each participant rotates through the rule-maker role.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game has 5 rounds; Stage 2 consists of 5 games (25 rounds total per participant in treatment phase).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication between players except for the private, one-way comment mechanism to the rule-maker in relevant treatments. No chat or public communication between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players allocate any number of points (0-25) between private and group accounts.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each point contributed returns 0.4 to each of 4 players; total group return per point is 1.6).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is exogenous: if a player is punished, they lose their entire endowment (25 points) for that round. The cost to the punisher is not applicable, as punishment is imposed by the experimenter, not by other players.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is a fine equal to the full endowment (25 points) with 50% probability if a player contributes less than the MCR. Thus, expected punishment per round of noncompliance is 12.5 points.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of rounds in each game (5 rounds per game, 5 games in Stage 2).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, players see: their own contributions, the MCR, whether they were sanctioned, anonymous contributions of other group members, and their total earnings for the round.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not directly report or analyze efficiency, group payoff, or welfare as an outcome; all main results are in terms of contribution and compliance.
- Punishment is exogenous and probabilistic, not peer-administered; results may not generalize to peer punishment settings.
- The comment mechanism is private, one-way, and directed only to the rule-maker; no public communication or chat between players.
- The rule-maker is a participant but is not incentivized based on group outcomes, only paid a fixed salary for setting the MCR.
- The experiment is conducted in a lab with student participants, which may limit external validity.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanism is present, so results do not inform about reward effects.
