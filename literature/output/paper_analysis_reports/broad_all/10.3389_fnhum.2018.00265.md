# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation Altered Voluntary Cooperative Norms Compliance Under Equal Decision-Making Power
Authors: Li, J. B., Liu, X. L., Yin, X. L., Li, S. Q., Wang, G. R., Niu, X. F., & Zhu, C. K.
Journal: Frontiers in Human Neuroscience
Year: 2018

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- voluntary cooperative norm compliance (measured as contribution in the public goods game)
- self-reported norm perceptions ("right" contribution)
- differences in compliance by endowment type (RICH/POOR)
- effects of tDCS stimulation (anodal, cathodal, sham) on compliance
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is contribution (compliance) in a linear public goods game, interpreted as voluntary cooperative norm compliance. No group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported. The study manipulates neural stimulation (tDCS) but does not include punishment or sanction options in the game.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that modulating rDLPFC activity via tDCS causally affects voluntary cooperative norm compliance (measured as contribution) in a linear public goods game with four players, asymmetric endowments, and no punishment or sanction options. Anodal stimulation increases contributions, cathodal decreases them, and sham is intermediate. The effect is observed for both high and low endowment players. The study also finds that participants' own norm perceptions (norm.own) are more predictive of compliance than their perceptions of others' norms (norm.other), and that anodal stimulation changes the relative influence of these norm perceptions. No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported, and the game does not include punishment or reward mechanisms.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it does not include any punishment or sanction mechanism, nor does it report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. Its findings are limited to the effect of neural stimulation on voluntary cooperation (contribution) in a standard linear PGG. The game design dimensions (player count, rounds, MPCR, etc.) are reported and may be useful for context, but the absence of punishment means this paper cannot inform the downstream prediction task regarding the effect of punishment on efficiency. It may be useful as a control or baseline for voluntary cooperation in the absence of sanctions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of four participants per game session.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 16 periods (rounds) per session.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed; participants interacted anonymously via computer terminals.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is continuous (not all-or-nothing); participants choose how much to contribute from their endowment.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.6 (each unit contributed returns 0.6 to each of 4 players, i.e., total multiplier 2.4).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were not informed how many periods they would play.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. No feedback about others' contributions or payoffs; participants only knew their own type and the types of others, not their actions.

## Important Limitations
- No punishment or sanction mechanism is present in the experimental design; the study cannot inform about punishment effects.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only contribution (compliance) is measured.
- The main manipulation is neural (tDCS), not a game design feature relevant to the prediction task.
- No feedback is given to participants about others' actions or payoffs, which may affect generalizability to standard repeated PGGs.
- The study uses asymmetric endowments, which may limit comparability to standard symmetric PGGs.
