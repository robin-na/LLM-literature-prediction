Journal: Journal of Theoretical Biology

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- evolutionary success of strategies (altruist punisher, altruist non-punisher, selfish punisher, selfish non-punisher)

- invasion analysis (ability of one strategy to invade another)
- effect of empty sites on evolutionary dynamics
Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the evolutionary dynamics and prevalence of cooperation and punishment strategies, not on explicit group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff. Outcomes are reported as which strategy dominates or coexists, not as aggregate payoffs or efficiency ratios.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The presence of empty sites in spatially structured populations (lattices) promotes the evolution of cooperation and does not reduce the effect of costly punishment. In contrast, in completely mixing populations, empty sites reduce the effectiveness of punishment in promoting cooperation. The evolutionary success of strategies depends on the benefit of cooperation, the cost and effectiveness of punishment, and the density of empty sites. The model does not report group efficiency or total payoff, but rather which strategies dominate under different parameter regimes. The findings suggest that spatial structure and population density (via empty sites) are important moderators of the evolutionary dynamics of punishment and cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task. It shows that spatial structure (analogous to networked or local interaction in PGGs) and the presence of empty sites (analogous to non-participation or dropout) can strongly moderate the effect of punishment on cooperation. In spatial settings, punishment remains effective even with empty sites, while in well-mixed settings, empty sites undermine punishment's effectiveness. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, its direct value for predicting efficiency outcomes is limited. The results are most useful for understanding when punishment is likely to promote cooperation (and by extension, possibly efficiency) depending on spatial structure and participation rates, but do not provide quantitative efficiency effects.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Lattice simulations use 50x50 sites (N=2500), with varying densities of players and empty sites. Each site can be occupied by one player or be empty. In well-mixed models, population size is not always specified but is large.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 50,000 time units; evolutionary time steps are used rather than discrete rounds as in standard PGGs.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost to punisher is parameter q (e.g., c=q=1 in simulations). The cost is explicitly modeled and varied.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment impact per unit cost is parameter p (cost to target). Both p and q are varied in simulations.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- Outcomes are reported as evolutionary success of strategies, not as group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff.
- The game is not a standard public goods game but a pairwise interaction model with four strategies.
- No explicit group-level efficiency or payoff outcomes are reported, limiting direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
- No human subjects or real-world data are used; all results are from mathematical analysis and simulations.
- No communication, reward, or information-sharing mechanisms are modeled.
