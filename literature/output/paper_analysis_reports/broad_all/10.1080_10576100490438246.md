## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper argues that in the absence of effective political institutions, groups may achieve cooperation and unity through costly punishment of defectors, a process the author terms 'altruistic punishment.' Drawing on public goods game experiments (Fehr & Gächter, 2002), the author claims that the availability of punishment increases cooperation, and extends this logic to explain extreme forms of group cohesion and violence in historical and contemporary political movements. However, the paper does not report any new empirical data or analyze efficiency or payoff outcomes directly.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical support for the idea that punishment mechanisms can increase cooperation in group settings, referencing public goods game experiments as evidence. However, it does not provide empirical data, efficiency outcomes, or direct analysis of game design dimensions. Its value for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in public goods games is limited to its summary of prior experimental findings (e.g., Fehr & Gächter, 2002), not to any new or directly relevant evidence.
