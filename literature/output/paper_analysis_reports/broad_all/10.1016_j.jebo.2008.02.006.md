# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: An investment game with third-party intervention
Authors: Charness, G., Cobo-Reyes, R., & Jiménez, N.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2008

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- Amount sent by first mover (A)
- Amount returned by responder (B)
- Amount spent on punishment/reward by third party (C)
- Ratio of B's return to A's transfer
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: amounts sent, returned, and sacrificed for punishment/reward. The paper does not report group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare. There is some discussion of whether A's benefit from sending, but no systematic analysis of efficiency or surplus.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The possibility of third-party punishment increases the amount sent by first movers by over 60% compared to the baseline with no punishment. Responders also return more when third-party punishment is possible. When both punishment and reward are possible, first movers send similar amounts as with punishment only, but responders return slightly less. Third parties spend more on total intervention (punishment plus reward) when both are available, but spend more on punishment when it is the only option. The study does not report efficiency or group payoff, so the effect on efficiency is not directly measured.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling third-party punishment in a trust/investment game increases trusting and reciprocal behavior (amounts sent and returned). However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-like games. The design is adjacent to PGGs, and the behavioral results suggest that punishment increases cooperation-like behaviors, but the lack of efficiency data limits its direct predictive value for the downstream task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of three players: A (first mover), B (responder), and C (third party).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Three rounds per session, with no feedback between rounds. One round is randomly selected for payment.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No communication or chat between players is described or allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (amount sent/returned) is continuous from 0 to 10 points.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each point sent by A is tripled for B (multiplier = 3, per-capita return = 3/2 = 1.5 for B, but not a standard PGG MPCR).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each point sacrificed by C reduces B's payoff by 3 points (punishment cost to punisher: 1; impact: 3).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: 1 point spent by C reduces B's payoff by 3 points.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is available in one treatment (treatment 3), not in the other (treatment 2).
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each point sacrificed by C increases A's payoff by 3 points (reward cost to rewarder: 1; impact: 3).
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness: 1 point spent by C increases A's payoff by 3 points.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are told there are three rounds, and that one will be randomly selected for payment.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Third parties are identified by label to A and B for the purpose of the decision, but the paper does not specify whether A and B know the identity of the punisher after the fact. The process involves labeling and passing sheets, but anonymity is maintained.

## Important Limitations
- The game is an investment (trust) game, not a public goods game; results may not generalize directly to PGGs.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only behavioral outcomes (amounts sent/returned/punished/rewarded) are analyzed.
- The effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined from this paper.
- Small group size (three players) and only three rounds per session.
- No feedback between rounds; repeated game effects are minimized but not eliminated.
- Third-party selection by vote may affect generalizability to random assignment or naturalistic settings.
