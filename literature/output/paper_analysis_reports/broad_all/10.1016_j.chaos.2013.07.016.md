## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that proportional punishment (where the cost is distributed across a proportion of the population and defectors are fined based on their payoff) enhances both cooperation and average payoff in spatial prisoner's dilemma and snowdrift games. As the proportion of the population bearing punishment cost (p) and the strength of punishment (beta) increase, the average payoff rises and more individuals achieve higher payoffs. The effect is robust across both game types. The paper provides detailed analysis of how payoff distributions shift with punishment parameters, showing that punishment reduces the number of individuals with very low or very high payoffs, concentrating payoffs in a higher, narrower range.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that, in spatial pairwise games (PDG and SD), introducing a proportional punishment mechanism increases average payoff (efficiency) compared to the baseline without punishment. The effect is stronger as the proportion of the population involved in punishment and the punishment strength increase. While the setting is not a public goods game, the adjacent nature of the games and the explicit analysis of average payoff and its distribution make the findings relevant for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in similar environments. The results suggest that, all else equal, enabling punishment (especially with higher coverage and strength) will increase efficiency relative to the control. However, the model is theoretical and does not address all PGG design dimensions (e.g., no chat, no reward, no explicit round structure), so direct quantitative transfer should be cautious.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a 100x100 lattice (10,000 players), but the effect of player count is not varied or analyzed.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 20,000 time steps, with outcomes averaged over the last 1,000 steps. No explicit round structure as in PGG; continuous evolutionary updating.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate or defect (all-or-nothing).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost is distributed proportionally across a fraction p of the population. Increasing p increases the effect of punishment, leading to higher average payoff.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment strength (beta) determines the fine as a proportion of the punished individual's payoff. Higher beta increases the positive effect on average payoff.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely theoretical/simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are presented.
- The games modeled are spatial prisoner's dilemma and snowdrift, not public goods games; results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- No analysis of several PGG-relevant design dimensions (e.g., communication, reward, round structure, information feedback).
- Punishment is implemented as a system-level proportional mechanism, not as individual voluntary punishment.
- No explicit analysis of the effect of player count or group size variation.
