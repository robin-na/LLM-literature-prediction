## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- average group contribution
- change in contribution after disapproval
- number of disapproval points sent
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is average group contribution (i.e., tokens contributed to the public good). No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings is reported, though the payoff structure is described and the efficient outcome is discussed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that peer disapproval (private enforcement) increases contributions to the public good compared to baseline. Adding a mild, centralized monetary punishment (government enforcement) to peer disapproval stabilizes contributions but does not significantly increase average contributions compared to peer disapproval alone. Critically, if government enforcement is removed after being in place, the effectiveness of peer disapproval is undermined: low contributors become unresponsive to disapproval, and average contributions drop significantly below the level observed when only private enforcement was ever present. The study does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, but the results on contributions imply that punishment can have both stabilizing and crowding-out effects depending on the sequence of enforcement mechanisms.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the sequence and combination of enforcement mechanisms (private and government) can have important effects on cooperation (as measured by contributions) in public goods games. While the study does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, the findings suggest that enabling punishment can stabilize or increase contributions, but that removing punishment after it has been in place can lead to a sharp decline in cooperation, even below the level observed with private enforcement alone. For prediction tasks, this implies that the history of enforcement mechanisms and their removal are important moderators of punishment's effect on efficiency. However, the lack of direct efficiency or payoff data limits the ability to make quantitative predictions about efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 subjects per game.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 30 periods per session, divided into three segments of 10 periods each.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each subject chooses how many tokens (0-20) to contribute.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (mpcr) is 0.4 (each token contributed yields 0.4 to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Government punishment: 0.3 Ecus subtracted per token not contributed (automatic, not peer-assigned). Peer disapproval: no monetary cost to send or receive disapproval points.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Government punishment: each token not contributed loses 0.3 Ecus. Peer disapproval: disapproval points are symbolic, not monetary.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism; only punishment/disapproval is available.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Total number of segments (and thus rounds) was common knowledge.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants could see others' contributions (since disapproval points could be assigned based on others' decisions).

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings as dependent variables; only average contributions are analyzed.
- The punishment mechanism is mild and symbolic, and the peer disapproval mechanism is non-monetary, which may limit generalizability to settings with costly or more severe punishment.
- The experiment is conducted in a laboratory setting with student participants, which may not fully capture real-world dynamics.
- The design does not allow for direct estimation of the effect of punishment on efficiency or welfare, only on contribution behavior.
- The sequence of enforcement mechanisms (order effects) is a key moderator, but the study does not explore all possible sequences or long-term effects.
