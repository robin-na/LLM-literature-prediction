Behavioral outcomes (strategy frequencies) are also reported, but payoff is central.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that peer punishment increases cooperation and group payoff (efficiency) in public goods games, but this effect is undermined if defectors can cheaply disguise themselves to avoid punishment. When the cost of disguise is high, or when punishment is strong (high fine, low cost), punishment is effective at promoting cooperation and high group payoff. Second-order punishment further stabilizes cooperation and punishment strategies, even in the presence of disguise. In finite populations, the stationary distribution of strategies shows that low disguise cost leads to defection (low efficiency), while high disguise cost or strong punishment leads to cooperation or punishment (high efficiency). Thus, the efficiency gain from punishment depends on the interplay between punishment parameters and the cost/effectiveness of disguise.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct theoretical evidence that the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency in public goods games depends on the cost and effectiveness of punishment, the fine imposed, and the possibility/cost of disguise by defectors. If the control game (no punishment) has low efficiency due to high defection, enabling punishment will increase efficiency only if punishment is strong enough and disguise is not too cheap. If disguise is cheap, punishment may have little or no effect on efficiency. The model also shows that second-order punishment can further increase efficiency by stabilizing punishment strategies. For prediction, the paper supports that lower punishment cost, higher punishment effectiveness, and higher fine on defectors all increase the efficiency gain from punishment, but this is moderated (and can be negated) by the ability of defectors to disguise at low cost.

## Important Limitations
- The model is purely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- Only well-mixed populations are considered; structured populations may yield different results.
- The model assumes fixed group size and contribution amount; real-world settings may have more heterogeneity.
- Only defectors can disguise in the main model; the possibility of cooperators disguising (especially under second-order punishment) is not modeled.
- No reward or exclusion mechanisms are included, though these may interact with punishment in real settings.
- No explicit time horizon or round structure; results are for evolutionary steady states, not finite repeated games.
