## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that a peer rating-based heterogeneous distribution system (PRHDS) can promote cooperation in a structured population, but is fragile to random exploration (strategy noise). The main threat is the invasion of egoistic cooperators, which undermines prosocial clusters. Introducing monetary punishments for minority raters (either as a fixed fine or as a reduction in distribution share) can restore and stabilize cooperation, making the system robust to low levels of noise. Non-monetary punishment (reducing the voice of minority raters) is ineffective. The effect of the system depends on the matching of the public goods multiplier and the weight of ratings in the distribution formula. The model is all-or-nothing in both contribution and rating, and the main reported outcomes are behavioral (cooperation rates, strategy fractions), not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task. It models a close variant of the public goods game with a peer rating-based distribution system, which acts as a combined reward/punishment mechanism. The main outcomes are behavioral, not efficiency or group payoff, so direct prediction of efficiency is not possible. However, the results suggest that monetary punishment mechanisms (with explicit costs or share reductions) can stabilize cooperation in noisy environments, while non-monetary punishments are ineffective. The model is all-or-nothing, with no chat, and the marginal per-capita return (mpcr) is a key parameter. The findings suggest that, in similar designs, enabling monetary punishment is likely to increase cooperation rates (and thus, by implication, efficiency), especially when the system is subject to noise. The absence of direct efficiency outcomes limits the strength of inference for the prediction task.

## Important Limitations
- The model does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare as an explicit outcome; all main results are in terms of cooperation rates and strategy fractions.
- Contribution and rating choices are binary (all-or-nothing); the model does not address continuous strategies.
- The peer rating-based distribution system (PRHDS) is a close but not exact match to standard public goods games; the redistribution mechanism may violate strict non-excludability.
- The model assumes costless and perfectly accurate monitoring; real-world monitoring may be costly or error-prone.
- The remedies (minority punishment) require tracking minority raters, which may be complex in large groups.
- No communication or chat is modeled.
- No explicit analysis of efficiency or group payoff limits direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
