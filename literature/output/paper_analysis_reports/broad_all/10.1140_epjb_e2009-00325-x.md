Outcome notes:
The model focuses on the dynamics of cooperation, free-riding, and participation in a voluntary community. Payoff-related outcomes are defined as utility/surplus, but the main reported outcomes are behavioral (fractions of types, cycles). There is no explicit calculation of group efficiency as in standard PGGs, but the model's surplus and community value are analogous.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model predicts that, depending on parameters, communities can settle into fixed points (Nash equilibria) with stable mixtures of cooperators and free-riders, or exhibit persistent cycles in the fractions of cooperators and free-riders. The presence and width of the distribution of moral costs (idiosyncratic disutility for free-riding) are crucial for the emergence of cycles. Stronger social interactions (bandwagon effects) can stabilize high-cooperation equilibria and suppress cycles. The model does not include explicit punishment or sanctioning actions, but models the deterrent to free-riding as an internalized moral cost. The efficiency or total surplus of the group is not directly compared across different institutional designs (e.g., with/without punishment), so the findings are not directly predictive for the effect of punishment on efficiency in PGGs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is of limited direct use for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games, as it does not model explicit punishment or sanctions, nor does it report efficiency outcomes in the standard sense. However, it provides indirect evidence that internalized costs for free-riding (moral disapproval) can sustain cooperation and lead to complex group dynamics, including cycles. The model's structure and results may be informative for understanding how heterogeneity in social preferences and costs can affect cooperation, but it does not provide quantitative predictions for the effect of punishment interventions or for the mapping from control to treatment efficiency in PGGs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model considers the limit of very large populations (N→∞), but also presents simulations for finite N (e.g., N=100, N=1000, N=100000, N=10^6). The group is not a fixed small group as in standard PGGs.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is iterated over time, with both parallel and sequential updating. There is no fixed number of rounds; the focus is on asymptotic or long-run behavior.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is a binary choice: cooperate (contribute) or free-ride (do not contribute), or not participate at all.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents are assumed to have full information about the current fractions of cooperators and free-riders in the population at each time step.

## Important Limitations
- The model does not include explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanisms; instead, it models a moral cost for free-riding as an internalized disutility.
- The payoff structure is not a standard public goods game; the community value is proportional to group size and composition, not to contributions redistributed among members.
- Efficiency or group payoff is not reported in a way directly comparable to standard PGGs; the main outcomes are behavioral (fractions of types, cycles).
- No empirical or experimental data are presented; the results are entirely theoretical and simulation-based.
- The model assumes full information about group composition at each time step, which may not generalize to all real-world or experimental settings.
- No analysis of the effect of introducing explicit punishment or reward mechanisms is provided.
