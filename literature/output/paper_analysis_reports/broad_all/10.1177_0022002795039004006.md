Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:

Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the frequency/proportion of cooperative choices. No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings is reported, though payoffs are described in the game structure.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in both public goods and resource dilemma games, the presence of fear (risk of zero payoff for cooperation) interacts with individual trust to affect cooperation rates. High trusters cooperate more than low trusters when fear is present, but this difference disappears when fear is absent. Removing fear increases cooperation among low trusters in resource dilemmas, but decreases cooperation among high trusters in public goods games. The paper does not experimentally manipulate or measure punishment, but discusses its theoretical relevance. No direct efficiency or group payoff outcomes are reported; all results are in terms of cooperation frequency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. It is useful for understanding how trust and fear interact to affect cooperation rates, but does not manipulate or measure punishment, nor does it report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. The discussion suggests that punishment may be more relevant in no-fear situations, but this is not tested. Thus, the paper offers only indirect, contextual support for prediction tasks involving punishment and efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games were played with 5 players per group.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games lasted 20 trials/rounds, though the exact number was not specified to subjects.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players was allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Public goods game: binary choice (contribute or not). Resource dilemma: continuous harvest choice.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Public goods game: 4-point endowment, 3 contributors needed, public good value 8 points per person. MPCR can be inferred but is not explicitly stated.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The total number of rounds (20) was not specified to subjects; they did not know when the game would end.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects received written feedback regarding their payoff for each trial, but no mention of summary information about others' contributions or payoffs.

## Important Limitations
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism was implemented or experimentally manipulated; all discussion of punishment is theoretical.
- No direct efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings outcomes are reported; all results are in terms of cooperation frequency.
- The study uses preprogrammed strategies rather than real interactive groups, which may affect ecological validity.
- The sample consists of undergraduate students, which may limit generalizability.
- The number of rounds was not known to participants, which may affect cooperation dynamics.
