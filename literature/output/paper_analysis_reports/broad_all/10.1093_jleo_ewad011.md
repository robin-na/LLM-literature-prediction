# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The role of personal and impersonal relational contracts on partner selection and efficiency
Authors: Muñoz-Herrera, M., & Reuben, E.
Journal: Journal of Law Economics & Organization
Year: 2023

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- market efficiency (total surplus as a fraction of maximum attainable surplus)
- total surplus
- sender and receiver earnings
- share of total earnings accrued by sender
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of endowment sent/invested/returned
- agreement rates
- compliance with agreements
- emotional valence of chat
- partner selection (incumbent vs entrant)
- loyalty to preexisting partners
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is market efficiency (total surplus as a fraction of maximum attainable surplus). Behavioral outcomes such as trust, reciprocity, and partner selection are also analyzed as mechanisms.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated trust game with and without competition and with either personal (free-form chat) or impersonal (numeric-only) communication, the study finds: (1) Without competition, personal relational contracts significantly increase market efficiency compared to impersonal ones, both when agreements are non-enforceable and partially enforceable. (2) With competition (i.e., when a more productive entrant is available), personal relational contracts do not increase efficiency relative to impersonal ones; in fact, impersonal contracts may yield higher efficiency, especially when agreements are partially enforceable. The mechanism is that personal contracts foster loyalty to incumbents, leading senders to continue trading with less productive partners, which reduces overall surplus. Impersonal contracts facilitate switching to more productive partners, capturing the efficiency gains from competition. The effect of communication on efficiency is thus context-dependent: positive without competition, mixed or negative with competition.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of enabling personal communication (chat) on efficiency is moderated by the presence of competition. In environments without competition, enabling chat (personal relational contracts) increases efficiency, regardless of enforceability. In environments with competition, enabling chat does not increase efficiency and may reduce it, especially when agreements are partially enforceable, because it increases loyalty to less productive partners. For prediction tasks, the presence of chat should be expected to increase efficiency only in non-competitive settings; in competitive settings, chat may have no effect or a negative effect on efficiency. The study does not use a standard PGG or explicit punishment, but the mechanisms (trust, reciprocity, loyalty, switching) are adjacent to those in public goods and punishment games.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are played with 2 players (no competition) and 3 players (competition: 1 sender, 2 receivers).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each part (no competition and competition) consists of 10 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Chat (free-form communication) is enabled in the 'Personal' relational contract treatment. Chat increases efficiency without competition, but not with competition.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/investment/return choices are continuous (0-10 units), not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is determined by the multiplier: in 2-player, m = 1 + 0.3*y (max 4.0); in 3-player, incumbent m = 1 + 0.3*y (max 4.0), entrant m = 1 + 0.4*y (max 5.0).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told the number of rounds (10 per part).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Senders are shown the history of both receivers before competition; receivers see sender's history. No cross-receiver information.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a repeated trust game, not a standard public goods game; results may not fully generalize to PGG settings.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanism is present; only verbal sanctions/rewards via chat in the 'Personal' treatment.
- Competition is limited to one sender and two receivers; larger or more complex markets are not studied.
- Partner selection is limited to switching between two receivers; broader network effects are not addressed.
- The effect of endogenous formation of relational contracts is not studied; all treatments are exogenously assigned.
- Participants could not anticipate the arrival of more productive partners, so strategic avoidance of personal ties is not tested.
