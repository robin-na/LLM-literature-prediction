## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper proves that with a suitable contract that imposes a fine for deviation in the final period, full cooperation (i.e., maximal efficiency) can be uniquely sustained in finitely repeated prisoners' dilemma games. The required fine can be small relative to total payoffs if the game is long enough. The penance contract is robust to renegotiation, unlike trigger contracts. The result generalizes to other two-player games with similar payoff structure. The findings are theoretical and show that the design of the punishment mechanism (timing, size, and contingency) is crucial for achieving efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support for the idea that, in two-player finitely repeated social dilemma games, the introduction of a well-designed punishment mechanism (specifically, a contract that imposes a fine for final-period deviation) can move the group from inefficient (all-defect) equilibria to fully efficient (all-cooperate) equilibria. The effect is robust to renegotiation if the punishment is contingent on final-period behavior and the fine is large enough to outweigh the gain from deviation. The result is directly relevant for predicting that enabling such a punishment mechanism will increase efficiency to the maximum possible, provided the contract is enforceable and the fine is sufficiently large. However, the result is limited to two-player, finitely repeated, contractible games and does not address larger groups or public goods games directly.

## Important Limitations
- Results are entirely theoretical; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- Analysis is limited to two-player games (prisoners' dilemma and generalizations), not public goods games or larger groups.
- Punishment is implemented via enforceable side-payment contracts, which may not be feasible in many real-world or experimental settings.
- The model assumes perfect monitoring and contract enforcement.
- No analysis of stochasticity, noise, or errors in implementation.
- No consideration of communication, social preferences, or bounded rationality.
