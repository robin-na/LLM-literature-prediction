# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The Effects of Social Distance and Asymmetric Reward and Punishment on Individual Cooperative Behavior in Dilemma Situations
Authors: Zhang, L., Jin, Y., Xia, L., Xu, B. B., & Abdullah, S. M. S.
Journal: Frontiers in Psychology
Year: 2022

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- defection rate
- punishment rate
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are cooperation rate, defection rate, and punishment rate. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings as primary outcomes, though payoffs are described in the game matrices.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that (1) cooperation rates are higher with friends than with strangers; (2) symmetric rewards and punishments lead to higher cooperation rates than asymmetric ones; (3) introducing a punishment option reduces cooperation rates compared to a baseline with only cooperation/defection; (4) asymmetric punishment especially reduces cooperation among friends. The main outcomes are behavioral (cooperation, defection, punishment), not efficiency or group payoff. The results suggest that punishment, especially when asymmetric, can undermine cooperation, but the paper does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that introducing punishment (especially asymmetric punishment) can reduce cooperation rates in iterated prisoner's dilemma games, and that this effect is stronger among socially close participants. However, because the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, it cannot be used directly to predict efficiency outcomes. The findings suggest that in similar environments, enabling punishment may not increase efficiency and may even reduce it if it reduces cooperation, but this is an inference from behavioral outcomes, not a direct measurement of efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are two-player (dyadic) iterated prisoner's dilemma.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each block contains 50 rounds (trials) per partner (friend or stranger).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication allowed between subjects during the experiment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Choices are discrete: cooperate, defect, and (in Experiment 2) punish. No continuous contribution.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost is 1 unit to punisher in both symmetric and asymmetric conditions; in asymmetric, the impact on the punished varies (2 or 4 units).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness per unit cost is manipulated: in symmetric, 1 cost = 2 loss for target; in asymmetric, 1 cost = 2 or 4 loss for target depending on role.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the number of rounds (50 per block).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants see their own and the other’s score after each round.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses iterated prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game or direct variant, so results may not fully generalize to PGG environments.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (cooperation, defection, punishment rates), not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or group payoff, limiting direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
- All games are dyadic (two-player), which may not capture group dynamics present in larger PGGs.
- No manipulation or reporting of marginal per-capita return (mpcr), which is a key dimension in PGGs.
