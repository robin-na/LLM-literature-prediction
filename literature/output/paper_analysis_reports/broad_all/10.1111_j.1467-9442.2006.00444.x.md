# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Achieving compliance when legal sanctions are non-deterrent
Authors: Tyran, J. R., & Feld, L. P.
Journal: Scandinavian Journal of Economics
Year: 2006

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- net efficiency (average realized income gain from cooperation as a percentage of the potential income gain from cooperation)
- gross efficiency (average actual contribution to the public good as a percentage of full contributions)
- average group payoff
- individual payoff
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate
- voting behavior
- expectations about others' contributions
- conditional cooperation
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are net and gross efficiency, both directly related to group and individual payoffs. Contribution rate is also reported but is interpreted as a measure of efficiency. Voting behavior and expectations are analyzed as mechanisms.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a one-shot, linear public goods game with groups of three, the authors compare no law, exogenous mild law (non-deterrent sanction), exogenous severe law (deterrent sanction), and endogenous (voted) versions of mild and severe law. Exogenous mild law does not significantly increase efficiency or compliance compared to no law (net efficiency: 33% vs. 30%). Exogenous severe law achieves near-maximal efficiency (89%). When mild law is enacted by group vote, net efficiency rises to 62%, nearly double the exogenous mild law and about three times the no law condition. Endogenous severe law achieves 96% net efficiency. The increase in efficiency under endogenous mild law is not due to selection effects but to the causal effect of the law: voting for mild law signals commitment, raises expectations of cooperation, and activates conditional cooperation. If mild law is rejected, efficiency is even lower than in the no law condition. The results show that the effectiveness of mild punishment depends critically on whether it is self-imposed or externally imposed.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games depends not only on the severity of the sanction but also on whether the punishment regime is endogenously chosen (via group voting) or exogenously imposed. For prediction tasks, the presence of endogenous (voted) mild punishment should be expected to yield much higher efficiency than exogenous mild punishment, even when the punishment is non-deterrent. Severe punishment (deterrent) is highly effective regardless of endogeneity. The game is one-shot, with no communication, group size of 3, and a marginal per-capita return of 0.5. The findings are directly relevant for predicting efficiency outcomes in similar lab PGGs with and without punishment, especially when considering the mode of law enactment (voted vs. imposed).

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 3 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is one-shot (single round), known to all subjects.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed; decisions are anonymous.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous from 0 to 20 points; not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (a) is 0.5.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sanction is a deduction from the subject's payoff; cost is 4 points (mild) or 14 points (severe) per non-compliant subject. No cost to other players or to a punisher, as punishment is automatic.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is automatic and certain; 4 or 14 points deducted per non-compliance. No cost to others; full impact per unit cost.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game is one-shot (single round).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects indicate expectations about others' contributions, but do not receive feedback about others' actual choices until the end. No real-time summary information is provided during decision-making.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is automatic and anonymous; no identification of who is punished or who punishes.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is one-shot; results may not generalize to repeated or long-run settings.
- Group size is small (n=3); effects in larger groups may differ.
- No communication is allowed; real-world settings may involve communication or signaling.
- Punishment is automatic and certain, not peer-administered; this may differ from decentralized punishment settings.
- No reward mechanism is present; results do not address the interaction of punishment and reward.
- Framing is neutral; real-world laws may have more salient or moral framing.
- Subjects are university students; external validity to other populations is uncertain.
- The study does not address long-run norm formation or the durability of effects.
