## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- evolutionary stable strategies (compliance, cooperation, regulation)
- convergence speed to stable strategies
- probabilities of compliance/cooperation/regulation
- impact of reward and punishment parameters on strategy adoption
Outcome notes:
The paper analyzes the evolution of strategies (compliance, cooperation, regulation) among three parties (government, pharmaceutical companies, public) in a public health emergency context, using evolutionary game theory and simulation. Outcomes are probabilities of adopting certain strategies and convergence rates, not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. Payoff is discussed only as a driver of strategy, not as a reported outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that government reward and punishment mechanisms (subsidies and fines) for pharmaceutical companies and the public promote compliant production and cooperation in prevention and control, respectively. Increasing the magnitude of rewards or punishments accelerates the convergence to full compliance and cooperation. The initial willingness of parties to participate also affects the speed of convergence. However, the model does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, and all results are in terms of the probability of adopting certain strategies and the speed of convergence to stable equilibria.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, theoretical support for the idea that increasing the strength of punishment and reward mechanisms can promote compliance and cooperation in a multi-party public-goods-like setting. However, it does not provide quantitative predictions about efficiency or group payoff, nor does it report empirical results. The findings may inform the qualitative direction of punishment effects in similar environments, but cannot be used for direct prediction of efficiency outcomes as required by the downstream task.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- Outcomes are probabilities of strategy adoption and convergence speed, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- The setting is a tripartite evolutionary game in a public health emergency context, not a standard public goods game.
- No direct mapping to marginal per-capita return or standard PGG payoff structure.
- The cost to the punisher or rewarder is not explicitly modeled as a separate parameter.
- No analysis of the effect of punishment or reward on group efficiency or total welfare.
