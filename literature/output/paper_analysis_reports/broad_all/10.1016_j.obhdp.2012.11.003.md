## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The review concludes that both punishment and reward mechanisms can promote cooperation in social dilemmas, including public goods games, but their effectiveness depends on factors such as trust, group size, decentralization, and cultural context. The paper notes that punishment can sometimes have adverse effects, such as reducing cooperation if sanctions are low or undermining intrinsic motivation. It also discusses the importance of communication, reputation, and structural solutions (e.g., leadership, local management) in sustaining cooperation. While the review references meta-analyses and empirical studies showing positive effects of punishment and reward on cooperation, it does not provide direct evidence or quantitative estimates of efficiency or group payoff changes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides high-level theoretical and conceptual support for the idea that punishment (and reward) mechanisms can increase cooperation in public goods and related social dilemmas, and that their effects are moderated by structural and psychological factors. However, it does not provide direct empirical evidence or quantitative estimates of efficiency or group payoff, nor does it systematically analyze the impact of specific game design dimensions. For prediction tasks, this review supports the general expectation that enabling punishment will tend to increase cooperation, but the lack of direct efficiency data limits its value for precise quantitative prediction. The review is most useful for identifying moderators and mechanisms that may inform the interpretation of empirical results from other studies.
