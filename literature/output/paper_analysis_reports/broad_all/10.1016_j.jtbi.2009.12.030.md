## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in a rotating indivisible goods game (a model of ROSCAs), cooperation collapses unless both a peer selection rule (based on reputation) and a forfeiture rule (excluding non-contributors from receiving goods) are present. Peer selection alone or forfeiture alone is insufficient. The model tracks the evolution of cooperation probabilities before and after receiving goods, showing that only the combination of both rules leads to sustained high cooperation. The paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, so its findings are limited to behavioral outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, contextual evidence for the prediction task. While it does not report efficiency or payoff outcomes, it shows that in a public-goods-adjacent environment with indivisible goods, the presence of both peer selection (reputation-based group formation) and a forfeiture rule (exclusion of non-contributors) is necessary to sustain high cooperation rates. For prediction, this suggests that punishment-like mechanisms (here, exclusion and peer selection) may only be effective in combination, and that the structure of the game (indivisible goods, rotation, exclusion) is critical. However, the lack of direct efficiency or payoff data limits its direct applicability to efficiency prediction.

## Important Limitations
- The model is not a standard public goods game; it is a rotating indivisible goods game (ROSCA-like), so findings may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- No direct analysis or reporting of efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings; only behavioral outcomes (cooperation probabilities) are reported.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanisms; only cost-free sanctions (peer selection, exclusion) are modeled.
- Simulation-based results may depend on specific parameterizations and assumptions (e.g., reputation reset, labeling rules).
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are from evolutionary simulations.
