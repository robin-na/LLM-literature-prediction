# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: AID DISTRIBUTION AND COOPERATION IN UNEQUAL COMMUNITIES
Authors: D'Exelle, B., & van den Berg, M.
Journal: Review of Income and Wealth
Year: 2014

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average aid attracted (total group contributions times multiplier)
- average aid received by non-representative group members
- aid share kept by representative
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual contribution levels
- probability of being representative
- distributional fairness in aid allocation
- rotation of representative role
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are individual and group contributions, and the distribution of aid among group members. While the total aid attracted is a payoff-related outcome, the paper does not report group efficiency as a ratio to the social optimum, nor does it directly analyze total group payoff relative to the maximum possible. The focus is on behavioral responses to distribution rules and the distributive consequences for group members, especially the representative.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of a representative who allocates aid (selected as the highest contributor) increases group contributions compared to equal sharing, due to both competition for the representative role and anticipation of punishment (i.e., receiving less aid for low contributions). However, representatives keep a large share of the aid for themselves, so the increased total aid does not translate into higher payoffs for the rest of the group. Representatives do consider fairness, giving more to low-endowment and higher-contributing members, but still capture more than their proportional share. Endowment inequality increases contributions under equal sharing but not under representative sharing. The study does not report group efficiency or total group payoff as a ratio to the social optimum, so the effect of the representative mechanism on efficiency is not directly assessed.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence relevant to predicting the effect of punishment-like mechanisms (here, representative allocation with the ability to reward or punish via aid shares) on group contributions and the distribution of payoffs. The representative mechanism increases contributions (a proxy for cooperation), but the efficiency gain for the group as a whole is ambiguous because the representative captures a disproportionate share of the aid. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency (total group payoff relative to the maximum), this study suggests that mechanisms allowing targeted allocation can increase contributions, but may not improve average group payoffs for all members unless the allocation is constrained. The absence of direct efficiency analysis limits the ability to make quantitative predictions about efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 8 players per session.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 10 rounds per session: 5 rounds equal-sharing, 5 rounds representative-sharing.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is continuous: players choose how much to contribute from their endowment.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Efficiency factor b = 1.6; with 8 players, MPCR = 0.2.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Representative can reward by allocating more aid to certain members.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants do not know the number of rounds in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After contributions, individual contributions are made public before aid allocation.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. The identity of the representative (who allocates aid) is public.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report group efficiency or total group payoff as a ratio to the social optimum, limiting direct inference about efficiency effects.
- Punishment is not implemented as a costly, explicit mechanism; instead, the representative can allocate zero aid to low contributors, which is a form of endogenous sanctioning.
- The representative mechanism allows for substantial elite capture, so increased contributions do not necessarily translate into higher payoffs for all group members.
- The experiment is conducted in a specific field-lab context in rural Nicaragua, which may limit generalizability.
- The number of rounds per treatment is low (5), which may affect the stability of observed behavioral patterns.
- Participants do not know the number of rounds in advance, which may influence strategic behavior.
