## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that embedding a punishment system within a group selection framework reduces the evolutionary success of altruism. While punishment can statically deter selfishness and improve immediate social welfare, it simultaneously weakens the group selection mechanism that would otherwise promote the evolution of intrinsic altruism. This leads to a lower equilibrium level of altruism and, in the long run, can reduce dynamic social welfare. The model shows that the requirements for group selection to sustain altruism become more stringent when punishment is available, and that the use of punishment may have negative dynamic welfare implications, even if it improves static outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
For the downstream prediction task, this paper suggests that enabling punishment in a group environment may increase static efficiency (short-term group payoff) but can reduce dynamic efficiency (long-run welfare) by undermining the evolution of intrinsic cooperation. The model is not a standard public goods game but is adjacent, focusing on sequential punishment and group selection. The findings imply that, in environments where intrinsic motivation is important for long-run efficiency, introducing punishment may have ambiguous or even negative effects on overall efficiency, especially over evolutionary or cultural timescales. The paper is most informative for settings where the persistence of cooperation and welfare over time is of interest, rather than immediate, one-shot efficiency gains.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The core model is a 3-player sequential punishment game, with analysis of group-structured populations of arbitrary size n and m groups.
- number of rounds: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. The model is evolutionary and repeated over generations, not in discrete rounds as in standard PGGs. Each period, individuals are randomly matched.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is modeled as costless to the punisher (the punisher gains a felicity benefit, not a cost). The model abstracts from costly punishment.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment inflicts a loss of 1 on the target and gives a benefit \hat{\pi} (0 < \hat{\pi} < 1) to the punisher. The effectiveness is thus 1 per act.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players have full knowledge of the coefficient of altruism of others with whom they interact, and punishment is targeted and observable.

## Important Limitations
- The model is highly stylized and not a standard public goods game; it focuses on sequential punishment and group selection, not on contribution to a public good.
- Punishment is modeled as costless to the punisher, which is not realistic for most empirical settings.
- The model abstracts from direct inter-group competition, costly punishment, and other mechanisms that may affect the evolution of altruism.
- Findings are theoretical and may not generalize to empirical or experimental PGGs with different structures.
- The model does not address short-run (static) efficiency in standard PGGs, but rather long-run (dynamic) welfare and the evolution of altruism.
