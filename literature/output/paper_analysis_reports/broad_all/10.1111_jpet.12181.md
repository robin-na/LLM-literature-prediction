# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Voluntary Contributions to a Mutual Insurance Pool
Authors: Lévy-Garboua, L., Montmarquette, C., Vaksmann, J., & Villeval, M. C.
Journal: Journal of Public Economic Theory
Year: 2017

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- mean coverage rate (proportion of losses covered)
- aggregate earnings
- distribution of earnings between high and low contributors
- average group contribution
- social loss relative to full insurance
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of null contributions
- individual contribution behavior
- heterogeneity of strategies (clusters of high and low contributors)
- conditional cooperation
- effect of risk attitudes
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the mean coverage rate (proportion of losses covered), which is a direct measure of efficiency in this context. Aggregate earnings and the distribution of earnings are also reported. Non-payoff outcomes include contribution rates, behavioral clusters, and determinants of contribution.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Contribution-based coverage, which ties insurance payouts to individual contributions, significantly increases the mean coverage rate (efficiency) compared to equal coverage, where payouts are independent of contribution. In the experiment (n=12, 50 rounds), the mean coverage rate is about 26% under equal coverage and 47% under contribution-based coverage for the full sample, with even higher rates for risk-averse (high-contributing) subgroups. However, full insurance is not achieved due to coordination failures and heterogeneity in risk attitudes. The endogenous 'punishment' of low contributors (via low coverage) is effective at raising efficiency, but only partially. Aggregate earnings are similar across treatments, but the distribution of earnings is more equitable under contribution-based coverage, with reduced transfers from high to low contributors.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that mechanisms which endogenously punish low contributors (by reducing their insurance coverage) can substantially increase efficiency in public-goods-like environments with risk and voluntary contributions. The effect is robust but incomplete: efficiency rises from about 26% to 47% of the optimum when moving from equal to contribution-based coverage, but does not reach full efficiency. The findings are directly relevant for predicting the effect of introducing a punishment-like mechanism (here, endogenous sanctioning via coverage rules) in similar games. The results are most applicable to settings with continuous contributions, no explicit communication, and group sizes around 12. The paper does not study explicit costly punishment, but the contribution-based coverage mechanism functions as an endogenous, payoff-based sanction. Control efficiency (equal coverage) is low, and the treatment (contribution-based) nearly doubles efficiency. Heterogeneity in risk attitudes is a key moderator.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All experimental groups had 12 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consisted of 50 periods (rounds).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat was allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices were continuous between 0 and 100 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is not explicitly labeled, but the structure is: each token contributed increases the coverage pool, and the group loss is 400 tokens for 4 randomly selected players. The effective MPCR is less than 1, as surplus is burned if contributions exceed losses.
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented endogenously: low contributors receive less insurance coverage. The 'effectiveness' is the degree to which low contribution reduces one's own coverage. This mechanism increases efficiency compared to equal coverage.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players knew the total number of rounds (50) in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players were informed about others' contributions and outcomes (see discussion of conditional cooperation and information about others' behavior).

## Important Limitations
- The study does not implement explicit, costly punishment or peer-sanctioning; the 'punishment' is endogenous via the coverage rule.
- The setting is a mutual insurance pool with risk and loss, not a standard linear public goods game; results may not generalize to all PGGs.
- Group size is fixed at 12; effects may differ in smaller or larger groups.
- No communication or chat is allowed; effects of communication are not tested.
- Heterogeneity in risk attitudes is a key moderator, but the experiment does not manipulate or control for this directly.
- The experiment uses a between-subjects design; individual learning or adaptation across mechanisms is not observed.
- The efficiency gains, while substantial, do not reach full efficiency; coordination failures persist.
