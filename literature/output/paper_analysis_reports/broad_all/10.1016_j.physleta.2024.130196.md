## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing manipulators—who both punish defectors and reward cooperators—into a spatial public goods game can sustain cooperation and increase average group payoff, even when the synergy factor (r) is low and cooperation would otherwise fail. The model shows that for a range of parameter values (punishment/reward efficiency, manipulation cost), manipulators and cooperators can coexist, and the group achieves higher payoffs than in the absence of punishment/reward. When punishment is sufficiently efficient (beta/gamma high), the system can reach the all-cooperator state, maximizing group payoff. If the cost of manipulation is too high or punishment/reward is inefficient, cooperation collapses and payoffs drop. Reward withdrawal (manipulators reducing rewards when surrounded by cooperators) reduces the average payoff of cooperators and narrows the payoff gap between manipulators and cooperators. The model demonstrates that the combined effect of punishment and reward is robust but sensitive to parameter settings.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct, high-signal evidence that enabling punishment (and reward) in a spatial public goods game increases group efficiency and average payoff, especially when the control (no punishment/reward) condition would result in low cooperation and low payoff. The effect is moderated by the efficiency of punishment (punishment impact per cost), the cost to punishers/rewarders, and the synergy factor (mpcr equivalent). The findings suggest that, for a given control efficiency, enabling punishment (especially when combined with reward) will generally increase efficiency, provided the punishment is not too costly and is sufficiently effective. The model also highlights that the presence of defectors and the structure of the population (spatial lattice) are important, but the main prediction is robust across a range of parameter values. This paper should be weighted heavily when predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in similar spatial PGG designs.

- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: C contributes c, D and M do not contribute.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental with human subjects.
- The population is structured as a square lattice; results may differ in well-mixed or other network structures.
- Manipulators combine both punishment and reward; pure punishment-only or reward-only treatments are not isolated.
- The model assumes synchronous updating and specific strategy sets (C, D, M); real-world dynamics may be more complex.
- No direct comparison to a control condition with punishment disabled but reward enabled, or vice versa.
- No consideration of communication, reputation, or other social mechanisms beyond local strategy imitation.
