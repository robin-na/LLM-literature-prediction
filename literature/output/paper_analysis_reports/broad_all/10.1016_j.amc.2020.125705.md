The main outcome is cooperation frequency (proportion of cooperators), not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare. The paper discusses the analogy between mobility/alienation and punishment, but does not report payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma model, allowing individuals to move away from defectors with low probability (limited mobility) increases the frequency of cooperation by enabling the formation and expansion of cooperative clusters. High mobility, however, undermines cooperation by disrupting network reciprocity. The authors interpret mobility away from defectors as a form of punishment, but the model does not include explicit punishment or reward mechanisms. The main reported outcome is cooperation frequency, not efficiency or group payoff. The paper claims that limited mobility of minorities can promote cooperation more effectively and with less public resource cost than explicit punishment or reward, but this is not empirically tested.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. The model is a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma, not a PGG, and the main outcome is cooperation frequency, not efficiency or group payoff. The analogy between mobility/alienation and punishment is discussed, but there is no explicit manipulation of punishment cost, effectiveness, or related design dimensions. The findings may be contextually informative for understanding how indirect forms of sanctioning (like alienation) can promote cooperation, but they do not provide quantitative or direct evidence for the prediction task focused on efficiency outcomes in PGGs with or without punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The lattice size is fixed at M=128 (i.e., 128x128 grid), but the effective player count depends on population density (rho).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 100,000 time steps; outcomes are averaged over the last 20,000 steps after equilibrium.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperate or defect (all-or-nothing).

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- No explicit efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only cooperation frequency is analyzed.
- Punishment is only modeled indirectly via mobility/alienation, not as a parameterized or explicit mechanism.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are from theoretical simulations.
- No manipulation or analysis of key game design dimensions relevant to punishment (e.g., punishment cost, effectiveness, identification).
