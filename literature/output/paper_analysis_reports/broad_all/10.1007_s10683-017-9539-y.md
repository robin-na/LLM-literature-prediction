## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that when groups can vote to restrict access to a common resource, group efficiency (measured as economic growth of the group endowment) increases dramatically, particularly in the high-resource (large endowment) condition. Without the regulatory institution, groups with large resource pools deplete resources quickly and achieve very low growth (1.5%), while groups with small pools do somewhat better (7.2%). With the institution, growth rates rise to 31.8% (small pool) and 40.2% (large pool), with the difference between large and small pools not statistically significant. The institution's positive effect on efficiency is much greater in the high-resource condition, reversing the 'curse' of resource abundance. The mechanism is that group members are more willing to vote for strong protection when resources are abundant, and the institution effectively constrains over-exploitation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling a regulatory institution (here, a group voting mechanism to restrict access) in a public-goods-like resource depletion game leads to large increases in group efficiency, especially when the resource pool is large. For prediction tasks, the presence of an institution that allows collective restriction of extraction should be expected to substantially increase efficiency relative to the control (no-institution) condition, with the effect being especially pronounced in high-resource or high-stakes environments. The findings are directly relevant for settings where the 'punishment' or 'sanction' mechanism is implemented as a group-voted restriction, rather than individual peer punishment. The results suggest that the interaction between resource abundance and institutional capacity is critical: the efficiency gain from enabling regulation is much larger when resources are abundant.

- seeing peer outcomes: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. Players are informed about the outcome of the voting decision and the extent of resource protection, but it is not explicitly stated whether they see others' extraction choices or payoffs.

## Important Limitations
- The regulatory institution is implemented as a group voting mechanism, not as individual peer punishment or costly sanctions, so results may not generalize to standard punishment treatments.
- The resource depletion game is a close but not exact variant of the standard public goods game; extraction is from a common pool rather than contribution to a public good.
- The experiment is conducted in a lab setting with student subjects, which may limit external validity for real-world resource dilemmas.
- No communication between players is allowed, which may affect generalizability to settings where discussion is possible.
- The public good return structure (fixed transfer and one-time interest) differs from standard MPCR implementations.
