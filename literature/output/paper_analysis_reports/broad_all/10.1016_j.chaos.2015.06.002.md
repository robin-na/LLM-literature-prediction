## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study uses agent-based simulations of a spatial public goods game on a square lattice, where each player participates in five overlapping groups. The key manipulation is the introduction of a symmetric, mutual punishment: whenever two neighbors have different strategies, both pay a punishment fine. The main result is that higher punishment fines lead to higher steady-state cooperation rates, and for sufficiently high fines, the population becomes fully cooperative. The mechanism is robust to changes in system size and noise. The paper provides a formula for the payoff difference between cooperators and defectors at the interface between clusters, showing that this difference increases with the punishment fine, favoring the spread of cooperation. However, the paper does not report group-level efficiency or total payoff, so the effect on efficiency is not directly established.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that mutual punishment increases cooperation rates in spatial public goods games, especially as the punishment fine increases. However, because the paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare, it does not directly inform the prediction of efficiency outcomes. The findings are most useful for predicting changes in cooperation rates, not efficiency. The model is theoretical and does not include communication, reward, or other institutional features. The punishment is symmetric and always applied when strategies differ, which is a specific variant of punishment not always present in experimental or real-world settings.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are arranged on a square lattice (typically 100x100 or 200x200), so the player count is 10,000 or 40,000. The effect of system size is tested and found not to qualitatively change results.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 30,000 time steps, with outcomes averaged over the last 2,000 steps. The number of rounds is fixed and known in the simulation.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: players are either pure cooperators (contribute 1) or pure defectors (contribute 0).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The multiplication factor r is varied in the simulations. Each group has 5 players, so MPCR = r/5.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as a fine \alpha paid by both parties when their strategies differ. The cost is symmetric and absorbed into the fine; there is no separate cost to the punisher.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the fine \alpha; each instance of differing strategies results in both players paying \alpha.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of time steps is fixed and known in the simulation.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is always mutual and symmetric between neighbors with different strategies; the identity of the punisher is always known by construction.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare; only cooperation rates and spatial patterns are analyzed.
- The model is theoretical and agent-based, not experimental or empirical.
- Punishment is always symmetric and mutual between neighbors with different strategies, which is a specific and less common variant of punishment.
- No communication, reward, or other institutional features are present.
- Findings may not generalize to non-spatial or non-symmetric punishment settings.
