## Outcomes Measured

Non-payoff outcomes:
- group achievement (η_G): ratio of groups meeting cooperation threshold

Outcome notes:
The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings. Outcomes are behavioral: group achievement and institution prevalence, which are not direct measures of efficiency or welfare.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that in networked collective-risk dilemmas with local punishment institutions, seeding a small fraction of punishers (institution supporters) at random or in network hubs can shift the system from low to high group achievement and institution prevalence, even when initial conditions are otherwise unchanged. Seeding cooperators is much less effective. The presence of punishment institutions is necessary for high cooperation; network structure alone does not suffice. Seeding network hubs is more expensive but not more effective than random seeding. The main outcomes are group achievement and institution prevalence, not direct efficiency or payoff measures.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that introducing punishment institutions (via seeding punishers) in a networked public-goods-like environment can strongly increase group-level cooperation outcomes (group achievement and institution prevalence), even when the control (no punishment) condition yields low cooperation. However, because the paper does not report efficiency or payoff-based outcomes, its findings should be used as informative but indirect support for predicting efficiency effects of punishment. The results suggest that, in similar networked games, enabling punishment (especially via institutional mechanisms) is likely to increase efficiency if group achievement is a good proxy for efficiency. The paper also highlights that the effectiveness of punishment depends on both network structure and the presence of institutional mechanisms, and that seeding punishers is more effective than seeding cooperators.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size is set to Z=5000 in all experiments; group sizes are typically N=4 (well-mixed) or variable (networked).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 1000 time steps, with results averaged over the last 25%.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishers pay a tax π_t=0.03 to create a punishing institution; defectors pay a fine π_f=0.3 if institution is present.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the fine π_f=0.3 imposed on defectors when institution is present.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely simulation-based and does not include human subjects or empirical data.
- Outcomes are behavioral (group achievement, institution prevalence), not direct measures of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- Findings are specific to the modeled collective-risk dilemma with local punishment institutions and may not generalize to all PGGs or other game structures.
- The effect of seeding leaders may differ in other network topologies or game settings.
- No analysis of marginal per-capita return (MPCR) or explicit efficiency outcomes.
