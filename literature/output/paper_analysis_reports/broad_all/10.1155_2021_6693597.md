## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model demonstrates that increasing punishment for enterprises promotes their strict implementation of regulations, but with diminishing returns. Higher rewards to the public increase their willingness to supervise, but also slow the government's adoption of strict governance due to fiscal burden. Subsidies to enterprises incentivize strict implementation but may reduce the government's willingness to govern strictly if subsidies are too high. Lowering the cost of public supervision increases supervision rates. The ideal outcome (all parties cooperate) is achieved when punishment, rewards, and subsidies are high enough and supervision costs are low. However, the paper does not provide quantitative predictions of efficiency or group payoff, focusing instead on the evolution of behavioral strategies.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides qualitative theoretical support for the idea that punishment mechanisms can increase compliance (analogous to cooperation) in multi-actor public goods-like settings, especially when combined with rewards and low supervision costs. However, it does not provide direct evidence or quantitative predictions about efficiency or group payoff, nor does it use a standard PGG or report efficiency ratios. Its findings may inform the expected direction of punishment effects on compliance, but not the magnitude of efficiency changes. The model's structure and results are adjacent to the prediction task, offering indirect support for the positive effect of punishment on cooperative behavior, but not on efficiency per se.
