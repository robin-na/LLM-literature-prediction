Outcome notes:
The paper reports on emergent network properties (centrality, organization) as outcomes of rule-breaking behavior in simulated social networks. No direct payoff, efficiency, welfare, or group earnings outcomes are reported. Centrality is used as a proxy for social prominence, not for material payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that when individuals in a network break social conventions for choosing partners, the effects depend on the convention in use. In 'intermediary-based' (betweenness) networks, rule-breakers primarily affect their own social position, and only at higher frequencies do they impact group organization. In 'popularity-based' (in-degree) networks, rule-breakers do not affect their own or others' individual positions, but as their frequency increases, they significantly alter the overall network structure (decentralization). The paper discusses the potential for rule-breaking to transform social systems, but does not measure or report efficiency, payoff, or welfare outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. It does not manipulate or measure punishment, efficiency, or payoff, nor does it use a PGG or close variant. Its findings on how rule-breaking alters network structure may be contextually relevant for understanding the broader consequences of norm violation, but do not inform quantitative prediction of efficiency changes due to punishment. The paper may be useful for mechanism discussion or for understanding the importance of social structure, but not for direct prediction in the target task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use a fixed group size of 50 individuals.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations proceed in discrete time steps; the number of steps is not specified, but the process is iterative and dynamic.

## Important Limitations
- No public goods game or direct analog is used; the model is a dynamic social network with partner choice based on centrality.
- No punishment, sanction, or reward mechanisms are implemented; discussion of punishment is purely interpretive.
- No efficiency, payoff, welfare, or group earnings outcomes are measured or reported; outcomes are limited to network centrality metrics.
- Findings may not transfer directly to environments where material payoffs, cooperation, or punishment are central.
- The model assumes fixed group size and fixed number of out-neighbors per agent, which may not generalize to other social or economic settings.
