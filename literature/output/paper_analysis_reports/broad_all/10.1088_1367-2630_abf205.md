The main dependent variables are the stationary or oscillatory levels of the common resource (group payoff/sustainability) and the fraction of cooperators. Payoff formulas for cooperators and defectors are explicitly modeled. The focus is on the long-term group-level outcome (resource level, sustainability, and coexistence) as a function of punishment, time delay, and resource growth.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that institutional punishment (probabilistic detection and fine for defectors) can stabilize cooperation and maintain the common resource at sustainable levels, but only if the product of detection probability and fine (pβ) is sufficiently large relative to the cost of cooperation or the reinvestment rate. When punishment is strong, the system converges to a stable, efficient state with high cooperation and resource sustainability. When punishment is weak, defectors dominate and the resource is depleted. If cooperator reinvestment is proportional to endowment and there is a time delay in environmental feedback, the system can exhibit persistent oscillations in cooperation and resource levels, especially when punishment is moderate and resource growth is not too fast or too slow. The critical threshold for punishment effectiveness and the impact of time delay are analytically characterized. The model provides explicit conditions under which punishment improves or fails to improve group efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct theoretical evidence for how institutional punishment (with known cost and effectiveness) affects group efficiency in a common-pool resource game with feedback dynamics. The results are most relevant for adjacent PGG-like environments with renewable resources and environmental feedback. The model gives explicit formulas for the stationary group payoff/resource level as a function of punishment parameters, player count, resource growth, and reinvestment structure. For prediction, the key insight is that increasing punishment effectiveness (higher pβ) generally increases efficiency up to a threshold, above which full cooperation and sustainability are achieved. However, if punishment is too weak, efficiency remains low. Time delay in feedback can destabilize the system and induce oscillations, but does not always reduce average efficiency. The findings are most transferable to settings with institutional punishment, renewable resources, and feedback between group action and group payoff.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The game is adjacent to, but not an exact, public goods game: it is a common-pool resource model with feedback between group action and resource state.
- Punishment is institutional and not peer-based; the cost to the punisher is not modeled as an individual cost.
- The model assumes large, well-mixed populations and continuous-time dynamics.
- No communication, reward, or information feedback mechanisms are included.
- The model does not address stochasticity, finite population effects, or implementation costs of punishment institutions.
