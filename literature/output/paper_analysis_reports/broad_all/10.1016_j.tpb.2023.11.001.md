## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in the shirker's dilemma, as group size increases, the proportion of volunteers at the stable equilibrium increases (for low enough costs), but the expected payoff (efficiency) decreases. The probability that the public good is provided can either increase or decrease with group size, depending on the cost of cooperation. In the limit of very large groups and low costs, the proportion of volunteers approaches one, and the expected payoff and success probability converge to positive values. However, the basin of attraction for the cooperative equilibrium shrinks with group size, making cooperation less likely to be reached from random initial conditions. The model does not include explicit punishment or reward mechanisms.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that, in a binary-action threshold collective action game (shirker's dilemma), increasing group size generally reduces efficiency (expected payoff at equilibrium), even though the proportion of volunteers may increase. The findings suggest that, in the absence of explicit punishment or reward mechanisms, larger groups are less efficient, and the probability of successful collective action may not improve with group size. For prediction tasks, this paper supports the expectation that, in similar threshold public goods environments without punishment, efficiency will decrease as group size increases. However, because the model does not include punishment, it does not directly inform the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency, but it does provide a theoretical baseline for control (no-punishment) conditions in threshold games.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Group size n is a central parameter; the effect of increasing n is directly analyzed for all main outcomes.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: each player either volunteers (cooperates) or shirks (defects).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is implicit: volunteering costs c, and the public good (value 1) is provided if at most zeta shirkers. The benefit is shared by all n players.

## Important Limitations
- The model is entirely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanisms are modeled; the analysis is limited to threshold collective action without sanctions.
- The game is binary-action and one-shot; repeated interactions, communication, and continuous contribution levels are not considered.
- The model assumes well-mixed populations and does not address relatedness, spatial structure, or other social network effects.
- The findings may not generalize to standard public goods games or to environments with explicit punishment or reward options.
