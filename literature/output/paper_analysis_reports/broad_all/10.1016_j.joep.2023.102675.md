# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: When emotional responses conflict with self-interested impulses: A transcranial direct current stimulation study of cognitive control in cooperative norm compliance
Authors: Yin, X. L., Li, J. B., Li, D. H., & Chen, S. Y.
Journal: Journal of Economic Psychology
Year: 2023

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- third-party punishment assigned (TPP)
- self-reported negative emotional response
- beliefs about cooperative norms
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the amount of punishment assigned by third parties (TPP) and their emotional responses. No group payoff, efficiency, or total earnings are reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that third-party punishment (TPP) is higher when punishment is costless and right DLPFC activity is inhibited (cathodal tDCS), driven by stronger negative emotional responses to norm violations. When punishment is costly, neither tDCS nor emotional response significantly affects TPP. The results suggest that the cognitive control process for norm compliance involves a conflict between emotional impulses to punish and self-interested motives to avoid cost. However, the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or any direct payoff-based outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence about the psychological mechanisms of third-party punishment in a prisoner's dilemma variant, especially the role of cost in moderating punishment behavior. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency. It does suggest that costly punishment may be less frequent and less sensitive to emotional impulses, which could imply a limited effect on efficiency, but this is not measured. The design and findings are most relevant for understanding when and why punishment is used, not its impact on group welfare.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of three players: A, B (PD players), and C (third-party punisher).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each task (cost and no-cost) includes two rounds; each subject plays both tasks.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is all-or-nothing: players either keep all 10 points or transfer all 10 points.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. If a player transfers 10 points, the recipient gets 30 points (tripled), so MPCR = 3/3 = 1 for the recipient, but the structure is a PD, not a standard PGG.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Two conditions: cost (1 point cost per deduction point) and no-cost (0 cost per deduction point). The effect of punishment cost is central to the design.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction point assigned by C reduces the target's payoff by 3 points.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know there are two rounds per task; the number of rounds is fixed and known.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Third party observes the actions of A and B and their payoffs before assigning punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or any direct payoff-based outcomes, so it cannot inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency.
- The design is a prisoner's dilemma with third-party punishment, not a standard public goods game, so results may not fully generalize to PGG settings.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (punishment assigned, emotional response), not economic (payoff, welfare, surplus).
- The number of rounds is very small (two per task), which may limit dynamic or learning effects.
- Punishment is only administered by a third party, not by peers or second parties.
