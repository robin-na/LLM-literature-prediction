## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in voluntary public goods games, allowing group members to exclude (ostracize) candidates with low reputation (defectors) leads to the evolution and maintenance of high cooperation rates. Optional participation (where individuals can choose whether to join a group based on group reputation) does not sustain cooperation. The strictest exclusion rule (requiring unanimous acceptance for membership) is most effective at promoting cooperation. The model is evolutionary and simulation-based, and outcomes are reported as the proportion of cooperators, not as group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that costless ostracism (exclusion of defectors) is a highly effective mechanism for sustaining cooperation in public goods games, more so than optional participation alone. While the paper does not report efficiency or group payoff directly, the implication is that group efficiency would be higher when ostracism is enabled, since the share of cooperators is much higher. The findings are most relevant for settings where punishment is costless and takes the form of exclusion, rather than costly point-based punishment. The results are less directly applicable to predicting the quantitative effect of standard costly punishment on efficiency, but they support the qualitative prediction that effective exclusion or ostracism can greatly increase group efficiency if measured.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and simulation-based, not empirical or experimental.
- Outcomes are reported as cooperation rates, not as group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- Punishment is modeled as costless ostracism (exclusion), not as costly point-based punishment.
- No direct measurement or reporting of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes.
- No communication, reward, or more complex social mechanisms are included.
- The model assumes full information about reputations and deterministic group formation rules.
