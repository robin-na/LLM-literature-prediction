## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that introducing a global exclusion mechanism (a centralized institution that can exclude defectors from the public good) into the public goods game can lead to higher efficiency and average group payoff, provided the cost of exclusion is not too high and the probability of successful exclusion is sufficiently large. The model predicts that, in these parameter regions, cooperation can emerge and be stabilized, sometimes through cyclic dominance among defectors, cooperators, and excluders. The results are robust across a wide range of functional forms for the exclusion probability. When exclusion is too costly or too unlikely, the system reverts to defection and low efficiency. The paper does not analyze empirical data but provides theoretical and numerical phase diagrams supporting these conclusions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that, in public goods games, the addition of a sufficiently effective and not overly costly global exclusion (institutional punishment) mechanism can substantially increase group efficiency compared to the baseline game. The effect is robust to the details of how exclusion is implemented, as long as the exclusion probability is high enough and the cost is not prohibitive. For prediction tasks, this suggests that, when the game design includes a global exclusion mechanism with moderate or low cost and high effectiveness, one should expect a significant increase in efficiency relative to the control. The model also clarifies that if exclusion is too costly or ineffective, efficiency gains will not materialize. The findings are directly relevant for predicting the efficiency impact of institutional punishment/exclusion in PGG-like environments.

## Important Limitations
- The analysis is entirely theoretical and based on replicator dynamics in infinite, well-mixed populations; no empirical or experimental data are provided.
- The model assumes infinite population size and does not account for stochastic effects or finite group dynamics.
- The exclusion mechanism is centralized and global, which may not correspond to most real-world or experimental PGG settings.
- No consideration is given to communication, reputation, or other social mechanisms that may interact with punishment.
- The model does not include reward mechanisms or compare exclusion directly to other forms of institutional incentives in terms of cost-efficiency.
- The results depend on the assumption that exclusion can be implemented with a sufficiently high probability and not-prohibitive cost; if these conditions are not met, efficiency gains do not occur.
