# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Trust within human-machine collectives depends on the perceived consensus about cooperative norms
Authors: Makovi, K., Sargsyan, A., Li, W. D., Bonnefon, J. F., & Rahwan, T.
Journal: Nature Communications
Year: 2023

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- trust-gain (difference in trust sent to norm-followers vs non-followers)
- sharing rate
- punishment rate
- perceived norm consensus
- qualitative justifications for behavior
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is 'trust-gain', defined as the difference in trust (amount sent) to Helpers or Punishers who follow vs do not follow norms. No group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported. The design is a third-party punishment game followed by a trust game, but the focus is on trust and norm perception, not on group payoff or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a third-party punishment game followed by a trust game, humans earn less trust by sharing with or punishing bots than with humans. Trust-gain is attenuated but not eliminated in mixed human-bot collectives. Perceived consensus about cooperative norms predicts trust-gain, and experimentally making norm consensus salient increases trust-gain in some conditions. The main outcomes are trust and norm perception, not group efficiency or total payoff. The design is adjacent to public goods games, but the outcome focus is on trust and norm-following rather than efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff in public-goods-game-like environments. Its design is adjacent (third-party punishment game with binary sharing), and the main outcomes are trust-gain and norm perception, not efficiency or total payoff. The findings suggest that punishment can increase trust in norm-followers, especially when norm consensus is salient, but do not quantify effects on group efficiency. The paper may inform mechanism design around norm salience and trust, but is not directly informative for efficiency prediction tasks.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each third-party punishment game involves 3 players (Helper, Beneficiary, Punisher), followed by a trust game with a 4th player (Trustor).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Single-shot (one-shot) games; no repeated rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Helper's sharing choice is binary: share all or keep all.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not labeled as MPCR, but sharing splits resources 50/50; in the trust game, sent resources are tripled. The marginal return for sharing is 0.5 for the Helper, 0.5 for the Beneficiary.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 25% of their resources to make the Helper lose 50% of theirs.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each unit of punishment costs the Punisher 0.25 and reduces the Helper's resources by 0.5 (per round).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game is single-shot; the structure is fully explained.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishers observe Helper's action; Trustors are informed of Helper's or Punisher's behavior. No aggregate summaries, but relevant actions are revealed to the next-stage player.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishers' actions are known to Trustors when Trustors are paired with Punishers; the identity (human/bot) is always revealed.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is trust-gain (difference in trust sent to norm-followers vs non-followers), not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- The design is a third-party punishment game with binary sharing, not a standard public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG efficiency.
- No repeated rounds; all games are single-shot.
- No communication or chat between players.
- No reward/positive sanction mechanism is present.
- Sample is from MTurk and not representative of the general population.
- Bots are programmed to act randomly; their lack of real preferences may limit ecological validity.
- No direct analysis of group-level efficiency or surplus; all findings are at the level of trust and norm perception.
