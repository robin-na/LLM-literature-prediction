# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Constructing the Facets of Altruistic Behaviors (FAB) Scale
Authors: Windmann, S., Binder, L., & Schultze, M.
Journal: Social Psychology
Year: 2021

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- self-reported peer punishment trait (FAB-PP subscale)
- self-reported help-giving and moral courage traits
- Dictator Game giving (amount shared)
- amount spent on punishing unfair players in a public goods game
- decision to intervene in a third-party intervention scenario
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are self-reported trait scores and their correlations with economic game behaviors. The public goods game with punishment option is used as a behavioral probe for peer punishment, but the outcome measured is the amount spent on punishment, not group efficiency or total payoff. No direct analysis of efficiency, welfare, or group payoff is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The FAB scale reliably measures three distinct facets of altruistic behavior, including peer punishment. In a pilot study, the peer punishment subscale correlates with the amount participants spend on punishing free riders in a public goods game with punishment. The public goods game used is a standard design (groups of four, endowment, MPCR=0.75, punishment with 1:3 fee-to-fine ratio), but the only behavioral outcome reported is the amount spent on punishment, not group efficiency or total earnings. The study provides evidence that self-reported peer punishment trait predicts willingness to punish in a PGG, but does not analyze or report the effect of punishment on group efficiency or payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that self-reported peer punishment trait is associated with actual punishment behavior in a public goods game with punishment. However, it does not provide any evidence about the effect of enabling punishment on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare. The game design dimensions (player count, MPCR, punishment cost/tech, etc.) are described for the pilot PGG, but no efficiency or payoff outcomes are reported. Therefore, this paper does not directly inform prediction of treatment efficiency from game design dimensions plus control efficiency. It may be useful for understanding individual differences in punishment behavior, but not for predicting efficiency effects of punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The public goods game with punishment in the pilot study used groups of four players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants played three practice rounds and one data collection round in the PGG with punishment.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The sum of all contributions is tripled and distributed equally (MPCR = 0.75 for 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment fee-to-fine ratio is 1:3 (cost to punisher per unit is 1, reduces target's payoff by 3).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3 (each unit spent reduces target's payoff by 3).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Participants are told about the practice rounds and the data collection round, implying knowledge of the number of rounds.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes in the public goods game with punishment.
- The public goods game was not incentivized; all payoffs were fictitious.
- Sample size for the economic game pilot was small (N=68, with only 30 for the third-party intervention).
- The main focus is on self-report trait measurement and construct validity, not on the effects of punishment on efficiency.
- No control (no-punishment) condition is reported for the public goods game, so the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency cannot be assessed.
