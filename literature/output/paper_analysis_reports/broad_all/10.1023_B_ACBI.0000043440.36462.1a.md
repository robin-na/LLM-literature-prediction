## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper uses agent-based simulations to examine the effects of altruistic punishment on aggregate wealth (efficiency) in artificial societies. It finds that while punishment increases generosity and norm compliance, it reduces aggregate wealth compared to societies without punishment, unless the punished behaviors trigger synergistic benefits for society. The effect is observed in both structured (with division of labor) and unstructured societies, though structured societies are more susceptible to social control via punishment. The study concludes that punishment is a strong mechanism for norm enforcement but is not, by itself, efficiency-enhancing.

## Prediction Guidance
For the prediction task, this paper provides evidence that enabling punishment in a public-goods-like environment (here, an agent-based resource-sharing simulation) increases norm compliance and generosity but reduces group efficiency (aggregate wealth), unless there are additional synergistic benefits from the enforced behaviors. The negative effect on efficiency is robust to variations in social structure and punishment intensity. Thus, when predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency, this paper suggests that, in the absence of explicit synergistic returns, punishment is likely to reduce efficiency relative to the control. The findings are most directly relevant to settings where punishment is costly and does not directly generate group-level surplus.

## Important Limitations
- The study is based on agent-based simulations, not human experiments; results may not generalize to real human behavior.
- The model does not include a public goods multiplier (no MPCR), so it is not a direct PGG but rather a resource-sharing environment.
- No communication, reputation, or reward mechanisms are modeled.
- Punishment is always costly and symmetric (punisher and punished pay equal cost), which may not match all real-world or experimental settings.
- Synergistic effects (where cooperation produces more than the sum of individual contributions) are not present in the main simulations; results may differ if such effects are included.
- Agents do not learn or adapt except via parameter replacement upon death; no strategic updating or memory.
