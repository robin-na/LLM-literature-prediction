# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Decisions on Extending Group Membership-Evidence from a Public Good Experiment
Authors: Grund, C., Harbring, C., Thommes, K., & Tilkes, K. R.
Journal: Games
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- individual payoff (tokens earned)
- group payoff (sum of tokens earned by group members)
- expected income for temporary group members
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual contribution to public good
- mean group contribution
- conditional willingness to cooperate
- probability of group membership extension
- beliefs about others' contributions
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are individual and group contributions to the public good. Payoff is discussed as a function of contributions, but efficiency or group welfare is not directly analyzed as a primary outcome. The study focuses on how group membership extension mechanisms affect cooperation, with some discussion of monetary incentives and payoffs.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that temporary group members contribute marginally more when the possibility of group membership extension is decided endogenously by a group member, compared to an exogenous random draw. However, this effect is weak and does not translate into significantly higher overall group contributions. Permanent members' contributions are not significantly affected by the extension mechanism. When a temporary member is replaced (especially exogenously), the newcomer initially contributes more than other group members, but this effect diminishes quickly. The study discusses monetary incentives and payoffs, but the main focus is on contribution behavior rather than efficiency or group payoff. There is no direct analysis of how the extension or replacement mechanisms affect group efficiency relative to the social optimum.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task. It shows that mechanisms resembling social sanctions (replacement or exclusion) can have incentive effects on contribution behavior, but these effects are modest and short-lived. The design does not include explicit costly punishment or reward mechanisms, and efficiency is not directly measured. For prediction, the results suggest that changing the group membership mechanism (endogenous vs. exogenous extension) has limited impact on overall group efficiency, and that replacement (as a form of exclusion) may temporarily boost newcomer contributions. However, these effects are primarily on contribution rates, not on efficiency or total group payoff. The study is most informative for understanding how exclusion or replacement (as a weak form of sanction) affects cooperation, rather than for predicting the efficiency impact of explicit punishment mechanisms.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups consist of 4 players (3 permanent, 1 temporary) in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 rounds per session, with a restart after 10 rounds (two parts of 10 rounds each).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player chooses any integer from 0 to 20 tokens to contribute.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (total contribution multiplied by 1.6 and divided among 4 players).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the total number of rounds (20), with a restart after 10 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, players are informed about their own contribution, all contributions to the project, and their payoffs. Individual contributions cannot be traced back to specific group members.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not include explicit costly punishment or reward mechanisms; replacement is not parameterized as punishment.
- Efficiency or group welfare is not directly analyzed as a primary outcome; main outcomes are contribution rates.
- Replacement is not costly to the decision-maker, and the mechanism does not allow for targeted punishment based on observed individual contributions.
- Group size is fixed at 4; results may not generalize to larger groups.
- The effects observed are modest and short-lived, especially for newcomer contributions after replacement.
- The design does not allow for communication or reputation effects beyond the group membership mechanism.
