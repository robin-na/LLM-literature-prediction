## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper reviews the tragedy of the commons as a framework for understanding how individual-level selfishness can reduce group-level outcomes, including group fitness and productivity, in both human and non-human systems. It highlights that mechanisms such as punishment, policing, kin selection, and ecological feedbacks can prevent or reduce the severity of the tragedy, sometimes restoring group productivity or preventing collapse. The review draws analogies to public goods games and discusses the importance of distinguishing between complete collapse (collapsing tragedy) and partial reduction in group fitness (component tragedy).

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides conceptual support for the idea that punishment and related mechanisms can improve group outcomes (efficiency) in public-goods-like environments by altering incentives and deterring selfish behavior. However, it does not provide quantitative or experimental evidence that would allow direct prediction of efficiency changes from game design dimensions. Its value lies in supporting the general expectation that punishment, when effective and not too costly, can prevent or mitigate efficiency losses due to free-riding, but it does not specify how this effect depends on specific game parameters.
