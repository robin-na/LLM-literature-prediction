Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are the fraction of cooperators, regime shifts, and hysteresis effects. The model includes payoffs and utility in its dynamics, but the reported results focus on behavioral regime (cooperation/defection) and resource levels, not on group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare. Resource level is tracked as an ecological variable, not as a direct measure of group payoff or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that social ostracism (punishment) can maintain cooperation in a common-pool resource setting, but the resilience of cooperation and the occurrence of regime shifts depend strongly on the structure of the social network. Higher network degree (more social ties) increases the width of the hysteresis curve, making the system more resilient to regime shifts (sudden collapse of cooperation and resources). In sparse networks, regime shifts occur more easily and hysteresis is reduced or absent. The model shows that both social and ecological parameters (e.g., ostracism strength, opportunity cost, resource inflow) can drive regime shifts. However, the main outcomes are the fraction of cooperators and resource levels, not direct measures of group efficiency or total payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the prediction task by showing that punishment (ostracism) can stabilize cooperation in a public-goods-like environment, and that the effect of punishment is moderated by network structure (player connectivity). However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff outcomes, so it cannot be used to directly predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings suggest that punishment is more effective at maintaining cooperation (and thus, by implication, higher group welfare) in denser networks, but the lack of direct efficiency or payoff data limits its predictive value for the target outcome.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run with N=50 and N=200; player count is a fixed parameter in each simulation.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run for 10^4 time steps per parameter value to reach equilibrium; the model is iterated over many time steps.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a Cobb-Douglas production function with parameters (gamma, alpha, beta); marginal returns are determined by these, but not reported as MPCR per se.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is modeled as ostracism, with cost to defectors (social cost); the cost to punishers is not explicitly modeled as a payoff deduction, but ostracism strength (h) is a key parameter.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is determined by the ostracism function parameters (h, tau, g); these are varied in simulations.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical or experimental data are reported.
- The main outcomes are behavioral (fraction of cooperators, regime shifts), not direct measures of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- Punishment is modeled as ostracism (social cost to defectors), not as a costly act by punishers; cost to punishers is not explicitly modeled.
- The model is a close variant of a public goods game with punishment, but not a standard PGG; results may not transfer directly to standard PGG settings.
- No direct analysis of the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff is provided.
