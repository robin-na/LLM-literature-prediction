# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Creating sanctioning norms in the lab: the influence of descriptive norms in third-party punishment
Authors: Lois, G., & Wessa, M.
Journal: Social Influence
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- final payoff for punisher, dictator, and recipient (calculated for illustrative purposes in methods section)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- average third-party punishment assigned
- frequency of free riding (not punishing)
- within-group variance in punishment
- self-reported perception of peer punishment
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: amount of punishment assigned by third-party punishers and frequency of free riding. Payoff outcomes are not the primary focus and are only discussed in the methods for illustrative purposes, not as main results.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Participants' third-party punishment behavior in a repeated dictator game was strongly shaped by social feedback about peer punishment. Exposure to high or low average peer punishment led to persistent differences in participants' own punishment levels, even after feedback was removed. The presence of a single free rider (a peer who never punished) in a group with otherwise high punishment reduced average punishment and increased the frequency of free riding, despite the same average peer punishment as the high group. These effects persisted over time, indicating the formation of stable sanctioning norms. The study demonstrates the importance of descriptive norms and the 'bad apple' effect in shaping costly punishment behavior, but does not directly measure or report efficiency or group payoff as a main outcome.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that descriptive norms and the presence of free riders can substantially moderate the level of costly punishment in repeated norm enforcement games. However, because the main outcomes are behavioral (punishment assigned, free riding) rather than efficiency or group payoff, the findings are only indirectly informative for predicting efficiency effects of punishment in public-goods-like environments. The study suggests that the presence of free riders can undermine the effectiveness of punishment as a norm enforcement tool, which could plausibly reduce efficiency gains from punishment, but this is not directly tested. The game is a repeated third-party punishment dictator game, not a standard PGG, so direct quantitative predictions for PGG efficiency should be made with caution.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consisted of 5 third-party punishers (including the participant), but only one was randomly selected to have their punishment enacted each round. The group size is fixed and directly described.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. There were 49 rounds, divided into 7 blocks of 7 rounds each. All participants played all rounds as third-party punishers.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication among participants was allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment and dictator transfers were both continuous/graded choices (dictator: 0, 25, 50, 75, 100; punisher: 0-50 points). Not all-or-nothing.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 1 point to reduce dictator's payoff by 3 points. Maximum 50 points can be used for punishment per round.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 3:1 (1 point spent by punisher reduces dictator's payoff by 3 points).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is present; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The experiment was described as having 49 rounds, divided into 7 blocks. Participants were aware of the block structure.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. During feedback phases (blocks 2-4), participants saw the individual punishment choices of the other four third-party punishers and the average peer punishment after each round.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a third-party punishment dictator game, not a standard public goods game, so findings may not generalize directly to PGG environments.
- Efficiency or group payoff is not analyzed as a main outcome; the focus is on punishment behavior and free riding.
- Social feedback about peer punishment was preprogrammed (not based on real peer decisions), which may limit ecological validity.
- The 'high' and 'low' feedback groups differ not only in average peer punishment but also in variance and range, which could confound interpretation.
- Sample size is moderate and the study is conducted with university students in a lab setting, which may limit generalizability.
