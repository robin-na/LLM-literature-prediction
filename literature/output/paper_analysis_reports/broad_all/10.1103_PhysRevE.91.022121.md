Journal: Physical Review E
Year: 2015

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- fraction of cooperators (\rho_c)
- time series of cooperation
- spatial distribution of strategies
Outcome notes:
The main outcome is the fraction of cooperators in the population at equilibrium or over time. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. All results are in terms of cooperation rates, not payoffs.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that mutual (symmetric) punishment, where individuals punish neighbors with different strategies, can enhance cooperation in the prisoner's dilemma game. In well-mixed populations, cooperation can only be sustained if the initial density of cooperators is above a threshold, and if the punishment fine is above a critical value. In structured populations (square lattice), there is often an optimal, moderate punishment fine that maximizes cooperation, especially when the initial density of cooperators is not too low. Too weak or too harsh punishment suppresses cooperation. The main outcome is the steady-state fraction of cooperators, not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not directly inform prediction of efficiency or group payoff in public goods games with punishment. However, it provides indirect evidence that symmetric, mutual punishment can increase cooperation rates under certain conditions, especially with moderate punishment severity and sufficient initial cooperation. The results are based on the prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game, and do not report efficiency or payoff outcomes. Thus, the paper is only adjacent for the prediction task, and should be used with caution, mainly as qualitative support for the idea that punishment can promote cooperation, but not necessarily efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Well-mixed population: each individual plays with all others (effectively N players); structured population: 100x100 or 200x200 lattice (10,000 or 40,000 players). Player count is large and fixed in all simulations.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 30,000 time steps, with steady state measured over last 2,000 steps. Not a fixed finite repeated game, but evolutionary dynamics.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between pure cooperation or pure defection (binary choice).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is symmetric: the cost to the punisher is absorbed into the punishment fine (\alpha), so only the fine is modeled. No separate cost parameter.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment fine (\alpha) is the effectiveness per punished neighbor; varied systematically in the analysis.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game or direct variant.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are theoretical and simulation-based.
- No analysis of payoff-based outcomes; cannot directly inform efficiency predictions.
- Punishment is symmetric and automatic, not voluntary or targeted as in most PGG experiments.
