Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are the equilibrium fraction of the population at the social optimum (A), which directly maps to group efficiency, and the average payoffs under different incentive regimes. The paper does not report on contribution rates, punishment frequency, or norm compliance per se, but rather on the population-level equilibrium and payoffs.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a population playing a stag-hunt game (representing a social contract), the introduction of a moderate diversity incentive (modeled as a snowdrift game in a focal group) allows the population to overcome the invasion barrier and reach the globally optimal equilibrium (all adopting the efficient norm A). This transition increases group efficiency (total payoff). If the diversity incentive is too strong, the system may stabilize at a mixed equilibrium, but turning off the incentive after the transition can still result in full adoption of the optimal norm. The effect is strongest when the number of group connections is moderate; if individuals are too highly connected, the social pressure of the stag-hunt dominates and the incentive is less effective. The findings are supported by analytical and simulation results.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that introducing a moderate incentive for diversity (analogous to a reward or tolerance for deviance) can increase group efficiency in coordination games with multiple equilibria, by enabling the population to escape suboptimal equilibria. While the model does not directly study public goods games or punishment, it is adjacent in that it addresses how incentive structures and group connectivity affect the ability to reach efficient outcomes. For prediction tasks, the results suggest that mechanisms which lower the cost or risk of deviating from the majority (even temporarily) can facilitate transitions to higher-efficiency equilibria, especially in moderately connected groups. However, the model does not include explicit punishment or sanctioning mechanisms, so direct quantitative predictions for punishment-enabled PGGs are not possible from this paper alone.

## Important Limitations
- The model is entirely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The game structure is a stag-hunt (coordination) game with a diversity incentive (snowdrift), not a public goods game or a game with explicit punishment or sanctioning.
- No explicit punishment, sanction, or cost to punisher is modeled; the focus is on incentives for diversity rather than enforcement of cooperation.
- Payoff structures are not directly comparable to standard PGGs; results may not transfer quantitatively.
- The model assumes binary strategy choice (A or B) and does not allow for continuous contributions.
- No communication, information sharing, or identification of others' actions is modeled.
- The model assumes well-mixed or structured populations but does not address heterogeneity in group size or payoff structure.
