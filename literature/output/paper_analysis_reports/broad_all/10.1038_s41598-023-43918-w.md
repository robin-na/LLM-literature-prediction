## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The model finds that high initial cooperation and low contribution costs favor the emergence and stability of high cooperation and moderate norm sensitivity. When contribution costs are high or initial cooperation is low, cooperation fails to establish. The noise parameter (imperfect information about past cooperation) can modulate but not destroy cooperation unless it is very high. The model does not include explicit punishment or reward actions; instead, norm enforcement is internalized as psychic costs for free-riding, which depend on the number of cooperators, the cost of contribution, and individual norm sensitivity. The model does not report efficiency or group payoff, so its findings are not directly translatable to efficiency prediction tasks.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, mechanistic insight into how internalized norm enforcement (psychic costs) and conditional cooperation can sustain cooperation in repeated PGGs, especially under low contribution costs and high initial cooperation. However, it does not model or report the effects of explicit punishment mechanisms, nor does it report efficiency or group payoff. Therefore, it is not directly informative for predicting the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency in PGG-like games. Its main value is in highlighting the importance of initial conditions, contribution cost, and norm sensitivity for the emergence of cooperation, but not for quantifying efficiency changes due to punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Population size is fixed at N=100 in all simulations.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 20,000 generations; each generation involves repeated PGG rounds.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: agents either contribute their full endowment or nothing each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is h/N, with h=1.5 and N=100, so MPCR=0.015.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents have access to the previous round's cooperation level (empirical expectations), which informs their conditional cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model does not include explicit punishment or reward mechanisms; norm enforcement is internalized as psychic costs for free-riding.
- Outcomes are reported in terms of cooperation rates and trait distributions, not group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare.
- The model assumes all-or-nothing contributions and a fixed population size of 100.
- The model does not account for communication, network structure, or top-down norm enforcement.
- Findings may not generalize to real-world settings with explicit punishment, reward, or more complex social structures.
