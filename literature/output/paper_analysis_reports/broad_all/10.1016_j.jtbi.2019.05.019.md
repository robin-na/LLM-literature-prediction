## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing punishment with shared cost among cooperators in a public goods game can promote and stabilize cooperation, thereby increasing group efficiency and average payoff, provided the penalty imposed on defectors is large enough compared to the punishment cost. In well-mixed populations, this can transform the game from one dominated by defection to one where full cooperation is stable. In structured populations, cooperation can emerge at lower fine thresholds and coexist with defection, but as the penalty increases, well-mixed populations become more favorable for cooperation than structured ones. The effect of punishment is thus generally positive for efficiency, but the benefit depends on the relative size of the fine, the punishment cost, and the group size.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct theoretical evidence that, in public goods games, enabling punishment (with shared cost among cooperators) increases efficiency and group payoff when the fine is sufficiently large relative to the cost. The effect is modulated by group size (player_count), punishment cost (punishment_cost), and punishment effectiveness (punishment_tech, i.e., fine per cost). Structured populations (networked interactions) can allow cooperation to emerge at lower fine thresholds, but as the fine increases, well-mixed populations yield higher efficiency. The findings support the prediction that, for a given control efficiency, enabling punishment will increase efficiency, especially when the punishment is effective and not too costly. The model is theoretical, so quantitative predictions should be interpreted as qualitative guidance.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Group size N is a key parameter; larger N makes cooperation harder to sustain and reduces the basin of attraction for cooperation.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: each player either contributes a fixed amount (1) or nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. MPCR is r/N, where r is the enhancement factor. Higher r/N makes cooperation more attractive.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost (alpha) is a key parameter; higher cost reduces the effectiveness of punishment in promoting cooperation and efficiency.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is the fine (beta) per unit cost (alpha); higher beta/alpha ratio increases cooperation and efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The study is entirely theoretical and does not include empirical or experimental data.
- The model assumes infinite or very large populations and evolutionary dynamics, not finite repeated games.
- Only binary (all-or-nothing) contribution is considered; no continuous contribution levels.
- Punishment is institutional (pool punishment with shared cost), not peer-to-peer.
- No reward mechanisms or other cooperation-promoting mechanisms are modeled.
- Structured populations are limited to regular graphs; heterogeneous networks (e.g., scale-free) are not analyzed.
- Assumes cooperators always both contribute and punish; does not allow for pure cooperators or pure punishers.
