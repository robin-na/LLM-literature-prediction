## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:

- group-separation structure as a function of discount factor
- partitioning of players into groups based on equilibrium support
Outcome notes:
The paper is entirely theoretical and analyzes the conditions under which groups of players can sustain cooperation in pairwise repeated prisoners' dilemma games using trigger strategies. The main outcomes are the existence and structure of equilibrium groupings, not payoffs or efficiency per se. No explicit analysis of group payoff, welfare, or efficiency is provided.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper provides a theoretical framework for understanding how the ability to sustain cooperation via punishment (trigger strategies) in repeated pairwise prisoners' dilemma games depends on the discount factor and the players' mixed strategies. It shows that as the discount factor increases, more pairs can sustain cooperation, leading to larger groups of mutually cooperating players. The main findings are the mathematical characterization of the group-separation structure as a function of the discount factor, and the identification of intervals where complete-group-separations (where all within-group pairs can sustain cooperation) are possible. However, the paper does not analyze or report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is of limited direct use for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or their variants, as it does not analyze efficiency or payoff outcomes. Its main contribution is theoretical: it shows how the ability to sustain cooperation via punishment in repeated pairwise games depends on the discount factor and the heterogeneity of players' strategies. For prediction tasks, it suggests that the effectiveness of punishment in sustaining cooperation (and thus potentially efficiency) is sensitive to the discount factor and the similarity of players' strategies, but it does not provide quantitative or empirical evidence on efficiency effects. The model is also for pairwise repeated PDs, not for n-player public goods games.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is for arbitrary n-player sets; examples use n=8. The number of players is a parameter of the model.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are infinitely repeated (infinite horizon) with discounting.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Stage game is a two-action (C or D) prisoners' dilemma, but players can use mixed strategies (probabilistic choice of C or D).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented via trigger strategies: after a deviation, both players play D forever (minmax punishment). The cost is implicit in the loss of future cooperation, not a direct cost per se.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is maximally effective: a single deviation triggers permanent defection (grim trigger), which is the harshest possible punishment in repeated games.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are infinitely repeated; the horizon is common knowledge.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. In each pairwise game, players observe each other's actions (perfect monitoring), so the identity of the punisher is always known.

## Important Limitations
- The model is entirely theoretical and does not provide empirical or quantitative evidence on efficiency or payoff outcomes.
- The analysis is for networks of pairwise repeated prisoners' dilemma games, not for n-player public goods games or their direct variants.
- No explicit analysis of group payoff, welfare, or efficiency is provided; the focus is on equilibrium structure and group-separation.
- Punishment is implemented only via trigger strategies (grim trigger), not via costly punishment or other mechanisms.
- The model assumes infinite repetition and perfect monitoring, which may not generalize to finite or noisy environments.
