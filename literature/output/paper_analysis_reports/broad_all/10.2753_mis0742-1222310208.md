# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Digital Piracy, Teens, and the Source of Advice: An Experimental Study
Authors: Hashim, M. J., Kannan, K. N., Maximiano, S., & Ulmer, J. R.
Journal: Journal of Management Information Systems
Year: 2014

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average group profit (record label profit)
- average number of songs purchased
- average number of songs pirated
Non-payoff outcomes:
- piracy rate
- purchasing rate
- response to advice
- effect of social tie and stake in advice
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the number of songs purchased and pirated (behavioral), and the profit of the record label (payoff). Efficiency as a ratio to the fully cooperative outcome is not directly reported, but group profit and purchasing rates are closely related. No explicit group-level efficiency ratio is calculated.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Advice from parents, especially when they are punished for their child's piracy, increases purchasing and group profit and reduces piracy compared to other advice sources. Advice from record labels is the least effective and can even reduce group profit. Advice from a regulator is largely ignored. The effect of advice is strongest when the adviser has both a social tie and a stake in the outcome. The study does not include standard peer punishment or reward mechanisms; the only 'punishment' is an exogenous penalty to parents in one treatment. The game is a close variant of a public goods game, but not a standard PGG. Efficiency-related outcomes (group profit, purchasing) are reported, but not as a ratio to the fully cooperative outcome.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence for the prediction task. It shows that exogenous punishment of a third party (parents) for a player's free-riding can increase public good provision and group profit, especially when combined with a social tie. However, it does not test endogenous peer punishment or standard PGG punishment institutions. The findings suggest that punishment mechanisms may be more effective when they are socially salient and affect someone with a close tie to the player, but direct transfer to standard PGG with peer punishment is limited. Control efficiency (group profit without punishment) is reported, and the effect of introducing a punishment-like mechanism (parent penalty) can be inferred, but the mechanism differs from standard punishment. The absence of peer punishment, reward, or communication means the results are only adjacent for direct prediction of PGG punishment effects.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 4 music consumers (players) per session.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 rounds per session; advice is given after rounds 8 and 14.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No communication or chat between players was allowed during the session.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is not standard; purchasing a song costs 1 token, gives 1.1 tokens to the buyer, and makes the song available for others to pirate (0.5 tokens per pirated song). The public good structure is present but not as a standard MPCR.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In the 'parent with punishment' treatment, parents lose 0.6 tokens per song pirated by their child. This is an exogenous penalty, not peer punishment. No cost to the punisher (parent is not the decision-maker).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is a fixed penalty to parents per pirated song (0.6 tokens). No endogenous punishment by players. The penalty is effective in increasing purchasing and group profit when combined with a social tie.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the session has 20 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Advice messages always included information about the record label's earnings and average piracy among group members.

## Important Limitations
- The game is a close but not exact variant of a standard public goods game; it is a volunteer's dilemma with piracy/purchase choices.
- No endogenous peer punishment or reward mechanisms are present; the only 'punishment' is an exogenous penalty to parents in one treatment.
- Efficiency is not reported as a ratio to the fully cooperative outcome; only group profit and purchasing rates are reported.
- The punishment mechanism (parent penalty) is not directly comparable to standard PGG punishment institutions.
- No communication or chat between players; advice is unidirectional from external sources.
- The study does not vary the degree of social tie or test field settings.
- Findings may not generalize to standard PGGs with peer punishment or to settings without strong social ties.
