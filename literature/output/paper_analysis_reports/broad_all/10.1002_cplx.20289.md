## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- final proportion of cooperators (X_final)
- time evolution of cooperation ratio (X(t))
- clustering patterns of cooperators/defectors
Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are the proportion of cooperators in the population at equilibrium or after a number of iterations, and the dynamics of this proportion. No group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings are reported. The payoffs are used to determine agent behavior, but aggregate or average payoffs are not analyzed as outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that the final proportion of cooperators depends strongly on the shape and relative position of the parabolic payoff functions, the agent update rule, and the definition of neighborhood (local vs. global). For some parameterizations, stable intermediate levels of cooperation emerge; in others, the system converges to all-cooperation or all-defection. The results are often unpredictable except in special cases (e.g., greedy or conformist agents with global neighborhoods). The paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes, nor does it include explicit punishment or reward mechanisms as in standard public goods games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency in public-goods-game-like environments. The game studied is adjacent to PGGs in that it is an N-person game with binary choices and payoff externalities, but it lacks explicit punishment/reward actions and does not report efficiency or group payoff. The findings may be useful for understanding how nonlinear payoff structures and local interactions affect cooperation rates, but they do not inform the prediction of efficiency changes due to punishment interventions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use 250,000 agents (500x500 grid), but the model is scalable to millions. The large N is fixed in the main results.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for a fixed number of iterations (e.g., 30, 50), or until equilibrium is reached. Number of rounds is a simulation parameter.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Agents choose between two discrete actions: cooperate (C) or defect (D).

## Important Limitations
- No explicit punishment or reward actions; all incentives are embedded in the payoff functions.
- No analysis or reporting of efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or total earnings.
- Outcomes are limited to behavioral measures (proportion of cooperators), not payoff-based outcomes.
- The game structure is adjacent to, but not equivalent to, standard public goods games.
- Findings may not generalize to environments with explicit punishment/reward mechanisms.
