# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Extending the Cooperative Phenotype: Assessing the Stability of Cooperation across Countries
Authors: Reigstad, A. G., Stromland, E. A., & Tinghög, G.
Journal: Frontiers in Psychology
Year: 2017

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution in Public Goods Game
- giving in Dictator Game
- cooperation in Prisoner's Dilemma
- punishment behavior (amount subtracted from others)
- generalized trust
- temporal stability of cooperation
Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on behavioral measures of cooperation (contributions, giving, cooperation choices) and punishment behavior, as well as their correlations and stability over time and across countries. It does not report efficiency, group payoff, or other payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that individuals' cooperative behavior is positively correlated across different economic games (Public Goods Game, Dictator Game, Prisoner's Dilemma) and is stable over time and across countries (Norway, Sweden, Austria, United States). Punishment behavior (measured in a Dictator Game with Punishment) is not correlated with prosociality in other games. The study does not report on efficiency or group payoff, nor does it analyze the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. It does not manipulate punishment in a PGG context, nor does it report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. Its main relevance is in showing that punishment behavior and cooperation are uncorrelated at the individual level, suggesting that the presence of punishment may not be strongly linked to prosociality. However, it does not inform the efficiency impact of punishment mechanisms in PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The PGG in the Norwegian sample used groups of three, but with a twist: one participant was randomly excluded before the pool was divided between the two remaining members. Other games (DG, PD) are two-player. No manipulation of group size.
- number of rounds: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. All games are one-shot (single round) as described in the methods. No repeated interaction.
- chat: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. No communication or chat between players; explicitly stated that no reputational incentives were involved.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. PGG and DG allow continuous contribution/giving (in increments), PD is binary (cooperate/defect).
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. PGG: Each participant could contribute any amount of 1,000 NOK; the amount contributed was multiplied by a factor larger than one and then evenly distributed among group members. The exact multiplier is not specified.
- punishment cost: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Punishment in the Dictator Game with Punishment was costless to the punisher.
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Punishment was implemented as a reduction in the payoff of the least generous dictator in a group of three; the exact impact per unit is not specified.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Games are one-shot; participants know the structure in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. No information about other players' choices or payoffs is provided during the games; all decisions are made without feedback.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; participants do not know who punished whom.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not analyze efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; all outcomes are behavioral (contribution, giving, cooperation, punishment behavior).
- Punishment is only implemented in a Dictator Game context, not in the Public Goods Game; thus, no evidence is provided on the effect of punishment on efficiency in PGG-like environments.
- Punishment is costless to the punisher, which is not typical of standard PGG punishment treatments.
- The PGG design is nonstandard: one of three group members is randomly excluded before the pool is divided.
- No manipulation or systematic variation of punishment parameters (cost, effectiveness, identifiability, etc.) is performed.
- The study is limited to WEIRD (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) populations.
