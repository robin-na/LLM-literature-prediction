# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: No contract or unfair contract: What's better?
Authors: Bruttel, L., & Eisenkopf, G.
Journal: Journal of Socio-economics
Year: 2012

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- joint payoff
- average welfare
- individual payoffs
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- conditional cooperation
- defection
- contract choice (fair/unfair)
- reciprocity type
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is joint payoff (welfare), with secondary analysis of cooperation/defection and contract choices. Punishment is not implemented as a direct costly action, but as defection in response to unfair contracts (negative reciprocity).

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a lab experiment comparing a sequential prisoner's dilemma (PD) to a vertically integrated version with incentive-compatible contracts (ICC), the introduction of contracts—regardless of fairness—increases joint payoffs from an average of 7.33 (PD) to 9.33 (ICC). Even unfair contracts yield higher welfare (9.02) than the best possible matching in PD. Most players cooperate under contracts, and the risk of defection in response to unfairness does not offset the efficiency gains. The study also finds that individual reciprocity types in PD do not predict negative reciprocity (defection) in ICC. Thus, the presence of incentive-compatible contracts robustly increases efficiency, even when some contracts are perceived as unfair.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a two-player sequential prisoner's dilemma, introducing an incentive-compatible contract mechanism (analogous to a principal-agent or vertical integration structure) increases efficiency (joint payoff) compared to the baseline without contracts. The effect is robust to the fairness of the contract: even when the contract is unfair (unequal payoffs), efficiency is higher than in the baseline. The mechanism for increased efficiency is that contracts induce more cooperation, and the risk of 'punishment' via defection in response to unfairness is not large enough to negate the efficiency gains. For prediction tasks, this suggests that enabling a contract-like mechanism (even if not perfectly fair) in a similar environment is likely to increase efficiency relative to the control. However, the setting is a sequential PD, not a standard PGG, and punishment is not implemented as a costly action but as defection in response to unfairness.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are two-player (dyadic) interactions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject plays each game once (one-shot, not repeated).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/cooperation choices are binary (cooperate/defect or A/B).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is 4 (total output increases from 6 to 10 if both cooperate; each player's cooperation increases group output by 4).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the experiment consists of two one-shot games; no uncertainty about the number of rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. All information about behavior and revenues in both games is revealed at the end of the experiment, not during.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a sequential prisoner's dilemma, not a standard public goods game; results may not generalize to multi-player or simultaneous-move PGGs.
- Punishment is not implemented as a costly action but as defection in response to unfair contracts; this is not equivalent to standard punishment mechanisms in PGGs.
- All interactions are one-shot; repeated game effects are not studied.
- Only two players per group; group size effects are not addressed.
- No communication or reputation effects are present.
- The contract mechanism is principal-agent (vertical integration), not a peer-to-peer sanctioning system.
