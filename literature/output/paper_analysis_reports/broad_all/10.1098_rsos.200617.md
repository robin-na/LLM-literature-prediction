# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The social function of the feeling and expression of guilt
Authors: Julle-Danière, E., Whitehouse, J., Vrij, A., Gustafsson, E., & Waller, B. M.
Journal: Royal Society Open Science
Year: 2020

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=close; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- reward split in dictator game (proportion of coins given to partner)
- change from original unfair split to final split
Non-payoff outcomes:
- self-reported guilt and shame
- judged guilt (by partner)
- facial action units (FACS coding)
- friendship index
- self-annoyance
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the reward split in a dictator game after a cooperative task and guilt induction. This is interpreted as a measure of relationship repair, pro-sociality, or punishment, but not as group efficiency or total welfare. No direct measure of group efficiency, total payoff relative to maximum, or surplus is reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that individuals who feel more guilt after being blamed for a cooperative failure allocate more of a shared reward to their partner, regardless of friendship strength. When the victim of a transgression (Player 2) perceives their partner as guilty, they are more likely to 'punish' (i.e., take more for themselves) if the partner is a close friend, though this effect is sensitive to outliers. The expression of guilt thus has a complex social function, sometimes leading to harsher punishment from close friends. The main behavioral outcome is the reward split in a dictator game, not efficiency or group payoff. The study does not directly address the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides limited direct support for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. The experimental design is adjacent to PGGs, using a one-shot dictator game after a cooperative task with induced guilt, but does not involve repeated contribution decisions, group efficiency, or explicit punishment mechanisms as in standard PGGs. The main outcome is the allocation of a fixed reward, interpreted as relationship repair or punishment, but not as efficiency. The findings suggest that social context (friendship, perceived guilt) can modulate punitive or reparative behavior, but do not provide quantitative evidence on how punishment affects efficiency in PGG-like games. Thus, the paper is more informative about the social and emotional moderators of punishment-like behavior than about efficiency outcomes relevant to the prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in dyads (2 players per group).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Single-shot dictator game after a one-off cooperative task; not repeated rounds.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the structure: one cooperative task, then one allocation decision.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed about the outcome of the cooperative task and the initial split; Player 2 is told about Player 1's performance and shown a video.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know each other's identity (friends or strangers); all interactions are face-to-face or with video.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not use a public goods game or repeated social dilemma; it uses a one-shot dictator game after a cooperative task.
- No direct measure of efficiency, group payoff, or surplus is reported; outcomes are behavioral (reward split) and emotional (guilt, shame).
- Punishment is not implemented as a costly, explicit mechanism; it is inferred from the victim's allocation decision.
- Findings on punishment of close friends are sensitive to outliers and should be interpreted with caution.
- The ecological validity is higher than hypothetical vignette studies, but the design is not directly transferable to PGG-like environments.
- No variation or manipulation of standard PGG design dimensions such as MPCR, punishment cost, or reward technology.
