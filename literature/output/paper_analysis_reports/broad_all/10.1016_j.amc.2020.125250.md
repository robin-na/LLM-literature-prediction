Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are the fractions of each strategy (C, D, A) and the overall cooperation level (C + A). Average payoff per strategy is reported, but group efficiency (total group payoff relative to full cooperation) is not directly analyzed. No direct analysis of group efficiency or welfare; focus is on cooperation prevalence and spatial dynamics.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The introduction of an alliance strategy, where players pay an aid fee to help exploited allies, leads to a dramatic increase in the prevalence of cooperative behavior (C + A) in spatial PGGs. Alliance clusters are robust to defector invasion and can expand to dominate the population, especially for intermediate values of the synergy factor (r) and aid intensity (beta). The cooperation level is a non-monotonic function of both r and beta, with optimal intervals for each. Average payoffs per strategy are reported, showing that alliance players eventually outperform defectors and cooperators. However, the paper does not report group efficiency or total welfare as a function of the alliance mechanism.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that mechanisms analogous to mutual aid or alliance (distinct from punishment) can promote cooperation and increase the prevalence of cooperative strategies in spatial PGGs. However, because the model does not include a punishment or sanctioning mechanism, and does not report group efficiency or total welfare, it is only adjacent to the prediction task of estimating efficiency effects of punishment. The findings suggest that payoff redistribution among cooperators can be highly effective in suppressing defection, but the results cannot be directly transferred to settings with explicit punishment. The paper is most useful for understanding the effects of mutual aid or alliance-type mechanisms, not punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group has 5 players (G=5), and the lattice size varies from 2500 to 10000 players. The group size is fixed, but the total population size is varied to check for finite-size effects.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 10^7 Monte Carlo steps (MCS), until strategy frequencies stabilize. No fixed number of rounds as in finite repeated games.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is all-or-nothing: players either contribute c=1 (C or A) or nothing (D).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r is varied (e.g., r=2.5, 3, 3.5, 4), group size G=5, so MPCR = r/5.

## Important Limitations
- The model does not include a punishment or sanctioning mechanism; alliance aid is a form of mutual support, not punishment.
- Group efficiency or total welfare is not directly analyzed; only average payoffs per strategy and cooperation prevalence are reported.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with explicit punishment or to non-spatial, well-mixed populations.
- No empirical or experimental data; results are based on agent-based simulations.
- No analysis of alliance defectors or heterogeneous investment, which may affect the dynamics.
