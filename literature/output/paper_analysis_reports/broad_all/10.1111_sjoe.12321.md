# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Public Goods and Minimum Provision Levels: Does the Institutional Formation Affect Cooperation?
Authors: Martinsson, P., & Persson, E.
Journal: Scandinavian Journal of Economics
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution level
- conditional contribution
- vote for minimum level
- beliefs about others' contributions
- contributor type classification (conditional cooperator, free rider, etc.)
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the contribution to the public good (tokens contributed), both unconditional and conditional. No direct analysis of efficiency, group payoff, or welfare is reported. The paper discusses welfare and efficiency in the discussion, but does not present explicit payoff or efficiency calculations.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Imposing a minimum contribution level (5 tokens out of 20) in a linear public goods game increases average contributions compared to a standard voluntary contribution mechanism. This effect is robust to whether the minimum is imposed exogenously or chosen endogenously by group vote. The increase in contributions is mainly due to free riders being forced to contribute at least the minimum; there is little evidence of crowding out among those who previously contributed more than the minimum. The mode of institutional formation (democracy vs. authority) does not significantly affect the outcome. The study does not analyze efficiency or group payoff directly, but the results suggest that minimum contribution rules can increase public good provision in settings where punishment is not feasible.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper is informative for predicting the effect of minimum contribution rules in public goods games, especially in the absence of punishment or reward mechanisms. It does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency, but it does show that institutional interventions (minimum levels) can increase contributions, mainly by raising the floor for low contributors. For the downstream prediction task, this paper suggests that minimum contribution rules can be effective in increasing contributions, but does not inform about the effect of punishment. The game design dimensions (e.g., player count, MPCR, no chat, continuous contributions, no punishment/reward) are clearly specified, which helps in mapping to other studies. However, since efficiency or group payoff is not directly measured, the findings should be used with caution for efficiency prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 3 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject makes unconditional and conditional contribution decisions in two stages (baseline and minimum level), but the game is not repeated over multiple rounds with feedback; rather, it is a one-shot or two-stage design.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are continuous (0-20 tokens), not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.5 (each token contributed returns 0.5 to each group member).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the structure of the experiment (two stages: baseline and minimum level).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. No feedback about others' contributions is given until the end of the experiment; beliefs about others' contributions are elicited.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not include a punishment or sanction mechanism; it only examines minimum contribution rules.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare are not directly measured or reported; only contribution levels are analyzed.
- The experiment is not a repeated multi-round public goods game with feedback; it uses a two-stage (baseline and minimum level) design with no feedback between stages.
- Findings may not generalize to settings with punishment, reward, or other institutional features.
- The sample is limited to university students in Sweden, which may affect external validity.
