The main dependent variable is the frequency of cooperators (cooperation rate) in the population. Payoff is discussed as a mechanism but not as a reported outcome. No group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that a reputation-based mechanism, where players can disconnect from low-reputation neighbors and reconnect with others, significantly increases the frequency of cooperation in repeated PDG simulations on dynamic networks. The effect is stronger when the reputation factor (alpha) is high and when the network's information connectivity (environmental degree r, rewiring probability p) is high. Conversely, when the selection intensity (sigma) is high, the effect of reputation is diminished and cooperation collapses. The results are consistent across both square lattice and small-world network topologies. However, the study does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes, focusing instead on behavioral cooperation rates.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence relevant to the prediction task. While it does not study public goods games or efficiency/payoff outcomes directly, it shows that reputation-based dynamic linking mechanisms can strongly increase cooperation rates in repeated PDG environments. The findings suggest that, in analogous PGG-like settings, mechanisms that allow for social selection or partner choice based on reputation may increase cooperation, which could plausibly translate into higher efficiency if payoffs are aligned with cooperation. However, the lack of direct efficiency or payoff data limits the ability to make quantitative predictions about efficiency changes. The results are most relevant for settings where dynamic partner selection or reputation-based exclusion is possible.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use N = L^2 = 100^2 = 10,000 players. Player count is fixed in all simulations.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 10^5 time steps (rounds). Number of rounds is fixed and long.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate or defect (binary choice).

## Important Limitations
- The study uses the Prisoner's Dilemma, not the public goods game, so results may not fully transfer to PGG-like environments.
- The main outcome is cooperation rate, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare; no direct payoff-based outcomes are reported.
- The reputation-based mechanism is implemented in agent-based simulations, not in human experiments.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanism is present; the focus is on dynamic linking based on reputation.
- The model assumes large populations (N=10,000) and long time horizons (10^5 rounds), which may differ from typical experimental or real-world settings.
- No analysis of the effect of the mechanism on group efficiency or surplus, only on behavioral cooperation.
