# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The effect of financial constraints on in-group bias: Evidence from rice farmers in Thailand
Authors: Boonmanunt, S., & Meier, S.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2023

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate in prisoner's dilemma
- punishment assigned in third-party punishment game
- amount shared in dictator game
Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on behavioral outcomes: cooperation, norm enforcement (punishment), and sharing. It does not report group efficiency, total payoff, welfare, or surplus. Payoff is discussed only as the structure of the games, not as an outcome variable.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that being more financially constrained (before harvest) does not increase in-group bias in cooperation or norm enforcement. In-group bias in cooperation and punishment is observed only after harvest (less constrained), but these effects are not statistically significant in regression analyses. Sharing behavior is stable across financial conditions. The main outcomes are cooperation rates, punishment assigned, and sharing amounts, not efficiency or group payoff. The results suggest that financial constraints do not inherently increase parochialism in cooperation or punishment among low-income rice farmers.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it does not directly inform the prediction of treatment efficiency from game design dimensions and control efficiency. However, it provides evidence that, in a prisoner's dilemma with third-party punishment (an adjacent but not exact PGG variant), the presence of punishment allows for the study of norm enforcement, but the main outcomes are behavioral (punishment assigned, cooperation rate) rather than efficiency. The design details (player count, punishment cost/tech, etc.) are well specified, but the lack of efficiency or payoff outcomes limits the paper's direct value for efficiency prediction tasks.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Prisoner's dilemma games are played in pairs (2 players per game); the third-party punishment game involves 4 players per group (2 A-players, 2 B-players).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are one-shot (single round).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Prisoner's dilemma: binary choice (keep or pass endowment). Punishment: B-players choose punishment in increments. Dictator: continuous choice (0-100 Baht).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Prisoner's dilemma: passing 40 Baht is doubled (80 Baht to partner). MPCR = 2 for the recipient, but not a standard PGG; not group-based.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each 1 Baht spent on punishment reduces the punisher's endowment by 1 Baht and the punished player's by 3 Baht. Max 20 Baht can be spent.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: 1 Baht cost to punisher reduces target's payoff by 3 Baht.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are one-shot; participants know the number of rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. No feedback about experimental earnings or others' choices is provided during the experiment; only after all games and the post-experiment questionnaire.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; punished players do not know the identity of the punisher.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; only behavioral outcomes (cooperation, punishment, sharing) are analyzed.
- The games are not public goods games but rather prisoner's dilemma and third-party punishment games (adjacent to PGGs).
- The sample is limited to low-income rice farmers in rural Thailand, which may limit generalizability.
- Potential differences in anonymity between in-group and out-group partners could affect behavior.
- Between-subjects design may not fully control for unobserved differences between before- and after-harvest groups.
- Statistical power may be insufficient to detect some effects, especially interaction effects.
