# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Cognitive load decreases cooperation and moral punishment in a Prisoner's Dilemma game with punishment option
Authors: Mieth, L., Buchner, A., & Bell, R.
Journal: Scientific Reports
Year: 2021

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- moral punishment rate
- hypocritical punishment rate
- antisocial punishment rate
- punishment bias
- height of punishment (amount spent to punish)
Outcome notes:
The study focuses on behavioral outcomes: probability of cooperation, probability of different types of punishment, and punishment bias. No direct analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is reported. The only monetary outcomes discussed are the costs and deductions associated with punishment, not aggregate group payoffs or efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that cognitive load reduces both cooperation and moral punishment in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma game with punishment. Hypocritical and antisocial punishment are not significantly affected by cognitive load, but a general punishment bias (punishing regardless of partner behavior) increases under cognitive load. The amount spent on punishment, when punishment occurs, does not differ between conditions. The results suggest that both cooperation and moral punishment require cognitive resources and are not purely intuitive. The study does not report on efficiency or group payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that, in a two-player one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma with a punishment option, the presence of punishment (and its purposeful use) depends on cognitive resources. However, because the study does not report efficiency or group payoff, it does not directly inform predictions about the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-game-like environments. The findings are most relevant for understanding how cognitive load moderates the behavioral use of punishment and cooperation, not their impact on group efficiency. The game is a Prisoner's Dilemma, not a public goods game, so transfer to PGG settings should be cautious.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is a two-player (dyadic) simultaneous Prisoner's Dilemma.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each participant played 20 one-shot rounds with 20 different partners. Each interaction is one-shot; no repeated interaction with the same partner.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players; all interactions are anonymous and computer-mediated.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is all-or-nothing: cooperate (invest 30 cents) or defect (invest nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (MPCR) is 1/3: the bonus is always 1/3 of the sum of investments, which is then split equally.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 1 cent to reduce partner's account by 10 cents (cost ratio 1:10).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 10:1 (1 cent spent reduces partner's payoff by 10 cents).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward mechanism does not exist; only punishment is available.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the number of rounds (20 trials with 20 different partners).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants see their own and partner's choices and payoffs after each round; account balances are displayed.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is attributed to the partner; participants see how much the partner punished them and vice versa.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma game, not a public goods game; results may not generalize to PGG settings.
- No direct measurement or analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare; only behavioral outcomes are reported.
- All partners are computer-controlled, not human, which may affect ecological validity.
- Punishment is always available and is the only sanctioning mechanism; no reward option is present.
- The effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff is not analyzed, limiting direct relevance for efficiency prediction tasks.
