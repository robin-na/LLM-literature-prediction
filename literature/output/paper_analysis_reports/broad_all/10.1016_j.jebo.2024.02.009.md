# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Inequality and the allocation of collective goods
Authors: Cox, C. A., & Stoddard, B.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- group-level efficiency (sum of contributors' earnings divided by maximum possible earnings)
- group-level total earnings
- individual earnings
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate (as percentage of endowment)
- allocated share of collective good
- deviation from fairness benchmarks in allocation
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is group-level efficiency, with extensive analysis of how allocation mechanisms affect efficiency. Contribution rates and allocation patterns are also analyzed, but efficiency is the primary focus.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment compares standard linear public goods games (equal-share) to treatments where a third-party allocator divides the collective good among contributors. The allocator mechanism increases efficiency in all cases, but the effect is strongest in homogeneous groups. When group members differ in endowments or productivity, the allocator's ability to boost efficiency is significantly diminished, though still positive relative to the equal-share baseline. Allocators generally reward contributors in proportion to their relative effort (percentage of endowment contributed), but in heterogeneous groups, conflicting fairness ideals make it harder to sustain high efficiency. The study provides direct evidence that flexible allocation mechanisms can substitute for punishment/reward institutions in promoting efficiency, but their effectiveness is moderated by group heterogeneity.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that mechanisms allowing for flexible allocation of the collective good (by a third-party allocator) can substantially increase efficiency in public-goods-like environments, especially when groups are homogeneous. However, the efficiency boost is reduced in the presence of heterogeneity in endowments or productivity. For prediction tasks, the presence of an allocator (as a form of centralized sanctioning/reward) should be expected to increase efficiency relative to equal-share baselines, but the magnitude of the effect will be smaller in heterogeneous groups. The findings are directly relevant for predicting efficiency in games with allocation mechanisms, and indirectly relevant for understanding the limits of punishment/reward institutions in heterogeneous environments.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 4 contributors; allocator treatments add a 5th player (the allocator), but only contributors' efficiency is analyzed.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use 10 periods (rounds) of repeated play.
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No chat or communication between players is allowed; decisions are made privately and anonymously.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players can contribute any whole number of tokens from their endowment.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.6 in homogeneous and heterogeneous endowment treatments (multiplier 2.4, 4 players); in heterogeneous productivity, high types have MPCR 0.9 (3.6/4), low types 0.3 (1.2/4).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The allocator mechanism functions as a centralized reward system, allocating more to higher contributors.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. The allocator does not pay a cost to reward; they allocate the collective good among contributors. The allocator receives a fixed share (0.25) of the collective good, but cannot take from contributors.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The allocator can allocate any share of the collective good to any contributor, subject to the total sum constraint. This is a highly flexible, high 'reward technology' mechanism.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game lasts 10 periods; the number of rounds is fixed and known.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each period, all group members see the total group contribution, value of the collective good, their own earnings, and a history table with their own and others' past contributions and allocations.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Allocators and contributors are identified by ID letters; the allocator observes all contributors' actions and types, and contributors see their own allocations. There is no punishment, but allocation is not anonymous to the allocator.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not include peer punishment or decentralized sanctioning; the mechanism is a centralized allocation by a third party.
- The findings may not generalize to settings with peer punishment, endogenous group formation, or communication between players.
- The allocator's incentives are aligned with group efficiency, but real-world allocators may have self-serving motives.
- The experiment uses fixed groups and a known, finite number of rounds, which may affect dynamic incentives.
- Heterogeneity is exogenously assigned and does not capture earned or endogenous differences.
