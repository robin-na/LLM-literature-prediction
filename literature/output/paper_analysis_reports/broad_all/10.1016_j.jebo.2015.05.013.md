# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: It's the thought that counts: The role of intentions in noisy repeated games
Authors: Rand, D. G., Fudenberg, D., & Dreber, A.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 2015

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- expected payoff per strategy
- average group payoff
- payoff comparisons across strategies and conditions
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- strategy type (e.g., intention-based, outcome-based, lenient, forgiving)
- learning rate
- decision time
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are cooperation rates and strategy types, but expected payoffs for each strategy and condition are also reported (see Table 5). Efficiency per se is not directly calculated, but group payoffs and expected payoffs are provided and can be mapped to efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in repeated prisoner's dilemma games with implementation errors, making intentions observable (explicit-intentions treatment) leads to significantly higher cooperation rates and group payoffs than when intentions are unobservable (implicit-intentions control). The cooperation and payoff levels in the explicit-intentions treatment are statistically indistinguishable from those in error-free games. Most subjects use simple, intention-based strategies, and the negative impact of noise on cooperation is eliminated when intentions are observable. The effect holds for both high and low benefit/cost ratios. Expected payoffs for the most common intention-based strategies are the highest among all strategies, and the overall group payoff is maximized in the explicit-intentions and no-error conditions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in repeated prisoner's dilemma games with noise, making intentions observable can fully restore efficiency (group payoff) to the level seen in error-free games. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the negative effect of noise on efficiency can be mitigated or eliminated if the game design allows players to observe each other's intentions. While the setting is not a public goods game per se, the findings are likely to transfer to PGG-like environments with binary choices and noise. The paper does not include explicit punishment mechanisms, but the analysis of punitive and tolerant strategies provides indirect evidence about sanctioning behavior. The main design dimensions relevant for prediction are the presence of noise, observability of intentions, and the benefit/cost ratio. Other dimensions (e.g., player count, chat, reward, punishment cost) are not varied or present.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are two-player repeated prisoner's dilemmas.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are indefinitely repeated with a continuation probability of 7/8 per round; average number of periods per supergame is about 8.1–8.4.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate (pay cost to give benefit) or defect (do nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Two benefit/cost ratios are used: b/c = 1.5 and b/c = 4. This is equivalent to varying the marginal per-capita return.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is indefinitely repeated with a known continuation probability; players do not know the exact number of rounds in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are shown the intended and realized actions of their partner (in explicit-intentions treatment), or only realized actions (in implicit-intentions and no-error controls).

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a repeated prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not fully generalize to multi-player or continuous-contribution PGGs.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanisms are present; 'punishment' is implemented only via strategy (e.g., tit-for-tat), not as a separate costly action.
- All games are two-player; effects of group size are not tested.
- Only binary (all-or-nothing) choices are used; continuous contribution settings are not studied.
- No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- The main manipulation is observability of intentions, not punishment or sanctioning technology.
