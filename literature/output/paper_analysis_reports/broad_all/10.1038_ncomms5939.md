# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Humans display a 'cooperative phenotype' that is domain general and temporally stable
Authors: Peysakhovich, A., Nowak, M. A., & Rand, D. G.
Journal: Nature Communications
Year: 2014

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution in PGG
- giving in Dictator Game
- trust and trustworthiness in Trust Game
- punishment in Ultimatum Game, 2PP, 3PP
- real-effort helping
- self-reported cooperation and punishment values/behaviors
- generalized trust
Outcome notes:
The primary outcomes are behavioral measures of cooperation (e.g., contributions, giving, trust) and punishment (e.g., minimum acceptable offer, points spent on punishment), as well as self-report and real-effort helping. No group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported or analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The main finding is that individuals' cooperative behavior is consistent across different cooperation games (including the PGG, Dictator Game, and Trust Game) and over time, supporting the existence of a 'cooperative phenotype.' However, this cooperative tendency does not extend to norm-enforcing punishment games (Ultimatum Game, 2PP, 3PP), as there is little to no correlation between cooperation and punishment behaviors. The study also finds that cooperation in games predicts real-world helping and generalized trust, but not self-reported cooperative behaviors. The research is correlational and does not examine the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff in PGGs or related games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in PGG-like environments, as it does not manipulate the presence or absence of punishment in PGGs, nor does it report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. Its main relevance is in showing that cooperative and punishment behaviors are distinct at the individual level in one-shot games, suggesting that the presence of punishment may not be strongly linked to underlying cooperative tendencies. For the downstream prediction task, this paper offers little direct guidance on how punishment affects efficiency, but it does caution against assuming that those who cooperate are also those who punish, or that punishment and cooperation are tightly coupled in one-shot settings.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. PGG is played with 4 players; other games have 2 or 3 players. The player count is specified for each game, but not varied experimentally within a game type.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are one-shot (single round) with no feedback or repetition.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players in any game.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. PGG and DG allow continuous contribution choices (0 to 100 points); not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. In the PGG, each point contributed is doubled and split among 4 players, so MPCR = 0.5.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment games specify the cost to the punisher (e.g., in 2PP, up to 14 points can be spent, each point reduces the other's payoff by 5). However, no PGG with punishment is included.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is specified (e.g., each point spent reduces the other's payoff by 5 in 2PP and 3PP). No variation or analysis of this parameter.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Games are explicitly one-shot; participants know there is only one round.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not include any PGGs with punishment enabled, nor does it compare PGGs with and without punishment.
- No efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or surplus outcomes are reported or analyzed; all outcomes are behavioral (contribution, punishment, helping, self-report).
- All games are one-shot and anonymous, limiting generalizability to repeated or real-world settings.
- Punishment is only studied in non-PGG games (Ultimatum Game, 2PP, 3PP), and the punishment games used are nonstandard variants.
- The study is correlational and does not manipulate game design dimensions to test causal effects on efficiency or payoff.
