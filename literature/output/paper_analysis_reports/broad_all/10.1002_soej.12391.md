# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: An Honest Day's Pay: Cooperation among Entrepreneurs vs. Students, and Linkages to Real-World Business Success
Authors: Batsaikhan, M., & Putterman, L.
Journal: Southern Economic Journal
Year: 2019

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- tokens allocated to production
- tokens allocated to theft
- tokens allocated to private protection
- tokens allocated to collective protection
- message choice (cooperation intention)
- trend in cooperation (production/theft) over rounds
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are behavioral: allocation of tokens to production, theft, private protection, and collective protection. The paper does not report group efficiency, total group payoff, or welfare as a primary outcome, though it discusses how behavioral allocations relate to social optimum and efficiency in the text.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Entrepreneurs in Mongolia display higher and increasing cooperation in a repeated property rights dilemma game compared to students, as measured by more tokens allocated to production and collective protection and fewer to theft. The opportunity to vote for mandatory collective protection (akin to a tax) is more frequently chosen by entrepreneurs, but this does not substantially change their already high cooperation levels. The chat treatment (pre-scripted messages) is associated with higher collective protection among entrepreneurs. There is no formal punishment mechanism (costly sanctions) as in standard PGGs; theft can be used as retaliation, but this is not a direct analog. The paper does not report group efficiency or total payoff, but discusses how behavioral allocations relate to social optimum. More successful entrepreneurs in real life are more cooperative in the lab. The main outcomes are behavioral, not payoff-based.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency in public goods games or close variants. The experimental design lacks a formal costly punishment mechanism; instead, it features theft (which is a form of negative externality, not a sanction) and collective protection (akin to public good provision). While the paper discusses how cooperation (less theft, more production, more collective protection) would increase efficiency, it does not report efficiency or group payoff as an outcome, nor does it manipulate punishment in the relevant sense. Thus, the paper is not directly informative for predicting efficiency changes due to punishment in PGG-like environments, though it provides contextual evidence about cooperation, institutional choice, and behavioral trends in repeated social dilemmas.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 5 players per session, fixed for the repeated game.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 24 periods (rounds) per session.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. S-CHAT treatment allows pre-scripted message exchange; associated with higher collective protection among entrepreneurs.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices (production, theft, protection) are continuous: players allocate 10 tokens among activities each round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not a standard PGG, but collective protection tokens increase group protection by 0.06 per token for all; production has diminishing returns (see Table 1).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is finitely repeated with 24 periods; subjects are aware of the number of rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Collective protection allocations are shown to all group members before other decisions each period.

## Important Limitations
- No formal punishment or costly sanction mechanism is present; theft is a negative externality, not a punishment.
- Primary outcomes are behavioral (production, theft, protection), not efficiency or group payoff.
- Does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare as a main outcome.
- Design is a property rights dilemma, not a standard public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG-with-punishment settings.
- No direct manipulation or analysis of punishment cost or effectiveness.
