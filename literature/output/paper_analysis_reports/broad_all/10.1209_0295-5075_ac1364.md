Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the evolutionary dynamics and stationary fractions of different strategies (cooperation, defection, loner, partial cooperation) in a spatial voluntary prisoner's dilemma with a new 'partial cooperation' strategy. No explicit analysis of group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare is provided. Payoff is only used to define strategy update rules, not as a reported outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that introducing a partial cooperation strategy (representing moderate risk preference) into a spatial voluntary prisoner's dilemma leads to rich evolutionary dynamics, including new forms of cyclic dominance and phase transitions between different stable strategy combinations. The willingness to participate parameter (u) determines whether partial cooperators act more like cooperators or loners, affecting which strategies coexist. The presence of partial cooperators can reduce the prevalence of unconditional cooperators and inhibit second-order free-riding. However, the study does not report on efficiency, group payoff, or the effects of punishment or reward mechanisms.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or their close variants. It does not manipulate or analyze punishment, nor does it report efficiency or group payoff outcomes. The model is adjacent to the PGG family (voluntary PD with loner and partial cooperation strategies) and focuses on risk preference and participation willingness. The findings may be relevant for understanding how participation options and risk attitudes affect cooperation dynamics, but not for quantitative prediction of efficiency under punishment. Thus, this paper should not be weighted heavily for the downstream prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run on a square lattice with size L x L, where L varies from 500 to 2000. Each player interacts with 4 neighbors (von Neumann neighborhood).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 5x10^4 to 1x10^6 Monte Carlo steps, with steady-state fractions measured over the last 5x10^3 steps.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial voluntary prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game or its close variant.
- No punishment or reward mechanisms are included; the study does not analyze the effects of sanctions.
- No efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are reported; only strategy fractions and evolutionary dynamics are analyzed.
- Findings are based on simulation and theory, not empirical or experimental data.
- Results may not generalize to well-mixed populations or to games with explicit punishment/reward options.
