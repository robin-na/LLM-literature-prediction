# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Social Heuristics and Social Roles: Intuition Favors Altruism for Women but Not for Men
Authors: Rand, D. G., Brescoll, V. L., Everett, J. A. C., Capraro, V., & Barcelo, H.
Journal: Journal of Experimental Psychology-general
Year: 2016

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- amount given in Dictator Game (percentage of endowment given to recipient)
- effect of cognitive processing mode (intuition vs. deliberation) on giving
- moderation by gender
- moderation by sex role identification
Outcome notes:
The primary dependent variable is the percentage of the endowment given to the recipient in the Dictator Game. No group payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported, as the Dictator Game is a unilateral giving game with no strategic interaction or group surplus.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that promoting intuition (via time pressure, conceptual priming, or cognitive load) increases altruistic giving in the Dictator Game for women, but not for men. This effect is further moderated by gender role identification: women who identify with masculine attributes become less altruistic when deliberating, while those who identify with feminine attributes are unaffected by deliberation. Men are generally less altruistic, and their giving is not significantly affected by cognitive processing mode or gender role identification. The study does not involve strategic interaction, punishment, or group efficiency outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence relevant to predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. The experimental paradigm is the Dictator Game, which is a unilateral giving game with no strategic interaction, no punishment or sanctioning mechanisms, and no group payoff or efficiency concept. While the paper discusses social norms and the internalization of altruism (and mentions that women are punished more for failing to be altruistic), it does not experimentally manipulate or measure punishment, nor does it report any efficiency or group payoff outcomes. Thus, it offers little direct support for the downstream prediction task, except as very indirect context about gender, social heuristics, and norm compliance.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All studies use the Dictator Game with two players: one dictator and one recipient.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All studies use a one-shot (single round) Dictator Game.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed; decisions are made anonymously.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: dictators can choose any amount from 0% to 100% of the endowment to give.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the game is a one-shot (single round) interaction.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses the Dictator Game, which is a unilateral giving game with no strategic interaction, no punishment or sanctioning, and no group efficiency or surplus.
- No efficiency, welfare, or group payoff outcomes are reported or analyzed.
- No manipulation or measurement of punishment, sanctions, or peer enforcement.
- Findings about gender, intuition, and altruism may not generalize to public goods games or environments with strategic interaction and punishment.
- The effect sizes may be underestimated due to the partial effectiveness of cognitive processing manipulations.
