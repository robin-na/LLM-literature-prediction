Outcome notes:
The paper reports on the proportions of different strategy types (special cooperator, ordinary cooperator, defector) and their coexistence, but does not directly report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare. The outcomes are behavioral (strategy frequencies) rather than payoff-based.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that introducing special cooperators who can both reward and punish leads to a variety of stable states, including coexistence of all three strategies or pairs of strategies. Punishment is necessary to suppress defectors and enable cooperation, but excessive punishment can reduce the prevalence of special cooperators. Rewards only promote ordinary cooperation when punishment is at least moderate. The effects of reward and punishment on cooperation are non-linear: moderate levels are most effective, while too much of either can be counterproductive. The model does not report efficiency or group payoff, so findings are limited to behavioral strategy frequencies.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect, mechanistic evidence that punishment (especially when combined with reward) can promote the prevalence of cooperative strategies in spatial evolutionary games. However, because the outcomes are proportions of strategy types rather than efficiency or group payoff, the results are only adjacent to the prediction task. The findings suggest that moderate punishment is most effective for promoting cooperation, and that the effect of reward depends on the level of punishment. For prediction of efficiency, this paper suggests that the relationship between punishment and efficiency may be non-monotonic, with moderate punishment being optimal, but does not provide direct quantitative evidence for efficiency outcomes.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes; only behavioral strategy frequencies are analyzed.
- The game is a spatial evolutionary prisoner's dilemma variant, not a standard public goods game, so results may not transfer directly to PGG settings.
- Punishment and reward are implemented as parameters in a stylized model; real-world implementation details and costs may differ.
- No empirical or experimental data are provided; all results are from simulations.
- The model assumes a very large, fixed population and does not vary player count or group size.
