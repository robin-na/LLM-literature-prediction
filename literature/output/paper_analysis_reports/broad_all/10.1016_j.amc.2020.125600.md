Outcome notes:
The paper's main dependent variable is the average contribution rate at evolutionary equilibrium. Payoff functions are derived and used to drive evolutionary dynamics, but the main reported outcomes are behavioral (contribution rates), not group payoff, efficiency, or welfare.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in evolutionary PGGs with three types of players (cooperators, defectors, conditional cooperators), the presence of conditional cooperators does not increase average contribution in standard PGGs or with institutional reward. However, with institutional punishment, conditional cooperators enable high contribution rates even for small punishment sizes, making punishment more effective than reward in promoting cooperation. These findings are robust to the inclusion of decision errors (bounded rationality). The main reported outcome is the stable contribution rate, not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical evidence that, in PGGs with institutional punishment, the presence of conditional cooperators can substantially increase the equilibrium contribution rate, especially for small punishment sizes. However, because the main outcome is contribution rate rather than efficiency or group payoff, the direct transfer to efficiency prediction is limited. The model assumes infinite populations and evolutionary dynamics, which may differ from finite, repeated lab or field experiments. The results suggest that, all else equal, punishment is more effective than reward at increasing cooperation rates when conditional cooperators are present, but do not provide direct quantitative predictions for efficiency or group payoff.

## Important Limitations
- The main outcome is contribution rate, not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- The model is theoretical and uses infinite population evolutionary dynamics, which may not match finite, repeated experimental or field settings.
- Institutional punishment and reward are modeled as third-party mechanisms; individual-level punishment/reward costs and impacts are not separated.
- No direct analysis of efficiency or group payoff is provided.
- No empirical or experimental data are presented.
- The model assumes no communication, no feedback, and no identification of punishers/rewarders.
- The model simplifies conditional cooperation as matching the population average, which may not capture all real-world behaviors.
