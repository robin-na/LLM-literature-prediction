Outcome notes:
The paper reports on the fractions of different strategies (cooperators, defectors, justicers) in the stable state and their evolution over time, but does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. Payoff is discussed at the individual level for strategy updating, but not as an aggregate outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that introducing a justice strategy (where players punish defectors at equal cost) alongside dynamic aspiration updating can suppress or eliminate defectors and promote cooperation in a spatial prisoner's dilemma. The effectiveness depends on the initial aspiration level: higher aspirations lead to fewer defectors and more justicers. The justice strategy can dominate even when initially rare, especially when defectors form clusters. The model is agent-based and simulation-driven, with outcomes reported as the stable fractions of each strategy. No aggregate payoff or efficiency outcomes are reported.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the idea that punishment (even when costly to the punisher) can reduce defection and promote cooperation in spatial social dilemmas, especially when combined with dynamic aspirations. However, because the outcomes are reported as strategy fractions rather than group efficiency or payoff, the paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting efficiency changes due to punishment. The model is also a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game, though the mechanisms are related. The findings may be useful for understanding the behavioral pathways by which punishment can affect cooperation, but cannot be directly used to predict efficiency outcomes in PGG-like environments.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model uses a square lattice of size L=100, so there are 10,000 players in the simulation.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run for 100,000 steps (rounds) to reach a stable state.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Strategy choice is discrete: cooperate, defect, or justice. No continuous contribution.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost to the punisher is equal to the loss imposed on the defector (l = -0.3).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment impact per unit cost is 1:1 (punisher pays same as defector loses, l = -0.3).

## Important Limitations
- The model is a spatial prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game, so direct transfer to PGG settings is limited.
- No group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff outcomes are reported; only fractions of strategies are analyzed.
- The punishment mechanism is self-costly and differs from typical experimental PGG punishment settings.
- All results are from agent-based simulations, not empirical or experimental data.
- The model assumes a fixed network structure (square lattice) and does not test other network types.
