## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that spiteful and conditionally spiteful strategies can evolve and predominate in finite, fluctuating populations, especially when population sizes are small and/or pre-play signaling is possible. Spite increases the risk of extinction, but conditional spite (which targets out-group members based on signals) can persist and even dominate under a wide range of conditions. The presence of signaling allows for the evolution of conditional spite, which the authors suggest may be a proto-form of punishment. However, the outcomes are about evolutionary prevalence and extinction risk, not about efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games. Its models are adjacent, focusing on the evolution of spite and conditional spite (as a possible origin of punishment) in a Prisoner's Delight game, not a public goods game. The outcomes are about which strategies predominate and the risk of extinction, not about group efficiency or payoff. The findings may inform high-level reasoning about the evolutionary origins of punishment and the role of signaling, but do not provide quantitative or direct evidence for the efficiency effects of punishment in PGG-like environments.

## Important Limitations

- Outcomes are about evolutionary prevalence and extinction risk, not about group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff.
- No direct analysis of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes relevant to the prediction task.
- Punishment is modeled as spite or conditional spite, not as norm-enforcing punishment in a PGG context.
- Simulation results may be sensitive to parameter choices and model structure.
- No empirical or experimental data; all results are from computational evolutionary simulations.
