## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that allowing agents to rewire their social connections when unsatisfied (i.e., when their payoff is less than their contribution) leads to higher average contributions in public goods games on networks, compared to static networks. The transition from low to high contribution occurs at lower enhancement factors when rewiring is allowed, and higher rewiring probabilities further increase average contributions. These effects are robust across both synthetic and real social network structures. However, the model does not include explicit punishment or reward options, and does not report efficiency or group payoff as outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect evidence that enabling a form of costless partner switching (exit) can increase average contributions in networked public goods games, which may be relevant for predicting the effects of punishment-like mechanisms that allow players to avoid or sanction defectors. However, because the model does not include explicit costly punishment, and does not report efficiency or group payoff, its direct value for predicting efficiency effects of punishment is limited. The findings are most relevant for understanding the impact of dynamic network structure (partner choice) rather than punishment per se.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations use 500 players for synthetic networks and 889 for the Wikipedia network. Player count is fixed per simulation.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 40 rounds per treatment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is discrete: agents choose from {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}. Not all-or-nothing; allows partial contributions.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Enhancement factor r is varied from 1.2 to 4.0; group size is typically 7 (mean degree 6 + focal node). MPCR = r / group size.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Number of rounds is fixed and known in simulation (40 rounds).

## Important Limitations
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanism is modeled; only costless partner switching (link redirection) is allowed.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are not reported; only average contribution is analyzed.
- Findings are based on simulation, not empirical or experimental data.
- Partner switching is costless, which may not reflect real-world or experimental conditions.
- The model assumes minimal information: agents only know their own contribution and payoff.
