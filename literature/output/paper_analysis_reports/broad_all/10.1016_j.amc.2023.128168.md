Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the frequency of cooperation (proportion of cooperators). The model tracks the number of revengers and sufferers, but does not report group payoff, efficiency, welfare, or total earnings. Payoff is used in the model mechanics but not as a reported outcome.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that introducing a revenge-based costly punishment mechanism in the PDG on networks increases the frequency of cooperation, especially when the cost to the punisher is low and the cost to the punished is high. The effect is robust across network types (square lattice and small-world), cost-to-benefit ratios, and network sizes. However, the main outcome is the frequency of cooperation, not efficiency or group payoff. The model does not report on group welfare, surplus, or total earnings, so direct implications for efficiency are not available.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides indirect support for the idea that punishment mechanisms (specifically, revenge-based costly punishment) can increase cooperation rates in social dilemma games on networks. However, because the main outcome is the frequency of cooperation and not efficiency or group payoff, the findings cannot be directly used to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or similar environments. The model structure and parameterization may inform mechanistic expectations, but quantitative prediction of efficiency outcomes is not possible from this paper alone.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Network size N is varied in simulations (e.g., N=100 to N=6400). Main results use N=1600.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for T=10,000 time steps (rounds).
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between cooperation (C) and defection (D); binary choice.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost to the punisher (revenger) is parameterized by rho_r (varied from 0 to 1). Lower cost to punisher increases cooperation frequency.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment impact per unit cost is parameterized by rho_s (cost to sufferer per act of revenge). Higher punishment effectiveness (higher rho_s) increases cooperation frequency.

## Important Limitations
- The model is a theoretical simulation, not an empirical or experimental study.
- The main outcome is the frequency of cooperation, not efficiency, group payoff, welfare, or total earnings.
- The game is a prisoner's dilemma, not a public goods game; results may not transfer directly to PGG environments.
- No direct analysis of efficiency or payoff-based outcomes is provided.
- No human subjects or real-world data are used; all results are from agent-based simulations.
