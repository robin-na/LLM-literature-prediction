Outcome notes:
The paper focuses on the evolution of cooperation (fraction of cooperators) in pairwise social dilemma games (prisoner's dilemma, snowdrift, stag-hunt) on a lattice, under different rules for how past payoffs are incorporated into strategy updating. No explicit payoff, efficiency, or welfare outcomes are reported; all results are in terms of cooperation rates and spatial patterns.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that when agents in spatial social dilemma games (prisoner's dilemma, snowdrift, stag-hunt) update their strategies based on a combination of current and past payoffs, cooperation is strongly promoted compared to the standard model where only current payoffs matter. This effect is robust to the details of how the past is incorporated (weighted average or single past payoff with decaying probability). The mechanism works by slowing down the invasion of defectors into cooperative clusters, allowing cooperation to persist and spread. The effect is most pronounced when the memory window is sufficiently long or the selected past payoff is not too far in the past. The results are shown for the stationary fraction of cooperators, not for efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games or closely related environments. It does not manipulate or analyze punishment, nor does it report efficiency, welfare, or group payoff outcomes. Its findings are about how memory of past payoffs (a strategy-neutral mechanism) can promote cooperation in spatial social dilemmas. While the results may be conceptually relevant for understanding how information and history affect cooperation, they do not inform the prediction of efficiency changes due to punishment in public goods games. Thus, this paper should not be used as direct evidence for the downstream prediction task, except perhaps as very weak, indirect context for the role of history or memory in cooperation.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations are run on square lattices of size L x L, with L ranging from 100 to 800. Each player interacts with 4 neighbors (von Neumann neighborhood). The exact number of players is L^2, but the focus is on large populations.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for 10,000 Monte Carlo steps for relaxation, then 20,000 steps for measurement. Each step gives every player a chance to update once on average.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players choose between two discrete strategies: cooperate or defect (binary choice).

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical/modeling and does not report any empirical or experimental data.
- The model is based on pairwise social dilemmas (prisoner's dilemma, snowdrift, stag-hunt) on a lattice, not on public goods games.
- No punishment or reward mechanisms are present or analyzed; the focus is on memory of past payoffs as a strategy-neutral mechanism.
- No efficiency, welfare, group payoff, or related payoff-based outcomes are reported; all results are in terms of cooperation rates.
- Findings may not generalize to public goods games or to environments with explicit punishment or reward.
