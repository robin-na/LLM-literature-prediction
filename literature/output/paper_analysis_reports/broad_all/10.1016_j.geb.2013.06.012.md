Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff

Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable for payoff is the present discounted value of earnings, which is directly compared across punishment strategies. Non-payoff outcomes include the classification and analysis of punishment strategies, but the focus is on their impact on earnings.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that the vast majority of subjects do not use harsh, disproportionate punishment strategies. Instead, most use graduated or lenient strategies, such as Tit-for-tat, which respond proportionally to deviations. Regression analysis shows that adopting Tit-for-tat leads to significantly higher present discounted value of earnings than harsher punishment strategies, even after controlling for past earnings. Communication between subjects increases the likelihood of cooperation (high prices) but does not significantly increase the use of harsh punishment. The findings suggest that in repeated games with rich action spaces, softer punishment strategies are more effective for sustaining cooperation and maximizing group payoffs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that in repeated games with a rich action space (such as price competition), enabling punishment does not require harsh, maximal punishment to achieve high efficiency. Instead, proportionate, graduated punishment (e.g., Tit-for-tat) is more effective for maximizing group payoffs. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the presence of a punishment mechanism will likely increase efficiency, but the effect depends on the nature of punishment strategies adopted. Communication further supports cooperation but does not necessarily increase the use of harsh punishment. The findings are most directly relevant to repeated duopoly/oligopoly settings, but the payoff-based results are informative for adjacent public-goods-like environments.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in fixed pairs (duopoly, 2 players per market).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Random continuation rule after round 12; average market lasted 28.1 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. One quarter of markets allowed one text message per round; communication increased cooperation (higher prices) but did not increase harsh punishment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (price choice) is continuous: subjects choose integer prices from 1 to 25.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is implemented by lowering price, which directly reduces the other player's payoff. The effectiveness of punishment is determined by the payoff table; harshness and proportionality of punishment are analyzed.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game uses a random continuation rule after round 12; they do not know the exact number of rounds in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects can see their own and the other subject's past prices and associated payoffs.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented via price choice, which is observable; identities are not revealed, but the paired subject is always the same and known as 'the other seller'.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is a repeated Bertrand duopoly (price competition) game, not a public goods game; results may not fully generalize to standard PGGs.
- Punishment is endogenous via price choice, not via a separate costly punishment mechanism as in PGGs.
- Only two players per group; effects may differ in larger groups.
- The action space is continuous (prices 1-25), which differs from binary or discrete contribution settings.
- The study does not manipulate the cost or effectiveness of punishment directly; punishment is inherent in the payoff structure.
- Communication is limited to one message per round and identities are not revealed.
- The random continuation rule means subjects do not know the exact number of rounds, which may affect strategy choice.
