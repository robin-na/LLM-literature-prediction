# Analysis Report

Authors: Gavrilets, S.
Journal: Evolutionary Human Sciences
Year: 2020

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- frequency of norm compliance
- frequency of punishment/disapproval
- dynamics and stability of equilibria in norm compliance and punishment
Outcome notes:
The paper is entirely theoretical and models the frequency of norm compliance and punishment, not group efficiency, welfare, or total payoff. Payoff-related outcomes are discussed as model parameters (costs, benefits), but not as reported outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the presence of punishment (active disapproval) increases the stability and prevalence of norm compliance, can create multiple stable equilibria, and increases the complexity of group behavioral dynamics. Heterogeneity in individual values and costs leads to diverse group outcomes. Punishment stabilizes cooperative equilibria and is mainly administered by those with high norm internalization. The models are general and can be applied to a range of social norm contexts, including public-goods-like settings, but do not directly model or report efficiency or group payoff as an outcome.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical support for the idea that punishment mechanisms can stabilize norm compliance and cooperative behavior, especially in heterogeneous populations. However, it does not provide quantitative predictions about efficiency or group payoff, nor does it report empirical data. For the downstream prediction task, the paper suggests that the presence and cost of punishment, as well as heterogeneity in values and beliefs, are important moderators of the effect of punishment on group behavior. The models are adjacent to public goods games but do not map directly onto the efficiency prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. The models are for populations of individuals, sometimes with explicit mixing or subgroups, but do not specify a fixed player count as in a standard PGG.
- number of rounds: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. The models are dynamic and iterative but do not specify a fixed number of rounds; convergence to equilibrium is discussed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Contribution/compliance is modeled as a binary choice (comply or not), analogous to all-or-nothing.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. The cost of punishing (parameter delta) and the cost of being punished (parameter c) are explicitly modeled and varied. Higher punishment cost reduces frequency of punishment and can affect norm compliance equilibria.
- punishment technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is modeled as the cost imposed on norm violators (parameter c). Higher c increases the stabilizing effect of punishment on norm compliance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper provides contextual guidance on this dimension. Individuals are assumed to have beliefs or information about the frequency of norm compliance in the population (parameter p), but the mechanism of information is not specified.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is entirely theoretical and does not report empirical or experimental data.
- Outcomes are behavioral (norm compliance, punishment frequency), not efficiency, group payoff, or welfare.
- The models are adjacent to public goods games but do not directly model or report marginal per-capita return, group size, or other standard PGG parameters.
- No direct mapping to efficiency or payoff-based outcomes; findings are not directly quantitative for the prediction task.
- No discussion of communication, reward, or identification of punishers.
