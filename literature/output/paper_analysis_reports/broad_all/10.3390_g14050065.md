# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: When Efficiency Requires Arbitrary Discrimination: Theoretical and Experimental Analysis of Equilibrium Selection
Authors: Gueth, W., & Otsubo, H.
Journal: Games
Year: 2023

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- group payoff
- efficiency-enhancing outcome (i.e., avoiding mutual harm)
- individual payoffs
Non-payoff outcomes:
- sanctioning behavior (discriminatory vs. equal)
- coordination between agents
- choice of action (Red/Blue) by agents
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is whether the sanctioning schedule (by the third party) induces the two agents to coordinate on an efficient outcome (i.e., only one agent chooses the harmful action, avoiding mutual harm and maximizing group payoffs). The paper also analyzes the sanctioning choices of the third party and the response of the agents, but the efficiency of the group outcome is a central focus.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that third-party discriminatory sanctioning (especially when preannounced and when agents differ in wealth) increases the likelihood that the two agents coordinate to avoid mutual harm, resulting in higher group efficiency. When the third party sanctions equally, agents are less likely to coordinate efficiently. The effect is robust to the order protocol (sequential vs. simultaneous), but is strongest when the sanctioning schedule is observable. Asymmetric wealth increases the tendency for the third party to sanction the richer agent more, confirming the 'deep-pocket' hypothesis. Overall, the ability to use discriminatory punishment, and to preannounce it, enables higher efficiency in this coordination dilemma.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that, in a coordination game adjacent to the public goods game, enabling a third party to use discriminatory punishment (especially when the punishment schedule is observable) increases group efficiency by facilitating coordination and avoiding mutual harm. The findings suggest that, in similar environments, the design dimensions of punishment observability and the ability to discriminate in punishment can be leveraged to improve efficiency. The results are most relevant for settings where a third party can precommit to a punishment schedule and where agents may differ in wealth or status. For prediction tasks, the presence of observable, flexible, and potentially discriminatory punishment is likely to increase efficiency relative to control conditions without such punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is always played with three players: one third party (X) and two agents (Y, Z).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consists of three rounds; only one round is randomly selected for payment.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication between participants was allowed at any time.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Y and Z choose between two discrete actions (Red or Blue), i.e., all-or-nothing choices.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Sanctions are imposed by X on Y and Z; the cost is in terms of points deducted from Y and Z, not from X. X does not incur a direct cost for sanctioning.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is fully effective: X allocates up to 10 points of sanction between Y and Z (via black/white balls), directly reducing their payoffs if both choose Blue.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are informed that there are three rounds in total.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each round, participants are informed about all decisions and earnings in their group.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In the sequential protocol, Y and Z observe X's sanctioning schedule before making their choices, which increases efficiency. In the simultaneous protocol, they do not observe it.

## Important Limitations
- The game is not a standard public goods game but a coordination/sanctioning game with a third-party punisher; results may not generalize to standard PGGs.
- Punishment is costless to the punisher (X), which differs from most PGG punishment designs where punishment is costly.
- The group size is fixed at three, with a specific role structure (one punisher, two agents); findings may not extend to larger or differently structured groups.
- The experiment only uses three rounds per session, with only one round paid, which may limit learning or repeated-game effects.
- No reward mechanism is present, so the interaction of punishment and reward cannot be assessed.
- Wealth asymmetry is implemented via show-up fees, which may not fully capture real-world wealth differences.
