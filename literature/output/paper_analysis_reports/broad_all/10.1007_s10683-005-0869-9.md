## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The main finding is that the punishment technology used in many public goods experiments (notably Fehr and Gaechter, 2000) embeds a variable fine-to-fee ratio, which is highly correlated with other variables such as group size and marginal per capita return. This variable fine-to-fee ratio can bias the observed frequency and targeting of punishment, making it difficult to disentangle the true effects of punishment on cooperation and, by extension, efficiency. The paper shows that the fine-to-fee ratio is a strong predictor of punishment behavior, but does not provide direct evidence on efficiency or group payoff outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency or group payoff in public goods games. However, it is highly relevant for interpreting results from studies that use variable fine-to-fee punishment technologies: such studies may overstate the positive effects of punishment on cooperation and efficiency due to confounding with the fine-to-fee ratio. For prediction tasks, this paper suggests that the design of the punishment technology (especially whether the fine-to-fee ratio is constant or variable) is a critical moderator, and that results from variable-ratio designs may not generalize to other settings. Control efficiency and game design dimensions should be interpreted with caution if the punishment technology is not neutral.
