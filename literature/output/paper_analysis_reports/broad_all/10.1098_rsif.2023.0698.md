## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that: (1) A prosocial institution (maximizing average population payoff) can increase cooperation and efficiency, but only if it is sufficiently forward-looking (low discount rate); if it is myopic, it fails to intervene and the outcome is the same as anarchy. (2) A pro-cooperator institution (maximizing cooperator payoff) always increases cooperation and efficiency above anarchy, even if it is myopic. (3) A selfish institution (maximizing its own revenue) can sometimes increase efficiency above anarchy if it is forward-looking, but if it is myopic, it exploits the population and reduces efficiency below anarchy. The model provides explicit analytical conditions for when each regime leads to higher or lower efficiency than the no-institution baseline.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical guidance for predicting the effect of institutional interventions (taxation and redistribution) on efficiency in public-goods-like environments. It shows that the effect of 'punishment' or institutional intervention is not universally positive or negative for efficiency; rather, it depends on the institution's objective and its ability to value future outcomes (discount rate). For prediction tasks, the model suggests that institutional efficiency (analogous to punishment/reward effectiveness), the institution's foresight, and the alignment of institutional objectives with group welfare are critical moderators. The results are most directly applicable to settings where the 'punishment' is implemented as a tax-and-redistribute mechanism, rather than direct costly punishment between individuals.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model assumes an infinite well-mixed population; not a finite player count. Results are derived in the infinite-population limit.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is continuous-time and infinite-horizon (no fixed number of rounds); dynamics are ongoing.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperate (pay cost c, give benefit b) or defect (do nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return is b/c for the donation game; b > c is assumed.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Redistribution of tax revenue to cooperators functions as a reward mechanism.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward (redistribution) is funded from tax revenue, with administrative inefficiency parameter delta < 1.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Effectiveness of redistribution is parameterized by delta (efficiency of tax collection/redistribution). Higher delta increases positive effect on efficiency.

## Important Limitations
- The model is fully theoretical and assumes an infinite well-mixed population; results may not generalize to finite or structured populations.
- Institutional intervention is modeled as taxation and redistribution, not as direct costly punishment or peer-to-peer sanctions.
- No experimental or empirical data are provided; all results are analytical or numerical solutions of the model.
- No communication, reputation, or information feedback mechanisms are included.
- The model assumes binary (all-or-nothing) cooperation choices, not continuous contributions.
- The model does not address the effects of stochasticity, finite population size, or real-world implementation constraints.
