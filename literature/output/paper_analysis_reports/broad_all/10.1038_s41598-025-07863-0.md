# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Empirical evidence for the spread of Cooperation through copying successful groups
Authors: Kido, Y., & Takezawa, M.
Journal: Scientific Reports
Year: 2025

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Payoff-related outcomes:
- mean payoff per round
- total points earned (converted to monetary reward)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution amount
- reciprocity rate
- general trust (cross-cultural survey)
- trust in out-groups (cross-cultural survey)
- trust in family (cross-cultural survey)
- willingness to fight for country (cross-cultural survey)
- social norm for obedience (cross-cultural survey)
Outcome notes:
The main experimental dependent variable is contribution amount in a repeated PGG. Payoff (mean points per round) is reported as a manipulation check and for out-group information, but not as a primary outcome. Efficiency or group payoff is not directly analyzed as a main outcome. The cross-cultural survey uses country-level prosociality measures, not efficiency or payoff.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiments demonstrate that providing information about a successful, cooperative out-group increases participants' contributions in a repeated PGG, but this effect is transient and does not persist or spread within the group. The absence of mechanisms such as punishment means that cooperation is not stably maintained. The authors argue that additional mechanisms (e.g., punishment, reputation) are likely necessary for the stable diffusion and maintenance of cooperation. The cross-cultural survey finds that greater press freedom is associated with higher impersonal prosociality (e.g., general trust), but not with personal prosociality (e.g., family trust). No direct evidence is provided on the effect of punishment or on efficiency outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency in PGG-like environments, as punishment is not present in the experimental design. However, it provides indirect support for the idea that, in the absence of punishment, even interventions that increase cooperation (such as exposure to successful out-groups) do not lead to sustained or spreading cooperation or higher group payoffs. The discussion and literature review suggest that punishment may be necessary for stable, high-efficiency outcomes in repeated PGGs. For prediction tasks, this paper supports the expectation that, without punishment, efficiency gains from social learning or imitation are likely to be transient. The experimental design and results can help calibrate the expected baseline (control) efficiency in the absence of punishment, but do not inform the treatment effect of enabling punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All experimental groups have 4 players per group in both experiments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each experiment consists of two blocks of 30 rounds each (60 rounds total).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed; all decisions are made individually and anonymously.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players choose any amount from 0 to 20 points per round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each point contributed yields 0.4 points to each group member).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told in advance that the game will last 60 rounds (two blocks of 30 rounds).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants see their group members' average contribution and payoff. After block 1, summary feedback is given (own group only in control; own + out-group in treatment).

## Important Limitations
- No punishment or sanctioning mechanism is present in the experimental design, so the paper cannot provide direct evidence on the effect of punishment on efficiency.
- Efficiency or group payoff is not analyzed as a primary outcome; the main focus is on contribution behavior.
- The effect of out-group information on cooperation is transient and does not lead to sustained or spreading cooperation.
- The cross-cultural survey uses country-level proxies for prosociality, not efficiency or payoff, and cannot establish causality.
- The fixed, known number of rounds may have created ceiling effects in cooperation, limiting the ability to detect treatment effects.
- All participants are Japanese university students, limiting generalizability.
