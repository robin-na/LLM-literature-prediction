# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: The relative price of fairness: Gender differences in a punishment game
Authors: Eckel, C. C., & Grossman, P. J.
Journal: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
Year: 1996

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=adjacent; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average payment to subjects
- probability of choosing lower own-payoff to punish/reward
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment choice (binary: punish or not)
- gender differences in punishment choice
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the subject's own monetary payoff, which is directly affected by their choice to punish (i.e., accept a lower own payoff to reward a fair partner). The paper also analyzes the frequency of punishment choices and gender differences in these choices.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that when subjects can choose to reward a fair partner at a personal cost (i.e., accept a lower own payoff), a substantial fraction do so, especially when the cost is low. Women are more likely than men to sacrifice earnings for fairness when the cost is low, but their willingness drops sharply as the cost increases. Men are less sensitive to the cost and maintain a relatively constant (lower) rate of sacrificing for fairness. The presence of the punishment/reward option leads to lower average payoffs compared to always choosing the higher own-payoff option, indicating a negative effect on efficiency. The study does not use a repeated public goods game or a standard PGG, but the design is adjacent in that it involves costly punishment/reward decisions affecting both own and others' payoffs.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides evidence that the availability of a punishment/reward option (even in a dictator-like setting) can reduce average efficiency, as some subjects are willing to accept lower payoffs to reward fairness or punish unfairness. The effect is moderated by the cost of punishment/reward and by gender. In environments where punishment/reward is costly and voluntary, efficiency may decrease relative to a control with no such option, especially if the cost is not prohibitively high. However, the design is not a repeated PGG, so direct quantitative prediction for standard PGGs should be cautious. The main implication is that costly punishment/reward options can reduce group efficiency, particularly when the cost is low and when participants are more likely to act on fairness preferences.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject makes a one-shot choice affecting themselves and one partner (dyadic interaction).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. The game is one-shot; each subject makes a single decision.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between subjects is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects choose between two discrete options (all-or-nothing choice between higher own payoff with unfair partner or lower own payoff with fair partner).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment/reward is costly: choosing to reward the fair partner means accepting a lower own payoff ($1 or $2 less). The cost is experimentally manipulated (low vs high relative price). Higher cost reduces frequency of punishment/reward, especially for women.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment/reward effectiveness is fixed: choosing the fair partner always results in both subject and partner receiving the lower payoff. The 'impact per unit cost' is not varied, but the total cost is manipulated.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Reward mechanism exists: subjects can reward a fair partner by choosing them, at a cost to themselves.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Reward is costly: choosing the fair partner means accepting a lower own payoff. Cost is manipulated (low vs high).
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward effectiveness is fixed: both subject and partner receive the lower payoff if the fair partner is chosen.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game is a one-shot decision.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is not a repeated public goods game; it is a one-shot, two-person dictator-like game with a punishment/reward framing.
- The design does not allow for strategic or dynamic effects that are present in repeated PGGs.
- Efficiency is measured as average payoff, but there is no group-level public good or collective action problem.
- Punishment/reward is implemented via partner choice, not direct assignment of costs or fines.
- Findings may not generalize to standard PGGs or environments with more complex group interactions.
