# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Centralized sanctioning and legitimate authority promote cooperation in humans
Authors: Baldassarri, D., & Grossman, G.
Journal: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America
Year: 2011

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution to public good
- change in contribution after punishment
- number of players sanctioned
- maximum amount sanctioned
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is contribution to the public good. The paper does not report group payoff, efficiency, or total welfare, but focuses on behavioral outcomes (contribution rates, response to punishment, etc.).

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Centralized sanctioning, where a single monitor can punish, increases contributions to the public good compared to a baseline with no punishment. When the monitor is elected (legitimate authority), contributions increase even more than when the monitor is randomly assigned. The effect is attributed to the legitimacy of the authority, not to differences in the monitors' intrinsic characteristics or sanctioning behavior. The study is conducted in the field with Ugandan farmer cooperatives, using a repeated PGG with 8-12 players per group. The main outcome is contribution, not efficiency or group payoff.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that centralized punishment (by a single monitor) increases cooperation (contributions) in public goods games, and that legitimacy of the authority (election vs. random assignment) further enhances this effect. However, because the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare, it cannot be used directly to predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings are most useful for predicting the effect of centralized punishment and authority legitimacy on contribution rates, and suggest that similar effects may be expected for efficiency if contributions translate directly to group payoff in the game design. The study is highly relevant for understanding the impact of centralized punishment and authority legitimacy, but less so for direct efficiency prediction.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session had 8-12 players; this is explicitly reported in the Methods.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game had 6 rounds (2 preliminary rounds, then 4 rounds with/without monitor).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication was allowed between players during the experiment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution was continuous: players could allocate any portion of their 10-unit endowment.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The public account was doubled and redistributed evenly; with 8-12 players, MPCR = 2/(number of players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Monitor spent 1 MU to reduce a player's payoff by 3 MUs.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: 1 MU spent by monitor reduces target's payoff by 3 MUs.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players knew the number of rounds in advance (6 rounds).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players could see the contributions of others, as the monitor had to decide whom to punish based on contributions.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. The identity of the monitor was known to the group.

## Important Limitations
- The study does not report efficiency, group payoff, or welfare; only contribution rates and behavioral responses are measured.
- The monitor's payoff was not linked to group cooperation, which may differ from some PGG punishment designs.
- The experiment was conducted in Ugandan farmer cooperatives, which may limit generalizability to other populations.
- The effect of centralized punishment on efficiency cannot be directly inferred from the reported outcomes.
- No peer punishment or decentralized punishment condition was included for direct comparison.
