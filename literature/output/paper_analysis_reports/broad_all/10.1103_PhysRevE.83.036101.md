## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that pool punishment in spatial public goods games can support the coexistence of cooperation and punishment, but only within limited parameter regions (low synergy factor, low punishment cost, and intermediate fines). For high fines, the system often evolves into a cyclic dominance phase where defectors, cooperators, and pool punishers coexist in a rock-paper-scissors dynamic. Pool punishment is less effective than peer punishment at eliminating defectors, and cooperators can invade pool punishers' territories due to the cost of punishment. The paper provides detailed phase diagrams for different values of the synergy factor, showing the boundaries between homogeneous, coexistence, and cyclic phases. However, the paper does not report group efficiency or total payoff, so its findings are most directly relevant to predicting the prevalence of cooperation and punishment strategies rather than efficiency outcomes.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides detailed evidence on how the cost and effectiveness of pool punishment, as well as the synergy factor (mpcr), affect the prevalence of cooperation and punishment in spatial public goods games. While it does not report efficiency or group payoff, the phase diagrams and strategy frequencies can inform predictions about when punishment is likely to increase or decrease efficiency, especially in structured populations with spatial interactions. The findings suggest that pool punishment is only effective in a narrow range of parameters and can lead to complex coexistence rather than full cooperation. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, the paper's results are only indirectly informative, as they do not quantify group payoff or efficiency directly.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare; all main outcomes are in terms of strategy frequencies and phase structure.
- Findings are based on agent-based simulations of spatially structured populations, which may not generalize to well-mixed or real-world settings.
- Only pool punishment (institutional punishment) is modeled; peer punishment and reward mechanisms are not included in the main analysis.
- No direct comparison of group payoff with and without punishment is provided, limiting direct applicability to efficiency prediction tasks.
- The model assumes infinite or very large repeated play (stationary state), which may differ from finite-round experimental games.
