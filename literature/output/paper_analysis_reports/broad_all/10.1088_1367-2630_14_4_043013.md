## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Adaptive punishment in spatial public goods games enables the spontaneous emergence of rare, localized punishers who respond to the spread of defection. This mechanism maintains smooth interfaces between cooperators and defectors, invigorates spatial reciprocity, and prevents the emergence of cyclic dominance that would otherwise allow defectors to persist. As a result, adaptive punishment can achieve full cooperation (pure C phase) in parameter regions where, without punishment, only defectors would survive. The cost of punishment, when adaptively deployed, is globally negligible, making the mechanism highly efficient. Compared to steady (non-adaptive) punishment, adaptive punishment is more effective at eliminating defectors and does so with lower cost.

## Prediction Guidance
For prediction tasks, this paper provides strong theoretical evidence that adaptive, context-sensitive punishment mechanisms in spatially structured public goods games can substantially increase the likelihood of full cooperation and, by implication, group efficiency, even when punishment is costly. The effect is especially pronounced when punishment is inexpensive, but remains positive even for costly punishment. The findings suggest that models or interventions that allow for adaptive, rather than fixed, punishment are likely to yield higher efficiency than those with no punishment or with steady punishment. However, the paper does not provide direct quantitative estimates of efficiency or group payoff, so predictions should be based on the qualitative pattern that adaptive punishment expands the parameter space for high efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each player interacts with k=4 neighbors on a square lattice; each group has k+1=5 players. The system size (number of players) is varied for simulation purposes, but the local group size is always 5.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Simulations run for up to 10^7 Monte Carlo steps to reach stationary states; the game is iterated until equilibrium, not a fixed number of rounds as in lab experiments.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is binary: cooperators contribute 1, defectors contribute 0.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Synergy factor r is varied; MPCR is r/(k+1), with k+1=5. r is a key parameter in phase diagrams.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment cost per unit is parameterized by alpha; varied in simulations (e.g., alpha=0.1, 0.5, 1, 2). Lower cost (alpha) increases the effectiveness of punishment.

Punishment impact per unit cost is determined by the ratio of fine (Delta) to cost (alpha*Delta); both are varied. Higher Delta (fine) increases effectiveness, especially when cost is low.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is a theoretical/modeling study and does not report empirical or experimental data.
- Efficiency or group payoff is not directly calculated or reported; conclusions about efficiency are inferred from the dominance of cooperation and the negligible cost of punishment.
- The model assumes spatial structure (square lattice) and local interactions, which may not generalize to well-mixed or other network structures.
- Punishment is adaptive and context-sensitive, which may differ from fixed or institutionally imposed punishment in real-world settings.
- No reward or positive sanctioning mechanisms are considered.
- No direct measurement of efficiency as a ratio of actual to maximal group payoff; only qualitative inferences are possible.
