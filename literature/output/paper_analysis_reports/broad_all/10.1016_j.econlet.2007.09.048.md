# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Does stake size matter for cooperation and punishment?
Authors: Kocher, M. G., Martinsson, P., & Visser, M.
Journal: Economics Letters
Year: 2008

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate
- punishment frequency
- punishment points assigned
Outcome notes:
The paper reports on average contributions to the public good and punishment behavior, but does not directly report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare. The closest reported outcomes are contribution rates and punishment assigned.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that increasing the monetary stakes in a one-shot public goods game, both with and without punishment, does not significantly affect the average contribution rate or the frequency and intensity of punishment. The presence of a punishment stage increases average contributions compared to the no-punishment condition, but this effect is not moderated by stake size. The study does not report group efficiency or total payoff, so the direct effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be determined from this paper.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence on the design of public goods games with and without punishment, including group size, MPCR, punishment cost and technology, and the absence of communication. However, it does not report efficiency or group payoff, so it cannot be used to directly predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. It does confirm that, in this design, punishment increases average contributions, and that this effect is robust to stake size. For prediction tasks, the paper supports the idea that punishment increases cooperation, but does not provide direct evidence on efficiency outcomes.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Group size is fixed at 3 in all sessions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject plays a one-shot game in each part (no repeated rounds).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Communication among participants was prohibited.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous between 0 and endowment.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR (gamma) is 0.5.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Cost to punisher is 1 per punishment point.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the punished player's payoff by 3.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game is one-shot in each part.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After the contribution stage, subjects receive information on contribution levels of their group members before the punishment stage.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Group members remain anonymous; punishment is not attributed to specific individuals.

## Important Limitations
- The paper does not report group efficiency, total payoff, or welfare outcomes, only contribution and punishment behavior.
- The experiment is one-shot, so findings may not generalize to repeated public goods games.
- The sample consists of high school students in South Africa, which may limit generalizability.
- The study only manipulates stake size and does not vary other key game design dimensions.
