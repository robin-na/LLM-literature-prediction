# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Punitive sentiment as an anti-free rider psychological device
Authors: Price, M. E., Cosmides, L., & Tooby, J.
Journal: Evolution and Human Behavior
Year: 2002

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=observational; experimental subtype=N/A

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=adjacent

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: non_payoff_behavior
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punitive sentiment toward nonparticipants
- pro-reward sentiment toward participants
- willingness to participate in collective action
- self-interest in group goal
- general punitiveness (attitudes toward punishing crime)
Outcome notes:
The study uses survey data to measure psychological sentiments (punitive and reward) in hypothetical collective action (warfare) scenarios. No actual payoffs, efficiency, or group earnings are measured or reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that individuals' willingness to participate in a collective action (e.g., military draft) is a strong predictor of their desire to punish nonparticipants (free riders), but not of their desire to reward participants. Perceived self-interest in the group goal predicts pro-reward sentiment but not punitive sentiment, once willingness to participate is controlled for. The results suggest that punitive sentiment is a specialized psychological adaptation for eliminating the fitness advantage of free riders, rather than for recruiting labor or maximizing group efficiency. The study does not measure or report efficiency, payoff, or actual behavioral outcomes in a public goods game or similar environment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper does not provide direct evidence for predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff in public goods games or similar experimental settings. Its findings are based on survey responses to hypothetical scenarios, not on observed behavior or payoffs. However, it does provide indirect support for the idea that punitive sentiment is psychologically linked to participation in collective action, and that punishment may function to deter free riding. For the prediction task, this paper is only weakly informative, as it does not report efficiency or payoff outcomes, nor does it manipulate or measure the game design dimensions relevant to public goods games.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- reward availability: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. Survey includes questions about pro-reward sentiment toward participants, but no actual reward mechanism is implemented.

## Important Limitations
- The study uses hypothetical, survey-based scenarios rather than actual public goods games or observed behavior.
- No efficiency, payoff, or group earnings outcomes are measured or reported.
- No manipulation or measurement of key game design dimensions (e.g., player count, MPCR, punishment cost, etc.).
- The context is large-scale (national) collective action, not small-group laboratory or field settings.
- Findings are about psychological sentiments, not actual punishment or cooperation behavior.
