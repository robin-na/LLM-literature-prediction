## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that the presence of conditional cooperators in a common-pool resource setting can lead to complex and sometimes abrupt transitions between cooperative and non-cooperative equilibria. Efficiency, measured as resource stock and group profit relative to the maximum economic yield, is highly sensitive to social dynamics. When temptation to defect increases or when group size increases, the system can shift from high-efficiency (cooperative) to low-efficiency (defective) states, sometimes with hysteresis or limit cycles. The model highlights that social norm enforcement via moral persuasion can sustain cooperation, but is vulnerable to collapse if conditional cooperators lose motivation. The absence of explicit punishment means that norm enforcement is 'soft' and collapses are possible even without changes in external parameters.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical evidence that, in public-goods-like common-pool resource games, the efficiency of group outcomes is highly sensitive to the social composition (intrinsic vs. conditional cooperators vs. defectors) and to the strength of norm enforcement mechanisms. While it does not model explicit punishment, it suggests that in the absence of strong enforcement (e.g., costly punishment), efficiency is fragile and can collapse due to endogenous social dynamics. For prediction tasks, this paper implies that enabling or strengthening norm enforcement (potentially via punishment) could stabilize high-efficiency outcomes, especially in larger groups or when temptation to defect is high. However, the model also warns that even with some enforcement, efficiency can be lost if social dynamics shift. The findings are most relevant for settings where punishment is absent or weak, and thus provide a baseline for understanding the potential value of adding punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental data.
- No explicit punishment or reward mechanisms are modeled; norm enforcement is via moral persuasion only.
- The model assumes agents can observe defectors, which may not hold in larger or more anonymous groups.
- The model uses a continuous-time framework, which may differ from discrete-round public goods games.
- Ecological and social parameters are stylized and may not capture all real-world complexities.
