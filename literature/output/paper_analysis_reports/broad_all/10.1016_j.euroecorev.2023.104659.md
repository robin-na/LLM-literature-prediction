# Analysis Report

## Source
Title: Cooperation and norm enforcement differ strongly across adult generations
Authors: Praxmarer, M., Rockenbach, B., & Sutter, M.
Journal: European Economic Review
Year: 2024

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=adjacent; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- per capita earnings
- overall welfare (sum of player and third-party earnings)
- joint payoff
Non-payoff outcomes:
- cooperation rate
- punishment frequency
- expectations about partner's cooperation
- expectations about being punished
Outcome notes:
The main behavioral outcomes are cooperation rates and punishment rates, but the paper also reports per capita earnings and overall welfare (sum of player and third-party earnings), which are directly related to efficiency.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that seniors are both more cooperative and more willing to punish defectors than juniors. However, the presence or generation of the third-party punisher does not significantly increase cooperation rates for either generation. As a result, the efficiency (measured by per capita earnings or welfare) is higher in groups of seniors due to their higher baseline cooperation, not due to the effect of punishment. Punishment is costly and does not lead to higher cooperation, so its net effect on efficiency is limited or even negative within each generation. The main moderator of efficiency is the generation of the players, not the punishment treatment.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper suggests that, in repeated two-player prisoner's dilemma games with third-party punishment, enabling punishment does not reliably increase efficiency, as cooperation rates are largely unaffected by the presence or type of punishment. Efficiency is higher among seniors due to their higher baseline cooperation, not because punishment increases cooperation. For prediction tasks, the presence of third-party punishment (with the parameters used here) should not be expected to increase efficiency relative to control, especially among juniors. The main design dimension affecting efficiency is the generation (age) of the players, not the punishment mechanism. The findings are most directly relevant to two-player PDs with binary third-party punishment, and less so to standard public goods games.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game is a two-player prisoner's dilemma (plus a third-party punisher, but only two players make contribution/defection decisions).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 periods (rounds) per game.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choice is binary: cooperate or defect (all-or-nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. If both cooperate: each gets 200 tokens; if both defect: each gets 125 tokens. The marginal per-capita return for cooperation is 75 tokens per player (from 125 to 200).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is binary: third party can deduct 50 points from a player at a cost of 20 points to themselves.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness: 50-point deduction per 20-point cost (2.5:1 impact/cost ratio).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game has 20 periods; this is common knowledge.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each period, players receive information about the other player's choice and the third party's punishment decisions.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the generation (junior/senior) of the third-party punisher, but not the individual identity.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a two-player prisoner's dilemma with third-party punishment, not a standard public goods game, so generalizability to PGGs is limited.
- Punishment is binary (yes/no) and not continuous, which may limit the external validity for environments with graded punishment.
- The sample is not representative: both juniors and seniors are better educated than the general population.
- The study is conducted in a highly-developed country (Austria), which may limit generalizability to other contexts.
- No reward mechanism is present, so findings do not address the interaction of punishment and reward.
- The main moderator of efficiency is the generation of the players, not the punishment treatment.
