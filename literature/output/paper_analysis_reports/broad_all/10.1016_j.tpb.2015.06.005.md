Outcome notes:
The main outcomes are theoretical payoffs (profits) for individuals and the group, and the stability of cooperative (fully faithful) outcomes under different penalty regimes. The model analyzes how penalties affect the equilibrium and stability of cooperation, with explicit focus on payoff maximization and group welfare.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Theoretical analysis demonstrates that introducing an effective penalty for unfaithfulness (selling outside the cooperative) leads to stable, efficient cooperative outcomes where all members sell to the cooperative. If the penalty is limited (not strong enough), full cooperation is unstable. When the penalty is strong (exceeds the extra profit from defection), the system converges to full cooperation, maximizing group payoff. Redistribution of penalties among faithful members further strengthens this effect, leading to a unique, stable Nash equilibrium with high efficiency. The model provides explicit conditions under which punishment stabilizes cooperation and maximizes group welfare.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong theoretical support that, in public-goods-like settings with continuous contribution and the possibility of punishment, increasing the effectiveness and/or strength of punishment increases group efficiency and stabilizes cooperation. The results are directly relevant for predicting that enabling or strengthening punishment will increase efficiency, especially when the punishment is strong enough to outweigh the gains from defection. The model also highlights that redistributing penalties among cooperators can further enhance efficiency and stability. However, the results are derived from a stylized, theoretical model rather than empirical data, and the setting is a marketing cooperative with continuous contribution rather than a canonical PGG, though the structure is closely related.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. The model is for n players (members of the cooperative), with n as a parameter. Results are derived for general n, and some stability conditions depend on n.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each member chooses the proportion of their product to sell to the cooperative (x_i in [0,1]).
- mpcr: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. The marginal per-capita return is implicit in the payoff structure: the difference between cooperative and market price, and the effect of one member's action on group payoff. The model uses a Cournot-type market with linear inverse demand, so the marginal return depends on parameters a, b, L, n.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment is modeled as a penalty rate y (0 to 1) and a penalty multiplier beta (>=1). The cost to the punisher (the cooperative) is not explicitly modeled as a loss, but the penalty is subtracted from the unfaithful member's profit and can be redistributed. The effectiveness of punishment (beta) is a key parameter: higher beta leads to more stable, efficient cooperation.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is parameterized by beta (penalty multiplier). Higher beta (>1) makes punishment more effective, stabilizing cooperation and increasing efficiency.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In Section 5, part of the penalty collected from unfaithful members can be redistributed as a reward to faithful members, strengthening cooperation.
- reward technology: The paper gives an indirect but usable signal on this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward effectiveness is determined by the share of penalty redistributed to faithful members. Higher redistribution increases stabilization of cooperation.

## Important Limitations
- The model is theoretical and not empirically tested; results are derived from mathematical analysis rather than data.
- The setting is a marketing cooperative with continuous contribution, not a canonical public goods game, though the structure is related.
- Punishment is institutional (by the cooperative), not peer-to-peer, which may limit generalizability to standard PGG settings.
- No explicit modeling of punishment or reward costs to the punisher/rewarder beyond redistribution of penalties.
- No consideration of communication, repeated discrete rounds, or information feedback as in many experimental PGGs.
