## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that redundancy in group cooperation can evolve without targeted punishment or coercion if individuals are ignorant of others' strategies and/or face harsh environments. In a threshold public goods game, when players do not know what others will do, they cooperate with an intermediate probability, leading to more cooperators than strictly necessary (redundancy). Harsher environments (higher group-level cost of failure) increase the probability of cooperation and thus redundancy. In contrast, if individuals are well-informed about others' strategies, redundancy does not evolve without punishment. The paper does not provide empirical or simulated data on efficiency or group payoff, but discusses these concepts in relation to the model.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides theoretical context for how information availability and environmental harshness affect cooperation and redundancy in threshold public goods games. It suggests that punishment is not strictly necessary for redundancy or intermediate cooperation rates if players are ignorant of others' actions. However, it does not provide direct evidence or predictions about efficiency or group payoff with or without punishment, nor does it manipulate or analyze punishment parameters. For the downstream prediction task, this paper is most useful for understanding the role of information and environmental context as moderators, but does not provide quantitative or empirical evidence on the efficiency effects of punishment.

## Important Limitations
- The paper is purely theoretical and does not provide empirical or experimental data.
- Efficiency, group payoff, or welfare outcomes are not directly modeled or reported; the focus is on cooperation probability and redundancy.
- Punishment is discussed only as a theoretical contrast and is not modeled or analyzed in the main framework.
- The model is one-shot and does not address repeated interactions or dynamic learning.
- Findings may not generalize to standard linear public goods games or to settings with explicit punishment mechanisms.
