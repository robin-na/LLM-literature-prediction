For prediction tasks, this paper supports the expectation that lowering punishment cost or enabling punishment in low-mpcr environments will yield large efficiency gains. The model also highlights the importance of memory length as a moderator. The results are most directly applicable to spatial, voluntary, mixed-strategy PGGs with local information and self-adjusting update rules.

The findings are directly relevant for settings where institutional incentives are endogenous and both punishment and reward are possible.

The effect is robust across a wide range of group sizes, rounds, and incentive strengths, provided the punishment is above a threshold that decreases with group size. The model also predicts that institutional rewards are much less effective in this context. The findings are directly relevant for predicting treatment efficiency from game design dimensions, especially when optional participation and institutional punishment are present. However, the results are theoretical and assume infinite populations and well-mixed groups, so empirical generalizability may be limited.
