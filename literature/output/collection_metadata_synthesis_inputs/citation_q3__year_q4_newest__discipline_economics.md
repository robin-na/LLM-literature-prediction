- source: Zhou, X. Y., Alysandratos, T., & Naef, M. (2023). Rice Farming and the Origins of Cooperative Behaviour. *Economic Journal*.
  type: empirical | empirical=experimental | experimental=lab_experiment
  relevance: pgg=exact | punishment=exact | payoff=adjacent
  outcomes: primary=non_payoff_behavior | overall_effect=N/A
  dimensions: player_count, num_rounds, chat, all_or_nothing, mpcr, punishment_cost
  findings: The study finds that (1) participants from wetland rice regions contribute more in both the no-punishment and punishment conditions of a repeated PGG, and (2) participants from rice regions assign more punishment points to free riders than those from non-rice regions. The difference in contribution is larger in the punishment condition. However, the paper does not report group efficiency, total earnings, or welfare, so the effect of punishment on efficiency cannot be directly determined. The results are robust to a wide range of controls and IV strategies, and are interpreted as evidence of persistent cultural norms of cooperation and punishment rooted in historical rice farming.
  prediction_guidance: This paper provides strong evidence that the presence of a punishment option increases contribution rates in a repeated PGG, and that the magnitude of this effect is moderated by cultural background (rice vs. non-rice regions). However, because the paper does not report efficiency, group payoff, or total earnings, it cannot be used to directly predict the effect of punishment on efficiency. The findings are useful for predicting contribution behavior under different cultural backgrounds, but not for direct efficiency prediction as required by the downstream task.

- source: Zhou, Y. X., Chen, S. W., Chen, Y. F., & Vollane, B. (2022). Does parental migration impede the development of the cooperative preferences in their left-behind children? Evidence from a large-scale field experiment in China. *China Economic Review*.
  type: empirical | empirical=experimental | experimental=field_experiment
  relevance: pgg=exact | punishment=exact | payoff=adjacent
  outcomes: primary=non_payoff_behavior | overall_effect=N/A
  dimensions: player_count, num_rounds, chat, all_or_nothing, mpcr, punishment_cost
  findings: The study finds that (1) children's cooperation (measured by contribution in a public goods game) increases with age; (2) paternal migration significantly reduces children's cooperation, while maternal or both-parent migration does not; (3) both exogenous and endogenous punishment mechanisms significantly increase cooperation, with exogenous punishment being more effective; (4) exogenous punishment can offset the negative effect of paternal migration on cooperation. The main outcome is contribution level, not efficiency or group payoff, so the findings are most directly about behavioral cooperation rather than payoff-based efficiency.
  prediction_guidance: This paper provides strong evidence that introducing punishment mechanisms (especially exogenous punishment) increases cooperation (contributions) in one-shot public goods games with children, and can offset negative effects of paternal absence. However, because the outcome is contribution level rather than efficiency or group payoff, the findings must be mapped to efficiency outcomes with caution. The game design dimensions are well specified (e.g., player count, MPCR, punishment cost/tech), and the effect of punishment is robustly positive on contributions. For prediction tasks focused on efficiency, this paper provides indirect but informative evidence that punishment is likely to increase efficiency in similar settings, especially when baseline cooperation is low due to social factors.
