# 1) Evidence Base

The paper set comprises 14 studies, including empirical lab experiments, theory papers/modeling, and observational field studies. The set is moderately broad in disciplinary and conceptual scope but contains only a subset of papers directly relevant to the prediction task of estimating efficiency effects of enabling peer punishment in public-goods-game (PGG)–like environments. Five empirical laboratory papers (e.g., Harrell & Wolff, 2023; Karakostas et al., 2023; Gallo et al., 2022) are most directly informative for payoff-based prediction, as they include efficiency or closely related group payoff outcomes in the context of PGGs or close variants with or without punishments/sanctions. Several theory papers (e.g., Dughera, 2022; Li et al., 2024) address mechanisms and welfare analytically, though not always in payoff terms. Other contributions (e.g., Adams et al., 2022; Haucap et al., 2024; Holubcik et al., 2023) are only adjacent or weakly relevant, lacking manipulations of punishment or reporting only non-payoff outcomes.

Overall, the empirical base is moderate for lab-based economic games with payoffs, but thin for observational, field, or large-scale natural environments. Theory is well-represented for mechanism exploration but less so for detailed, parameter-specific prediction of efficiency. Direct measurement of the impact of peer punishment on group efficiency is present in several studies, especially in standard PGG lab experiments. Design dimension coverage is uneven, with some dimensions directly studied (e.g., player count, punishment cost, MPCR), and others only contextually or not at all.

# 2) Task Relevance

Task relevance can be separated into three core areas:

- **pgg_or_variant:**  
  - **Exact**: Several papers study canonical PGGs or linear/standard variants (Harrell & Wolff, 2023; Espín et al., 2022; Karakostas et al., 2023).
  - **Close/Adjacent**: Some papers extend to related games (volunteer’s dilemma, networked PD, allocation games) or non-standard structures (Dughera, 2022; Gallo et al., 2022; Li et al., 2024).
  - **Weak/None**: Field studies and several theory works lack a PGG or its structural core.

- **punishment_or_sanctions:**
  - **Exact**: Studies directly manipulating peer punishment or sanctioning in PGG settings (Harrell & Wolff, 2023; Espín et al., 2022; Karakostas et al., 2023; Gallo et al., 2022).
  - **Close**: Implicit or tangential sanctions/rewards, or broader enforcement (Karakostas et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024).
  - **Adjacent/None**: Many studies do not manipulate or measure punishment, focusing instead on communication, norms, or environmental structure.

- **efficiency_or_related_payoff_outcome:**
  - **Exact**: Several empirical and theory papers report efficiency or group payoff (Harrell & Wolff, 2023; Karakostas et al., 2023; Gallo et al., 2022; Dughera, 2022).
  - **Adjacent/Weak/None**: Others focus on cooperation rates, strategy frequencies, or behavioral outcomes without quantifying group payoff/efficiency (Espín et al., 2022; Li et al., 2024; Adams et al., 2022).

**Summary:**
For the task of predicting efficiency gains from enabling punishment in PGGs, the paper set offers a moderate base of directly relevant evidence for standard laboratory settings. Directly matching evidence is available for several core prediction dimensions, with important adjacent contributions from theory. Coverage is weaker for more complex, field, or highly contextual dimensions, and payoff-based outcomes are sometimes only approximated or not measured.

# 3) Outcomes Measured In The Literature

- **Payoff-Related Outcomes:**  
  - *Efficiency (group profit/earnings relative to full cooperation):* Explicitly measured or reported in a minority of lab-based and theoretical studies (Harrell & Wolff, 2023; Karakostas et al., 2023; Dughera, 2022; Gallo et al., 2022).
  - *Related concepts:* Total earnings, welfare, surplus, group payoff (often used synonymously with efficiency).
- **Non-Payoff Behavioral Outcomes:**  
  - *Contribution/cooperation rate:* Widely measured as primary outcome (Espín et al., 2022; Adams et al., 2022; Li et al., 2024).
  - *Punishment frequency/assignment, norm compliance, and psychological correlates:* Reported in numerous studies focusing on mechanisms or moderators (e.g., antisocial punishment, gender/culture effects).
- **Not measured:**  
  - Some papers neither measure payoff nor behavioral outcomes directly relevant to group economic performance (Holubcik et al., 2023; Garfield et al., 2023).

**Distinction:** Many studies report increases in cooperation rates or norm compliance but do not track whether these increases lead to higher efficiency, net payoff, or welfare. Empirical studies focused on PGGs with explicit payoff analysis provide the strongest evidence for downstream prediction.

# 4) Main Findings Relevant To Prediction

**a. Peer Punishment Increases Efficiency—Conditionally:**  
- In canonical/linear PGGs, allowing peer punishment leads to higher cooperation and, critically, to increased group efficiency (earnings) over time—but primarily in large groups and dense networks. The effect is less consistent or even negative in smaller groups or sparse networks (Harrell & Wolff, 2023).
- Initial rounds may see payoff-reducing punishment costs, but the increase in cooperation rapidly outweighs these, leading to net efficiency gains later on.

**b. Production Technology Matters:**  
- The net effect of enabling sanctions or reward-like mechanisms (e.g., a discretionary allocator) depends on the mapping of contributions to group payoff. For linear PGGs, effects are positive; for best-shot or weakest-link variants, effects range from negative to neutral (Karakostas et al., 2023).

**c. Sanctioning Mechanism Design and Monitoring:**  
- The effectiveness of punishment for increasing efficiency is highly sensitive to the accuracy of monitoring/reputation and the costs of punishment. High noise or uncertainty weakens the efficiency-boosting effect of punishment, even if punishment is available (Gallo et al., 2022).
- Lower punishment costs can sometimes lead to antisocial punishment or reduce norm adherence, possibly undermining efficiency (Li et al., 2024).

**d. Reward/Motivation versus Punishment:**  
- Theory suggests that while punishment can raise efficiency compared to no control (especially when monitoring is robust and skill is low), reward-based motivation can lead to even higher efficiency in optimal circumstances (Dughera, 2022).

**e. Communication/Chat as Moderator:**  
- The presence and quality of chat/communication foster cooperation, but their direct effect on efficiency (especially under punishment) is less well quantified (Adams et al., 2022).

**f. Antisocial Punishment and Group Composition:**  
- Cultural and gender moderators can affect who is punished and by whom, but these generally influence contribution patterns more than measured group efficiency. Direct efficiency analysis is missing (Espín et al., 2022).

# 5) Prediction Guidance

The literature suggests the following for predicting efficiency in response to enabling peer punishment given specific game designs and observed control efficiencies:

- **Linear PGGs with moderate/large groups and clear information:** Anticipate an increase in group efficiency with punishment enabled, especially in later rounds, as initial costs are offset by cooperation gains; the effect size increases with group size and network density (Harrell & Wolff, 2023).
- **Production function matters:** For best-shot or weakest-link designs, the impact may be neutral or negative, suggesting control efficiency is a less reliable predictor post-treatment if the underlying technology changes, or if the punishment/reward mapping is poorly aligned (Karakostas et al., 2023).
- **Sanction system parameters:** High monitoring/observation accuracy and moderate punishment cost predict stronger positive efficiency effects; noisy monitoring or low-cost punishment (enabling antisocial punishment) may reduce or negate efficiency gains (Gallo et al., 2022; Li et al., 2024).
- **Reward options/alternative motivation:** If the game design allows both punishment and reward, or worker skill is high, theory predicts that efficiency gains are larger in the reward/motivation regime than with punishment alone (Dughera, 2022).
- **Other moderators (indirect):** Chat and group composition (cultural, gender) can mediate the effectiveness of punishment but their quantitative effect on efficiency requires separate measurement (Adams et al., 2022; Espín et al., 2022).

For out-of-sample prediction, efficiency should be expected to increase moderately to substantially with punishment in standard linear PGGs, conditional on network structure, group size, monitoring reliability, and production technology. The baseline efficiency of the control (punishment-disabled) group is a strong reference point, but variation due to sociology, design features, and implementation details should be expected.

# 6) Design Dimensions Highlighted Across Papers

**Directly Informed (standards):**
- `player_count`: Informed by laboratory studies and theory; group size strongly conditions the impact of punishment on efficiency (Harrell & Wolff, 2023; Dughera, 2022).
- `num_rounds`: Repeated games are standard; effect of punishment on efficiency strengthens over rounds (Harrell & Wolff, 2023).
- `mpcr`: PGG margin per capita return is directly manipulated and analyzed (Harrell & Wolff, 2023; Karakostas et al., 2023).
- `all_or_nothing`: Variant designs are included (some explicitly, others contextually).
- `punishment_cost` and `punishment_tech`: Central for both empirical and modeling papers (Harrell & Wolff, 2023; Karakostas et al., 2023; Dughera, 2022; Gallo et al., 2022).
- `reward_exists`: Directly contrasted with punishment in theory (Dughera, 2022).

**Indirectly Informed:**
- `chat`: Addressed for its effect on cooperation, less so on efficiency via punishment (Adams et al., 2022; Harrell & Wolff, 2023).
- `show_n_rounds`, `show_other_summaries`: Less commonly manipulated, but sometimes present in design.
- `show_punishment_id`: Very limited direct evidence, a parameter in some models.

**Only Contextually Discussed or Missing:**
- `default_contrib`: Framing of default contribution is rarely a manipulated factor.
- `punishment_magnitude`: Sometimes part of the overall 'punishment_tech', but not always dissected.
- `reward_cost`, `reward_tech`: Seen in select theoretical models (Dughera, 2022), but sparse empirical variation.
- **Network structure/density:** Sometimes handled outside the standard 14 (Harrell & Wolff, 2023; Gallo et al., 2022).

# 7) Important Limitations

- **Sparse payoff-based evidence:** Several studies provide behavioral or cooperation-rate outcomes but do not track group efficiency/payoffs, limiting their predictive power for the efficiency task (Espín et al., 2022; Li et al., 2024; Adams et al., 2022).
- **Nonlinear and context-dependent findings:** The effect of enabling punishment is mediated by production technology, monitoring accuracy, group composition, and game framing—generalizations may not extrapolate to unfamiliar settings (Karakostas et al., 2023; Gallo et al., 2022).
- **Limited coverage of design dimensions:** Not all 14 prediction variables are informed in every study, with some (e.g., default contribution, show_punishment_id) barely addressed.
- **Theory–empirics translation gap:** Some theory models articulate detailed mechanisms or equilibria but lack direct empirical validation of efficiency outcomes (Dughera, 2022; Li et al., 2024).
- **External/generalizability constraints:** Laboratory findings may not scale directly to large, complex, real-world collectives; evidence on peer punishment in naturally occurring, field, or organizational PGGs is thin or missing.
- **Ambiguity in ambiguous or conflicting contexts:** For nonlinear production or best-shot/weakest-link games, efficiency effects are ambiguous or may even be negative.
- **Limited discussion of reward:** While some models compare reward and punishment, empirical comparative trials of reward-enabled regimes are limited.

In sum, the reviewed literature offers moderate support for predicting the impact of peer punishment on efficiency in standard linear PGGs, conditional on several major game design dimensions, but is less informative or ambiguous outside these contexts. Care must be taken when extending predictions to unfamiliar structures, technologies, or population compositions.
