# 1) Evidence Base

The paper set is limited, consisting of only two studies: one experimental lab study closely related to public goods games (PGG) but without punishment (Zhang et al., 2019), and one field study on organizational cooperation (Holubcik et al., 2023) with only weak conceptual ties to the PGG paradigm. Both papers are empirical in nature, with no direct theoretical treatments. The set is narrow regarding the prediction task, as neither study investigates punishment mechanisms—central to the downstream prediction objective. The first paper addresses payoff-related (efficiency) outcomes experimentally in a step-level PGG-like context, while the second focuses on non-payoff, strategic cooperation outcomes in business alliances, outside a game-theoretic framework.

# 2) Task Relevance

For the three core target-relevance dimensions, the relevance is as follows:

- **pgg_or_variant:**  
    - Zhang et al. (2019): *close*—the setting is a step-level public-goods-dilemma game, structurally similar to PGGs.
    - Holubcik et al. (2023): *weak*—organizational cooperation, not experimental or game-theoretic PGG.

- **punishment_or_sanctions:**  
    - Both papers: *none*—neither includes punishment, sanctions, or similar mechanisms; they do not analyze or manipulate such features.

- **efficiency_or_related_payoff_outcome:**  
    - Zhang et al. (2019): *exact*—measures group/individual earnings and efficiency.
    - Holubcik et al. (2023): *adjacent*—discusses economic value and competitiveness, but not as efficiency or group payoff in a PGG/game sense.

Thus, the literature in this set is unable to speak directly to the effect of punishment on efficiency in PGGs; at best, it provides control-game baselines or contextual framing.

# 3) Outcomes Measured In The Literature

- **Payoff-related outcomes:**
    - Zhang et al. (2019): Directly measures group and individual earnings/efficiency in lab-based step-level public-goods-dilemma games.
- **Non-payoff (behavioral) outcomes:**
    - Zhang et al. (2019): Also tracks cooperation rates and psychological processes (moral elevation, SVO).
    - Holubcik et al. (2023): Focused on qualitative and survey-based measures—competitiveness, innovation, trust—without quantitative group payoff or efficiency data.

Notably, neither paper measures payoff-based outcomes in the presence of punishment or sanctions.

# 4) Main Findings Relevant To Prediction

- **Empirical findings:**  
    - In the absence of punishment or sanction mechanisms, introducing a consistent contributor (role model) increases both cooperation and efficiency (payoff) in a step-level public-goods game (Zhang et al., 2019).
    - The motivational pathway differs across participant profiles: pro-self players are affected via moral elevation, while pro-socials respond directly to behavior.  
    - These results are consistently positive across both group and individual payoff measures.

- **Limits for prediction:**  
    - No evidence is provided regarding the effect of introducing punishment: the available findings relate to a non-punishment baseline.
    - The organizational study (Holubcik et al., 2023) suggests that well-managed cooperation can produce synergistic effects, but provides only qualitative or adjacent links to efficiency, and does not use any PGG or punishment manipulation.

Thus, the only directly relevant empirical guidance is for establishing expected efficiency levels in control games without punishment, not for predicting efficiency changes due to punishment.

# 5) Prediction Guidance

Given the absence of any treatment conditions (in either paper) involving punishment, the literature here does not support direct prediction of efficiency in the *presence* of peer punishment.  
- **For prediction tasks:** These papers can only inform the likely efficiency of games *without* punishment, providing baseline (control) expectations for step-level public-goods games when motivational interventions (e.g., consistent contributors) are present (Zhang et al., 2019).
- **For estimating the effect of adding punishment:** No evidence is provided. There are no empirical or theoretical results in this set isolating the impact of punishment or sanctions on efficiency. The magnitude, direction, or context dependencies of punishment effects on group payoff remain unaddressed.
- **Recommendation:** Use the Zhang et al. (2019) results for control conditions in predictive models, but do not update, extrapolate, or hypothesize effects for punishment-enabled designs based on this literature. Holubcik et al. (2023) should not be used to inform game efficiency of any kind.

# 6) Design Dimensions Highlighted Across Papers

Among the 14 prescribed prediction dimensions:

- **Directly informed:**  
    - Zhang et al. (2019): *player_count*, *num_rounds*, *chat*, *all_or_nothing*, *mpcr*, *show_n_rounds*.  
      These dimensions are explicitly reported and manipulated in the step-level PGG lab experiments.

- **Indirectly/contextually discussed:**  
    - *default_contrib* (contribution framing) is not discussed.
    - *show_other_summaries*, *show_punishment_id*, *punishment_cost*, *punishment_tech*, *punishment_exists*, *reward_exists*, *reward_cost*, *reward_tech*: Not included or discussed in either study.

- **Missing for punishment and reward mechanisms:**  
    - The complete set of punishment and reward-related dimensions (*punishment_cost*, *punishment_tech*, *reward_exists*, etc.) is unaddressed; neither study manipulates nor analyzes these features.

# 7) Important Limitations

- **Absence of punishment manipulation:**  
    - Critically, neither paper analyzes or manipulates peer punishment or sanctions, making them irrelevant for the core prediction task of estimating efficiency under punishment.
- **Generalizability:**  
    - Zhang et al. (2019) only offers control-baseline outcomes for a step-level, non-punitive public goods game, and its findings are not generalizable to environments with active sanctioning.
    - Holubcik et al. (2023) does not contribute to payoff or efficiency modeling in any comparable way.
- **Design dimension coverage:**  
    - The vast majority of game design features tied to peer punishment and reward mechanisms remain unexamined.
- **Lack of theoretical or mechanistic modeling:**  
    - No formal or informal theory links the observed outcomes to mechanisms or provides extrapolation to punishment-enabled designs.
- **Outcome focus:**  
    - Where behavioral outcomes (e.g., increased cooperation) are discussed, they are not tied to payoff effects in the presence of punishment—making them insufficient for payoff predictions relevant to design changes.
- **Paper set size and scope:**  
    - Only two papers; one offers a partial baseline, the other is contextually distant. The evidence base is therefore narrow and incomplete for the target prediction task.

**In summary:** This literature set provides control-game efficiency data and some context for non-punitive designs but is entirely uninformative regarding the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods game environments. All predictive inference about punishment-enabled efficiency would require external evidence or different literature.
