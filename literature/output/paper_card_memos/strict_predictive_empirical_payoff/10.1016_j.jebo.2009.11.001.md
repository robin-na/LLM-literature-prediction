Non-payoff outcomes:

- ostracism/voting frequency

Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are average group earnings and average group contributions (both net and gross), which are directly tied to efficiency. Non-payoff outcomes such as voting and ostracism rates are also analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that allowing group members to ostracize (exclude) others by majority vote in a repeated linear public goods game leads to a substantial and statistically significant increase in group contributions and average earnings (efficiency) compared to the standard game without ostracism. This effect is robust except in the first and last periods, where contributions are similar across treatments due to initial uncertainty and end-game effects. Ostracism is targeted at low contributors, and after initial exclusions, remaining members tend to converge to high contribution levels. The positive effect on efficiency is observed both when ostracism is introduced first or second in the session, and is higher than in the control. The design ensures that the only difference between treatments is the possibility of exclusion, and there is no direct monetary punishment or cost to voting. The results suggest that ostracism is an effective endogenous sanctioning mechanism for sustaining cooperation and increasing group welfare in public goods environments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling ostracism (exclusion by majority vote) in a repeated linear public goods game with fixed partners, full information on others' contributions, and no cost to voting, leads to a substantial increase in group efficiency (average earnings as a fraction of the social optimum) compared to the same game without ostracism. The effect is robust across session orderings and is not offset by the reduction in group size due to exclusion. For prediction tasks, the presence of an ostracism mechanism (even with no direct monetary punishment) should be expected to increase efficiency relative to control, especially in games with similar design parameters (6 players, 10 rounds, continuous contributions, MPCR=0.4, no chat, no reward, no punishment cost, anonymous feedback on contributions). The effect is strongest in the middle rounds and may diminish at the end due to end-game effects.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 6 players, fixed partner design.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each treatment lasts 10 periods (rounds), with two treatments per session (order counterbalanced).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player chooses how much (0-10 tokens) to contribute.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each token contributed increases total group payoff by 0.4 per player).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. There is no cost to voting for ostracism; the only cost is the loss of group size if a member is excluded.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is implemented as exclusion: if a member receives ≥50% of votes, they are fully excluded from all future rounds (no public good income, no voting).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only ostracism (punishment) is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of periods (10 per treatment).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are shown the contribution levels of all other group members each round, but not their identities (randomized order).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed of the number of votes they and others received, but not who voted for whom; punishment (ostracism) is anonymous.

## Important Limitations
- The punishment mechanism is ostracism (exclusion), not direct monetary punishment, so results may not generalize to other forms of punishment.
- The group size is fixed at 6 and the number of rounds at 10; effects may differ in larger groups or longer games.
- No communication is allowed, which may limit external validity for settings with richer social interaction.
- Ostracism is irreversible and permanent for the remainder of the session, which may not match all real-world exclusion scenarios.
- The experiment is conducted with university students in a lab setting, which may limit generalizability.
