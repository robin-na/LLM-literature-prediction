The main dependent variables are efficiency, average earnings, and average contribution. Non-payoff outcomes such as contribution and punishment are also reported, but efficiency is explicitly analyzed and compared across treatments.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a 2x2 lab experiment with baseline, punishment, regrouping, and combined treatments, both punishment and regrouping increase efficiency and contributions relative to baseline. Regrouping (voluntary association) yields higher efficiency than punishment, as punishment incurs direct costs to both punisher and punished. The combined treatment (regrouping + punishment) does not significantly outperform regrouping alone. The experiment also finds strong evidence of conditional cooperation and group sorting by contribution level. Efficiency rates are: Baseline 77%, Regrouping 88%, Punishment 81%, Combined 86%.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that, in a standard linear public goods game with 4 players, 20 rounds, no chat, continuous contributions, and MPCR of 0.4, adding a punishment mechanism (cost 0.25 per 1 unit punished, impact 1 per 0.25 spent) increases efficiency from 77% (baseline) to 81%. However, the efficiency gain is smaller than that from enabling voluntary regrouping (88%). The results suggest that, under these parameters, punishment increases efficiency but the effect is limited by the costliness of punishment. The findings are directly relevant for predicting treatment efficiency from game design dimensions and control efficiency in similar lab PGGs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players in all treatments; fixed group size maintained across regroupings.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 periods (rounds) in all treatments.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication allowed; all interaction via computer terminals.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous in integer amounts from 0 to 10.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each unit contributed yields 0.4 to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment costs 0.25 per 1 unit reduction in target's earnings.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Each 0.25 spent reduces target's earnings by 1 unit (punishment impact per cost = 4).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game is 20 periods; finite horizon is common knowledge.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, subjects see each group member's contribution and their own earnings. In regrouping, subjects see average past contributions of all other subjects for ranking.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is conducted with undergraduate students in a lab setting, which may limit generalizability to field or real-world environments.
- The punishment mechanism is linear and anonymous; results may differ with nonlinear or identifiable punishment.
- No reward mechanism is tested, so findings do not address the effects of positive incentives.
- The efficiency benefit of punishment is limited by its cost structure; different punishment costs or effectiveness could yield different results.
- Group size, MPCR, and other parameters are fixed; results may not generalize to other parameterizations.
