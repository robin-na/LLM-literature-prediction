Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group and individual earnings (payoff), as well as contribution rates and exclusion/voting behavior. Efficiency is discussed in terms of group earnings relative to the maximum possible.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated public goods game with and without the possibility of exclusion, and with/without a central network position, the ability to exclude group members increases average group earnings (efficiency), though the effect is not always statistically significant. Central players contribute less than periphery players, but this does not reduce group efficiency. The presence of exclusion leads to higher contributions and earnings overall, and centrality increases inequality but not efficiency loss.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that enabling exclusion (a form of sanction) in a standard linear public goods game increases group efficiency, even when network structure introduces heterogeneity (centrality). The effect of exclusion is positive on efficiency, and the presence of a central player does not reduce efficiency. For prediction, this suggests that adding exclusion/voting-based sanctions to a standard PGG with known control efficiency will likely increase efficiency, and that network centrality does not undermine this effect. The exclusion mechanism here is costless to the voter, but exclusion itself is costly to the excluded and can fragment the group. The findings are most directly applicable to settings with 5 players, 5 rounds per block, MPCR=0.6, no chat, continuous contributions, and full information about others' contributions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 5 players per round in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each block has 5 rounds; each session consists of 5 blocks.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players choose any amount from 0 to 50.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.6 in all treatments.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Voting to exclude is costless to the voter; exclusion is costly to the excluded (they lose access to the group).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Exclusion is all-or-nothing: excluded players lose all access to the group and its payoffs for the rest of the block.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of rounds per block (5 rounds).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. At the end of each round, players are informed of the individual contributions of each other player in their group.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Votes to exclude are anonymous, but the number of votes each subject receives is announced; excluded players are identified by position (e.g., North, Center).

## Important Limitations
- Exclusion is costless to the voter, which differs from costly punishment mechanisms in some other PGG studies.
- The exclusion mechanism is all-or-nothing and may not generalize to settings with graded or monetary punishment.
- The experiment uses fixed groups of 5 and 5 rounds per block, which may limit generalizability to other group sizes or time horizons.
- No communication is allowed, so results may not generalize to settings with chat or discussion.
- Centrality is randomly assigned, which may differ from real-world settings where central positions are earned or chosen.
- No reward mechanism is present, so results do not address the interaction of punishment and reward.
