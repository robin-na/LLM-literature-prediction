## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a lab experiment in Spain, groups of four played two types of public goods games: a standard linear VCM and a weakest-link minimum game (WLM), each for 20 rounds without punishment and then 20 rounds with punishment. In the VCM, punishment led to only moderate increases in public good provision and did not increase group efficiency, due to substantial antisocial punishment (punishing high contributors). In the WLM, punishment led to a dramatic increase in public good provision and group efficiency, with antisocial punishment being rare and most groups earning more with punishment than without. The effectiveness of punishment in raising efficiency is thus highly dependent on the production technology of the public good, with complementarity (WLM) supporting positive effects of punishment even in a culture with high antisocial punishment in linear games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games is highly sensitive to the production function: in linear VCMs, punishment may not improve efficiency in cultures with high antisocial punishment, but in weakest-link (complementary) games, punishment can substantially increase efficiency. For prediction, the game structure (linear vs. weakest-link) is a critical moderator. Control efficiency (without punishment) is similar across games, but the treatment effect of punishment is much more positive in the WLM. The paper also provides parameter values for player count, rounds, MPCR, punishment cost/tech, and information structure, which can be used to inform predictions in similar designs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players in all sessions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each game/block lasted 20 rounds; 20 rounds without punishment, then 20 rounds with punishment.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices were continuous from 0 to 50 ECUs (not all-or-nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.5 in both games (VCM: b=0.5, group size 4).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point costs the punisher 2 ECUs and reduces the punished's earnings by 6 ECUs.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 6 ECUs lost per 2 ECUs spent (ratio 1:3).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants knew each block would last exactly 20 periods.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants were told the individual contributions profile and their own earnings.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment was anonymous; participants did not know who punished them.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment was conducted in a single cultural context (Spain/Southern Europe), so generalizability to other cultures is limited.
- Only two types of public goods production functions were tested (linear and weakest-link); results may not generalize to other forms.
- No reward mechanism was included, so findings are limited to punishment-only environments.
- The study uses a fixed group size (4) and fixed punishment parameters; effects of varying these are not tested.
- Participants were university students, which may limit external validity.
