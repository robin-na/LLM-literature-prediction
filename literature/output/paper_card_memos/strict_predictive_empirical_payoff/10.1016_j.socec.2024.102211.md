## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment compares several treatments, including a baseline public goods game (PGG), a version with punishment (PGGwP), and a 'climate club' treatment that combines conditional cooperation and mandatory punishment. The key finding is that adding punishment opportunities (and counter-punishment) does not significantly increase cooperation rates and leads to a statistically significant decrease in net earnings (efficiency) compared to the baseline. The climate club treatment increases cooperation rates but still results in lower net earnings than the baseline, due to the costs associated with punishment. The results are robust across different group compositions and treatments. The paper provides direct evidence that, in this design, punishment reduces efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a three-player, 30-round, binary-contribution public goods game with costly punishment and counter-punishment, enabling punishment reduces group efficiency (net earnings) compared to the control without punishment. The effect is robust and statistically significant. For prediction tasks, this suggests that, under similar design parameters (binary choice, no chat, known rounds, punishment cost per unit = 1, punishment effectiveness varies by target, no rewards), enabling punishment is likely to decrease efficiency relative to the control. The paper also shows that the presence of counter-punishment does not mitigate this effect. The findings are directly relevant for predicting the impact of punishment on efficiency in similar lab PGG environments.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a stylized, binary-contribution public goods game with fixed group size and no communication, which may limit generalizability to more complex or real-world settings.
- Punishment and counter-punishment are both possible, but only non-cooperators can be punished, and punishment is not individually targeted.
- The marginal per-capita return is heterogeneous across players, which may affect the external validity for symmetric PGGs.
- The lab setting enforces strong institutional rules for punishment and club formation, which may not reflect real-world enforcement challenges.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanisms are studied, so results may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
