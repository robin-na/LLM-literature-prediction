# Paper Memo: 10.1002_pchj.259

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=close

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- PGG-player earnings
- group earnings
- changes in earnings after third-party intervention
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution level (cooperation)
- dispersion of cooperation (deviation from group mean)
- frequency and expenditure of third-party sanctions
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is contribution (cooperation) level, but the paper also analyzes PGG-player earnings and how third-party sanctions affect payoffs and their distribution. Efficiency per se is not directly calculated, but group earnings and payoff changes are analyzed in detail.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Third-party punishment and the combination of third-party punishment and reward both significantly increase the average level of cooperation in a public goods game compared to a baseline with no sanctions. Third-party reward alone has little effect on cooperation. The combination of punishment and reward (TP/R) leads to the highest average cooperation, though the difference with punishment alone is not statistically significant. Both punishment and TP/R treatments maintain high cooperation throughout the game, while cooperation declines in the baseline and reward-only treatments. In terms of payoffs, third-party interventions (especially punishment and TP/R) change the earnings of PGG-players, generally improving the alignment between contribution and payoff. The paper provides evidence that third-party sanctions, especially those involving punishment, can increase group earnings and improve the outcome of cooperation.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling third-party punishment in a standard PGG increases group earnings and cooperation compared to a baseline with no sanctions. The effect is even more pronounced when both punishment and reward are available, suggesting a complementary effect. The game design dimensions (e.g., player count, number of rounds, MPCR, punishment cost/tech) are clearly specified, making the results directly relevant for predicting the efficiency impact of enabling punishment in similar lab PGGs. The findings support the prediction that, holding control efficiency constant, enabling punishment (especially with a 3:1 punishment tech and moderate cost) will increase efficiency. Reward alone is much less effective. The results are most applicable to lab PGGs with 4 players, 10 rounds, no chat, and full information about others' contributions and payoffs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Baseline treatment uses 4 players per group; sanction treatments use 4 PGG-players plus 4 third-party players (8 total per group, but only 4 are PGG-players).
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use 10 rounds (periods).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player can contribute any amount from 0 to 20 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (total contributions multiplied by 1.6, divided among 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 1 token per deduction point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction point costs the punisher 1 token and reduces the punished player's payoff by 3 tokens (3:1 punishment technology).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is available in the TR and TP/R treatments.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Rewarder pays 1 token per increment point assigned.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each increment point costs the rewarder 1 token and increases the rewarded player's payoff by 3 tokens (3:1 reward technology).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game is 10 rounds (finitely repeated).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, players are shown the contributions of all group members and their own earnings.

## Important Limitations
- Efficiency is not directly calculated; group earnings and payoff changes are analyzed, but the ratio to the fully cooperative outcome is not explicitly reported.
- The experiment uses third-party sanctions, not second-party (peer) punishment, which may limit generalizability to standard PGG punishment settings.
- Sample size is moderate (10 groups per treatment), and all participants are students from a single university in China.
- No communication is allowed, which may differ from some real-world or other experimental settings.
- The study does not explore long-term or repeated exposure to sanction regimes beyond 10 rounds.
- Findings may not generalize to larger groups, different MPCRs, or different punishment/reward technologies.
