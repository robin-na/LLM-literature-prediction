## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment compares two treatments: BASE (only social punishment) and LAW (social plus mild legal punishment). In LAW, legal sanctions are nondeterrent in isolation but, when combined with social punishment, they increase cooperation (mean contribution rises from 11.8 to 15.1 out of 20) and total efficiency (mean payoff rises from 18.59 to 24.65, a 33% increase). Social punishment is partially crowded out (mean punishment points drop by 57%), but the overall cost of norm enforcement falls and group welfare rises. The marginal incentive to cooperate is higher in LAW due to the additive effect of legal and social sanctions. The results are robust to both between- and within-subject comparisons.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct, high-quality evidence that, in a standard linear public goods game with four players, continuous contributions, and no communication, adding a mild legal punishment mechanism to existing social punishment increases group efficiency substantially. The effect is driven by higher contributions and lower social punishment costs, even though legal sanctions partially substitute for social ones. The design dimensions (player count, MPCR, punishment cost/tech, no chat, continuous contributions, etc.) are all clearly specified, making this study highly informative for predicting efficiency outcomes in similar game designs.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. One-shot game; each treatment is a single round per subject.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player chooses how many tokens (0-20) to contribute.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each token contributed increases each player's payoff by 0.4).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point costs the punisher 1 token.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the punished player's payoff by 3 tokens (punishment effectiveness = 3).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism; only punishment is available.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game is one-shot (single round per treatment).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players observe each other's contributions before punishment decisions.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; no evidence that punished players can identify punishers.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is one-shot; results may not generalize to repeated or dynamic settings.
- The legal sanction is mild and nondeterrent in isolation; effects may differ with stronger legal sanctions.
- No communication or endogenous institution formation; real-world environments may differ.
- The study does not include a reward mechanism, so results may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
- The cost of implementing the legal system is not included in the welfare calculation.
