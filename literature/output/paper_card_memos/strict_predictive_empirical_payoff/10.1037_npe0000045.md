## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study finds that exogenous punishment mechanisms (both cash and electronic token penalties) significantly increase cooperation and group earnings in a repeated public goods game compared to a no-punishment control. Cash penalties are more effective than token penalties at increasing both contributions and earnings, especially in the early rounds, but the difference between cash and token punishment diminishes over time. The deterrent effect of punishment is strong enough to compensate for the deadweight loss from penalties, resulting in higher overall group welfare. The results suggest that the tangibility of punishment (cash vs. digital) matters for short-run deterrence, but adaptation reduces this effect with experience.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct, high-quality evidence that introducing exogenous monetary punishment in a standard linear public goods game increases group efficiency (earnings as a fraction of the cooperative optimum) relative to a no-punishment control. The effect is robust for both cash and digital (token) punishment, but is stronger for cash, especially in early rounds. The design dimensions are well specified: 4 players, 10 rounds, no chat, continuous integer contributions, MPCR=0.4, punishment is exogenous and automatic, and no reward mechanism is present. The control group efficiency (average earnings) is lower than in the punishment treatments, and the difference is statistically significant. This paper should be used as a strong positive case for predicting that exogenous monetary punishment increases efficiency in similar lab PGG designs, and that the tangibility of punishment (cash vs. digital) can moderate the effect, especially in the short run.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 4 players, fixed across all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consists of 10 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed; subjects are in separate booths.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous in integer tokens from 0 to 20.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each token contributed yields 0.4 tokens to each of 4 group members).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is exogenous; cost to the punished is 1 Yuan (10 tokens), but there is no cost to any punisher (since punishment is automatic, not peer-assigned).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is a direct deduction of 1 Yuan (10 tokens) from the lowest contributor; 1:1 cost-impact ratio for the punished, no cost to others.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is present in any treatment.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game has 10 rounds; this is made clear in instructions and quiz.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects see summaries of their own and group contributions, lowest contribution, and payoffs each round.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know if they themselves are punished, but cannot identify who punished whom; punishment is exogenous and anonymous.

## Important Limitations
- Punishment is exogenous and automatic, not peer-assigned, so results may not generalize to endogenous punishment settings.
- The tangibility effect (cash vs. token) may be confounded by the display of tokens as 10x the cash value, though the authors argue this is unlikely to drive the main result.
- The experiment is conducted with Chinese undergraduates in a lab setting, which may limit generalizability to other populations or field contexts.
- No reward mechanism is tested, so results only apply to punishment-based interventions.
- The effect of punishment on efficiency is measured in a setting where the penalty is a deadweight loss (not redistributed), which may differ from settings where punishment is redistributed.
