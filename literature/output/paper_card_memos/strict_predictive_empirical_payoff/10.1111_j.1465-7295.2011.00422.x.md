The main dependent variables are group/individual earnings (payoff-based) and contribution rates. The paper explicitly analyzes group welfare and average earnings as monotonic functions of contribution, given the absence of costly punishment/reward.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated linear public goods game with groups of four, the introduction of non-monetary, costless disapproval points (social sanctions) after each round leads to significantly higher group contributions and group earnings (efficiency) compared to a baseline with no such mechanism. Approval points (social rewards) alone have a small, weakly significant effect on efficiency. The combination of approval and disapproval points produces the highest efficiency, with group earnings and contributions both significantly higher than in any other treatment. The effect is robust over 15 rounds, though some decay is observed. The study demonstrates that even costless, payoff-irrelevant social sanctions can substantially improve group welfare in public goods settings.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling a costless, non-monetary punishment mechanism (disapproval points) in a standard linear public goods game increases efficiency (group earnings) relative to a baseline with no such mechanism. The effect is larger than that of a costless reward mechanism (approval points), and the combination of both is most effective. Because the punishment is costless and does not reduce group payoffs, all efficiency gains are realized. For prediction tasks, this suggests that introducing a non-monetary, costless punishment mechanism in a repeated, anonymous, four-player, 15-round linear PGG with MPCR=0.4 will increase efficiency substantially over the control. The effect is likely to be even larger if both approval and disapproval are available. The findings are most directly applicable to settings with costless, anonymous, non-monetary sanctions, and may not generalize to costly or monetary punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players per session, fixed membership.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 15 periods (rounds) per session.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or pre-play communication allowed; only structured approval/disapproval point assignment via computer.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each subject chooses how many of 20 tokens to contribute (0-20).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR = 0.4 (each token contributed yields 0.4 to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment (disapproval points) is costless to assign and has no direct payoff effect.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is non-monetary, costless, and has no direct payoff impact; only social/psychological effect.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Approval points (non-monetary, costless) are available in some treatments.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward (approval points) is costless to assign and has no direct payoff effect.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reward is non-monetary, costless, and has no direct payoff impact; only social/psychological effect.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the game lasts 15 periods.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, subjects see the distribution of contributions and points received by group members (anonymous).
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Point assignment is anonymous; subjects do not know who assigned points to whom.

## Important Limitations
- Punishment and reward are non-monetary, costless, and have no direct payoff effect; results may not generalize to settings with costly or monetary sanctions.
- All point assignment is anonymous; effects may differ in settings with identity revelation or social distance reduction.
- Fixed group size (4) and number of rounds (15); effects may differ with other group sizes or time horizons.
- Subjects are university students in a lab setting; external validity to real-world organizations or diverse populations is uncertain.
- No pre-play or post-play communication; only structured point assignment is allowed.
