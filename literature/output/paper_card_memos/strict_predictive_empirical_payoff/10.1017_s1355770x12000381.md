## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a laboratory experiment simulating a two-commons (two-harbor) resource dilemma, groups of 8 participants (students and professional fishermen) repeatedly chose how to allocate effort between two commons and a safe alternative. The experiment varied institutional features across blocks: baseline (no restrictions), enforcement (costly movement between commons), communication, and communication plus sanctioning. Efficiency (group payoff relative to the social optimum) was the primary outcome. Communication led to large, significant increases in efficiency for all groups, with average net efficiency rising from ~0.8 to ~0.98. The addition of a sanctioning mechanism (warnings, then monetary penalties for repeated defection) further increased efficiency in most groups, sometimes reaching 1.0. The effect of sanctioning was generally positive but smaller than the effect of communication, and in one group, sanctioning led to a breakdown in cooperation. The results show that communication and sanctioning can both be effective in increasing efficiency in commons dilemmas, with communication having the largest and most consistent effect.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling communication among group members in a commons dilemma can dramatically increase efficiency, even before any formal or monetary sanctioning is introduced. The addition of a sanctioning mechanism (warnings and monetary penalties) yields further, though more modest, efficiency gains. For prediction tasks, the presence of communication and/or sanctioning should be expected to increase efficiency substantially relative to a control with neither. The effect of communication is robust and large; the effect of sanctioning is positive but may be context-dependent and sometimes less pronounced. The experiment's design and reporting allow for direct mapping to game design dimensions relevant for prediction.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a specific commons structure (two areas with quadratic production functions), which may limit generalizability to other CPR or PGG settings.
- The punishment mechanism is nonstandard: initial sanctions are warnings (costless), with monetary penalties only after repeated defection, and punishment is costless to the punisher.
- Sample sizes are modest, especially for professional fishermen groups.
- No reward mechanism is tested, so results may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
- The experiment is conducted in a lab setting, which may not capture all relevant field dynamics.
