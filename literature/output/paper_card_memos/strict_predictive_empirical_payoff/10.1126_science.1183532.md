## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- gross tokens collected by group
- net tokens collected by group (after punishment costs)
- resource depletion rate
Non-payoff outcomes:
- punishment frequency
- retaliation rate
- self-reported reasons for punishment
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is the number of tokens collected by the group, both gross and net of punishment costs, which directly maps to group payoff/efficiency. Non-payoff outcomes such as punishment frequency and retaliation are also reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that in a dynamic, spatially explicit CPR lab experiment with five players per group, costly punishment is used by participants but does not increase group payoffs or efficiency unless combined with communication. When punishment is available without communication, net group earnings decrease due to the cost of punishment, and resource depletion is not prevented. Communication alone leads to higher group payoffs and more sustainable resource use. When both communication and punishment are available, the positive effects of communication are not enhanced, and the use of punishment can undermine cooperative agreements once communication is removed. The study highlights that in this more complex, dynamic environment, punishment without communication is not an effective tool for increasing efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that in dynamic, spatially explicit CPR games with real-time harvesting and five players, enabling costly punishment without communication is likely to reduce efficiency relative to the control (no punishment, no communication) due to the direct costs of punishment. Communication is a much more effective intervention for increasing efficiency. The findings suggest that, for similar game designs, the predicted efficiency with punishment enabled (but no communication) should be lower than the control, and that the presence or absence of communication is a critical moderator. The punishment mechanism here (cost of 1 token to remove 2 tokens from another) is not sufficient to offset the cost in terms of group payoff. These results are most applicable to dynamic, spatially explicit CPR games rather than standard static PGGs, but the payoff-based outcome is directly relevant.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consisted of five participants.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each treatment consisted of six 4-minute periods (three of each condition).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Written communication (chat) was allowed in some treatments and found to significantly increase group payoffs.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Harvesting is continuous and real-time (not all-or-nothing); participants collect tokens one at a time by moving avatars.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not a standard MPCR; each token is worth 2 cents to the harvester, and resource regeneration is density dependent. The marginal return is private, not shared, but resource depletion is a group externality.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishing costs the punisher 1 token and removes 2 tokens from the target. The cost of punishment reduces group earnings.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 2 tokens lost per 1 token spent. This ratio is not sufficient to increase group efficiency.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the structure: six 4-minute periods, with conditions announced.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants can see other avatars in real time and observe resource extraction, but no explicit summary statistics are mentioned.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; participants cannot discuss or identify who punished whom unless communication is enabled.

## Important Limitations
- The experimental environment is a dynamic, spatially explicit CPR game, not a standard public goods game, so results may not generalize to static PGGs.
- Punishment is only possible by subtracting tokens at a fixed cost/effectiveness ratio; other punishment mechanisms are not tested.
- No reward mechanism is present, so the interaction of punishment and reward is not addressed.
- Group size is fixed at five; effects may differ with other group sizes.
- The real-time, continuous decision environment differs from round-based or discrete-choice games.
- Communication is only via written chat before the period, not during, and is not always available.
