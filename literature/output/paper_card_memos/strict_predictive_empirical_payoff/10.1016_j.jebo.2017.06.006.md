# Paper Memo: 10.1016_j.jebo.2017.06.006

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=close; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- efficiency (ratio of observed to benchmark payoffs for insiders and outsiders)
- group payoffs
- individual payoffs
- initial and final payoffs (before and after sanctions)
- aggregate harvests
Non-payoff outcomes:
- individual and group harvests (contributions/investments)
- sanctions assigned and received
- variation in harvests
- communication content
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are efficiency and payoffs, both for insiders and outsiders. Harvests (contributions/investments) and sanctions are also analyzed, but efficiency is the primary outcome for the prediction task.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment finds that allowing insiders to observe and punish outsiders (poachers) has a non-monotonic effect on efficiency. With zero monitoring (no punishment possible), outsiders show some restraint and insiders achieve moderate efficiency. With partial monitoring (randomly able to punish one outsider), poaching increases over time, sanctions are low and random, and efficiency for insiders drops to Nash equilibrium levels or below. With full monitoring (all outsiders observable and punishable), poaching is lower and more stable, insiders coordinate better, and efficiency is highest, though still far from the social optimum. Sanctions are generally insufficient to fully deter poaching. The results suggest that imperfect punishment opportunities can backfire, reducing efficiency, while full monitoring and punishment can improve but not maximize efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of enabling punishment in common-pool-resource-like games depends critically on the monitoring structure. If punishment is only partially possible (imperfect monitoring), efficiency can decrease relative to no punishment at all, due to increased poaching and poor coordination of sanctions. If punishment is fully enabled (full monitoring), efficiency increases relative to both the control and partial monitoring, but does not reach the social optimum. For prediction, the presence and effectiveness of punishment, the monitoring structure, and the ability to coordinate sanctions are crucial moderators. Control efficiency (no punishment) is not always a lower bound: enabling punishment can reduce efficiency if monitoring is imperfect. Game design dimensions such as punishment cost, punishment tech, group size, communication, and information structure (who sees what) are all relevant. The results caution against assuming that adding punishment always increases efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consists of 8 players: 5 insiders and 3 outsiders. This is fixed across all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each session consists of 15 periods (rounds).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. Insiders (Group 1) can communicate via chat in odd-numbered periods. Outsiders cannot communicate at all.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution (harvest/investment) is continuous from 0 to 12 tokens per round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The payoff function is non-linear (quadratic), not a standard linear MPCR. The marginal return depends on total group harvest, but the structure is fully specified in the paper.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment costs 1 experimental dollar to the punisher per deduction point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction point assigned reduces the target's payoff by 3 experimental dollars (1:3 punishment ratio).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told in advance that there are 15 periods.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each harvest stage, all subjects see total harvests of insiders and outsiders, and aggregate payoffs. Insiders see individual harvests of other insiders and, depending on treatment, of outsiders.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Insiders see the group and ID numbers of those they can punish. However, subjects cannot observe who punished whom (punishment is anonymous to the recipient).

## Important Limitations
- The game is a common pool resource (CPR) game with a non-linear payoff function, not a standard linear public goods game; results may not fully generalize to linear PGGs.
- Punishment is only available to insiders and only against those they can observe; outsiders cannot punish or communicate.
- Sanctioning is anonymous (recipients do not know who punished them), which may affect coordination of punishment.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanism is present; only costly punishment is studied.
- The experiment does not assign explicit property rights to insiders, and the framing is neutral (not theft or property violation).
- The monitoring structure (who can observe/punish whom) is exogenously fixed and not endogenous or strategic.
- The sample is university students in a lab setting; external validity to field settings may be limited.
- The study does not explore the effect of varying punishment cost or effectiveness (punishment tech) beyond the 1:3 ratio.
- The number of players and group composition are fixed (5 insiders, 3 outsiders); effects of different group sizes are not tested.
- Communication is only allowed among insiders and only in odd-numbered periods; effects of more or less communication are not tested.
