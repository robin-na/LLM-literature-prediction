Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group allocation (contribution to group project) and individual payoff. Group allocation is a behavioral outcome, but average payoff is also reported. Efficiency per se is not directly calculated as a ratio to the fully cooperative outcome, but average payoffs are reported for each condition. The study also analyzes how prior sanctions, deviations from group average, and deviations from maximum payoff affect subsequent group allocation.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated triadic supply network game with real managers, the introduction of sanctions (punishment) reduces both group allocation and average payoffs when the incentive structure favors cooperation (i.e., low temptation to defect). When the incentive structure is changed to increase the temptation to defect, sanctions increase group allocation compared to the no-sanction condition, but average payoffs remain lower than in the cooperative baseline. Sanctions are only effective at increasing group allocation when the underlying incentive structure is unfavorable to cooperation. The study does not report efficiency as a ratio to the fully cooperative outcome, but average payoffs are provided for each condition. The effect of punishment on efficiency is thus negative in cooperative contexts and ambiguous (possibly less negative) in defect-prone contexts.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency or payoff in public-goods-like network games depends critically on the underlying incentive structure. When the baseline (control) efficiency is high (cooperative context), enabling punishment reduces efficiency. When the baseline efficiency is low (defection is tempting), enabling punishment can increase group allocation but does not fully restore efficiency/payoff to cooperative levels. For prediction, this suggests that the marginal effect of punishment on efficiency is negative when control efficiency is high, and less negative or possibly weakly positive when control efficiency is low, but the overall efficiency with punishment rarely exceeds the cooperative baseline. The game design dimensions most relevant here are the payoff structure (temptation to defect), the presence and cost/effectiveness of punishment, and the information structure.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. 14 players participated, each representing a firm in a supply network. The network is composed of triads (groups of 3), but the overall network is 14 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 rounds per experiment; participants were aware of the total number of rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat was allowed between participants during the experiment.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices were discrete but not all-or-nothing: participants could allocate any integer amount (0-5 EMUs) between group and private projects.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. The marginal per-capita return (mpcr) can be inferred from the payoff matrices. In the base case, group allocation yields 3 EMUs to each if both cooperate (out of 5 possible), so mpcr is 0.6 (3/5) per dyad, but the network structure and additional sharing complicate the exact calculation. The temptation to defect is increased in condition 2.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishing a partner costs 1 EMU and reduces the punished player's payoff by 2 EMUs.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment effectiveness is 2 EMUs lost per 1 EMU spent by the punisher.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were told in advance that the game would run for 20 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. In Experiment 2 (with sanctions), after each round, all participants were shown the group allocation, private allocation, and payoff of all members. In Experiment 1, only the highest earning and associated strategy were revealed after each round.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment assignments were visible: after each round, all decisions and payoffs, including sanctions, were revealed to everyone.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment was conducted with only 14 participants from a single firm, limiting generalizability.
- Financial incentives were relatively small compared to real-world business stakes.
- The network structure was fixed and may not represent other supply network topologies.
- Efficiency was not directly reported as a ratio to the fully cooperative outcome; only average payoffs and group allocations are available.
- The game is a supply network variant of the public goods game, not a standard PGG, and the payoff structure is more complex due to triadic and network effects.
