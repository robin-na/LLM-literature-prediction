Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group-level efficiency (measured as the marginal effect of punishment on contribution, i.e., the increase in contribution per unit of punishment), group earnings, and mean contribution. Non-payoff outcomes such as punishment frequency and expectation of others' contribution are also analyzed, but the primary focus is on payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
This laboratory experiment with Han Chinese and Mongolian Chinese students uses a standard public goods game with and without a costly punishment stage. Each group has 4 players, and each subject plays 10 rounds without punishment and 10 rounds with punishment. The marginal per-capita return (MPCR) is 0.4. Punishment costs 1 token to the punisher and reduces the punished's payoff by 3 tokens. The main findings are: (1) Punishment increases mean contribution in all treatments, but the efficiency of punishment (measured as the increase in contribution per unit of punishment) varies by group composition. (2) In all-Han and mixed-ethnicity groups, punishment is efficient (marginal effect > 1), while in all-Mongolian groups, punishment is inefficient (marginal effect < 1). (3) Ethnic diversity (mixed groups) boosts punishment efficiency for both Han and Mongolian Chinese. (4) Mongolian Chinese punish more harshly within their own group due to cultural norms ('Anda' tradition), leading to lower efficiency. (5) Han Chinese show strong ingroup bias, contributing more in all-Han groups, especially with punishment. (6) Cultural fusion (Mongolian Chinese with Han cultural background) increases contribution in mixed groups. (7) Overall, the effect of punishment on efficiency is positive in all-Han and mixed groups, but not in all-Mongolian groups.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence for predicting the effect of introducing punishment on efficiency in public goods games with different group compositions. For games with 4 players, 10 rounds, MPCR 0.4, and a punishment cost/impact ratio of 1:3, punishment increases efficiency in all-Han and mixed-ethnicity groups (marginal effect > 1), but is inefficient in all-Mongolian groups (marginal effect < 1). If the control (no-punishment) efficiency is known, the expected efficiency with punishment will increase substantially in all-Han and mixed groups, but only modestly or not at all in all-Mongolian groups. The results are robust to a 'clear-cut' Han Chinese sample. The findings are most applicable to lab settings with similar design parameters (player count, rounds, MPCR, punishment cost/impact, no chat, continuous contribution, no rewards, anonymous punishment, and known round count). The paper is highly informative for the prediction task, especially regarding the moderating role of group composition and cultural background.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 4 players per session.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each subject plays 10 rounds without punishment and 10 rounds with punishment.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players choose how many tokens (0-20) to contribute.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each token contributed returns 0.4 to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment costs 1 token to the punisher per point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the punished player's payoff by 3 tokens.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are told in advance that there are 10 rounds per stage.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, players see the contributions and payoffs of all group members.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous: players know how much they were punished, but not by whom.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is conducted only with Han Chinese and Mongolian Chinese university students in Inner Mongolia, which may limit generalizability to other populations or cultural contexts.
- The group size, number of rounds, MPCR, and punishment parameters are fixed; results may not generalize to other parameterizations.
- No reward mechanism is tested, so findings are specific to punishment-only environments.
- Cultural fusion effects are only observed one-way (Mongolian Chinese with Han culture); the reverse could not be tested due to sample limitations.
- The study does not analyze long-term or repeated interactions beyond 10 rounds per stage.
- The effect of communication or chat is not tested, as all sessions prohibit communication.
