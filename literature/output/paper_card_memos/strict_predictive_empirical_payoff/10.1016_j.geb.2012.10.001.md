empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: mixed
Payoff-related outcomes:
- group efficiency (maximal group contributions)
- group earnings
- welfare (collective payoff)
- proportion of groups achieving full efficiency
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rates by role and group type
- punishment assigned and received
- emergence and enforcement of contribution norms
- normative expectations (questionnaire study)
Outcome notes:
The paper reports both behavioral outcomes (contributions, punishment) and payoff-based outcomes (group efficiency, group earnings, welfare). Efficiency is a central focus, especially the proportion of groups achieving maximal contributions.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that in the absence of punishment, public good groups (both homogeneous and heterogeneous) rapidly converge to low contributions and low efficiency, regardless of the normative appeal of efficiency or fairness rules. When punishment is enabled, contributions and efficiency increase sharply in all group types. About 40% of groups achieve full efficiency (maximal contributions by all) with punishment, while the rest enforce different relative contribution norms depending on group heterogeneity (e.g., equal contributions, proportional to endowment). The increase in efficiency from punishment is substantial and robust, though the exact efficiency level and norm enforced depend on the group structure. The paper provides direct evidence that punishment enables groups to overcome the collective action problem and coordinate on efficient or near-efficient outcomes, even in the presence of heterogeneity.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling punishment in linear public good games with three players and ten rounds leads to a large increase in group efficiency, regardless of whether the group is homogeneous or heterogeneous in endowments or marginal benefits. The effect is robust: without punishment, efficiency is low due to free-riding; with punishment, efficiency rises substantially, with many groups achieving full efficiency. The specific norm enforced (equal contributions, proportionality, etc.) depends on the group’s heterogeneity, but the positive effect of punishment on efficiency is consistent. For prediction tasks, this paper supports the expectation that, in similar lab PGG designs, enabling punishment will significantly increase efficiency relative to the control, with the magnitude of the effect moderated by group heterogeneity and the specific norm that emerges.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All experimental groups have 3 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group plays 10 consecutive periods (rounds).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are continuous from 0 up to a maximum (not all-or-nothing).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (MPCR) is 0.5 for most players; in one group type, one player has MPCR 0.75.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point costs the punisher 1 point.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the punished subject's earnings by 3 points.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects know the total number of rounds (10) in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, players see the contributions and earnings of each other group member.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects are informed of the total punishment points assigned to them, but not who punished whom (punishment is anonymous).

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses only three-player groups, which may limit generalizability to larger groups.
- All sessions are conducted in a laboratory setting with student subjects, which may not capture field or real-world dynamics.
- The punishment mechanism is fixed (cost 1, impact 3), so results may not generalize to other punishment technologies.
- No reward mechanism is studied, so the interaction of punishment and reward is not addressed.
- The study focuses on short games (10 rounds), so long-term or repeated-game effects are not tested.
- Heterogeneity is limited to endowment and marginal benefit; other forms of heterogeneity are not explored.
- Efficiency is measured as maximal group contributions, but the paper does not report exact efficiency ratios for all conditions.
