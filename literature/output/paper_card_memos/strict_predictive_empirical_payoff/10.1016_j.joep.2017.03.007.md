## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated public goods game with punishment, concentrating punishment power in the hands of the top contributor (with frequent turnover) significantly increases both cooperation and group earnings compared to the standard decentralized punishment baseline. Randomly assigning a single punisher per round does not improve efficiency. The mechanism works not because of immunity from punishment, but because the right to punish motivates higher contributions. However, antisocial punishment remains present. The findings suggest that the design of the punishment institution—specifically, how the punisher is selected—has a strong effect on both cooperation and efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that, in a standard linear public goods game with four players, 20 rounds, no chat, continuous contributions, and a convex punishment cost function, assigning punishment power to the top contributor (with frequent turnover) increases group earnings (i.e., efficiency) relative to both the no-punishment and the standard decentralized punishment baseline. The effect is not observed when the single punisher is selected randomly. For prediction, this suggests that the selection mechanism for the punisher is a critical moderator: 'top contributor as punisher' yields higher efficiency than both unrestricted and random single punisher regimes, holding other design features constant. The results are most directly applicable to games with similar structure (N=4, 20 rounds, MPCR=0.4, no chat, anonymous, continuous contributions, punishment cost and impact as specified).

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use groups of 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use 20 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat allowed between participants.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: participants choose any amount from 0 to 20 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each token contributed yields 0.4 tokens to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost is convex: 1 point costs 1 token, 2 points cost 2 tokens, 3 points cost 4 tokens, up to 10 points costing 30 tokens.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the target's earnings by 10% of their stage 1 earnings, up to 100%.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are told the game has 20 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants see the contributions of all group members and their own earnings.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous: punished players do not know who punished them.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses only groups of 4 players and 20 rounds; results may not generalize to other group sizes or time horizons.
- Efficiency is measured as average earnings, not as a normalized efficiency ratio; direct comparison to studies reporting efficiency ratios may require caution.
- The punishment cost function is convex and may differ from linear punishment schemes in other studies.
- No treatments without punishment are included; all comparisons are between different punishment institutions.
- No reward or positive sanctioning mechanisms are tested.
- All sessions are conducted in a single country (Italy) with undergraduate students, which may limit external validity.
