Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group earnings (efficiency) and average contributions. Punishment behavior and convergence are also analyzed, but efficiency is a primary focus.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a standard public good game with peer punishment, the format of feedback (contribution feedback, earnings feedback, or both) after each round has a large effect on group efficiency and cooperation. Contribution feedback leads to the highest efficiency and contributions, earnings feedback leads to lower efficiency and contributions, and providing both types of feedback leads to the lowest efficiency due to increased punishment and lack of convergence. The punishment mechanism itself (cost and effectiveness) is held constant across treatments. The results show that the same punishment institution can have very different efficiency outcomes depending on how information is presented to participants.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public good games is not only a function of the punishment parameters, but also of the information environment—specifically, the feedback format. For prediction tasks, this means that even with identical game design dimensions and control efficiency, the realized efficiency with punishment can vary dramatically depending on whether feedback is about contributions, earnings, or both. Contribution feedback supports higher efficiency, while earnings feedback undermines it. If both are shown, efficiency can be even lower than with earnings feedback alone. Thus, the 'show_other_summaries' dimension (and its content) is a critical moderator for predicting treatment efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 players in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 10 periods (rounds) per session.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: 0 ≤ c_i ≤ 20.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each unit contributed returns 0.4 to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point costs E$1 to the punisher.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the recipient's earnings by E$2.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism; only punishment is available.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game length (10 periods) is common knowledge.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Key experimental manipulation: feedback is either about contributions (C), earnings (E), or both (CE). Contribution feedback leads to higher efficiency, earnings feedback to lower efficiency, both to the lowest efficiency.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are assigned random IDs each period; punishment is anonymous across periods.

## Important Limitations
- The study only examines the effect of feedback format in the presence of punishment; there is no control group without punishment for direct comparison.
- All groups are of size 4 and play for 10 rounds; results may not generalize to other group sizes or time horizons.
- The experiment is conducted in a lab setting with student participants, which may limit external validity.
- No reward mechanism is studied; only punishment is available.
- The findings are specific to the feedback formats tested (contribution, earnings, both); other forms of feedback or summary information are not examined.
