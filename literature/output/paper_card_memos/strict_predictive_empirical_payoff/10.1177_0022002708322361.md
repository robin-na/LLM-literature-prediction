## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
This experiment compares standard (normal) and privileged public goods groups, with and without the option for decentralized costly punishment. In normal groups (all members have the same marginal per-capita return), punishment substantially increases contributions and group earnings, especially in later rounds. In privileged groups (one member has a high marginal return, others low), punishment increases contributions and earnings only slightly, and it increases inequality. The main reason is that punishment is less targeted and less effective in privileged groups: high-benefit members punish in an unstructured way, and low-benefit members respond weakly to punishment. As a result, the efficiency gains from punishment are much smaller in privileged groups than in normal groups.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency depends strongly on group composition, specifically on whether the group is homogeneous (normal) or contains privileged members (heterogeneous marginal returns). For standard linear PGGs with 3 players, 10 rounds, no chat, continuous contributions, and known MPCR, punishment increases efficiency, especially in later rounds. However, if the group is privileged (one high-benefit, two low-benefit), the efficiency gain from punishment is much smaller, and inequality increases. Thus, when predicting the effect of punishment on efficiency, it is crucial to account for heterogeneity in marginal returns. Control efficiency (without punishment) is higher in privileged groups, but the treatment effect of punishment is much weaker than in normal groups.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 3 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group plays 10 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player chooses any amount from 0 to 20 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. In normal groups, MPCR = 0.5 for all; in privileged groups, one member has MPCR = 1.5, others have 0.5.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point costs the punisher 1 token.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the punished participant's earnings by 3 tokens.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know in advance that the game lasts 10 rounds.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each contribution stage, participants are informed of the contributions of all other group members.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants are informed of the total number of punishment points assigned to them, but not the identity of the punisher.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses only 3-player groups, so results may not generalize to larger groups.
- Privileged groups are defined by a single high-benefit member; other forms of heterogeneity are not tested.
- No communication is allowed, so effects of chat or negotiation are not addressed.
- The punishment institution is fixed (cost = 1, impact = 3); other punishment technologies are not explored.
- No reward or positive sanctioning mechanism is included.
- The experiment is conducted in a lab setting with student participants, which may limit external validity.
- The study does not explore endogenous group formation or institution choice.
