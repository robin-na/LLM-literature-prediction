# Paper Memo: 10.1017_xps.2020.10

## Study Type
empirical; empirical subtype=experimental; experimental subtype=lab_experiment

## Task Relevance
PGG or variant=exact; punishment or sanctions=exact; efficiency or payoff outcome=exact

## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- group profit (net payoff after punishment)
- average group contributions
- difference in profits between punishment and VCM stages
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution behavior (by role and treatment)
- punishment assigned and received
- probability of being punished
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group profit (payoff), average contributions, and punishment behavior. Efficiency is operationalized as group profit relative to the VCM baseline. Non-payoff outcomes (contribution, punishment) are also analyzed, but payoff is the primary focus for treatment effects.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that centralized punishment increases group efficiency (measured as profit) compared to the no-punishment baseline. There is no significant difference in group efficiency or profit between groups with democratically elected managers and those with exogenously appointed managers, regardless of whether managers face repeated elections (political accountability) or not. The presence of punishment, not the method of authority selection or accountability, is the main driver of increased efficiency. The only notable behavioral effect is that managers in the democracy-with-accountability condition do not free ride more than other group members, unlike in other treatments.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a public goods game with centralized punishment, enabling punishment increases group efficiency relative to a no-punishment baseline. However, the specific design features of how the punishing authority is selected (democratic vs. exogenous) and whether there is repeated selection (accountability) do not further increase efficiency. For prediction, the main effect to expect is a positive jump in efficiency when punishment is enabled, but little or no additional effect from varying the selection or accountability mechanism for the punisher. The game design dimensions (player count, MPCR, punishment cost/tech, etc.) are clearly specified and can be mapped directly to the prediction task.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 5 players in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Stage 1: 10 rounds (VCM); Stage 2: 10 rounds (Single selection) or 12 rounds (Multiple selection/accountability).
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players can contribute any amount from 0 to 20 EU.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (multiplier m=2, n=5).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is administered from a group-funded pool (tax of 2 EU per player per round); manager allocates up to 10 points per round. No explicit cost to the manager for assigning punishment, but unused points are redistributed.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment points assigned are transformed via a convex function: e.g., 1 assigned = 1 reduced, 2 assigned = 2 reduced, 3 assigned = 4 reduced, up to 10 assigned = 30 reduced.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of rounds in each stage in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players see others' contributions and profits anonymously after each round; also see total punishment points used, but not the target.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players do not know who was punished; punishment is anonymous in terms of target identity.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a centralized punishment institution with a single manager per group, which differs from decentralized punishment designs.
- Punishment is funded from a group tax pool, not from the punisher's own endowment, which may affect generalizability to other punishment cost structures.
- The study does not vary the MPCR, punishment cost, or punishment technology across treatments, so findings may not generalize to other parameterizations.
- No reward mechanism is present, so results do not speak to the interaction of punishment and reward.
- The sample consists of university students in Ecuador, which may limit external validity.
- The design does not include a pure no-punishment control in stage 2; all stage 2 treatments have punishment enabled.
