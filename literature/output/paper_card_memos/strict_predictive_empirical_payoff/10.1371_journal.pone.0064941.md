## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated public goods game with 4-person groups over 30 rounds, the study compares three treatments: (1) punishment only, (2) norm-signaling (message) only, and (3) both punishment and norm-signaling (sanction). In the critical rounds where treatments differ (rounds 11-20), the sanction treatment yields significantly higher average net earnings (31% higher than punishment, 16% higher than message) and higher contributions, with less frequent and less intense punishment. The efficiency gains in the sanction treatment are attributed to the combination of explicit norm communication and the credible threat of punishment, which together increase norm salience and reduce the need for costly punishment. The findings are robust in both human experiments and agent-based simulations.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a standard linear public goods game with costly punishment, the addition of norm-signaling (explicit communication of normative expectations) to punishment substantially increases group efficiency compared to punishment alone. The efficiency gains are due to both higher cooperation and lower punishment costs. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the presence of norm-signaling or communication channels that make norms salient can be a critical moderator of punishment's effect on efficiency. The game design dimensions most relevant here are the presence of punishment, the ability to send norm-signaling messages, and the structure of feedback about others' actions. The control efficiency (without punishment or norm-signaling) is lower than in the combined treatment. The results imply that, all else equal, enabling both punishment and norm-signaling will yield higher efficiency than enabling punishment alone.

## Important Limitations
- The communication channel is structured and limited to pre-set normative messages, not free-form chat; results may differ with richer communication.
- The experiment uses fixed groups of 4 and a specific MPCR (0.4); generalizability to other group sizes or MPCRs is not tested.
- The study is conducted with university students in Spain, and contribution levels are noted to be lower than in some other populations.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanism is tested, so results may not generalize to settings with both punishment and reward.
- The norm-signaling mechanism is tightly coupled to the experimental design; real-world norm communication may be more complex.
