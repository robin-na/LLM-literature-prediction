## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that stochastic (noisy) punishment in a repeated public goods game significantly reduces group efficiency and welfare compared to deterministic punishment. As the noise in the punishment multiplier increases (while keeping the mean constant), average payoffs per round drop sharply (from 10.1 MU in control to 5.6 MU in high noise), contributions decrease, and both the frequency and intensity of punishment rise. Notably, antisocial punishment (punishing cooperators) becomes more prevalent under noise, further undermining cooperation and group payoffs. Regression analyses confirm that the negative effect of noise on payoffs is mediated by increased antisocial punishment and reduced contributions.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a standard repeated PGG with punishment, introducing noise into the punishment technology (i.e., making the punishment impact stochastic rather than deterministic) leads to a substantial and monotonic reduction in efficiency, even when the mean punishment impact is held constant. For prediction tasks, this suggests that the presence and degree of punishment noise is a critical moderator: efficiency gains from enabling punishment are much lower (or even negative) when punishment is noisy. The findings are directly relevant for settings with deterministic punishment (high efficiency) versus noisy punishment (low efficiency), holding other design dimensions constant. The results also highlight the importance of antisocial punishment as a mechanism for efficiency loss under noisy punishment.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 4 participants in all conditions.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 22 rounds per session, with first 2 as practice; only 20 rounds counted for analysis.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players; identities concealed and no communication channel described.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player can contribute any amount from 0 to 10 MU per round.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Enhancement factor r=2, group size=4, so MPCR=0.5.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 1 MU per unit of punishment assigned (up to 10 MU per target per round).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment technology is the main manipulated variable: in control, each MU spent reduces target by 3 MU (deterministic); in treatments, the multiplier is stochastic (mean 3, but drawn from [2,4], [1,5], or [0,6]). Increased noise in punishment technology leads to lower efficiency.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is present; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants were not told the total number of rounds in advance to avoid endgame effects.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, participants saw the contributions of other group members and the amount of punishment received by others.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment was anonymous: identities of punishers and punished were concealed; only the amount received was shown.

## Important Limitations
- The only punishment technology variation studied is the addition of noise to the punishment multiplier; other forms of punishment or institutional enforcement are not considered.
- The experiment uses a fixed group size (4) and a specific MPCR (0.5); results may not generalize to other group sizes or social dilemma strengths.
- No reward or positive sanction mechanism is present, so findings are limited to punishment-only environments.
- Participants were not told the number of rounds, which may affect strategies compared to games with known endpoints.
- The study does not explore cross-cultural differences in response to punishment noise, though it notes this as an open question.
- The experiment is conducted online with a global sample, but the representativeness of the sample for broader populations is not established.
