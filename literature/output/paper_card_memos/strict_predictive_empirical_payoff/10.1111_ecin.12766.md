## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:

- efficiency (earnings)
- public good provision (contribution levels as a share of endowment)
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rate
- punishment points sent/received
- conditional cooperation
- retaliation
- prosocial/antisocial punishment
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group profit (efficiency) and average contribution. Punishment behavior and cooperation are also analyzed, but efficiency is directly measured and compared across treatments.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The paper finds that enabling punishment in repeated public goods games increases contributions in all network structures, but the effect on efficiency (group profit) depends on the network. In the complete and star (hierarchical) networks, punishment leads to higher efficiency compared to the no-punishment baseline. In the circle and line networks, punishment increases contributions but the cost of punishment outweighs the gains, resulting in lower efficiency than the no-punishment baseline. Central players in the star network use punishment in a prosocial way and do not retaliate, leading to higher group efficiency. In contrast, antisocial punishment and retaliation are more common in the line and circle networks, undermining efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games is highly sensitive to the network structure. If the game design includes a hierarchical (star) or complete network, enabling punishment is likely to increase efficiency relative to the control. In other incomplete networks (circle, line), punishment may reduce efficiency due to costly antisocial punishment and retaliation. The paper also provides detailed parameterization of the game (player count, rounds, MPCR, punishment cost/tech), making it highly informative for prediction tasks that use these dimensions. Control (VCM) and treatment (PUN) efficiency values are reported for each network, allowing for direct calibration.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games use groups of 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each treatment consists of 20 rounds without punishment (VCM), followed by 20 rounds with punishment (PUN), for a total of 40 rounds per group.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed; the environment is fully anonymous and identity-free.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players can allocate any fraction of their 50 ECU endowment to the public good.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each ECU contributed yields 0.5 ECU to each group member (MPCR = 0.5).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost is nonlinear: assigning 1-10 points costs 2, 4, 8, 12, 18, 24, 32, 40, 50, 60 ECUs, respectively.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the target's first-stage earnings by 10%, up to 100%.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. The number of rounds is known and fixed (20 rounds per phase).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, players see the contributions of their linked neighbors and their own earnings. They do not see the contributions of unlinked players.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players observe the aggregate number of punishment points received, but not the identity of the punisher (identity-free, fully anonymous).

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses only four-player groups and specific network structures (complete, circle, line, star); results may not generalize to larger groups or other network topologies.
- Punishment is the only sanctioning mechanism; no reward or positive incentives are studied.
- The punishment cost function is nonlinear and may not match other studies with linear costs.
- Players only observe and can punish their network neighbors; information and punishment opportunities are restricted by network structure.
- The environment is fully anonymous and identity-free, which may differ from real-world settings with reputation or repeated interactions outside the lab.
- The study is conducted with undergraduate students in a lab setting, which may limit external validity.
