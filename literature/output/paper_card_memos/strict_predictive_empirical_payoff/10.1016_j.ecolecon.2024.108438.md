## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a lab experiment with a public bads game (3 players, 18 rounds), the introduction of voluntary pledge-and-review agreements, automatic financial penalties for exceeding pledges, and/or peer evaluation (reputation contest) did not reduce average production or disaster risk compared to baseline. Most participants joined agreements and complied with their pledges, but pledges were not ambitious enough to reduce emissions. Financial penalties increased compliance but did not improve group efficiency, as participants could avoid penalties by not joining or by making unambitious pledges. Peer evaluation did not reduce production and even reduced the ambition of pledges. The results suggest that, in decentralized and voluntary settings, punishment mechanisms do not improve efficiency unless participation and ambition can be enforced.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a voluntary, decentralized public bads game with 3 players and 18 rounds, adding automatic financial penalties for exceeding pledges does not improve group efficiency or reduce emissions compared to baseline, because participants can avoid penalties by not joining or by making unambitious pledges. Peer evaluation (reputation-based non-monetary punishment) also does not improve efficiency and may reduce ambition. For prediction, this suggests that in similar voluntary, opt-in, pledge-based games, the presence of punishment mechanisms (even if automatic and with high penalty rates) will not increase efficiency unless there are mechanisms to ensure ambitious pledges and broad participation. Control efficiency is a good predictor of treatment efficiency when punishment is voluntary and avoidable.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 3 players in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 18 rounds per game; first 3 rounds are baseline, next 15 rounds are treatment.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No free-form chat or communication allowed; only structured voting and peer evaluation.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Production choice is continuous: q_i in [0,40]. Not all-or-nothing.
- default contribution framing: The paper directly informs this dimension. Default actions imposed only if no decision is made: produce nothing, pledge max, reject pledge, or not join. Defaults are not central to the design.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not a standard MPCR; each unit produced gives 12 units to self, but increases disaster risk for all. The marginal return is 12, but the marginal cost is probabilistic and convex.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Penalty for exceeding pledge is 8 units per unit over; penalty is redistributed to joiners. No cost to the punisher; penalty is automatic.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is automatic: 8 units per unit over the pledge, deducted from violator and redistributed to joiners. Effectiveness is high if applied, but can be avoided.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. Non-monetary reward: in peer evaluation treatments, top reputation earner gets a tree planted in their name. No monetary reward mechanism.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. No cost to assign reputation points; reward is external (tree planting).
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Reputation points are zero-sum; only one participant per group receives the tree reward.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants know the total number of rounds (18) and the block structure.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Participants see disaster risk, potential and realized earnings, and in pledge treatments, who joined, pledges, and overproduction.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is automatic and not assigned by individuals; in peer evaluation, reputation points are assigned but not fully anonymous (group members see rankings).

## Important Limitations
- Small group size (3 players) may not generalize to large-scale climate negotiations.
- Laboratory setting with anonymous, one-shot interactions may not capture real-world reputational stakes.
- Penalty rates and mechanisms are exogenously set; real-world agreements may involve negotiation over these parameters.
- Game is a public bads game with probabilistic disaster, not a standard linear public goods game.
- No free-form communication; only structured voting and peer evaluation.
- Reputation/reward is limited to in-game status and a small external prize (tree planting), not real-world reputation.
