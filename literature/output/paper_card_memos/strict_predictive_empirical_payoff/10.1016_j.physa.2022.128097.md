The paper reports both group/individual payoffs and behavioral outcomes such as contribution rates and punishment behavior. Efficiency is not reported as a normalized ratio, but group payoffs and contributions as a fraction of maximum are provided, which are closely related.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
Introducing the option for costly punishment in a repeated public goods game increases group contributions and group payoffs compared to the no-punishment baseline. This effect holds both when sanctions are observable (feedback given immediately) and when sanctions are unobservable (feedback delayed until the end). The mechanism is that subjects expect free riders to be punished and adjust their behavior to avoid being punished, even when they do not observe sanctions in real time. Sanctioning is mainly non-strategic and norm-driven, targeting free riders. The presence of punishment stabilizes cooperation and prevents the unraveling of contributions, with some groups achieving high cooperation and others not. The overall effect of punishment on efficiency (as measured by group payoffs and contributions as a fraction of maximum) is positive.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that enabling costly punishment in a standard linear public goods game increases group efficiency (payoff) compared to the no-punishment baseline. The effect is robust to whether punishment is observable or not during play, suggesting that the mere possibility of punishment, and the associated social norm, is sufficient to increase cooperation and efficiency. For prediction tasks, this paper supports the expectation that adding punishment to a repeated linear PGG with 4 players, 10 rounds, and MPCR=0.4 will increase efficiency, even if punishment is not immediately observable. The magnitude of the effect may vary by group, but the direction is reliably positive. The paper also provides parameter values for key design dimensions.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All games are played in groups of 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each phase consists of 10 rounds; 10 rounds without sanctions, then 10 rounds with sanctions.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player chooses an integer from 0 to 10.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each token contributed returns 0.4 to each player).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction point costs the punisher 1 point.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction point reduces the punished player's payoff by 3 points (punishment effectiveness = 3).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment (deduction) is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed of the number of rounds in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, players observe the contributions of all other group members.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are assigned new integer labels each round; punishment is anonymous in the sense that identities change, but players know how many deduction points they received (in STANDARD), not from whom.

## Important Limitations
- Efficiency is not reported as a normalized ratio, but group payoffs and contributions as a fraction of maximum are provided.
- The sample is limited to university students in the Netherlands; cultural generalizability is discussed but not tested.
- Some groups still converge to low cooperation even with punishment, indicating heterogeneity in group outcomes.
- No reward mechanism is studied; only punishment is available.
- Punishment is anonymous due to relabeling, so effects of targeted or reputation-based punishment are not tested.
