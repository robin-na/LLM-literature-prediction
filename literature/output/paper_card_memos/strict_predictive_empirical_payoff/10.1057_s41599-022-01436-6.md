Individual contribution behavior and sanctioning frequency are also analyzed, but the primary focus is on group-level payoff outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a lab experiment with groups of 9 players, subdivided into 3 subgroups of 3, the opportunity for subgroups to impose costly sanctions on other subgroups (punishment treatment) did not increase the probability of reaching the collective target or improve group payoffs compared to a control without sanctions. Sanctioning was used frequently, but its costs reduced overall payoffs, and it failed to discipline free riders effectively because fair contributors who were also sanctioned reduced their own contributions. Thus, punishment in this polycentric, subgroup-based setting did not improve efficiency or welfare.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that, in a public-goods-like collective-risk game with subgroup-level costly punishment, enabling punishment does not increase group efficiency or the probability of reaching the collective target, and may even reduce efficiency due to the costs of punishment and negative effects on fair contributors. For prediction tasks, this suggests that, in similar designs (polycentric, subgroup-based, punishment between subgroups, costly punishment), enabling punishment is unlikely to improve efficiency over the control condition without punishment, and may even be detrimental.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses small groups of students in a lab setting, which may not generalize to real-world climate governance or larger-scale public goods problems.
- Punishment is implemented at the subgroup-to-subgroup level, not individual-to-individual, which may limit generalizability to standard PGG punishment designs.
- The game uses a threshold (collective-risk) structure rather than a linear public goods game, so results may not transfer directly to linear PGGs.
- No communication is allowed, which may affect the impact of punishment compared to settings with communication.
- The study does not explore variations in punishment cost or effectiveness beyond the fixed parameters used.
