The paper reports both behavioral outcomes (contributions, punishment, retaliation) and payoff-based outcomes (average earnings, efficiency). Efficiency is discussed explicitly in relation to group payoffs and the cost of punishment.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
This laboratory experiment compares three network structures (Complete, Circle, Asymmetric) in a repeated linear public goods game with decentralized punishment. In all treatments, punishment is possible, but the network structure determines who can punish whom. The Asymmetric network features one under-monitored player (D) who can only be punished by one other player (C), while C is responsible for punishing all others. The main findings are: (1) Average contributions and efficiency are significantly lower in the Asymmetric network than in the Complete or Circle networks; (2) The drop in efficiency is driven entirely by the under-monitored player D, who contributes less and retaliates more against punishment; (3) There is no evidence that players with sole punishment responsibility (C) punish their unique target (D) more harshly; (4) Retaliatory (anti-social) punishment is more frequent from the under-monitored player, deterring effective punishment and reducing efficiency. The study provides direct evidence that network asymmetry can undermine the positive effect of punishment on efficiency in public goods games.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the structure of the punishment network is a critical moderator of punishment's effect on efficiency in public goods games. When punishment is possible but monitoring/punishment is asymmetric (i.e., some players are under-monitored), efficiency gains from punishment are reduced or eliminated, primarily due to lower contributions and increased retaliation by under-monitored players. In symmetric networks (Complete, Circle), punishment supports higher contributions and efficiency. For prediction, this suggests that the effect of enabling punishment on efficiency depends not only on the presence of punishment but also on the symmetry and coverage of the punishment network. If the control (no punishment) efficiency is known, the expected efficiency gain from punishment will be lower or even negative in asymmetric/patched networks compared to symmetric ones, even with identical punishment parameters.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 4 players in every treatment.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each match consists of 10 periods (rounds); each subject participates in 7 matches.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: each player chooses how many of 10 tokens to contribute (0-10).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each token contributed yields 0.4 to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction point costs the punisher 1 token.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each deduction point reduces the target's earnings by 3 tokens (punishment effectiveness = 3).
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of periods (10) in each match.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each stage, players see the contributions and punishment assigned/received for their immediate neighbors only; they do not see information about other players or final payoffs of others.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players see the number of deduction points assigned and received from each of their neighbors (i.e., punishment is identified at the neighbor level).

## Important Limitations
- The experiment only examines three specific network structures (Complete, Circle, Asymmetric) with 4 players; results may not generalize to larger groups or other network topologies.
- Punishment is always possible; there is no control condition with punishment disabled, so the direct effect of enabling punishment versus no punishment is not measured in this paper.
- No reward mechanism is included; only punishment is studied.
- Players only observe information about their immediate neighbors, not the full group, which may affect coordination of punishment.
- The study uses fixed groups and partner matching, which may increase the opportunity for retaliation compared to random rematching.
- The findings on efficiency are based on group earnings including the cost of punishment, but the paper notes that statistical power for efficiency comparisons is limited by the number of sessions.
