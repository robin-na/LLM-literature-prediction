## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that second-order punishment institutions (which punish both defectors and those who do not support the punishment institution) result in higher average payoffs and cooperation rates than institutions without second-order punishment. When given a choice, groups using majority voting overwhelmingly select the more efficient second-order punishment institution, while groups using individual migration (foot voting) initially avoid it, resulting in lower payoffs. Over time, some foot-voting groups shift toward the more efficient institution, but not as consistently as with majority voting. The findings highlight the importance of democratic decision-making in establishing efficient punishment institutions that sustain cooperation and maximize group welfare.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that enabling second-order punishment in a public goods game increases efficiency (average payoff) compared to a control without such punishment, under otherwise identical game parameters. The effect is robust across both theoretical predictions and experimental results. The findings also show that the method of institutional choice (majority voting vs. individual migration) moderates the realized efficiency, with majority voting more reliably leading to the efficient outcome. For prediction tasks, the presence of second-order punishment, the method of institutional choice, and the group’s prior experience with institutional regimes are important moderators. The paper’s detailed reporting of payoffs under both control and treatment conditions makes it directly informative for predicting treatment efficiency from design dimensions and control efficiency.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each group consisted of 5 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 35 rounds per group: blocks I and II (10 rounds each), block III (15 rounds).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Total contributions multiplied by 3.1 and redistributed among all group participants. With 5 players, MPCR = 3.1/5 = 0.62.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Tax to fund punishment institution: €0.05 + €0.45/i (without 2OP) or €0.05 + €0.5/i (with 2OP), where i = number of tax payers.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Fine for defectors (and tax evaders, in 2OP) is €1.00 per offense. Punishment is centralized and automatic if institution is established.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Game is divided into blocks with a known number of rounds; block III is 15 rounds, and this is known to participants.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects are informed about whether someone paid taxes before deciding to contribute. Figures and text suggest feedback on group outcomes.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses a stylized laboratory setting with student participants, which may limit generalizability to real-world institutions.
- Punishment is implemented via a central authority, not peer-to-peer, so findings may not transfer to decentralized punishment settings.
- The only form of institutional choice studied is between presence/absence of second-order punishment, not variation in punishment severity or cost.
- No reward mechanisms are included, so the interaction of punishment and reward is not addressed.
- The experiment does not manipulate or systematically vary group size, MPCR, or punishment cost across treatments.
