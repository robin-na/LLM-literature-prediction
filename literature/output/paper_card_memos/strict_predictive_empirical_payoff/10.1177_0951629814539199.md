Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable is group yield (aggregate payoff as a fraction of the social optimum), which is equivalent to efficiency. The paper also analyzes extraction levels, punishment behavior, and chat content, but the primary focus is on payoff-based outcomes.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment tested four treatments (baseline, punishment only, communication only, both) in a repeated nonlinear common pool resource game with groups of six. Peer punishment (costly, 3:1 fine-to-fee ratio, up to 5 points per target) did not significantly increase group yield or efficiency relative to baseline. Communication (anonymous chat, twice per session) led to dramatic and statistically significant increases in yield/efficiency, with most groups achieving near the social optimum. When both mechanisms were available, communication made punishment largely redundant and unused. The inefficacy of punishment is attributed to the complexity of the nonlinear environment and insufficient use of strong punishment. The findings challenge the generalizability of positive punishment effects from linear public goods games to more realistic, nonlinear settings.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in nonlinear common pool resource environments (with concave private benefits and endogenous group costs), enabling peer punishment does not improve efficiency over baseline. Therefore, when predicting the effect of enabling punishment in similar nonlinear games, one should expect little or no efficiency gain, even with a punishment technology that is effective in linear games. The presence of communication is a much stronger moderator, with large positive effects on efficiency. The results suggest that the effect of punishment on efficiency is highly sensitive to the payoff structure (linear vs nonlinear), and that control efficiency alone is not sufficient to predict treatment efficiency without considering the game's nonlinearity and the availability of communication.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups had 6 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 30 periods per session; all subjects knew the number of periods in advance.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Anonymous computer chat allowed for 5 minutes before periods 11 and 21 in communication treatments. Communication had a large positive effect on efficiency.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution/extraction choice was continuous: decision number between 0 and 125.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Not a standard linear MPCR; group payoff is a linear function of total extraction, but private benefit is concave. Social cost per unit extraction is 0.3 (6 x 0.05), private cost is 0.05.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Each deduction point costs punisher 0.5 Experimental dollars and reduces target's payoff by 1.5 (3:1 fine-to-fee ratio).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `less_positive` under this dimension. Punishment technology: up to 5 points per target, 3:1 fine-to-fee ratio, multiple targets. This is considered strong/effective in linear VCMs but was ineffective here.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects were told in advance that there would be 30 periods.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each period, subjects saw the decisions of all group members, the group total, and their own payoff.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment was anonymous: subjects saw the total deduction points received, but not who assigned them.

## Important Limitations
- The environment is a nonlinear common pool resource game, not a standard linear public goods game; results may not generalize to linear settings.
- All subjects were university students, which may limit external validity.
- Communication was exogenously imposed and limited to two 5-minute sessions; endogenous or more frequent communication may have different effects.
- Punishment was always anonymous; effects of identifiable punishment were not tested.
- No reward or positive sanctioning mechanism was included, so results do not address the effect of rewards.
- The study does not vary punishment cost or effectiveness within this experiment, so cannot speak to their marginal effects in nonlinear settings.
