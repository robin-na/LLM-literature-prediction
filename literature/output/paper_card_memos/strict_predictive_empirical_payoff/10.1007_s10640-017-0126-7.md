## Outcomes Measured
Primary outcome type: efficiency_or_payoff
Payoff-related outcomes:
- average group earnings
- average individual earnings
- group payoff
- public good provision (total group contribution)
- comparison to theoretical efficiency
Non-payoff outcomes:
- contribution rates
- frequency of free-riding
- frequency of punishment/reward
- variation in contributions
- expectations about others' contributions
- investment in enforcement mechanism
Outcome notes:
The main dependent variables are group and individual earnings, group public good provision, and the effect of the mechanism on these. Behavioral outcomes such as contribution rates and sanctioning frequency are also reported, but efficiency and payoff are central.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment demonstrates that subjects are willing to pay to establish a norm enforcement mechanism that punishes or rewards deviations from the group average contribution. With the mechanism, average group contributions to the public good are substantially higher than in the baseline, and average earnings are also higher. The mechanism is almost always implemented, and its strength (enforcement parameter) is positively associated with higher public good contributions. Over time, groups learn to reduce over-investment in the mechanism. The mechanism treatment results in more stable and higher efficiency (measured as group earnings relative to the maximum possible).

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct, high-quality evidence that enabling an endogenous, average-oriented punishment/reward mechanism in a repeated public good game with three players and ten rounds increases both group efficiency and average earnings compared to a control without such a mechanism. The design closely matches standard PGGs, and the efficiency improvement is robust. The findings support predicting a more positive effect of punishment/reward mechanisms on efficiency, especially when the mechanism is endogenously funded and targets deviations from the group average. The results are most directly applicable to small, fixed groups with repeated interaction and endogenous institution formation.

- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. Groups of 3 players in both treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 10 consecutive payoff-relevant rounds (plus 2 practice rounds).
- chat: The paper mentions this dimension but gives limited predictive guidance. No communication or chat between players; groups are anonymous and fixed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices are continuous (0 to 50 LD), not all-or-nothing.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Nonlinear payoff function; marginal per-capita return is not constant but can be inferred from the payoff function. Each unit contributed increases group payoff by 1 LD, but private return is less than 1.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment/reward is funded by voluntary contributions in stage 1; cost is endogenous and comes from the group’s own endowment. The cost is the amount contributed to the mechanism.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Punishment/reward effectiveness is determined by the enforcement parameter beta, which is a function of total group investment in the mechanism. Higher beta increases the impact of deviations from the average.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. The mechanism both punishes below-average and rewards above-average contributions.
- reward cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward is funded from the same pool as punishment; cost is the group’s voluntary contribution to the mechanism.
- reward technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. Reward effectiveness is determined by the enforcement parameter beta, same as punishment.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game has 10 rounds; fixed and known in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each stage, players are shown group contributions, average contributions, incomes of others, and breakdowns of earnings.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment/reward is anonymous and based on deviation from group average; no identification of individual punishers or rewarders.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment uses small, fixed groups of three; results may not generalize to larger or changing groups.
- The mechanism is both punishment and reward, and is average-oriented; effects may differ for pure punishment or other targeting rules.
- The cost and effectiveness of the mechanism are endogenous and may not match exogenous or centrally imposed punishment schemes.
- The setting is a laboratory experiment with student subjects, which may limit external validity.
- The payoff function is nonlinear and differs from standard linear PGGs, which may affect comparability.
- No communication is allowed; effects of chat or communication are not tested.
