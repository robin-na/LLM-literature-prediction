The main dependent variables are average payoff and contribution rate. Efficiency is discussed as the average payoff relative to the maximum possible given group size. Non-payoff outcomes such as contribution rate and exclusion frequency are also analyzed.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
In a repeated linear public goods game with groups of 5, the option to exclude (ostracize) members is introduced either costlessly (B10) or with a 20% endowment cost to all (B8). Groups can vote to implement the exclusion institution (endogenous) or have it imposed (exogenous). The exclusion institution leads to significantly higher contributions in all treatments. When exclusion is costless (B10), average payoffs (efficiency) are higher with exclusion than without. When exclusion is costly (B8), average payoffs are lower with exclusion than without, despite higher contributions. The exclusion mechanism is used almost exclusively to remove the lowest contributors, and the threat of exclusion increases cooperation among those who remain. The effect of exclusion on efficiency is positive when costless, but negative or neutral when costly. Endogenous and exogenous implementation yield similar results.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides direct evidence that the effect of punishment (here, exclusion/ostracism) on efficiency in public goods games depends critically on the costliness of the punishment institution. When exclusion is costless, enabling punishment increases both contributions and efficiency. When exclusion is costly (reducing endowments), enabling punishment increases contributions but does not improve efficiency and may reduce it. The marginal per-capita return (MPCR), group size, and the presence/absence of communication are all specified, allowing for direct mapping to prediction tasks. The results are robust to endogenous vs exogenous institution choice. For prediction, the key moderator is whether the punishment institution imposes a cost on all group members; if so, efficiency gains are not realized even if cooperation rises.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups have 5 players (N=5) in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. 20 rounds total, divided into 4 phases of 5 rounds each.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No chat or communication between players is allowed.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players choose how much to contribute from their endowment.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 in all treatments.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `mixed` under this dimension. Two conditions: costless exclusion (B10, no endowment reduction) and costly exclusion (B8, 20% endowment reduction for all if exclusion institution is chosen).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Exclusion is by majority vote; excluded players cannot contribute or benefit from the public good for the rest of the phase, but keep their endowment.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the number of rounds in each phase (5), and the total number of phases (4) is implied by the design.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. After each round, individual contributions are displayed in random order (not linked to identities), so players see all contributions but cannot track individuals over time.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players are informed about the number of exclusion votes they receive, but not who voted for them (anonymity of punishment source).

## Important Limitations
- The exclusion institution is not a standard monetary punishment; excluded players lose access to the public good but keep their endowment.
- The cost of the exclusion institution in the B8 treatment is implemented as a reduction in endowment for all, not as a per-use cost.
- No communication or chat is allowed, which may limit generalizability to settings with communication.
- The experiment uses student subjects in a lab setting, which may not generalize to field or real-world environments.
- The exclusion mechanism is phase-based: excluded players rejoin at the start of the next phase, which may differ from permanent exclusion in other studies.
- Contribution information is shown in random order, preventing reputation tracking across rounds.
