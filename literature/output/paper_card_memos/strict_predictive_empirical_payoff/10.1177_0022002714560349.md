Outcome notes:
The main dependent variable for the prediction task is average total contribution, which is a direct proxy for efficiency in this design. Group payoff is not reported as a separate variable but is directly determined by total contributions and the payoff structure. Participation and punishment behavior are also reported.

## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The experiment shows that enabling punishment of both nonparticipants and noncompliant participants (i.e., enforcing both participation and compliance) leads to a substantial increase in total group contributions (efficiency) when punishment is introduced. In contrast, if only one type of free riding can be punished (either only insiders or only outsiders), introducing punishment does not increase efficiency. In the forced participation (FP) control, punishment increases efficiency as in standard PGGs. Thus, the effect of punishment on efficiency is conditional: it is positive only when both participation and compliance can be enforced, and negligible or even negative otherwise.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that the effect of punishment on efficiency in public-goods-like games depends on whether the game design allows for both participation and compliance enforcement. If the design allows punishment of both nonparticipants and noncompliant participants (or, in standard PGGs, if participation is forced), enabling punishment is likely to increase efficiency. If only one type of free riding can be punished, enabling punishment will not increase efficiency. For prediction, the presence or absence of both enforcement channels is a critical moderator. Control efficiency (without punishment) is not sufficient to predict treatment efficiency unless the enforcement regime is specified.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All groups had 4 players (n=4) in all treatments.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each treatment had 20 periods (rounds): 10 without enforcement, 10 with enforcement.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat between players was allowed; interaction was anonymous.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution choices were continuous (subjects chose how much to contribute from their endowment).
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. Marginal per-capita return (mpcr) is 1.6/4 = 0.4 in the main VP treatments (alpha=1.6, n=4). In FP, alpha is not specified but design is otherwise similar.
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punisher pays 1 unit per punishment point assigned.
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the punished player's payoff by 3 units (punishment effectiveness = 3).
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects knew the total number of periods (20) in advance.
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. Subjects received feedback on contributions and punishments made by other group members after each period.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment assignments were public within the group, but identities were anonymized and columns were rotated to prevent reputation building.

## Important Limitations
- The experimental environment is highly stylized and may not generalize to real-world settings, especially international agreements.
- Subjects could change participation status every round, unlike most real-world MEAs where participation is more stable.
- No explicit contribution commitments were made by participants, which differs from many real-world agreements.
- Only negative sanctions (punishment) were studied; no positive incentives or rewards.
- The marginal per-capita return and punishment technology are fixed and may not represent all relevant parameter spaces.
- Group size was fixed at 4; effects may differ with larger groups.
