## Main Findings Relevant To Prediction
The study experimentally compares three public goods game treatments: (1) Baseline (unrestricted punishment, full feedback), (2) Full R. (restricted/legitimate punishment, full feedback), and (3) Partial R. (restricted/legitimate punishment, partial feedback). Restricting punishment to only allow higher contributors to punish lower contributors (legitimate punishment) increases both cooperation and efficiency compared to unrestricted punishment. The highest efficiency and earnings are observed in the Full R. treatment, where both legitimacy and full feedback are present. In the Baseline, a significant share of punishment is antisocial, which reduces efficiency. When feedback about high contributors is removed (Partial R.), the positive effect of legitimate punishment on efficiency and cooperation is greatly reduced. Thus, both legitimacy and feedback are necessary for punishment to improve efficiency.

## Prediction Guidance
This paper provides strong evidence that, in a standard repeated public goods game with punishment, restricting punishment to legitimate cases (i.e., only higher contributors can punish lower contributors) and providing full feedback about all group members' contributions both increase efficiency and group earnings compared to unrestricted punishment. If the control (no-punishment) efficiency is known, enabling unrestricted punishment may not increase efficiency due to antisocial punishment, but enabling legitimate punishment with full feedback is likely to yield a substantial efficiency gain. The effect is much weaker or absent if feedback about high contributors is not provided. The findings are directly relevant for predicting treatment efficiency from game design dimensions, especially regarding the structure of punishment and information feedback.

## Design Dimensions Highlighted In This Paper
- player count: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use groups of 4 players.
- number of rounds: The paper directly informs this dimension. All treatments use 20 rounds.
- chat: The paper directly informs this dimension. No communication or chat is allowed between players.
- all-or-nothing contribution: The paper directly informs this dimension. Contribution is continuous: players can contribute any amount from 0 to 20 tokens.
- mpcr: The paper directly informs this dimension. MPCR is 0.4 (each token contributed returns 0.4 to each of 4 players).
- punishment cost: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment cost is convex: e.g., 1 point costs 1, 2 points cost 2, 3 points cost 4, up to 10 points costing 30 (see Table 2).
- punishment technology: The paper directly informs this dimension. Each punishment point reduces the target's earnings by 10% of their stage 1 payoff, up to 100%.
- reward availability: The paper directly informs this dimension. No reward mechanism is available; only punishment is possible.
- knowing when the game ends: The paper directly informs this dimension. Players know the game lasts 20 rounds (finitely repeated).
- seeing peer outcomes: The paper directly informs this dimension. It suggests punishment is `more_positive` under this dimension. In Full R. and Baseline, players see all group members' contributions; in Partial R., only average and contributions of lower contributors are shown. Full feedback is necessary for legitimate punishment to increase efficiency.
- seeing punisher identity: The paper directly informs this dimension. Punishment is anonymous; players do not know who punished them.

## Important Limitations
- The experiment is conducted in a laboratory setting with undergraduate students, which may limit generalizability to real-world or field contexts.
- The study only examines groups of size 4 and a fixed MPCR of 0.4; results may differ with other group sizes or payoff structures.
- No reward or positive incentive mechanisms are tested; only punishment is considered.
- The effect of legitimate punishment is only tested with and without full feedback; intermediate or alternative feedback structures are not explored.
- Cultural and demographic diversity is limited to mostly Italian university students.
