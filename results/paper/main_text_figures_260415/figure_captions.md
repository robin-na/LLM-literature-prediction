# Main-Text Figure Captions

## Figure 1

**Benchmark-paper augmentation improves LLM prediction of treatment effects.** Bars show Pearson correlation between predicted and true treatment outcomes across the 20 validation experiments, \(\mathrm{Corr}(y_{\mathrm{true}}, y_{\mathrm{pred}})\). Human wisdom-of-the-crowd bars were computed by averaging complete layperson or expert forecasts for each experiment before correlating with the true outcomes. LLM bars were computed by averaging predictions across 30 repetitions for each model and condition before correlating with the true outcomes. For each LLM, the paired bars compare no augmentation with benchmark-paper augmentation. Error bars show percentile bootstrap 95% confidence intervals from resampling the 20 experiments. Horizontal reference lines mark the no-treatment outcome baseline and the estimated noise ceiling. Stars indicate whether the paired bootstrap confidence interval for the augmentation-induced change in correlation excludes zero: * 95%, ** 99%, *** 99.9%.

## Figure 2

**Augmentation performance varies across inputs, and LLMs partly agree on which inputs help.** Panel A shows the distribution of augmented prediction performance, \(\mathrm{Corr}(y_{\mathrm{true}}, y_{\mathrm{pred}})\), when each model is augmented with one of 2,011 individual papers or one of 717 paper collections. Boxes show the median and interquartile range, whiskers show the 5th to 95th percentiles, and diamond markers show the corresponding no-augmentation performance. The vertical dotted line marks the estimated noise ceiling. Panel B shows cross-LLM agreement in item-level augmentation performance, computed as Pearson correlation between LLMs across shared augmented papers or collections. Lower-triangular heatmaps are shown because the agreement matrices are symmetric. All augmented correlations are computed from mean predictions across repetitions, not from the mean of repetition-level correlations.

## Figure 3

**Metadata characteristics are weak but systematic correlates of augmentation performance.** Points show standardized coefficients from separate multivariable OLS regressions predicting augmented performance, \(\mathrm{Corr}(y_{\mathrm{true}}, y_{\mathrm{pred}})\), for each LLM and input type. Predictors include study type, prestige or visibility proxies, publication timing, journal discipline, and collection scale. Predictors were median-imputed and standardized before fitting, so coefficients represent the expected change in correlation for a one-standard-deviation increase in the predictor, conditional on the other displayed metadata variables. Horizontal intervals show 95% HC3 robust confidence intervals. Number of papers is only defined for paper collections and is therefore marked as not applicable for individual papers. Positive coefficients indicate metadata associated with higher augmented prediction performance; negative coefficients indicate metadata associated with lower augmented prediction performance.
